Two work plans, A and B, were written for the same client job by the head of the engineering department of an AI-native holding. The job's brief comes first, then plan A, then plan B. Judge which plan is better for the team to execute and for the client to receive.

Judge only on substance: does it cover everything the client asked and invent nothing the client did not ask; is it correct (Turkish health-advertising rules and KVKK, keeping the old site's search rankings, the technical choices); can the team execute it from the page alone (pieces, owners, order, dependencies, checks); does it see the real risks and answer each; is what it asks of the client clear and on time. A longer plan is not better for being longer, and a shorter plan is not worse for being shorter. Read no other file and use no database; everything you need is below.

Answer in this form:
1. Better plan: A or B, and how sure you are — clear, slight, or no real difference.
2. Why: the three to six reasons that decided it, each tied to a concrete place in the plans.
3. What to ADD to the better plan: things the other plan has that it lacks, or that neither has. Additions only; do not rewrite what it already says.
4. Errors: anything wrong in either plan — a wrong legal claim, a wrong technical step, a missed requirement.
Keep it under 700 words.

=== THE BRIEF ===
Context: DXB Global is an AI-native digital holding. Its departments are run by AI employees; each department has a head who owns the jobs it takes. The planner was the head of the Engineering department. Team: a frontend developer, a CMS developer, a QA engineer; borrowable: a designer from the Design studio, a Turkish/English copywriter from Content, an SEO specialist from Marketing. The client talks only to the account owner in Sales; anything needed from the client goes through the account owner. Every piece of work is checked before it moves on, and a final check runs against the brief before delivery.

The job (signed; contract value USD 1,500; delivery 21 days from today):
Client: Kadıköy Gülüş Diş Kliniği, a family dental clinic in Istanbul with three dentists. They have an outdated website on a shared host and own the domain guluskadikoy.com.
They want:
1. A new website in Turkish and English: Home, Treatments (8 treatments, one page each), Our Team, About the Clinic, Contact.
2. An appointment request form (name, phone, preferred day, treatment, note) that reaches the clinic by WhatsApp and by e-mail. It is a request, not a booking system.
3. A WhatsApp button and a click-to-call button on every page; Google Maps on Contact.
4. Their Google reviews shown on the home page.
5. The clinic's receptionist must be able to change texts, notes and photos herself, without calling anyone.
6. Turkish health-advertising rules and KVKK (the Turkish data-protection law) respected: a consent text on the form, a cookie notice, no before/after photos, no price promises.
7. Basic SEO for searches such as "Kadıköy diş kliniği" and "implant Kadıköy"; the old site's pages that rank today must not be lost.
8. Hosting on Vercel; the domain stays theirs.
The clinic sends photos of the clinic and the team within a week; the copy is written by us.
The planner was asked: write the work plan your team will execute — the scope as you understand it, the pieces and who does each, their order and dependencies, how each piece and the whole are checked, what is delivered and how it is handed over, the risks and what you do about each, and what you need from the client and when — so the team can start tomorrow from it.
