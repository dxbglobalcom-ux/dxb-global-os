## Report: Nate Herk, "I Had Opus 5.5 Build me the Same App at Every Effort Level"

The video was uploaded on 2026-09-24, two days after Opus 5.5 was released on 2026-09-22. It runs 1598 s (26:38) and had 381,685 views at the time I checked. This test supports one useful rule: **xhigh** is the right level for long, unattended, creative build jobs, and **max** costs much more without being better. It does not justify any routing rule beyond that. Each level was run once and judged by one person on taste.

### How he ran the test
- He used the same PROMPT.md each time, launched with `/goal` and no human in the loop. Each run had its own session and a git worktree named "effort-test" (frame 0:50).
- The task was a walkable third-person 3D tech-conference world built from 105 GB of recordings of his past virtual event. The model was allowed to use Kie.ai for images and video and anything in his "Herk-2" project.
- The judging criteria were creativity, design, physics and overall feel. The model was told to deliver a localhost link and "the exact numbers in this verification process" (frame 1:02).
- He used his Claude subscription. The costs are his conversion to API-billing equivalents, and the method is not shown. The charts were generated by Claude from the session logs (a "Session logs cost analysis" session is visible at 0:50).

**Checked against the official documentation (all dated after 2026-09-22)**
- **OFFICIAL** (effort page, lines 144/148): Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 default to `medium`; other models default to `high`. This matches his "start on medium".
- **OFFICIAL** (effort page, line 163): `xhigh` is for "Long-running agentic and coding tasks (over 30 minutes) with token budgets in the millions". His winning task was a 1h31m unattended agentic build, which is exactly this case.
- **OFFICIAL** (effort page, line 190): for Opus 5.5, "Run an effort sweep on your own evals rather than carrying settings over". The prompting guide (line 106) says "Reserve `xhigh` and `max` for work where you've measured a quality gain." It also says Opus 5.5 "tends to think more per turn than Claude Opus 5, especially at `xhigh` and `max`" (line 103).
- **OFFICIAL** (model-config, "Choose an effort level" table): `max` "may show diminishing returns and is prone to overthinking". `medium` fits "day-to-day engineering work with a clear scope". `high` fits "work where verification matters". At higher levels Claude "tested more edge cases and verified more of its work". His check counts rising from 22 to 51 match the last point.
- **OFFICIAL** (prompting guide, line 93): Opus 5.5 at `medium` "matched or beat Claude Opus 5 at `high`" on multistep repo coding, "in fewer steps and with fewer tokens".
- **OFFICIAL** (model-config, lines 679–687): "Ultracode is a Claude Code setting rather than a model effort level". It "orchestrates dynamic workflows… at whichever effort level the session runs at". `--effort ultracode` also sets `xhigh`, but the `/effort` toggle leaves the level unchanged (v2.1.284+). His frame at 0:50 shows "Opus 5.5 · Ultracode" in the UI, so the actual effort level of his ultracode run cannot be read from the screen.
- **OFFICIAL** (effort page, line 166): `low` is for "Simpler tasks that need the best speed and lowest costs, such as subagents".
- **OFFICIAL** (prompting guide, line 109): changing the top-level effort between requests invalidates the prompt cache. Per-message effort (beta) keeps it.

### C. What it means for the question points

**Point 6 (which model and effort does which step, at what cost)**
- For one long, unattended, creative build of more than an hour, the evidence favours **xhigh** over max. The xhigh run cost about half as much ($25.92 vs $50.38) and took about 60% of the time, and his judgement was that it was better. The official text agrees: xhigh is for "over 30 minutes… millions" and max is "prone to overthinking".
- **max should not be a default anywhere.** It produced the most self-checks (51) and still had visible bugs. More checks did not mean better verified.
- **medium** is a credible default for scoped work. It is the official Opus 5.5 default and produced a "pretty good" app at 48% of xhigh's cost. The holding's routing table should start Opus 5.5 at medium and move up per job class only after measuring, as the docs say. This video is one data point for that, not the measurement itself.
- **low** looks fit only for short, reviewed steps or subagent leaf tasks (official line 166). Unsupervised on an open creative goal, it shipped the most bugs.
- Cost scales faster than tokens (12.9× vs 6.2× at max), so budgets must be set in dollars, not tokens.

**Point 1 (one-off jobs)**
- One prompt containing a goal, a judging rubric and a demand to report "exact numbers" of verification ran all six levels to the end with 0–1 questions. A clear brief with stated done criteria let a single unattended session finish.
- But no run hit "Ferrari" quality in one pass. Even the winner lacked features that other runs had. Delivery still needs a check against the brief, and probably one or two correction rounds (his own remark about high, 25:30).

**Point 7 (who checks)**
- The model's own "checks" count does not predict quality: max had 51 checks and visible bugs, high had 22 checks and was second best. Self-reported verification needs an outside test against the brief.

**Point 5/8 (orchestration and concurrency)**
- No run delegated to subagents unprompted (anecdote). A holding that wants parallel work cannot count on the model fanning out by itself. The orchestrator or the brief has to require it.
- Ultracode's "dynamic workflows" did not visibly start in his runs (anecdote, unverified). It should not be counted on as an orchestration layer, and it would also overlap the "no second job runtime" constraint.

**Point 9 (approval gate)**
- Nothing in the video bears on the approval gate.

### D. Contradictions, doubts, what could not be verified
- **n=1 per level, one judge, subjective taste.** Run-to-run variance at the same level is unknown, so the gap between high and xhigh could be noise. His own mid-video mix-up between high and xhigh (17:00) shows how loose the scoring is.
- **Spoken vs on-screen numbers:**
  - Low: he says $3.98 at 23:00, but the table shows $3.91.
  - Medium: he says "$1244" (caption at 7:00); the table shows $12.44.
  - High tokens: he says "half a million tokens, 59,000"; the table shows 509.3K.
  - Ultracode tokens: he says "66,000"; the table shows 606.2K.
  - I used the table values throughout.
- **The API-equivalent cost method is not shown,** including how cache reads were priced. Cost and token figures come from Claude's own analysis of the session logs, which he did not audit on screen.
- **The ultracode run's effort level is UNVERIFIED.** The UI shows only "Ultracode". He asserts it ran at xhigh "under the hood", which is official only for `--effort ultracode`, not for the toggle.
- **The 950K compaction window** ("or whatever the compaction window is", 21:45) is UNVERIFIED. I did not check the Claude Code auto-compact window value.
- **Medium was slower than high** (1h13m vs 1h7m). He gives no explanation, and with n=1 none can be drawn.
- **The "checks" metric is undefined** beyond "22 times it opened up the browser" (4:00).
- **Subagents were not shown being used** at any level. That contrasts with the official statement that Opus 5.5 sustains "multi-hour… work… with parallel subagents" (prompting guide, line 93). The docs describe what is possible; his runs did not use it.
- **The blog post "Spending your effort"** (claude.dev), linked from the official docs and showing the same tasks run at every level, was not fetched. It would be the stronger source for the next step.

### E. Verdict
The holding should take three things from this test:
1. Opus 5.5 starts at the official `medium` default for scoped steps.
2. `xhigh` is the level for long, unattended build or creation jobs. Here it beat `max` at half the cost and 60% of the time, which matches Anthropic's own guidance.
3. `max` is excluded from defaults. A model's self-reported verification count is not evidence of quality; checking against the brief must come from outside.

The holding should not take three things from it:
- A fixed routing table built from these six single runs. Anthropic's own instruction is to sweep effort on our own job classes.
- Ultracode as a source of free orchestration. It did not visibly orchestrate in his runs, and it would overlap the no-second-runtime constraint.
- The assumption that the model will delegate to subagents by itself. It did not here, so delegation has to be required by the orchestrator or the brief.

The dollar figures are a useful order of magnitude for budgets (about $4–$50 per long autonomous build), but they are unaudited estimates.
