## 1. The principle

**The foundation (B85) is the first job of the build, and its six stages are the spine** — the board's head says so,
and no row is ranked before it. Every other row has three dates, not one: **where its build starts** (the stage that
gives it what it stands on), **where it is proven on the bench**, and **what closes it** — its own closing cell, on
its own evidence. They can fall far apart: the agency is rehearsed on the bench before the key and closes on its
first money after it. So the key turn (B76) waits for every row's *bench proof* and for the whole holding's one run
on the construction copy — never for a closing that only the world after the key can give.

Today's order was drawn on the old engine; its first eighteen ranks repaired that engine one defect at a time. Those
repairs are not run: the foundation writes the part new. But **no row closes "with a stage"** — each keeps what is
its own, and closes on its evidence, accepted only when he has looked (section 2a). Where today's order recorded a
real dependency between rows, it is kept. The board's laws hold: one row after another, each part tried on the bench
and set aside, the key turned once.
