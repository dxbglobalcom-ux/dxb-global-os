# Brief — the holding's motion-graphics skill (2026-11-12)

## The holding

This holding produces high technology, aims at large revenue and will found large companies; its goals are very large.
Its business lines' clients are small, medium and large, reaching up to the biggest clients in the world.

## The business line this job serves first — the DxB Media Studio (board row B43)

**Mission:** the holding's studio and agency for everything about advertising and visual content, end to end — the
idea, the script, casting, shooting and generating films and stills, 3D and animation, short-form and user-style
video, long-form films and documentaries, edit, sound and finish, and whole campaigns run for the client from first
brief to last delivery — for clients of every size, from local businesses to the world's largest brands, each job
made at the depth it deserves. It is one of the holding's revenue engines and works together with the DxB Web Studio.

**Vision:** a studio whose films read as filmed, not generated, and compete for first place in the world's
advertising awards, and that clients of every size choose because what it makes sells for them.

Nothing written for the studio — a plan, a rule, a limit, a decision — may narrow this mission.

## The scope

One skill, `dxb-motion-graphics`: code-driven motion-graphics films (advertisements, product and app showcases,
kinetic typography, logo transformations, 2D and 3D), made by the model, for the Media Studio, the Web Studio, the
agency and the construction's own sessions. It carries the video's method and whatever makes the result better. What
the holding already has is called, not written again; the holding's own studios come first, outside sources when
needed. It must end in an actual film he can look at.

## Facts measured on 2026-11-12 (not to be re-litigated; each can be checked at its source)

- **The video's method**, read whole with its on-screen prompts: `.planning/research/watch-links/2026-11-12-motion/method.md`.
  In short: real high-quality assets in one job folder → a numbered storyboard approved before anything is animated →
  every shot transforms into the next (no cross dissolves) → the picture is locked → sound last (effects placed on
  the beats, voice written to the shots' length, music). Its renderer is one HTML page stepped frame by frame by a
  Node script into ffmpeg; physical products are rendered in Blender, headless. It ran on Opus 5.5 at medium effort.
  The creator's own skill file is behind an e-mail sign-up and was not taken; an unattributed public copy of unknown
  licence was read and is not shipped.
- **Already on this machine:** HyperFrames (his chosen studio tool since 2026-10-15: always the latest version, a
  new version first renders a 5-second test; latest 0.9.38), Remotion 4.0.484 inside OpenMontage, Blender 5.2.1 with
  Cycles on the RTX 5060 Ti (16 GB), ffmpeg with NVENC, ProRes and loudness filters, Chrome and Playwright, the
  studio's media hands (FLUX stills, H3 clips up to 15 s, SeedVR2 enlarging, ffmpeg assembly) and ComfyUI, the
  `kisayol` advertising recipes, ScrollCraft, the Emil Kowalski web-motion skills on the shelf, Poly Haven and
  ambientCG (free HDRIs and materials), 554 font faces but no display fonts.
- **Sound, as it stands:** Voicebox is installed and untested; Voice Studio was cloned on 2026-11-11 and is not
  installed; no sound-effect library is on the disk. **Music:** his rulings — no music in the holding's own work;
  in client work it is decided when the first client comes and is not asked again before then.
- **The skill he rejected** (deleted on his word the same day): it built every scene from Blender primitives with a
  default font on an empty dark frame, used no real asset, no reference, none of the installed engines, and its film
  was a diagram of its own tools.
- **No holding logo or brand kit exists in the repository.**
- **The renderer, measured the same day on HyperFrames 0.9.38 against a Playwright stepper of our own, same page:**
  a canvas page whose picture is a pure function of time (`window.renderAt(t)`) is driven by HyperFrames through its
  documented seek event (`hf-seek`), the root attribute `data-no-timeline` and `window.__hf.buildReady`; the same
  file runs unchanged under a plain stepper. Both put every frame at its own time (≈42 dB PSNR against a direct
  still, the encoding loss) and their stills are pixel-identical. HyperFrames renders 5 s of 1080p in 4.9 s against
  14.3 s, a full 4K render of 5 s in 8.8 s on the graphics card, places sound files at exact times with exact gain,
  and gives stills at chosen times with a contact sheet (`snapshot --at`). One trap: a render cut with `--end`
  silently falls back to software WebGL. It uses its own Chrome 152, a virtual clock (no timers or
  `requestAnimationFrame` loop as the clock), and carries commands that upload (`publish`, cloud deploys).
  Blender 5.2.1: one 1080p frame of a glossy cube, Cycles on OptiX at 64 samples, 4 s; EEVEE 0.7 s.
  `preferences.get_devices()` crashes this build; `get_devices_for_type('OPTIX')` works.
- **Where a skill for both audiences lives** (the `kisayol` precedent of 2026-11-08): the files in
  `packages/gateway/plugins/<name>/` (`.claude-plugin/plugin.json`, `skills/<name>/`), read by the building sessions
  through a link in `~/.claude/skills/` and by the holding's employees through a grant in their profile (`_skills`,
  written from library rows by a register script); its tool in `tools/`, its test in `tests/b43/`.
