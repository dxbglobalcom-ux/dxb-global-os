2. **The target design** — the lead, at `xhigh`: the foundation's architecture, written against what will be built on it.
3. **The verdict table** — each part: kept, repaired or rewritten; who writes it; in what order. Audited blind by Sol

4. **The build** — part by part, test first, on the construction engine (the bench rule). An old part is removed only

The lead's yardstick, eight questions asked of every part (the lead's engineering, not his words):

1. **One truth** — is each fact kept in one place?
2. **One door** — does every act of a kind pass one point: a model call, an outward act, a task's birth, a write?
3. **Who acts** — does every act carry a real identity that the database itself enforces?
4. **The unit fits** — is the part's core unit the one the holding will run on (a job with its master and desk; one Hamza)?
5. **State holds** — is a change and its record one step; does a restart lose nothing?
6. **It tells** — does it say what happened as events a live surface can stand on, or must it be polled?
7. **It rebuilds** — can it be built from nothing to the same state, and is its design written and current?
8. **The tests hold the target** — do they assert what the contract wants, or what the code happens to do?
