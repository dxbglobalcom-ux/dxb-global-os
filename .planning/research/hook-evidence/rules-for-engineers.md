## The old card's own notes (grades, duplicates, who paid)

- **Grades.** A = randomised tests of at least two different owners agree. B = one good test (more than 10,000 people), or a count across many tests, or several keyed/split tests whose size is not printed. C = agreeing experience of trusted experts with results, no test of the rule itself.
- **"Randomised" here** includes A/B tests and split runs where people were shown one version by chance. **"Keyed"** = replies counted per ad, not always side by side.
- **Counted once.** These rows describe the same test or study, so they are one source: r-abarsiv-022 = r-chatgpt-002 · r-abarsiv-012 = r-chatgpt-003 · r-abarsiv-006 = r-davranis-006 · r-abarsiv-016 = r-davranis-044 · r-bilimsoz-029 = r-davranis-031 · r-klasik-008 = r-schwartz-013 · r-abarsiv-005 and r-davranis-007 (both Wikipedia) · r-platform-001…005 = r-uzman-010…014 (all one Motion report) · every "Upworthy" row (r-bilimsoz-005, 007, 018, 020–026, 034–039, 045 and others) = one publisher's archive, 2013–15.
- **Who paid.** [seller] = the number comes from someone who sells the space, the tool or the service. Not tagged each time: the 6,700 shop experiments (r-davranis-057) are the testing firm Qubit's, the NextAfter tests (most r-abarsiv rows) an agency's own archive, Caples, Hopkins, Schwartz, Sugarman, Halbert and Ogilvy their own accounts.
- **Never.** Every deadline, stock figure, crowd number, "already yours" and flaw must be true. That is also what the rows show: r-abarsiv-014, r-hopkins-007, r-caples-016 (its advice to hide a call is not used), r-davranis-053.
