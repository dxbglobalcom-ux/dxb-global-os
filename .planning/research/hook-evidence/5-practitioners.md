# Practitioners and counted studies of short-video hooks (slice "uzman"), as of 2026-11-25

Gathered, not judged. Grades follow the brief (A field experiment ... F experience). Everything below was opened at the source unless marked UNVERIFIED-SNIPPET or "abstract only". Almost every counted study is a vendor blog or preprint (2025-2026), not peer-reviewed; N and method are stated by the vendor.

## LIST 1 - Studies that counted many videos

For each: who/when/URL - N - how winners were defined - what won and by how much - weakness (as the source or the method states it).

**1. Paddy Galloway + Chris Gileta, "We studied 3.3 billion views to decode the YouTube Shorts algorithm" (Twitter/X thread, 2023-04-14).** https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html (read through Takeaway 4 of 25 tweets). Grade B. N = 33 channels, 5,400 Shorts, 3.3 B views. Winner = more views. Findings: Shorts with viewed-vs-swiped-away (VVSA) under 60 % "rarely performed well", best ones 70-90 %; AVD above 50 s averaged 4.1 M views; likes/shares/comments showed no strong relation to views; Takeaway 2 in his words: "it's important to make your first second really punchy and engaging to hook viewers early into the video. Treat your intro like a thumbnail." Weakness: 33 channels, correlational, no hook-type coding, "initial findings (subject to more testing)". Read: thread.

**2. Trender, "Reels Hook Study: 231,626 Instagram Reels" (updated 2026-09-18).** https://www.trndr.app/blog/reels-hook-performance-study-2026 (read whole). Grade B. N = 231,626 Reels, 2,439 accounts (>=20 posts), 52 hook techniques classified by Trender's model, 12 months to 2026-08-14. Winner = views / the same account's median ("multiple"). Results: strongest "When you're the kind of person who..." 1.29, bad news up front 1.29, reacting 1.27, expectation vs reality 1.26, "You know that feeling when..." 1.25; weakest day-N series 0.80, greeting "hey guys" 0.81, aspirational lifestyle 0.88, credentials 0.90; "how to do X" 0.99 (17,999 Reels), numbered tip list 0.97. Recognition family 1.18 (29,075 Reels) vs instructional 0.94 (69,421); within 1,199 accounts using both, recognition beat instruction for 74.4 % of creators, median gap +33 % (74,126 Reels). Use frequency vs performance correlation -0.35. Weakness (their words): model-classified, no hand-validated sample, tracked-account sample, correlation, "3x threshold is ours", TikTok not covered, 30-180-day-old posts narrow the gap from 25 % to 20 %.

**3. Postdom, "What makes a video go viral? Data from 1,272 short videos" (2026-10-05).** https://postdom.com/research/what-makes-a-video-go-viral (read whole). Grade B. N = 1,272 TikToks/Reels (1,120 TikTok), 160 accounts, 59 features; views / account median; multiplier = videos with the feature vs same accounts' videos without. Results: hook written on screen 1.79x (95 % range 1.22-2.69; 500 videos, 82 accounts), spoken-only 0.62x; job/role hook ("as a dentist...") 3.16x (1.15-7.86; 30 videos, 18 accounts); first frame a real place 2.28x, product close-up 0.56x, usual presenter 1.40x, different person 0.69x; listicle 2.12x, story 1.50x, one uncut take 0.69x; hook length no effect (0.83-1.08x); typical account's best video 42x its median, 84 % of accounts had a 10x video. Weakness (theirs): "sample over-represents each account's best and worst videos", directional only. Data CC BY 4.0 (see (d)).

**4. Scrptly, "We took 4,385 viral Reels apart frame by frame" (2026-11-08).** https://scrptly.io/learn/reel-hook-study (read whole). Grade C-B. N = 4,385 Reels (4,365 with opening line + views), posted 2022-03-31 to 2026-11-03, median video 143,800 views. Winner = median views per bucket, share passing 1 M. Results: hook 1-4 words median 342,814 views (32.8 % pass 1 M, 348 videos) vs 19+ words 110,350 (18.2 %, 396 videos), i.e. 3.1x; question hook 170,447 vs 141,286 (+21 %); digit in hook 106,208 (425 videos) vs 150,236; "you" no effect (143,713 vs 143,890); "I" 136,879 vs 152,812; Reels under 10 s median 30,836 vs 203,801 for 10-20 s. Weakness (theirs): correlation, no retention data, short-hook advantage reverses in social-media and fitness categories, not account-normalised (raw medians across accounts).

**5. UseNadia, "TikTok hooks that work: a study of 25,993 videos" (measured 2026-10-06).** https://usenadia.com/hooks (read top third). Grade B. N = 25,993 videos, 61 accounts (mostly French-speaking; 4 English), 25,789 opening lines. Winner = views / median of the 10 videos before and 10 after on the same account ("banger" 2.0). Results: opening with "If you..." x1.36 raw, x1.29 same subject (14 of 16 accounts, p = 0.004); text on screen from the first second: 66 % of top videos vs 57 % of flops (35 of 48 accounts, p = 0.0006); "viewer leaves with something usable" x1.23; named tool x1.25; talking about your own account x0.74. Weakness (theirs): French corpus, 61 accounts.

**6. ViralMint, "Do Viral Hooks Work? We Matched 102 Viral Shorts With the Same Creator's Flops" (2026-10-25).** https://viralmint.net/blog/viral-hooks-data-study/ (read whole). Grade B (matched design, small N). N = 943 viral Shorts, 20 niches (top of hashtag pages, median 2.9 M views); 180 pairs (hit >= 3x channel median vs the same channel's typical Short), 102 with English speech in both. Results: question 21 % in both hits and flops; "you" 36 % vs 39 %; number 6 % vs 14 %; POV/imagine 14 % vs 24 %; median 13 words in first 3 s in both; no hook pattern separated hits from flops; 327 of 943 (35 %) have no speech in the first 3 s (home improvement/DIY 71 %, productivity 58 %, real estate investing 42 %, cooking 32 %). Hits start at 0.1 s, median 21 s long. Weakness (theirs): spoken words only (no visuals/text), 102 pairs, hashtag-page sample, one-day snapshot.

**7. SavedThat, "Why a Reel goes viral: 2,635 analysed" (2026-11-04; ES/DE/EN versions).** https://savedthat.app/blog/es/por-que-un-reel-se-hace-viral-estudio (read TL;DR + method + first table row). Grade B-C. N = 2,635 Reels/TikToks, 550+ creators, 14 niches; 1,027 "exploded" (>= 3x creator median and >= 10,000 views; median 8x) vs 1,445 normal (0.5-1.5x). A model watched each video. Result stated: hooks, length, captions, calls to action "almost identical"; what differs is the topic. Weakness: English-language sample, AI-coded features; I did not read the per-feature tables.

**8. Kaufman Lab, "Why do some Reels hit a million views and others not?" (research 03, Sept 2026).** https://kaufmanlab.com/research/what-makes-reels-viral/ (read top half). Grade C. N = 265 videos, 2 accounts, 33 features, permutation test (20,000 shuffles, p < 0.0015). Result: none of 33 features (incl. eight opening-second features: caption, question, number with stakes, "you", brand name) related to views for creator 1 (106 videos); topic family vs craft 2.4x (712,777 vs 291,673 median views); CTA at end 16.6 vs 0.9 comments per 1,000 views; all 49 top videos had a plot twist. Weakness: two accounts only.

**9. Retina, "Do TikTok hooks predict views? An AI model scored 3,000 posts" (2026-11-09).** https://retina.dev/log/do-tiktok-hooks-predict-views (read intro + takeaways). Grade B-C. N = 2,881 TikToks, 119 creators. AI-scored hook strength: top quarter of posts at the 50.4th percentile of the creator's views vs 47.1st for the bottom quarter; "looked like an ad" hurt most; speech slightly up, music slightly down; face in first 3 s no difference; post age predicts views as well as the model.

**10. HookTrend, "State of short-form video, November 2026" (2026-11-14, Spanish).** https://hooktrend.com/es/reports/state-of-short-form-november-2026 (read the 11 headline findings). Grade B for base rates. N = 199,454 TikTok + Shorts videos, 17,384 creators (human creators only), Nov 2025-Nov 2026; x = views / median of the creator's last 30 videos. Base rates: median video 1.0x; 22.1 % reach 2x, 8.5 % reach 5x, 4.6 % reach 10x; TikTok 11.9 % reach 5x vs Shorts 4.3 %; TikToks under 15 s 15.0 % vs 1-3 min 10.6 %; posting day no effect (0.6 pp). Not hook-specific; useful as the denominator for the 10x table below.

**11. Mishra (SocArXiv/OSF), "The Science of the First Three Seconds ... Viral TikTok Hooks" (2025-08-08, v2).** https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/rj2mz_v1 - ABSTRACT ONLY. Grade B-C. N = 46,605 TikTok hooks, 20+ categories. Abstract: emotional hooks about 3.2 % of posts but higher comments/shares; "Curiosity gaps and urgency statements outperform direct address or instructional phrasing across most categories"; optimal hook length about 9 words (news) to about 90 (entertainment). CC BY 4.0. Full PDF not retrieved.

**12. OpusClip, "Anatomy of a Viral TikTok in 2026" (2026).** https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026 (read key sections). Grade C (vendor, clips that went through their own engine). 34,635 clips for hook types, 14,213 storyline, 10,598 tone, Jan-Mar 2026; plus 1,449 TikToks scraped via Apify across 10 niches. "Project & Product Showcase" hooks average 6,037 views per clip, "2x the lowest-ranked hook type"; story-setup openers lowest; 80.2 % of viral-tier clips have burned-in captions. Weakness: raw views, not account-normalised; "viral tier" = top 10 % by views.

**13. Vidmob x TikTok, "The Science of the Hook" (2024).** https://vidmob.com/hubfs/Vidmob_May2024/Pdf/Vidmob-x-Tik-Tok-Hook-Analysis-.pdf (read whole, 4 pages). Grade B-D (paid ads, platform partner). N = 1,678 TikTok ads, 7.3 B impressions, 2023-01-01 to 2023-10-15, awareness objective, KPI 2-second and 6-second view-through rate. Results vs average: celebrity talent -13 % 6s VTR; talent looking at camera +14 % 2s VTR and +5 % 6s; shot outdoors -26 % 6s; human + product in first 6 s +5 %; brand-logo overlay -14 % 6s, product shot +17 % 6s. Measures watching, not buying.

**14. Pear Growth, "TikTok Shop Video Hooks: What Our Data Shows" (2026-10-15).** https://www.peargrowth.com/blog/tiktok-shop-video-hooks-hook-rate (read whole). Grade B (own paid campaigns, buying measured). N = 1,393 GMV Max videos in three UK TikTok Shop ad accounts, 90 days to 2026-10-14, >= 1,000 impressions each. Median video kept 31 % of impressions past 2 s, 10.4 % past 6 s, 1.8 % to the end. Within each account top quarter vs bottom quarter by 2-s view rate: 49 % vs 17 % kept past 2 s; 6-s rate 17.3 % vs 6.0 % (2.9x); click rate 5.1 % vs 1.5 % (3.3x); orders per 1,000 impressions 0.70 vs 0.53 (middle video) and 0.82 vs 0.73 (all impressions) - the buying gap is far smaller than the clicking gap; clickers on weak-hook videos converted better per click (3.8 % vs 1.8 %). Their own caveat: no control group, products/creators/offers differ, paid traffic only. A separate check of 113 manual video ads showed the same pattern (4.0 s vs 1.6 s held).

**15. BEEM (Japanese), "TikTok first 3 seconds: measured on 251 videos" (2026-10-26).** https://beem-inc.net/beemedia/tiktok-saisho-3byo/ (read the summary + first table). Grade C-B (one company account). N = 251 videos of @beem_official since 2023-01. Median share of starters still watching: 83 % at 1 s, 64 % at 2 s, 57 % at 3 s, 50 % at 5 s; biggest drop 1-2 s (19 points); video length did not change the 3-s hold (56-58 %); the quarter with 3-s hold above 60 % had 22.6 % "hit" videos vs 9.6 % for the group at or below 53 %. Their own caution: cannot tell which came first. Also analysed 300 videos from 244 companies: 47.0 % open by stating what the video is about.

**16. ReelsBot on public play logs (2026-10-30).** https://reelsbot.co/blog/first-three-seconds (read key sections). Grade B for the curve, platform not TikTok. 2.2 M plays (Kuaishou, KuaiRand, 2022) and 33.6 M plays (VK, 2025). 67.9 % of recommended plays still going at 3 s, 46.0 % at 10 s; random-slot plays 37.2 % at 3 s, median play 2.1 s; most swipes at 1.2-1.3 s; the 7-s hold tracked reach better than the 3-s hold (rho 0.37 vs 0.28; partial rho for 3 s given 7 s = -0.02). Correlational, a vendor's reading of public data.

**17. Shortform Kit, "How top creators open a Short" (measured 2026-11-17).** https://shortformkit.com/short-form-video-hook-study-2026/ (read key findings). Grade C (descriptive, not outcome-linked). N = 167 videos (57 Reels, 51 TikToks, 59 Shorts) from 21 large creators, newest 3 per platform: first cut at median 2.6 s (only 14 % cut within 1 s); 75 % spoken, 84 % of those speak within 2 s; 58 % show text on the first frame. CSV CC BY 4.0. German version exists (https://shortformkit.com/de/studie-hooks-reels-tiktok-shorts-2026/, not read).

**18. Overseer, "We compared 1,178 YouTube Shorts hooks with 2,788 long-form hooks" (2026-08-13).** https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-shorts-hooks-study (read intro + conclusions by grep). Grade C. N = 3,966 English hooks, 1,032 channels, videos >= 1 M views. Pooled: 23.4 % of Shorts hooks are questions vs 11.8 % long-form; inside the same channels the gap "almost completely disappeared". A caution about pooled comparisons, not a hook-effect study.

**19. Buffer, 2.7 M Reels length study (reported 2026-11-12).** https://reachbrief.com/articles/buffer-s-2-7-million-reel-study-90-seconds-to-3-minutes-wins-reach (read whole, secondary report of Buffer). Grade B on length only. 2,730,166 Reels, Jan 1-Aug 15 2026, each account vs itself: 90-179 s +7.4 % reach, 30-59 s +2.7 %, 60-89 s +1.6 %, buckets under 90 s within about 3 % of baseline; 180 s+ -49.3 % reach. Not a hook study.

**20. Youmi / Douyin cleaning-products report (Chinese, 2026 Q3-Q4).** https://youcloud.com/report/s/iWsqA73/ (read headings + grep). Grade E-F. 364 products, 704 ad creatives from the Youmi (有米有数) free-tier ad library, plus a "95万" line pool of golden-3-second scripts; claims "3s完播率 ... 钩子质量的唯一硬指标" (3-s completion rate is the only hard metric of hook quality) and recommends 3-4 different openings per creative raced against each other. No outcome numbers per hook type were found in what I read.

**Also opened, descriptive only:** TokTranscript, 1,624 TikTok openings, median first caption 2.12 s (https://toktranscript.com/blog/tiktok-first-three-seconds-study, read intro/method). ShortLens (own customers' Shorts: retention did not predict which Short won; https://shortlens.app/research/why-better-retention-lost, intro only, UNVERIFIED beyond the claim). ## (a) Contradictions between sources

1. Hook form: Trender (231,626 Reels) and UseNadia (25,993 TikToks, 61 accounts) and Postdom (1,272, 160 accounts) find hook-form differences against the account's own median (recognition "you know that feeling" 1.29, "If you..." x1.36, on-screen text 1.79x). ViralMint (102 matched pairs), SavedThat (1,027 exploded vs 1,445 normal, 550 creators) and Kaufman Lab (265 videos, 2 accounts, 0 of 33 features) find hooks of hits and flops of the same creator nearly identical; Retina finds a small link (50.4th vs 47.1st percentile). The three that agree on a difference are the ones whose unit is "all videos of an account"; the matched-pair ones compare only hits against typical videos.
2. On-screen text: Postdom 1.79x (written on screen) vs 0.62x (spoken only) and UseNadia 66 % of tops vs 57 % of flops (35 of 48 accounts, p = 0.0006) agree with each other; ViralMint (spoken words only, did not measure text) is silent on it.
3. Greeting / self-introduction: Trender, greeting "hey guys" 0.81 (3,470 Reels) and credentials 0.90; Dara Denney (ads) says the founder introducing themselves "often will become a top performer" (7:25-7:46, her ad accounts, no numbers); Postdom job/role hook "as a dentist..." 3.16x (30 videos, wide range 1.15-7.86).
4. Numbers in the hook: Scrptly digit hooks 29 % fewer median views (425 videos); ViralMint numbers 6 % in hits vs 14 % in flops (p about 0.08); Postdom listicle format 2.12x; Trender numbered tip list 0.97.
5. Product in the first frame: Postdom product close-up 0.56x vs OpusClip product/outcome showcase hooks best (6,037 average views, 2x the lowest type, not account-normalised), Vidmob product shot +17 % 6-s VTR, Pear Growth asks for the product in frame 1 and measured fewer buyers per click on broad-curiosity hooks.
6. Very short videos: Scrptly Reels under 10 s median 30,836 vs 203,801 (10-20 s); Buffer buckets under 90 s within about 3 % of baseline; HookTrend TikToks under 15 s reach 5x more often (15.0 % vs 10.6 % for 1-3 min).
7. Hook length: Scrptly 1-4 words 3.1x the median views of 19+ words; Postdom hook length no effect (0.83-1.08x); ViralMint median 13 words in both hits and flops.
8. Clicks vs buying: Pear Growth top-quarter hooks had 3.3x the click rate but orders per 1,000 impressions only 0.70 vs 0.53.
9. Pooled vs within-channel: Overseer shows a 23.4 % vs 11.8 % question gap that nearly vanishes within the same channel; any pooled hook comparison in this list is exposed to the same effect.
