## 1. When to touch a live ad set — the waiting rules (sharper, partly contradicts)

**[HER]**

- Do not check hourly and edit daily. "There's at least a two day window where Facebook is recalibrating…
  anytime you are increasing the budget, decreasing the budget, testing new ads, you essentially restart the
  learning phase" (`meyt-EdFB7QFwAzM @00:40-01:30`). Launching new ads one day and raising the budget the
  next resets twice, and then "it's harder for you to know if your changes are actually working" (`@02:00`).
- **Daily swings are normal.** "If the performance all of a sudden drops 50% for a particular day I don't even
  touch that… when the conversions are down 50% for five days straight then you know you have a problem"
  (`meyt-EdFB7QFwAzM @02:50-03:10`).
- **Reported conversions arrive late:** "go back two days earlier and see that your conversions are higher
  that day than when you were initially looking" (`@03:10`).
- **Never kill before three to four days**, "whether you're spending twenty dollars a day or a thousand";
  CBO needs two days; "I would even go as far to let things run for seven days", because some results come
  from people who opted out of tracking (`meyt-EdFB7QFwAzM @08:00-10:00`).
- **Read windows:** "if you are analyzing results for anything less than seven days you're royally [wrong]…
  we will only look at data over a seven day period, nothing less, sometimes even 14" (`meyt-NZjDPYgpiU4
  @01:30-02:00`).
- **The one exception — a stop-loss:** a large budget (over $500) whose cost per result is normally about $50
  jumps to $450 after two days: turn it off or change it, "you definitely have enough impressions to know"
  (`meyt-NZjDPYgpiU4 @02:00-03:00`).
- **A budget increase costs a dip:** $300 → $350 "I'm gonna see a slight decline in sales that day and
  potentially the next day… nine times out of ten your sales will dip"; panic only at "a continuous decline in
  performance for seven plus days straight" (`meyt-EdFB7QFwAzM @05:50-06:50`).
- At least 2,000 impressions on any change before reading it (`meyt-NZjDPYgpiU4 @03:50`); in 2020 she said
  four to five days (`meyt-5meZiQw5JSc @00:50-03:15`).

**[ADDED] What Meta itself says** — read at source on 2026-11-23 (Meta Business Help Center, "About the
learning phase", facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700, and "Significant edits and learning phase",
/business/help/316478108955072):

- An ad set leaves learning "as soon as it can deliver stably. This usually occurs after **about 50 results in
  the week after the ad set's last significant edit**." Ads Manager has a **Last significant edit** column and
  a Results column to count them.
- **Significant edits** (always): any change to targeting · any change to ad creative · any change to the
  optimisation event · **adding a new ad to the ad set** · pausing for 7 days or longer · changing bid strategy.
  **May or may not be significant, by magnitude:** spending limit · bid control, cost-per-result or ROAS goal ·
  **budget amount** ("$100 to $101… isn't likely… $100 to $1000… may"). Under Advantage+ campaign budget, a
  campaign budget change or bid-strategy switch can send several ad sets back to learning; an edit at one ad
  set's level does not reset the others.
- Best practice in Meta's words: wait to edit until learning ends; "only edit… when you have reason to believe
  that the edit should improve performance"; avoid high ad volumes ("when you combine similar ad sets, you
  also combine learnings"); a realistic budget, with no frequent budget changes.

**[TEST]** Her patience is right and Meta's page confirms it. What is **sharper** in Meta's version, and what
the first study did not have at all: the rule is **counted in results, not days** — 50 results in the week
after the last significant edit. Her "four to five days", "two days for CBO" and "2,000 impressions" are
rules of thumb that hold for some budgets and not others; an ad set with 5 purchases a week never leaves
learning however long one waits. Her "adding new ads restarts learning" is exactly Meta's list. Her "+20% is
safe" is not Meta's — Meta gives no percentage, only "by magnitude" (§3).

The **sound rule set** (for skill `ad-change-guard`, REGISTER-CHANGES): before any edit, read the ad set's
status and its results since the last significant edit; classify the edit by Meta's list; hold anything
significant while it learns unless a stop-loss written per client in advance has fired; judge in seven-day
windows; expect a dip after a budget step and judge the step after a week, not a day; re-read the last two or
three days later, because reported conversions grow after the fact.

## 2. Small samples — "you can't make confident decisions based on a small spend" (sharper)

**[HER]** "You'd have much more confidence in the data from spending $500 and 40,000 impressions than… $50 and
5,000." Her worked case (`meyt-NZjDPYgpiU4 @04:50-06:20`): neon signs; campaign A targets businesses, $100,
10,000 impressions, **8 sales**; campaign B targets homeowners, $20, 2,000 impressions, **1 sale**. Double down
on A — but do not write B off: "you don't have enough data about homeowners", so spend a little more on it,
with other creatives and copy, until the hypothesis is proved or disproved. Look at clicks, engagement and
site actions too, not only sales.

**[ADDED] The arithmetic she leaves out** — exact Poisson 95% intervals on the counts:

| Campaign | Spend | Sales | CPA | 95% interval on sales (Poisson) | CPA range it implies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, businesses | $100 | 8 | $12.50 | 3.45 – 15.76 | about $6 – $29 |
| B, homeowners | $20 | 1 | $20.00 | 0.025 – 5.57 | about $3.60 – $800 |

The ranges overlap almost completely: the data cannot say B is worse. Her instinct is right, and the table is
what turns it into a rule a worker can apply — **judge on the count of results, state the interval, and call
nothing a winner or a loser while the intervals overlap**. Impressions are not the sample; results are.

**[TEST]** The first study's skill D (`ad-winner-read`) says "with little spend a why is a guess; state
confidence". This gives it the method. Meta's 50-results line (§1) is the platform's own version of the same
idea.

## 3. Scaling a budget — her 2020-21 method, and what replaces it (contradicts, then new)

**[HER] 2020-21** (`meyt-AiPXyOJcMfo`, `meyt-6IjrjqcxHos`, `meyt-EdFB7QFwAzM @04:00-06:50`):

- Never jump ($20 → $100, $200 → $400). Raise by **10-20% at most per day or every two to three days**, and
  only on an ad set that held a good result for two to three days ("at least a 2 ROAS").
- **Horizontal:** duplicate the winning ad set ("three copies… one or two of those three will do really
  well"; the losses are "scaling casualties"); **duplicate-and-raise** by 20-50%; put the best ad sets from
  every campaign into **one CBO campaign at $50-100 a day**, audiences of similar size, at least 5 million
  people, enough budget per ad set.
- Lookalikes of each website event (purchase, add to cart, checkout, payment info) as separate ad sets
  competing in one CBO campaign — "sometimes initiate checkout has a higher ROI" (`meyt-6IjrjqcxHos
  @01:00-03:30`).
- Her model of why duplication works: "at most your ads will reach 10% of [the audience]… each ad set is only a
  slice of the pizza" (`meyt-AiPXyOJcMfo @05:00-07:00`).

**[TEST] — contradicts.** The first study already marks duplication as outdated (§12). Two further points:
(a) the "10% of the audience per ad set" limit is not in any Meta document found and is not how delivery is
described; it is a myth that justified duplication, and duplicates in the same audience bid against each other
(Meta's own advice is the opposite: combine ad sets, §1). (b) Her "+20%" is folklore with no Meta source; Meta
says only that budget changes may reset learning "depending on the magnitude". Her own later captions (Dec
2023-Feb 2024: "those 20-30% increases won't cut it") contradict her 2020-21 rule. Today a step is sized by
what the account can absorb and judged after a week (§1).

**[HER] — the part that lasts** (`meyt-NZjDPYgpiU4 @06:20-09:00`): scaling "is not a linear process": 5× on
$10k does not stay 5× at $15k — "maybe 4.8, 4.9, maybe 4.6", so the return must leave a cushion. Her client
story: $9,000 a month (~$300/day) at about 9× raised to about $450/day with new campaigns and audiences; sales
**fell**, some days to 5×, because the new campaigns **overlapped** the old ones; consolidating and
restructuring brought the increase, slowly. "In some cases you may reach a limit."

**[ADDED] — new: read the step by its marginal return, not the average.** Her own numbers show why:

| | Spend | Average ROAS | Revenue | Extra revenue for the extra $5,000 | Marginal ROAS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before | $10,000 | 5.0 | $50,000 | — | — |
| After, if 4.8 | $15,000 | 4.8 | $72,000 | $22,000 | **4.4** |
| After, if 4.6 | $15,000 | 4.6 | $69,000 | $19,000 | **3.8** |

A 0.4 fall in the average hides a fall to 3.8 on the money just added. The scaling decision compares the
**marginal** return with the client's break-even ROAS (1 ÷ contribution margin: a 40% margin breaks even at
2.5); the step stays while marginal ROAS is above break-even, and the store's own revenue (the MER the site
quotes, §12 of the first study) is the check on the platform's number. Her overlap story adds the first
diagnostic when a step fails: did the new campaigns compete with the old ones for the same people?

## 4. Ads Manager breakdowns — her "secret hack" is the error Meta names (contradicts)

**[HER]** (`meyt-C3DTsPWZL4A @05:20-07:40`) Use **Breakdown** (age, gender, country, placement) to see who
responds; e.g. mostly women 34-55 in the news feed → a new audience of only women 34-55, news-feed placement
only; drop Audience Network. "This is how you create hyper targeted audiences."

**[ADDED] Meta's page "About the 'breakdown effect'"** (facebook.com/business/help/770303663944673, read
2026-11-23): the breakdown effect is "the misinterpretation that our system shifts impressions and spending
into underperforming ad sets, placements or ads." Meta's worked example: $500 across Facebook Stories and
Instagram Stories; Facebook Stories was cheaper early ($0.35 against $0.72 on day 1) but its cost rose faster,
so the system moved the budget; at the end Instagram Stories shows $450 at $1.46 average CPA and Facebook
Stories $50 at $1.10. Judged on the average, the system "chose the worse placement"; judged at the margin it
bought more results. Meta's instruction: with campaign budget, **evaluate at the campaign level**; with
Advantage+ placements, at the ad set level.

**[TEST]** Her 2020 hack is exactly the misreading Meta documents. A segment's average cost per result does
not say what the **next** dollar would buy there; cutting segments by average CPA usually removes cheap
marginal results and raises total cost. Her own 2021 Power 5 praise of automatic placements ("I have never
seen a case where it spends like 50% of my budget in a placement like audience network if there hasn't been any
conversions", `meyt-iqv-6jD1gsA @08:10-09:20`) already points the other way. The sound use of breakdowns: as
a question for the **brief** (who is answering — does a persona or a market show up that the roadmap lacks?),
never as an instruction to narrow targeting. This rule belongs in skills 6 and 30.

## 5. Her 2020-21 testing structure (sharper on the record; outdated as a method)

**[HER]** Test interests one per ad set at micro budgets (~$10/day, about $70 each over seven days), read on a
seven-day window, then put the winners together in one **"super ad set"** at the sum of their budgets ($50/day)
(`meyt-NZjDPYgpiU4 @09:10-11:10`). Test "at least [n]" audiences (the number is lost in the subtitle) with
demographic splits; the travel-agency example builds audiences from proxies (premium travel-card holders, home
owners in relationships aged 30-50, travel-magazine readers) (`meyt-5meZiQw5JSc @05:20-07:20`). Budget for
losing money while learning: "a couple thousand bucks or a couple hundred" (`@08:20`). Dynamic creative
formula, from "$50,000 split testing over five months": ten media (five images, three videos, two GIFs), two
copies ("the magic number"), two headlines, two call-to-action buttons; "evergreen and low maintenance"
(`meyt-iqv-6jD1gsA @11:00-13:10`). Dynamic creative needs about 500 impressions per creative
(`meyt-dBsbkVDLswQ @04:05`).

**[TEST]** This is the record of the method gap 5 of the first study asked for — but the **earlier** one. As a
method it is outdated (interest targeting is now a suggestion; detailed-targeting exclusions removed; dynamic
creative absorbed into flexible formats — first study §12). What survives: a **test budget is planned as a
cost of learning before the first ad runs**, stated to the client in advance (her line to every client:
"you have to be comfortable with losing money out the gate"). That sentence is worth keeping in our

## 6. After iOS 14.5 — audiences from what people do on the platform (sharper)

**[HER]** (`meyt-WYqyukj8FY0`, whole; `meyt-h0Bndfo19R4 @10:10-11:50`; `meyt-iqv-6jD1gsA @15:10-17:40`)

## 7. Copy (sharper and contradicts)

**[HER]** (`meyt-C3DTsPWZL4A`)

- **Urgency** "in all my ads": "free shipping, limited time only. OK so it's slightly misleading but you're also
  not seeing what that time limit is… it's okay to be vague about the timing but you need to create the
  perception that this benefit will disappear" (`@01:03-02:04`).
- Sell what it does for the customer, not the product's cleverness (the self-cleaning bottle rewritten:
  "stop spending all that unnecessary time cleaning your bottle", `@02:04-03:40`) — already in the first study
  (§7.2).
- Lists ("people love lists… BuzzFeed") and a few emojis — "research also shows that ads with emojis increase
  engagement by 25%" (`@04:00-05:10`; no source given).
- "Get $100 off versus get 30% off, because a hundred is a bigger number than 30" (`meyt-5meZiQw5JSc @05:00`).

**[TEST]**
- **Contradicts — fake urgency is refused.** A deadline that does not exist is deception. The EU's Unfair
  Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC lists in Annex I, point 7 — practices unfair *in all
  circumstances* — "falsely stating that a product will only be available for a very limited time, or that it
  will only be available on particular terms for a very limited time, in order to elicit an immediate
  decision". The holding's boundary (no deception) says the same everywhere. The sound version: urgency only
  when it is real — a dated sale, real stock, a real season — and the date written. This is a line for the
  compliance check of the brief (first study idea 11, which does not name it).
- **Sharper — showing a discount.** Her reason ("100 is bigger than 30") is wrong as stated: $100 off is the
  bigger offer only when the price is under $333. **[ADDED]** The working rule is Jonah Berger's "Rule of 100"
  (*Contagious*, 2013): under $100, show the discount as a percentage; over $100, as an amount — whichever
  number is larger. In the brief's price reframe ("the math", first study §6.2), write the larger true number.
- The 25% emoji figure is hers, unsourced; not to be repeated.

## 8. Advertising in a crisis — the four needs (new)

**[HER]** (`meyt-QDcglPtOeCo`, June 2020) Keep advertising — attention moved online — but do not run the old
message as if nothing happened, and do not make the crisis the subject. People will read a tone-deaf ad as
"capitalizing on this terrible situation". Speak to what people are short of; she names **four needs: comfort,
staying healthy, feeling relaxed, feeling safe and secure** (`@03:00-04:10`). Show how the product fits the
changed life (her examples: food delivery offering free delivery; a paper brand's donation; IKEA's "stay home"
reframe of home). Her client: food and drink brands; "encouraged customers to stockpile on healthy snacks" —
"a new record ROAS of 24 for four weeks straight" (`@06:40-07:10`; her claim). Statistics in the video (usage
of Meta's apps +70%, social +83%, online shopping +30%, "80% of shoppers bought something from a digital ad")
are given without sources.

**[TEST]** New to the study, and the method is sound: when the context changes, the message is rewritten to
the need, not paused and not exploited. Two repairs. (a) Her "stockpile" angle encouraged hoarding during a
shortage; in the holding's terms an angle that profits from fear or from harm to others fails the brief
(the belief to shift must be one the customer gains by, not one that costs the people around them). (b) Her
statistics are not to be repeated. For a **global** holding the trigger is broader than a pandemic: a war, a
disaster, an election, a currency collapse, a national mourning in one market, a religious season (Ramadan,
the Hajj season) — each changes what an ad may say there. The form: a **context check** on every live brief
for a market where such an event is current (FOR-THE-HOLDING idea 4).

## 9. Ads that start a conversation, and the five-minute reply (new to the ad office)

**[HER]** (`meyt-dBsbkVDLswQ @01:30-03:50`; `meyt-DpMKfpQGjVc @07:10-08:50`) Messenger ads "are working
insanely well": the prospect writes first, "people lining up and knocking on your door". The worst mistake is
to send people to Messenger and not answer. "Businesses that follow up with their leads in under five minutes
are 12 times more likely to close" (her figure); a greeting and automated flows (ManyChat, keyword-triggered
sequences) must be in place; old conversations can be re-contacted with a message ad and an offer.

**[ADDED]**
- The research behind the reply-time claim: Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, "The Short Life of Online Sales
  Leads", *Harvard Business Review* 89(3), 2011 — firms that tried to contact a lead within an hour were nearly
  seven times as likely to qualify it as those that waited longer, and more than 60 times as those that waited
  24 hours (as quoted by secondary sources; the article was not opened). Her "12× in five minutes" is not that
  study's figure; the direction is the same.
- The channel today includes WhatsApp. WhatsApp's Business Solution terms, in force for all from 15 January
  2026, bar **general-purpose** AI assistants and allow bots that serve a specific business function (support,
  sales, orders) — reported by TechCrunch (18 Oct 2025) and others; Meta's terms were not opened (OPEN-QUESTIONS).

**[TEST]** New for the ad office, and it joins two things the library already has: the reply-in-a-minute
worker (skill 39 `first-touch`) and the ad loop. A click-to-message ad whose conversation is answered at once
by a worker that says it is an AI and works only from the signed price sheet is the shape (FOR-THE-HOLDING
idea 3). ManyChat-style keyword flows are the older, scripted form.

## 10. Tools (2020) — what is left of them

**[TEST]**
- Audience Insights was closed on 1 July 2021 (secondary reports; the interest view did not survive in
  Business Suite) — obsolete.
- *Sharper:* the idea under SpyFu is good — **a competitor's paid-search keywords are the words buyers type
  when they are ready to buy**. The holding's decided data tool, DataForSEO, returns a domain's ranked and paid

**[HER]**

  engagement decides distribution; tag brands and locations (`meyt-5HJX0-bvdmA`, whole).
- The profile: say what you do, what you stand for, what to do next; link the company account; "if you clicked
  on your Instagram page would you follow you?… less than three seconds" (`meyt-s6E_zJeY-ac @02:00-05:10`).
- Never "Boost post"; buy followers with **Stories ads in Ads Manager**: traffic objective, the profile URL as
  destination, "swipe up to follow", $5/day, worldwide ("the US is the most expensive country"), broad interest
  over 1 million — "30 to 50 followers a day" (`meyt-s6E_zJeY-ac @05:10-08:40`); part 2: a 1% lookalike of
  people who engaged with the account in the last 365 days, those engagers excluded, worldwide, age from 13
  (`meyt-NaR_dzyYycM`, whole). She contrasts it with bought followers, which risk a shadow-ban.

**[TEST]**
- Hashtag tiers: outdated. Instagram's head said in February 2025 that hashtags do not increase reach, and a
  cap of five hashtags per post was reported in December 2025 (secondary reports; Instagram's own announcement
  not found).
- Follower ads: the mechanics are gone (swipe-up was replaced by link stickers), and two settings are wrong
  for the holding. **Worldwide for cheapness** fills a brand's account with followers from markets it does not
  sell in, which then teaches the platform the wrong audience. **Age from 13**: since February 2023 Meta lets
  advertisers reach under-18s by age and location only, and the holding has no reason to court minors for a
  follower count (Meta, "Continuing to Create Age-Appropriate Ad Experiences for Teens", about.fb.com, Jan 2023).
- *New, small:* the underlying idea — paying for a **seed audience** for a new account, in the market it sells
  to, instead of buying followers — is a legitimate launch step for the holding's own new brands
  (FOR-THE-HOLDING idea 8). Followers count as distribution, never as proof.
