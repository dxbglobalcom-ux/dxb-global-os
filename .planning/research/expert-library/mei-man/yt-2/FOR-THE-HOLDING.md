# Mei Man, her other 19 YouTube videos — for the holding

Studied 2026-11-23. Companion to `PLAYBOOK.md` in this folder (sections cited as §n) and an addition to
`../FOR-THE-HOLDING.md` (cited as "first study, idea n"). What the board and the first study already own is not
repeated; each idea says what it **adds**.

**In one sentence.** These are her 2019-2021 videos, mostly about the media-buying mechanics the first study
already marks outdated; what they add is the part no study had yet — **how to behave toward a live ad account
between briefs**: when to touch it, how to read a small number, how to judge a budget step, and two of her old
habits that Meta's own documentation now names as errors.

---

## The ideas, ranked by value

### 1. The ad-account change guard — nothing is edited without a reason and a date to judge it

- **What.** Her waiting rules (§1) completed with Meta's own learning-phase pages: an ad set learns until about
  50 results in the week after its last significant edit; Meta lists which edits are significant (targeting,
  creative, optimisation event, a new ad, a pause of seven days, a bid-strategy change) and which depend on
  size (budget, caps, goals). Her additions that Meta does not state: a single bad day is noise; five days down
  is a signal; reported conversions grow for two or three days after the fact; a budget step costs a dip; a
  stop-loss written in advance is the only reason to act inside the learning window.
- **Where.** The agency seat (B28) — every client account the office runs, and the holding's own once it runs
  ads. It is the ad-office twin of the web office's `page-change-guard` (register, §6): the same discipline for
  a different asset.
- **Form.** A skill and a gate — `ad-change-guard` (REGISTER-CHANGES, new row) — and a change log per account.
- **What we build.** The rule set as a check a worker runs before proposing any edit: the ad set's status and
  results since its last significant edit (both columns exist in Ads Manager); the edit classified by Meta's
  list; the client's stop-loss (spend at which a zero, or a cost per result at a set multiple of target,
  allows a kill); the seven-day read; a log row — what, why, expected effect, date to judge.
- **First test.** None needs an account: write the guard and run it over Meta's own two help pages and her
  three videos as fixtures — a planted "raise the budget on day 2", "add three ads to a learning ad set",
  "kill after one bad day", "kill at nine times target CPA after two days" must come out hold / hold / hold /
  allow. Then on the first client export (B28 already plans the export-based audit).
- **What it earns or improves.** The commonest way an account loses money while "being managed" is edits that
  keep it learning. A guard that refuses them is what a client cannot get from a busy human buyer, and the log
  is what makes every later result readable.
- **What needs the CEO.** Nothing to build. Any edit in a client's account is made only under that client's
  written mandate; budget increases are the client's money.
- **Risk.** Meta changes its thresholds; the guard reads them from the `platform-change-ledger` row (register,
  §6), not from this study.

### 2. The breakdown rule — never narrow an account by a segment's average cost

- **What.** Her 2020 "secret hack" (§4) — find the age, gender and placement with the best average result in
  Breakdown, then target only that — is the exact misreading Meta's page "About the breakdown effect" names:
  the system spends where the *next* result is cheapest, so the segment with the worse average can be the
  better buy. Meta: judge a campaign with campaign budget at the campaign level, placements at the ad set level.
- **Where.** The funnel-leak read (skill 6) and the winner read (skill 30); and the account audit B28 already
  owns, as a finding: "this account was narrowed by breakdown averages."
- **Form.** A rule inside two skills and an audit finding.
- **What we build.** One line in each skill's "must never"; in the audit, a check for single-placement ad sets
  and narrow demographic fences that were cut after a breakdown, with Meta's page cited in the finding.
  Breakdowns stay useful as a question for the strategy room: does a persona or market answer that the roadmap
  lacks?
- **First test.** Read three public case write-ups of "we cut Audience Network and CPA went up/down"; check the
  guard's reasoning against each. Then on the first client export.
- **What it earns or improves.** It stops a cost increase that looks like an optimisation, and it is a finding
  with Meta's own page behind it — the kind a prospect cannot argue with.
- **What needs the CEO.** Nothing for a client's own account; shown to a prospect it is client-finding and waits
  on his word (decision 9).
- **Risk.** Meta's page speaks of lowest-cost bidding and automation; with cost caps or manual placements the
  logic weakens. The finding says which bid strategy the account uses.

### 3. The conversation ad — an ad that opens a chat answered by our worker within a minute

- **What.** Her Messenger-ads lane (§9): the prospect writes first; the loss is in not answering; research on
  lead response (HBR 2011) puts the gap at roughly seven times between answering within an hour and later.
- **Where.** The agency seat (B28) for clients whose sale is a conversation — clinics, services, real estate,
  education, B2B — and the sales desk (B83), whose `first-touch` skill (39) already answers in under a minute
  and quotes only the signed price sheet. The two have not been joined: no row runs ads whose
  destination **is** that worker.
- **Form.** An offer (ads plus answering, one fee) and a pairing of two skills.
- **What we build.** The click-to-message campaign set-up (Messenger, Instagram Direct, WhatsApp as the
  destination), the hand-off of each new conversation to the first-touch worker, the brief's question as the
  conversation's first line, the follow-up ladder (skill 41) for conversations that stop, and the reading: cost
  per conversation, per qualified conversation, per booking — the funnel-leak read's steps for a chat instead of
  a cart.
- **First test.** The holding's own web office as the advertiser: one prospect question, one worker, no spend —
  a staged conversation run against the first-touch script and scored with skill 42. Spend waits on the CEO.
- **What it earns or improves.** It sells the ad office and the sales desk as one product, which neither a
  creative agency nor a chatbot vendor offers; it is measured in bookings, not clicks.
- **What needs the CEO.** Ads run for the holding's own office reach prospects, and offering the product is
  client-finding, which board row B84's decision 9 holds at "not now" — both wait on his word; WhatsApp Business
  and a Meta business account are identity steps; any ad spend of ours is money out.
- **Risk.** WhatsApp's 2026 terms bar general-purpose AI assistants and allow business-function bots (reported,
  not read at source — OPEN-QUESTIONS); the worker must stay inside a business function. A conversation ad
  without an instant answer is worse than a landing page.

### 4. The context check — the brief is re-read when the world changes in a market

- **What.** Her 2020 crisis method (§8): keep advertising, do not run the old message as if nothing happened,
  do not make the crisis the subject; speak to one of four needs — comfort, health, relaxation, safety and
  security — and show how the product fits the changed life.
- **Where.** The belief brief (skill 28) and the weekly loop (B28). The holding is global: in any week some
  market is under a war, a disaster, an election, a currency fall, a mourning, or a religious season.
- **Form.** A check in the brief and a trigger on the calendar.
- **What we build.** A per-market context row (what is happening, since when, what it changes) kept by the
  research door; when a market has a live row, every running and planned ad there is re-read by one question —
  does it read as tone-deaf, as profiting from fear, or as ignoring what the viewer is living? — and the brief
  states which of the four needs, if any, the concept now serves.
- **First test.** Take the holding's own planned ads and one past event (the 2020 pandemic, as she did): rewrite
  three briefs under it and have the lead judge which survive unchanged.
- **What it earns or improves.** It prevents the one ad that costs a brand more than a month of results, and it
  is a reason a global client prefers an office that watches every market.
- **What needs the CEO.** Nothing; any brief touching a religious season or a conflict falls under his
  boundaries as usual.
- **Risk.** Her own "stockpile healthy snacks" campaign shows the trap: an angle that profits from hoarding or
  fear fails the holding's no-harm and no-deception lines, whatever it returns.

### 5. Reading a budget step at the margin

- **What.** Her non-linear-scaling point and her overlap story (§3) turned into a rule: a budget step is judged
  by the **extra** revenue it bought against the extra spend, compared with the client's break-even ROAS
  (1 ÷ contribution margin), over seven days, with the store's own revenue as the check. Her "5× at $10k becomes
  4.6-4.9 at $15k" is, at the margin, 3.8-4.4.
- **Where.** The winner read (skill 30) and the monthly business diagnosis (skill 44); the client's monthly
  report.
- **Form.** A rule and one table in the report.
- **What we build.** The marginal-return table per budget step; the overlap check as the first diagnostic when a
  step fails; the client's margin captured at onboarding so break-even is known.
- **First test.** Her two numeric cases as fixtures (§3); then the first client export.
- **What it earns or improves.** It answers the question clients actually ask — "can we spend more?" — with
  their own break-even, not with an average that hides the last dollar.
- **What needs the CEO.** Nothing.
- **Risk.** Day-to-day noise is large; a step is read over a week, and with small counts the §2 intervals apply.

### 6. Small numbers stated with their range

- **What.** Her neon-sign case (§2) — 8 sales against 1 — with the exact intervals: the data cannot call the
  second audience worse. Judge on counts of results, show the interval, call nothing a winner while intervals
  overlap.
- **Where.** Skill 30 (`ad-winner-read`) step 3, which already asks for "confidence and the spend it rests on";
  and the short-video postmortem (skill 33), which compares posts on small counts too.
- **Form.** A rule and a small function the reading room calls.
- **What we build.** The Poisson interval on a count and on a cost per result; the verdict words tied to it
  ("too few to say" / "leaning" / "clear").
- **First test.** Her case; then the holding's own published videos once the weekly loop has two weeks of
  numbers (as first study idea 6 planned).
- **What it earns or improves.** Fewer winners crowned on luck — the waste she names in 2026 ("did the creator
  just get lucky on one take?") prevented by arithmetic.
- **What needs the CEO.** Nothing.
- **Risk.** None worth naming; the interval is conservative.

Also from her 2020 onboarding line — "you have to be comfortable with losing money out the gate" — the test
budget is stated to every client before the first ad, as the cost of learning, with the date by which it will
have been read. That is one sentence in the offer (first study idea 8), not a separate idea.

### 7. Three lines for the brief and the compliance check

- **No invented deadline.** Her "free shipping, limited time only… slightly misleading" (§7) is blacklisted in
  the EU (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, Annex I point 7) and is deception by the holding's rule. The
  brief's compliance step adds: every time limit, stock count or "last chance" has its real date or number in
  the brief. The first study's compliance gate (idea 11) names testimonials, comments, senders and comparisons;
  this is the missing fifth case.
- **The larger true number.** A discount is shown as a percentage under a $100 price and as an amount above it
  (Berger's Rule of 100), not by her "100 is bigger than 30".
- **Message match.** The offer and the product the ad names appear on the page it opens and in the retargeting
  ad that follows (§6). A check in the brief and in the funnel-leak read's step 2.
- **What needs the CEO.** Nothing. **Risk.** None.

### 8. Saves pick the ad, and a paid seed audience for the holding's own new accounts

- **What.** (a) Her post-iOS ranking — views < likes < comments < saves — and "use the saves to drive your
  creative strategy" (§6): the most-saved organic posts are the first candidates to run as ads. (b) Her follower
  ads (§11), repaired: paying for a seed audience for a new account **in the market it sells to**, never
  worldwide for cheapness and never reaching minors for a count.
- **Where.** (a) The B28 weekly loop's "pick the winner" step and skill 33. (b) The launch of the holding's own
  brand accounts (B28, the holding's channels).
- **Form.** (a) A ranking rule. (b) A launch recipe with its settings written once.
- **What we build.** (a) Saves per reach as the promotion score of an organic post. (b) The recipe: objective
  that sends people to the profile, the selling market only, adults only, a fixed small daily budget, judged by
  the share of new followers from that market and their later engagement.
- **First test.** (a) On the holding's own posts once two weeks of numbers exist. (b) Paper only until the CEO
  decides to spend.
- **What needs the CEO.** (b) Money out and the account identity.
- **Risk.** (b) Followers bought cheaply teach the platform the wrong audience; the recipe's market rule exists
  for that.

### 9. A competitor's paid keywords as customer language

- **What.** Her SpyFu move (§10), done with the holding's decided tool: the keywords a competitor pays for in
  search are the phrases of buyers ready to buy.
- **Where.** The quote bank's "search phrasing" source (skill 27).
- **Form.** One source added to a skill.
- **What we build.** A DataForSEO call per competitor for its paid and ranked keywords; the phrases enter the
  quote bank marked as search language (not as a person's quote), counted, never shown as a customer saying it.
- **First test.** One holding line's three competitors; does the list add phrases the reviews and threads did
  not have?
- **What needs the CEO.** Nothing beyond the DataForSEO account already decided. Her "incognito to avoid paying"
  is refused.
- **Risk.** Paid keywords show what competitors bid on, which is their guess, not demand; the counts stay labelled.

## What these videos change in the reading of this expert

The first study read her as a creative strategist whose media buying of 2020-22 is "mostly outdated mechanics,
durable logic underneath". These nineteen confirm it and move the line a little.

**Her trade began as media buying for small e-commerce stores.** In 2019 the channel was a fitness diary; from
January 2020 it became a Facebook-ads channel aimed at stores with $20k a month in sales, with a free audit on
a call as the door. Her creative thinking appears only in passing (benefit-led copy, "content that looks
organic"). The belief-and-persona strategist of 2025-26 grew out of a buyer who learned, account by account,
that the mechanics stopped working.

**Her mechanics of that period are weaker than the first study's single-sentence verdict suggests.** Two of
her "secrets" — narrowing an account by breakdown averages, and duplicating ad sets because "each one reaches a
slice of 10%" — are not merely outdated; one is the error Meta's own documentation is written to correct, and
the other rests on a mechanism found in no Meta source. A third, the "free shipping, limited time only" she
calls "slightly misleading", is a practice the EU blacklists. Her later reversal on budget steps (2023-24
captions) shows she learned it; the study takes her newest position and these as history.

**What survives is her judgement about time and evidence**, and it is the sharpest thing in this set: wait for
data, read a week not a day, expect a dip after a step, distrust small samples, keep a stop-loss for the
obvious disaster, and look for overlap when more money buys less. Meta's learning-phase pages say the same in
results counted rather than days; with that correction it becomes a guard the office can run on every account.

**Her funnel has not changed since 2020** — free audit, a sales threshold, scarcity lines, a call — only the
threshold has risen (from $20k of sales to $30k of ad spend). The first study's reading of her business holds.

**The cost-cap and testing-structure series is not on YouTube.** All 36 videos of her channel are now accounted
for (34 read, 2 without subtitles); none is about cost caps or bid caps. The gap belongs to her Instagram posts
of December 2023 to February 2024 and stays open.
