## 2. Launch and index hygiene — the switches that make a finished site invisible (sharper)

The first study holds "readable, not blocked, indexed" (§8). These videos add the concrete places where a site
that looks finished is told to stay out of Google, and a cheap sweep to find leftovers.

**2.1 Four switches to check on every launch, relaunch and hand-over** (`YT:kGaHHT8IIcU 00:00–03:01`;
`YT:ikZoloEZCSk 02:04`):
1. Page source: `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` on pages meant to rank.
2. `/robots.txt`: `User-agent: *` + `Disallow: /` (blocks everything). WordPress's sensible default disallows
   only `/wp-admin/`.
3. WordPress **Settings → Reading → "Discourage search engines from indexing this site"** — "very common …
   a developer … leave[s] this on while it's in development but then forget[s]" (`02:01`).
4. HTTP and HTTPS both served (and www / non-www): one host must 301 to the other — his `.htaccess` force-SSL
   rule (`YT:ikZoloEZCSk 02:04`).

**[ADDED — what the checkbox really does today]** Since WordPress 5.3 the "discourage" setting no longer
writes `Disallow: /` into robots.txt; it outputs a robots **meta** noindex tag, so crawlers can see the
instruction (a robots.txt block would hide a noindex from them) [make.wordpress.org/core/2019/09/02/
changes-to-prevent-search-engines-indexing-sites]. So switch 3 now shows up as switch 1, and the check must
read the rendered head, not only robots.txt. The core developer's advice for staging sites: send an
`X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow` HTTP header on everything — which also means **that header must be gone at
launch**: a fifth switch, invisible in page source.

**2.2 WordPress attachment pages** (`YT:ypSRpWd2vHo 01:02`): every uploaded image gets its own thin URL; Yoast's
"redirect attachment URLs to the attachment itself" removes them. **[ADDED]** Since WordPress 6.4 (Nov 2023),
**new** installs have attachment pages off (`wp_attachment_pages_enabled = 0`, redirected to the file);
**sites upgraded from older versions keep them on** [make.wordpress.org/core/2023/10/16/
changes-to-attachment-pages]. So the check matters on exactly the sites a renewal job inherits.

**2.3 The `site:` sweep** (`YT:ypSRpWd2vHo 00:02–02:04`; `YT:g4aI-0SzpnE 04:17`; `YT:r74O2psc_os 03:05`):
search `site:domain`, then page to the **back** of the results — that is where Google shows error-text
descriptions (a broken collection template on a Shopify store), "pseudo-404" deleted blog posts, attachment
URLs and parameter junk. `site:` on one category URL shows what Google read there: random boilerplate means the
page carries no text of its own (`YT:j0FyjKzo-UA 01:16`; `YT:oi4lc30k42M 02:16`). The same list doubles as a
sweep of title tags. **[TEST]** The result count of `site:` is an estimate and the list is incomplete; it finds
examples, the Search Console page-indexing report counts. Use `site:` as a quick look before access to
Search Console exists — i.e. on a **prospect** (that use is client-finding and waits on the CEO's word).

**2.4 Mobile parity** (`YT:e3j5KKLqC_o 01:02–03:04`): the mobile render must carry the same main content as
desktop (Chrome device toolbar); his example competitor's mobile visibility stagnated while desktop grew.
**[ADDED]** Google completed the move to mobile-first indexing in October 2023 and moved the last
desktop-crawled sites to the smartphone crawler after 5 July 2024 [developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/10/
mobile-first-is-here; …/2024/06/mobile-indexing-vlast-final-final]. So content hidden on mobile is, for
Google, content that does not exist — a readable-first check, not a design note.

**2.5 Duplicate copies of a client's text elsewhere** (`YT:g4LJmSvz9UI 00:02–02:03`): paste ten words in
quotes into Google; other domains carrying them are copies. Tool "duplicate" flags (he shows 844 in one crawl)
must be read one by one — many are shared titles or descriptions, not duplicate pages.

## 4. Tool output — false alarms **and false passes** (sharper)

`YT:zvii3qPiZm4 01:08–02:08`: a free audit plugin (SEOquake) flags "text-to-HTML ratio" — "not really a
factor"; reports meta keywords — "not a ranking factor"; and gives a **green tick** to `lang="en-US"` on an
Australian shop. "Not everything flagged as an issue is an issue … you can get successes that aren't really
successes." The register's `suggestion-triage` handles the first half (drop on a stance). The second half — a
check a tool passes that is wrong *for this client's market* — is not in it.

**[TEST]** His own example needs a correction: Google says it does **not** use `lang` attributes to decide a
page's language — it reads the visible content; geotargeting comes from ccTLD, `hreflang` and, weakly, server
location [developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites]. Bing's
2011 guidance named the `content-language` meta tag as its first signal for a document's country and language
[blogs.bing.com/webmaster/March-2011/How-To-Tell-Bing-Your-Website-s-Country-and-Langua]; current Bing
practice not confirmed. So the false pass is real for accessibility, screen readers and possibly Bing, not
for Google. The sound version: a market-fit pass over every green tick — language and region tags, currency,
address and phone format, `hreflang`, units, date format — against the client's real market.

## 5. International and country differences (new; he gives half — completed)

**What he teaches** (`YT:noprSYGApa8`, Lawrence, 2023):
- The same keyword differs by country in volume, difficulty **and the questions asked**: "kangaroo" — in
  Australia "how to draw a kangaroo", in the US "are kangaroos dangerous" (`01:02–02:02`); "4th of July" —
  Australians ask what it is, Americans whether Costco is open (`03:02`). Content is researched per country,
  not translated.
- Entering another country is "quite an exercise": "different domains, international hreflang"
  (`04:03`); the strategy is re-made per market.
- **Market thesis** [HS]: the US has far more SEOs and more advanced practice; a smaller market is less
  competitive, so advanced practice transplanted there wins (`05:04`). Harry, same idea: in Australia "three
  companies doing SEO at a really good level", "triple that" in the UK — "very few companies have managed to
  scale out an SEO operation" (`YT:wqqjqcn7uI0 08:13–09:13`).

**What is missing, completed from Google's own pages [ADDED]:**
- **Structure** [managing-multi-regional-sites]: ccTLD (`example.de`) — clearest geotargeting, one country
  only, costly; subdomain (`de.example.com`) — easy, users may not read the target; subdirectory
  (`example.com/de/`) — low maintenance, one host; URL parameters — "not recommended".
- **No forced redirects**: "avoid automatically redirecting" users by guessed language or IP — it can stop
  users *and search engines* from seeing all versions; give explicit links to switch. One language per page;
  no side-by-side translation.
- **hreflang** [developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions]: each version
  lists **itself and every other** version; "if two pages don't both point to each other, the tags will be
  ignored"; `x-default` for no match; HTML link, HTTP header or sitemap — equivalent, one is enough; ISO 639-1
  language, optional ISO 3166-1 region, never a region alone. Same-language regional copies (en-AU / en-GB)
  need canonical + hreflang.

**[TEST]** The country-difference teaching is sound and, for a global holding, central. The market thesis is
his impression, not a measurement; it can be measured (FOR-THE-HOLDING idea 2).

## 6. E-commerce: faceted navigation and category text (sharper, one correction)

- Category and collection pages with **no text of their own** never rank; add a description of who the range is
  for, written in the shop's world (his example: Horde vs Alliance on a games-merchandise store)
  (`YT:j0FyjKzo-UA 01:16`; `YT:oi4lc30k42M 01:16–02:16`). Already in the first study (§7 category page).
- **Faceted navigation** — his fix was canonicals to the parent category so "thousands of pages … just facets"
  do not enter the index (`YT:j0FyjKzo-UA 02:17`). **[ADDED, contradicts]** Google now recommends, where filtered
  URLs need not be indexed, `robots.txt` disallow of the filter parameters (keeping the unfiltered listing
  crawlable) or filters in URL **fragments** (`#`), which Google ignores for crawling; canonical and nofollow
  are "generally less effective in the long term" [crawling-managing-faceted-navigation]. Facets that *are*
  real demand (a "mini wireless Bluetooth speaker" category, `YT:z8E2q846_0M 03:07`) get a proper static
  category page instead.
- **Architecture that will not scale**: navigation by licensed franchise works for a handful of brands and
  breaks at a hundred — plan the mega-menu taxonomy before it is needed (`YT:j0FyjKzo-UA 03:17–04:17`).
- **Sitewide review stars**: the same aggregate-rating markup on every page; "Google will come down on you hard"
  (`YT:g4aI-0SzpnE 04:17–05:17`) — consistent with the register's "no self-serving stars" (51); the manual
  action for structured-data spam exists.

## 7. Finding topics and keywords — the two methods the first study lacks (sharper)

Most of §7 of these videos (Keywords Everywhere, the keyword magic tool, export and score volume against
difficulty, Google Trends spikes, People-also-ask, AnswerThePublic; `YT:czEn0TVILb0`, `YT:lY0yqMCLjII`,
`YT:xB9Yp6BCWZg`) is held by registers 10, 11 and 18. Two are not:

- **The link-magnet read** (`YT:xB9Yp6BCWZg 03:03`): take the sites that rank in a niche, list their pages
  **by number of linking sites** — "if a page has a lot of links it must be a good topic". This finds which
  formats earn links in that trade (a calculator, a statistics page, a guide) before a data story or tool page
  is chosen. With DataForSEO's backlinks data this is one call per competitor. [TEST] Sound; a page's links
  can be old or bought — read the top ten by hand.
- **The winner's keyword set** (`YT:xB9Yp6BCWZg 02:03`): put the #1 URL (exact URL, not the domain) into the
  keyword tool; its whole ranking set is the coverage list for our page. Feeds `question-harvest` (10).

**Modifier families** (`YT:z8E2q846_0M`): objective (best), purchase (cheap, discount, buy — high intent,
high competition), geographic (city, state, country), size (mini), audience (for students, for seniors) and
emotion/occasion (gifts for someone sad). The audience and emotion families are the ones a translated keyword
list misses; HK's seven query families otherwise hold this.

## 8. Links: relationships — his gift tactic and its clean form (contradicts, with one new fact)

`YT:jBOM4o7ftGU 01:04–02:05`: after a blogger or editor gives a link, send wine, flowers or a present as
thanks; editors run "three to ten different websites at a given time" [HS], so one thanked editor offered
three or four more links. Book: *Giftology* (John Ruhlin). Also `YT:h33r_ZRUGrY`: export a competitor's
**broken** backlinks, offer our page as the replacement, expect a 5–10 % yes [HS] (first study: 1 in 10).

**[TEST]** Google's link-spam list includes "exchanging goods or services for links" and "sending someone a
product in exchange for them writing about it and including a link" [spam-policies]. A present that is
understood as payment for a link — and one that produces "more links" on request is — is a link scheme;

placement. **What is new and worth keeping** is the fact behind the tactic: one editor often controls several
publications — so the relationship record should hold *which sites each contact runs*.

## 9. Reviews — the evidence for "third-party proof beats first-party claims" (sharper, corrected)

`YT:z6pwA_792Ew 00:02–02:03`: an "MIT" study [HS: "over a million transactions", "12,000 consumers"] found
licensing credentials mattered less to buyers than ratings and reviews; "claiming to be the best on your
About page isn't going to do you any magic"; reply to every one-star with help, not defence (his general
manager showed him the data). **[ADDED]** The study is NBER Working Paper 26601, *Consumer Protection in an
Online World: An Analysis of Occupational Licensing* (Farronato, Fradkin, Larsen, Brynjolfsson, 2020), on a
large US home-services platform: more stringent licensing was associated with less competition and higher
prices but **not** with higher satisfaction; of more than 5,200 survey respondents, under 1 % put licensing
among their top three reasons for hiring; reviews and prices drove the choice [nber.org/papers/w26601; NBER
Digest April 2020]. His figures are not the paper's; the direction is. Use: when a local client has neither,
reviews come before the About page's credentials in the order of work — both still needed.

## 10. WordPress upkeep — the procedure (sharper)

`YT:gMa_T2THIik` (Lawrence, 2023): (1) test **every template** (home, service, blog, product), not only the
homepage (`02:06`); (2) move off a starter host as traffic grows (`03:07`); (3) update plugins **one at a
time, after a backup**, never all at once (`04:08`); themes and core the same; (4) current stable PHP, backup
first — "once that update comes through it's permanent" (`05:08`); (5) delete deactivated and forgotten
plugins (`05:08`); (6) only plugins from known makers — check reviews, maker, last update; distrust
home-made AI-written plugins (`06:08`); (7) a lightweight theme, tested on its demo (`07:08`); (8) images
compressed and served at their display size (`08:08`); a central dashboard for 10–20 sites (name misheard
as "koala boss" — unverified). **[TEST]** Sound maintenance practice. Its speed judgement follows the 2023 lab
reading; by his own 2026 rule the verdict is the field data (C5). The Hormozi offer study already prices a
