### #1 `readable-first` — from `technical-seo-audit` (pack version), `screaming-frog-analyser`, `clarity-ai-bot-auditor`, `claude-code-seo`

**Inputs.** A Screaming Frog internal export (address, status, indexability and its reason, title, meta, H1,
canonical, word count, crawl depth, inlinks/unique inlinks, redirect URL, and a joined traffic column from GA4 or
Search Console); the sitemap; the Search Console pages export; Clarity bot activity; for sites we build in code, the
repository.

**Steps, in this order.**
1. *Redirects first* (pack `technical-seo-audit/SKILL.md:41-49`). Trace every 3xx hop by hop from a flat export
   (method in `packs/…/references/crawl-export-formats.md:83-96`): stop at a 200, an error, a URL already seen (loop)
   or a destination not crawled. A chain is two or more hops; one redirect is not a chain.
2. *Indexability conflicts*: money-page-looking URLs with noindex or a canonical pointing elsewhere; canonical
   chains (A→B, B→C); robots-blocked URLs still linked internally; indexable thin utility pages (tags, parameters).
   A self-referencing canonical is never a finding.
3. *Broken pages* ranked by unique inlinks, not listed flat (`screaming-frog-analyser.md:42`).
4. *Orphans* only by comparison against the sitemap, the Search Console page list or analytics landing pages;
   "no orphans" is never reported from a link-following crawl (`crawl-export-formats.md:98-105`).
5. *Titles, metas, H1s, thin content* — grouped by template, not page by page; word count is a cue, never a rule.
6. *AI bots* (from `clarity-ai-bot-auditor.md:27-37`): one verdict per agent, judged by the agent's job (training,
   search, user-triggered fetch).

**Rubric.**
- Severity classes (`screaming-frog-analyser.md:37-40`): **BLOCKER** (stops crawling or indexing) > **EQUITY LEAK**
  (wastes links or crawl) > **QUALITY SIGNAL** (relevance and click). Within a class: URLs affected, then unique
  inlinks touched. "One accidental noindex on a category template beats 200 long titles."
- Chain severity (pack `technical-seo-audit/SKILL.md:44-47`): loop = critical; chain ending in 4xx/5xx = critical;
  3+ hops to a live page = high; 2 hops = worth fixing. A chain starting at a page that had real traffic leads the
  report, its figure quoted exactly, with no invented period.
- Bot verdicts: HEALTHY · NOT OBSERVED (zero requests is **not** a block) · BLOCKED (403/challenge evidence or a
  Disallow line) · THROTTLED (429s) · SERVER ERROR (5xx) · OVER-SCRAPING (volume with no return — only if referral
  data exists) · VIOLATION (asks before tightening). Blocking a training crawler while allowing the search crawler
  and user fetcher is a valid setup, not a finding.

**Refusals.** No invented URL, count or status. No "consider". Never call a bot blocked without evidence. A crawl
run without JavaScript rendering on a script-built site says its word and link counts may be wrong.

**Output.** Health snapshot (one number per check and a verdict) → the single worst issue in its own paragraph →
full list per issue group (severity, count, up to five sample URLs, the fix, effort minutes / hours / developer
ticket) → five fixes by impact-to-effort with impact High/Medium/Low and **no number on the gain** → what the crawl
cannot see (the rendered page on a phone, pop-ups, logs) → what is working.

**Fixture.** His `sample-crawl-screamingfrog.csv` (60 rows, a plumber with planted defects: a five-hop chain into a
404 on the second-busiest URL, an About loop, a noindexed money page, a robots-blocked quote page linked 14 times, a
503 booking page, a canonical chain, two orphans, four suburb pages sharing one title) and his expected report
`sample-audit-report.md` are exactly the calibration pair our engine needs (see §3 and `FOR-THE-HOLDING.md` #4).

**For sites we build ourselves** (`claude-code-seo.md:30-70, 82`): a repository-level pass — 27 checks in five
groups weighted structure 40 · AI readiness 20 · linking 20 · content 15 · infrastructure 5, each group's score over
the checks that apply, checks that do not apply skipped, not failed. This becomes candidate `repo-seo-gate`.

### #2 `entity-consistency` — from `entity-seo-auditor`, `3-ranking-laws-cheat-sheet` mode 1, `whats-my-website-missing` check 4, `author-authority-builder`

**Inputs.** The exact name, the entity type (organisation, local business, person, product), the one topic; the
homepage and About page; the live JSON-LD (raw source, because text fetches strip it); the Business Profile; the
profiles that exist.

**Steps.**
1. Establish the entity and the topic; every later check is relative to it.
2. **Entity home**: one page that carries what, who, when, where. Facts spread over ten pages and consolidated on
   none is the finding; the fix is to consolidate, not to add pages.
3. **Schema layer**: correct `@type`, a stable `@id` reused site-wide, name / description / logo matching the page,
   `sameAs` only to profiles that are truly the entity and ideally link back; for a business founder, foundingDate,
   address, knowsAbout (areaServed for service areas).
4. **Knowledge-graph presence**: panel, Wikidata item, Wikipedia or the notability to earn one, authoritative
   profiles. Never claimed present without checking.
5. **Consistency precondition**: name, description, category and core facts across site, schema, Business Profile
   and listings — **PASS or FAIL, and a FAIL caps every other layer** (`entity-seo-auditor.md:46, 80`). NAP variants
   counted ruthlessly ("Pty Ltd" missing, suburb capitalisation — `3-ranking-laws-cheat-sheet.md:31`).
6. **Disambiguation** and **co-occurrence** (the name beside its topic and related entities).
7. **Corroboration**: independent sources saying the same thing.

**Scale.** Six layers 0-10 (0 absent, 5 present but incomplete or inconsistent, 10 complete, correct and
corroborated), "(est.)" on any layer that could only be estimated; one fix per layer; top three as this week's work.

**Wikidata rule** (`entity-seo-auditor.md:81-82`): Wikidata before Wikipedia; create an item only when independent
sources exist, reference every statement, and say that an item sourced only to the brand's own site can be deleted.

**The person layer** is a separate skill (candidate `author-entity`), not a line of this one.

### #3 `ai-recommendation-test` — from `ai-visibility-dashboard`, `ai-citation-monitor`, `ai-visibility-check`

This is where his files add most to a row we already have. Our row says "ten buyer questions, three reads on
different days". His files make it a protocol.

**The prompt library** (`ai-visibility-dashboard.md:36-58`). 10-20 prompts written as a buyer types them, naming
the country and city where it matters, in three layers: **Head** (best X for Y in Z; X vs alternative; how much does X
cost in country), **Constrained** (the question behind the question with the buyer's size, budget, platform,
deadline — "where citation share is won or lost", so it gets the most prompts), **Sceptical** (is brand worth it,
alternatives to brand, brand vs rival, what customers say, one do-nothing or do-it-yourself prompt). Each prompt is
tagged **Brand** or **Category** and the two groups are always reported apart. Each carries a **scoring key**: the
page we hope gets cited and the page expected instead; "no page yet" is a content gap found before any run. The
client crosses out prompts no buyer would type.

**The frozen setup.** Library version and date, country, assistants, settings, and the citation line ("Provide
citations." appended in chat assistants, never on Google's own surfaces). **A prompt is never edited mid-series**; a
changed prompt gets a new ID and its own baseline. The customer query never carries the brand name unless the buyer
would type it (`ai-citation-monitor.md:57-61`).

**The run** (`ai-visibility-dashboard.md:62-74`). Each prompt three times, each in a fresh session (never the next
message of the same chat), memory off; record date, assistant, model shown, whether it searched; keep the full
answer and every cited URL. Two baselines before any change: **day 1 and day 8** — the gap between them is how much
answers move on their own. Failed runs (refused, errored, cut off, no AI answer shown) retried once and **never in
the denominator**. Budget agreed first: prompts × 3 × assistants (15 prompts on four assistants is 180 answers).

**The coding of each answer.** Four yes/no columns: **Named** (in the text; a mention only inside a URL does not
count) · **Recommended** (on the shortlist or the answer) · **Our site linked** · **External page about us linked**
(opened to check; "unchecked" counted apart). From the monitor: **status** Recommended / Cited as a source /
Recommended and cited / Mentioned only / Absent, and **position** First named / In a list / In passing / Footnote
only. A source citation counts as cited even without a recommendation; a bare mention never does. Competitors named,
wrong facts about the client, hallucinated competitors — recorded.

**The read.** Counts over completed answers, never bare percentages ("named in 9 of 42"). Each prompt sorted by
majority of runs into **three piles**: *named favourably or our page is the source* (guard it) · *competitors
instead* (a gap: offsite authority or depth) · *unclaimed* (nobody named — there for the taking) · "mixed" when no
majority. A change is "moved after", never "because of"; a trend only when it holds across several rounds on the
same prompts, assistants and settings. A single query in a single assistant moving is a reason to re-check, not a
trend.

**Thresholds for the short form** (`ai-visibility-check.md:112-115`), for the public tool only: visible 75 %+ ·
partially visible 30-74 % · invisible under 30 %, partial mentions listed apart so the score is not inflated.

**What his human reader cannot do and ours can.** Run the library on schedule from clean sessions, store every
answer, diff every round automatically, and keep the day-1/day-8 noise band per client. See §3 on which surface we
measure.

### #4 `why-not-ranking` — from `google-quality-rater`, `competitor-gap-check`, `3-ranking-laws-cheat-sheet` mode 2

**Order that cannot be skipped** (`google-quality-rater.md:35-62`): read the page or stop (an empty JavaScript shell
is "could not read", never a grade) → classify the query's intent (Know, Know Simple, Do, Website, Visit-in-person)
and write the dominant interpretation in one sentence → **read the top three to five ranking pages before grading
relevance**; without them the relevance grade is labelled *uncalibrated*.

**The card.** **O** Offsite: the two rater searches `[brand] -site:` and `[brand] reviews -site:`, sources named →
PASS / THIN / AT RISK, with the rater rule that a small business with little reputation is "thin", not failing.
**U** Uniqueness → ORIGINAL SOURCE / SOME UNIQUE INPUT / REWRITE OF WHAT EXISTS. **R** Needs Met → Fully (rare,
single-result intent only) / Highly (the realistic target) / Moderately / Slightly / Fails. **Quality check**: four
Low triggers and eight Lowest triggers (`google-quality-rater.md:117-133`); any one caps the verdict whatever the
letters. Trust judged first. YMYL pages checked hard for a named accountable person. One action per letter, "THE
ONE THING", an "after state" that names items and promises no rating.

**Calibration examples** (lines 216-224) — three graded pages kept with the skill so repeat runs stay consistent. We
should write our own, in our markets.

**Re-rate mode**: old → new per letter, "still open". "A fix that did not work is the most useful thing you can tell
someone."

**The first-100-words check** (`3-ranking-laws-cheat-sheet.md:35-39`): what the title promises in one sentence,
what the first 100 words deliver, alignment 0-10, the gap that sends a reader back, the rewritten opening.

**Site-level comparison** (`competitor-gap-check.md:60-70`), kept for the prospect page: five dimensions only —
topic coverage (page count with its source), content quality on the pages read, structure, **trust 0-5** (named
people · reviews on site · case studies · credentials or memberships · complete About and contact), presence across
the web ("not checked" unless searched). Backlinks and authority "not compared" unless a tool's numbers are given.

### #9 `lane-and-map` — from `packs/topical-authority-map` (skill, three references, sample), `search-intent-mapper`, `3-ranking-laws-cheat-sheet` mode 3

The biggest single addition. His map is the specification our one-line row needs.

**Five checkpoints with the client** (`topical-authority-map/SKILL.md:44-66`): central entity → source context and
the real author → attribute list and client situations → merge and split decisions → buyer-stage gaps.

1. **Central entity** — "the ONE thing the site is about, expressed as a keyword", tested by "if Google gave this
   site a Knowledge Panel, what would the title be?"; the attributes and values the site must cover. This is our lane
   sentence made machine-usable.
2. **Source context** — five questions: business type, market and jurisdiction, unique angle, customer language
   ("how customers describe it, not how the business does"), the two-sentence panel test. The same niche gives a
   different map for a maker, a retailer or a marketplace.
3. **The persona is a real person** who writes or reviews, with credentials that can be checked against a register;
   never invented; if nobody has the expertise, a real reviewer is recommended.
4. **Attribute sources, all six**: top five results (an attribute on three of five is mandatory), People Also Ask
   with nested questions, Wikipedia headings and "see also", autocomplete alphabet and modifiers (for, vs, how,
   cost, best), the business's own service list (each a mandatory core page whatever its volume), the laws and
   standards of the field. Unreachable sources are named, never filled with invented questions.
5. **Client situation map** (persona × situation × services × related topics) — it decides content *and* which
   internal links make sense ("you do not link to child custody from an article about a couple with no children").
6. **Core or supporting** by intent; **merge or split** by thresholds (`references/01-research-and-classify.md:233-239`):
   100+ monthly searches → own page · 10-99 → own page if two or more of the top five have a dedicated page, else merge ·
   under 10 → a section · a service actually sold → own page whatever the volume · an adjacent topic of medium or high
   importance that a specialist is expected to cover → one or two articles, capped. **Volumes from a keyword tool only;
   Search Console impressions never stand in for volume** and get their own dated column.
7. **Depth calibration**: a whole page, a paragraph or a sentence signals importance — size each topic by its weight
   near the entity.
8. **Buyer stage** on every page: research / compare / buy (his TOFU, MOFU, BOFU), optional retention; the gap check
   names the empty stage ("by far" the compare stage).
9. **Expanded queries** per page (the seven types: definition, instruction, comparison, reason, short fact, boolean,
   consequence; plus competitors' H2s): a competitor's dedicated page → a page in the map; a competitor's H2 → an H2;
   covered by nobody → an H2 marked information gain. A "cost of retrieval" score 0-10, labelled as his heuristic.
10. **Linking rules table** (`references/02-architecture-and-keywords.md:221-227`): supporting → core mandatory in the
    body; supporting ↔ supporting encouraged; core → supporting encouraged; home → core mandatory; home → supporting
    optional; a reader-relevance check on every link; no automated related-post widgets.
11. **Central entity template**: one natural mention in the intro, one in the closing section, one link to the main
    core page — "no more, no less".

### #18 `striking-distance-weekly` — from `striking-distance-finder`, `title-tag-optimizer`, `gsc-quick-start`

**The lift score** (`striking-distance-finder.md:30-40`):

11 % informational, 7 % commercial, labelled as placeholders), current_ctr from the row (clicks ÷ impressions),

tweak. Mostly branded data is flagged and re-run with a brand filter.

## 4. How he packages and gives (step 6)

**The objects.** Single `SKILL.md` files with a frontmatter description that lists trigger phrases; packs as zips

into a Project. A PDF cheat sheet for people who will never install anything, ending with a pointer to the installable
skill.

**The framing.** The core pack: "the same first-pass tools we use inside a 120-person SEO agency … free, no upsell, no
email gate inside the skills" (`packs/core-seo-skills-pack/README.md:5`). The afternoon pack turns his content test
into a positioning line: the jobs AI made fast are "the price of entry, not the edge" — do them this week and spend the
saved time on the slow work that needs other people's yes: reviews, links, awards (`packs/afternoon-pack/README.md:5`).
Every skill says out loud what it cannot do (the visual layer, the creative angle, the relationship). The topical map
licence: "Build agencies on top of it."

**What the free files lead to.** Each run is a moment of need answered by his method, and inside it:
- inline case studies with links to studiohawk.com.au in every vertical audit (two to four per audit);
- free Hawk Academy web tools named as the next step (AI crawler access checker, robots tester, schema validator,
  "Is My Page Better?", AI visibility tester, the digital-PR calendar, the free backlinks lists);

  writes the student's decisions into `business-context.md`;

**As a client-finding machine.** Practitioners and owners carry his method into their own work, his name and case
studies travel with every run, agencies build on it and spread the vocabulary (SOURCE, the afternoon test, the three
laws), and the people who cannot or will not do the work arrive already convinced of the method. It is a content engine
whose content is *working tools*; the comment-keyword reels (first study) are the delivery van.

**Our version — the Show station.** What transfers and what does not:
1. **Tools, not skill files, for buyers.** His skills need Claude; most owners do not run Claude. Our measuring engines
   run as plain public pages on our own domain, ungated, in the visitor's language, any market — the same engines the
   office uses as delivery gates. Each ends in a plain verdict, one named fix, and "we can do this for you".
2. **One tool he does not have**: *"Paste the audit your agency or tool gave you"* — `suggestion-triage` turned public:
   which five lines matter, which to check first, which to drop, with the reason. It meets the buyer at the moment he
   distrusts a report, and it is honest by construction.
3. **Proof drops, ours only.** No borrowed case studies; the holding's own site is case one, its measured before-after
   the first proof line.
4. **Fixtures as a quality practice.** His sample-input / sample-output pairs are, for an office of AI workers, a test
   suite: every skill we author ships with a planted-defect input and its expected output, and is re-run on change.
5. **Skill files for practitioners — later and the CEO's call.** Publishing our skill files would bring practitioners,
   not buyers, and hands rivals the method; the measured library (link type per source, per country) is the part
   worth publishing first, because it is ours and it decays without our re-measuring.
6. **Decision 9 holds client-finding at "not now".** Building the tools is the holding's own site's information gain
   and the office's gates either way; publishing them for prospects is the step that waits on his word.
