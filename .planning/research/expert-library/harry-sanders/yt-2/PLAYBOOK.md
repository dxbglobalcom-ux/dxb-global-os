## 1. AI answers

### 1.1 Read the passage the AI answer lifted, then act on it — NEW
- **Method [HS]** (Lawrence, YT:BEQeb-vpIRk 08:17-09:17, December 2023, on Google's SGE): open the citation link in
  the AI answer; the source page opens with the lifted passage highlighted ("it's going to highlight in purple…
  another hint and indication of what content it's really enjoying"). His worked example: a skin-collagen answer
  cited a passage buried in an article about bone fractures. Two moves: (1) **move that passage higher** on the
  page it lives on; (2) where the passage sits in an off-topic article, **write the dedicated page** for that
  sub-topic ("another keyword opportunity… just based on that SGE research").
- **Completed [ADDED].** The highlight is the browser's scroll-to-text fragment: the cited URL carries
  `#:~:text=<passage start>,<passage end>`, so the exact lifted passage can be read from the URL itself without
  opening a browser. A third-party study of 15,699,298 Google AI Mode citations across 148 industries — a vendor's
  own study, published as a **sponsored** column on Search Engine Land (29 July 2026, "Opinions expressed … are
  those of the sponsor"; the author, Pillarbase, sells a citation-readiness product; correlational) — found
  47.7 % of citations were scroll-to-text highlights, resolving to 4.6 M unique passages on 2.7 M pages; the median
  lifted passage was 117 words (a paragraph, not a sentence); 80 % put the answer in their first sentence; about
  85 % were self-contained. A separate SALT.agency study (2,318 URLs) found **no** relation between how high on the page a
  passage sits and whether it is cited — so move (1) is a hypothesis to test, not a rule. Google's own May 2026
  change put links inline beside the sentence they support. Sources: searchengineland.com (the study's column,
  403 to the analyst's fetcher, read through relevantaudience.com and dev.to write-ups; read directly by the
  verifier on 2026-11-23, which corrected "83 %" to "about 85 %" and "reported by" to "sponsored column"), Search
  Engine Land and 9to5Google on the May 2026 link change (not re-opened).
- **[TEST]** The mechanism is observable and verified at scale for **AI Mode**; whether **AI Overviews** carry
  the fragment as often was not found in a primary source (OPEN-QUESTIONS). The sound procedure: harvest the
  fragments from every citation our measurement rounds collect, store the passage, and judge passages by what
  they share (answer-first, self-contained, about a paragraph) — never by their position alone.

## 2. Technical and release

### 2.1 "Noindex in robots.txt" — CONTRADICTS (wrong since 2019)
- **[HS]** zombie pages: "I can either noindex it, either on the page itself or in the robots.txt, or 301 redirect"
  (YT:CQszYBuI6Qo 01:07).
- **[TEST]** Google retired all handling of `noindex` in robots.txt on **1 September 2019** (Search Central blog,
  2 July 2019). Sound version: robots meta tag or `X-Robots-Tag` header with `noindex` (the page must stay
  crawlable for it to be seen), 404/410, or a 301 to the closest real page; a robots.txt `Disallow` stops crawling,
  not indexing. Also: his zombie hunt by `site:` search (01:07) is a sampling trick; Google's `site:` is not a
  complete or counted index. The first study's method (Search Console, 12 months, no clicks and no links) is the
  one to keep, through `page-change-guard`.

### 2.2 A site-wide banner became every page's snippet — NEW
- **[HS]** (YT:F8G8DAF7Qv0 02:15-03:18, on-air makeover of a toy reseller): a COVID-lockdown banner placed above
  the main content was indexed, and Google "is using that for all of their meta descriptions"; he links the
  timing to their organic drop.
- **The check it gives us [ADDED as procedure]:** read the snippets Google shows for a sample of the site's pages
  and flag any sentence that repeats across many of them — a notice, cookie text, shipping banner or menu text
  that the engine takes as the page's description. Fix: move the notice out of the main-content flow (or into a
  dismissible element that is not the first text of the page), and give key pages a written description.
- **[TEST]** Consistent with Google's snippet documentation (snippets come mainly from page content; a written
  meta description is used when it describes the page better). The causal link to their traffic drop is his
  reading of one chart.

### 2.3 The release switches that silently de-index a site — SHARPER
- **[HS]** (YT:EsYN9WSlDcY 01:07-03:08): after a migration of one of his own affiliate sites he left WordPress's
  "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" ticked; he checked days later, not at once; "I lost about
  50,000 visits"; the lesson is "checklists and processes".
- **Missing before:** register row 53 `migration-guard` says "staging blocked before launch and unblocked at
  launch; robots line by line". It does not name the **platform switches** that block indexing outside
  robots.txt, nor require the check **within the hour of launch**. List to check on every launch [ADDED]:
  WordPress "Discourage search engines" (sets a robots `noindex` meta in current versions); Shopify store
  password page; host or builder staging toggles that add `noindex` or `X-Robots-Tag`; Cloudflare or host bot
  blocks; the canonical still pointing at staging.

### 2.4 Security is an SEO check — NEW
- **[HS]** (YT:Ajx7vVc8N-E 02:07-03:08): "when hackers get into your website and start spooling up different
  URLs… it can set your SEO back a couple of months, if not a couple of years"; a security plug-in and updates
  are part of the SEO set-up.
- **Our check [ADDED]:** Search Console's Security issues report and Manual actions report read at every audit;
  a sudden jump in indexed or discovered URLs, or URLs in a language or topic the site does not have, is treated
  as a possible hack before anything else. Google's spam policies define hacked content as "any content placed on
  a site without permission, due to vulnerabilities in a site's security"; the first study and `readable-first` do
  not carry this check.

### 2.5 Page weight and the waterfall — SHARPER, with one CONTRADICTS
- **Procedure [HS]** (YT:QDYEhBnGDWI 01:06-04:10; YT:WaVKdg7PM9c; YT:RQvPvKARCQ8): when a page is slow, open the
  request waterfall (GTmetrix or the browser) and sort by size; the culprit is often not visible on the page — his
  case, a homepage of 19.1 MB whose weight came from a social-media feed serving every image at full resolution
  into a 200 × 200 box. Fix: serve images at the displayed size, compress (Google's Squoosh, or a plug-in for
  hundreds of images), cap a feed at five or six items. When the report flags server response time, the host is
  the fix (better plan, VPS); he puts it high once a site passes ~10,000 visits a month [HS].
- **Missing before:** the first study says read field data and ignore the score (`../PLAYBOOK.md` §2.6) but gives
  no step for **finding the cause** once field data fails. This is that step.
- **CONTRADICTS:** "Google … deliberately places websites in the SERPs based on their page size" (QDYE 03:08).
  **[TEST]** Google's page-experience documentation names Core Web Vitals as used by ranking systems, says good
  scores guarantee nothing, and does not mention page size or page weight. Weight matters only through what users
  experience (LCP, INP). His 2026 position (speed scores near-useless) supersedes his 2020 one. His "anything
  above three seconds on mobile, you're losing about 50 % of visits" is Google's 2016 "Need for Mobile Speed"
  figure (53 %), from 2015-16 DoubleClick data — dated; do not quote it as current.

  outside the holding's boundaries as an example]; a bigger brand's link "passes more authority".
- **Which is right:** his 2026 rule, already in the register (row 22): the **linking page** must rank and draw

### 3.3 The incoming anchor profile — NEW
- **[HS]** (YT:ZXGsXdIOUcs 04:06-05:06, the Yandex leak, January 2023): Yandex's factor list penalises a link
  profile where "money anchors" (exact commercial phrases like "plumber Melbourne") pass a threshold — "if you've
  got more than 50 of those as your anchor profile" (the subtitle does not say whether 50 or 50 %) —
  "this anchor profile is unnatural"; "this is why we always preach diversifying your anchor texts".
- **[TEST]** That threshold is **Yandex's**, from a leak, not Google's. What Google states: its spam policies list
  "links with optimized anchor text in articles, guest posts, or press releases distributed on other sites" and
  "keyword-rich, hidden, or low-quality links embedded in widgets that are distributed across various sites" as
  link spam (Search Central, spam policies). Sound check: before any link work, read the incoming anchors
  (Search Console's Links report shows "top linking text" from a sample); a profile dominated by exact
  commercial phrases is a risk finding to explain, and our own outreach never asks for an exact-match anchor.

## 4. Content

### 4.1 Who, How and Why — the disclosure line for AI-made pages — NEW
- **[HS]** (Lawrence, YT:4e7Oj0DwiCE 01:10-02:10): before publishing AI-assisted content, answer Google's "who,
  how and why": who made it, how (which tools), why (to help a reader or to take traffic) — "when I speak like
  that… it doesn't sound particularly people-first".
- **Completed [ADDED]** from Google's "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content": *Who* — bylines where
  readers expect them, linked to author background; no "deceptive authorship information" (made-up names or
  credentials). *How* — "Is the use of automation, including AI-generation, self-evident to visitors through
  disclosures or in other ways?"; disclosures matter most where a reader would ask "How was this created?"; say
  why automation was useful. *Why* — "perhaps the most important question"; content made mainly to attract
  search visits is not what the systems reward.
- **Why it matters here:** the first study, the skill files and the register hold *Who* (`author-entity`, the
  only-we gate's named author) and *Why* (people-first); none holds *How* — a written, page-level statement of how
  AI was used. For an office whose writers are models, that line is the honest form of every page we ship, and it
  is Google's own guidance. What we disclose, and in what words, is the CEO's (OPEN-QUESTIONS).
- Also in the video, already held: paste Google's self-assessment questions and the page into a model; compare
  against a top-ranking page for gaps; a second human reader (03:11-06:12).

### 4.2 How long it takes to rank — CONTRADICTS (numbers updated)
- **[HS]** (YT:SuM163x6yJs 00:00-02:03, citing Ahrefs): 22 % of top-10 pages were under a year old; 5.7 % of new
  pages reached the top 10 within a year; the average #1 page was about three years old.
- **[TEST]** Those are Ahrefs' **2017** figures. Ahrefs re-ran the study (published 15 May 2025): 13.7 % of top-10
  pages are under a year old; 72.9 % are over three years old; the average #1 page is about five years old; of
  1 M random new URLs, 1.74 % reached the top 10 within a year (6.11 % on a filtered sample of 2 M English URLs).
  Ranking has become **slower** for new pages. Use the 2025 numbers in roadmaps and in what the seller says.

## 5. Measurement and care

### 5.1 Read rankings on four horizons; act on none of them daily — SHARPER
- **[HS]** (YT:Gbqv3dMJXz4 00:03-02:03): rankings are volatile "on purpose"; look monthly, quarterly, half-yearly and
  yearly, all four; looking daily is harmless only if nothing is changed on it.
- **Completed [ADDED]:** the "on purpose" claim has a primary trace — Google's patent US 8,244,722 "Ranking
  documents" describes a rank-transition function that varies a document's rank over time without a change in its
  factors and watches who reacts, to identify rank-modifying spammers (related US 8,924,380, expired). A patent
  shows what Google could do, not what it runs. The rule it supports: reacting to short movements is at best
  noise and at worst a signal.
- **Missing before:** `page-change-guard` fixes the wait after an edit; nothing fixed the **reading cadence** of
  the client report. The four horizons are that cadence.

### 5.2 Be told about a drop the day it happens — NEW
- **[HS]** (YT:XJImDgyskA4 00:00-01:00, GA4 at launch, 2020): the point of GA4's alerts is that "a lot of people,
  when they have massive drops after a migration… don't know about it until they next check".
- **Completed [ADDED]:** GA4 custom insights can email a named list when a metric moves past a threshold or when
  Analytics marks it as an anomaly ("Has anomaly"), evaluated hourly (web data), daily, weekly or monthly; up to 50
  per property; Editor role needed (Google Analytics Help 9443595). GA4's predictive metrics (purchase and churn
  probability, predicted revenue) need at least 1,000 returning users who did and 1,000 who did not trigger the
  condition in seven days within 28 (Help 9846734) — useless for most small clients; do not sell them there.
