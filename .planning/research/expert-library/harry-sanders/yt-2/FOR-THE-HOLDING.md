# Harry Sanders, yt-2 — what the holding does with it

Companion to `PLAYBOOK.md` (this folder; § numbers point there) and `REGISTER-CHANGES.md`. Lines served: **B84**
DXB Web Studio first, **B28** where press and film touch the agency seat. Nothing here is built or tried. Every
figure from the videos is the speaker's claim.

The answer in one paragraph: this half of his newer YouTube files is the channel's **2020-2024 back catalogue**, not
new teaching. It adds no new strategy; it adds seven working parts the first study lacked — the most valuable a way
to read *which paragraph* an AI answer took from a page, confirmed at scale in 2026 — and it corrects a dozen
platform statements that have aged. Read together with the first study, it shows an expert whose procedures
survive and whose forecasts do not.

## 1. The ideas, ranked by value

### 1. The cited-passage ledger — know the paragraph, not only the page
- **What.** An AI citation often carries the exact lifted passage in its URL (`#:~:text=`). Lawrence used the
  highlight in 2023 to move a cited passage up and to spin a new page off a passage cited from the wrong article
  (§1.1); a 2026 study of 15.7 M AI Mode citations found about half are such highlights, median 117 words,
  mostly answer-first and self-contained [third-party: a vendor's own study in a sponsored column; correlational].
- **Where.** B84 Measure (register 3) and Care (the report's "Chosen" gauge); the page-change guard.
- **Form.** A new skill, `cited-passage-read` (specified in `REGISTER-CHANGES.md` §2), and a ledger per client.
- **What we build.** A fragment parser and passage locator on the citation log that row 3 already plans; the
  protected-passage list; the page-brief hand-off to `next-article`.
- **First test.** On the holding's own site, once row 3's first rounds exist: parse every citation; count how many
  carry a fragment per engine; locate each passage; report passages per page. Separately, on twenty prompts in
  the holding's main market, record the shape of the lifted passages we lose to.
- **Earns / improves.** The sales line "this is the page the assistant quoted" becomes "this is the paragraph it
  quoted, and we did not touch it"; a defence against our own edits deleting what earns citations; briefs
  grounded in what engines lift.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing to build.
- **Risk.** Treating position as cause — two studies disagree. Repair: position is reported, never prescribed;
  any move of a passage is a dated test under `page-change-guard`.

### 2. The reactive-press lane rebuilt for 2026: the expert's voice, the worker's legwork
- **What.** His 2020 rules for journalist call-outs — answer within about ten minutes, one paragraph, take a side
  (§3.4) — meet the 2026 fact that the relaunched HARO screens pitches for AI authorship, a third of its
  journalists refuse fully AI-written pitches, human pitches are selected about three times as often, and
  volumes of low-quality AI answers are filtered (Featured's own data).
- **Where.** B84 monthly package (named mentions for the client); the holding's own authority.
- **Form.** Row 24's reactive lane rewritten (`REGISTER-CHANGES.md`).
- **What we build.** A call-out watcher (HARO, Qwoted, SourceBottle, Featured — free tiers) that filters exact fits
  against the client's lane and evidence ledger and pushes one card to the expert's phone; the expert answers by
  voice; the worker transcribes, attaches the one owned number, sends under the expert's name.
- **First test.** Two weeks of watching for the holding's lane: count exact fits per week and how many the
  expert could have answered inside the window. No sending until the identity decision below.
- **Earns / improves.** Named mentions in real outlets — the off-site layer every assistant reads — at minutes of
  the expert's time; a care-package line that does not break platform rules.
- **Needs the CEO.** Who is the holding's named expert, by name and title (identity); whether he or a named
  person answers in a ten-minute window.
- **Risk.** An AI-written reply under a person's name is deception and a ban risk. Repair: the rule is written into
  the skill; the worker cannot send without the voice note.

### 3. Every tool we publish arrives with its link list
- **What.** His skyscraper workflow at the page level (§3.1): for a tool, find the pages that link to rivals' tools
  for the same job; one personal email each.
- **Where.** B84 Show and Build: the four public tools of the first study, every client's calculator page.
- **Form.** Additions to rows 21 and 54.
- **What we build.** A DataForSEO backlinks query on each rival asset URL, merged, filtered through `link-vet`,
  de-duplicated against what already links to us.
- **First test.** For the first tool the holding publishes ("Can AI read this page?"), list the rival checkers and
  the pages linking to them; count usable rows. Sending waits on the decision below.
- **Earns / improves.** Links that come to a real tool rather than to a sales page — the kind his own hreflang
  checker and quiz earned [HS].
- **Needs the CEO.** Publishing public tools is held at "not now" (decision 9) — the link list waits on his word
  with the tool. The emails go to webmasters, not prospects, but nothing is sent before the tool exists.
- **Risk.** Template blasts. Repair: one page owner, one personal line, never a guessed address.

### 4. The *How* line — an honest statement on every AI-made page
- **What.** Google's own Who / How / Why (§4.1): pages should make it self-evident how automation or AI was used
  where a reader would ask; authorship must not be deceptive. The register holds Who and Why; none holds How.
- **Where.** B84 every page for clients and for the holding; B28 written content.
- **Form.** A clause of the only-we gate (row 14); a small "how this page was made" block in the page blueprints.
- **What we build.** The block and its variants (written with AI from the owner's interview and reviewed by
  [named person]; tool built from [client]'s figures, checked by [named person]); the gate check.
- **First test.** Add the block to three holding pages; check that it reads as a strength (the reviewer and the
  evidence), not as an apology; Search Console impressions over 28 days as a sanity check, not a proof.
- **Earns / improves.** The holding practises what it is — an AI-native company that says so — and stays on the
  right side of Google's guidance and of its own no-deception boundary.
- **Needs the CEO.** The wording, and where it is required (CEO's stance on AI disclosure, which row 65 already
  leaves to him for community answers).
- **Risk.** Clients who do not want it. Repair: the block names the human reviewer first; the client signs off.

### 5. The owner's own measure and the capacity question
- **What.** The client film (§6.1) proves results in the business's units — consultations a week, a four-week
  wait — and ends with "take your foot off the accelerator, we cannot keep up".
- **Where.** B84 `proof-capture` and the monthly report; the case-study films for B28.
- **Form.** Additions to `proof-capture` and row 19.
- **What we build.** One intake field (the owner's measure, with its current value) and one monthly question
  (can you take more work?); the report's next-month plan switches to value when the answer is no.
- **First test.** For the holding: its own measure is enquiries a week to the web office (zero today — a real
  answer); recorded now as the baseline.
- **Earns / improves.** Case studies a buyer believes; retention, because the package keeps paying when the client
  is full; a natural upsell from volume to value.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing.
- **Risk.** Owners who do not track bookings. Repair: the first month the worker builds the count from the
  enquiry form and calendar with the owner's permission.

## 2. What these videos change in the reading of this expert

They move the date line. The first study read him in 2025-2026 and could not see how he got there. This set
shows the agency's channel from 2020 to 2024: in 2020 he judged links by domain authority, taught page size as a
placement factor, sold a $40 / $300 course and told viewers to noindex through robots.txt — a method Google had
retired a year earlier. By 2026 he says the opposite on each point — the linking page, not the domain; field data,
not scores; free and ungated, not a course. His correction is real and visible. It also shows that his platform
statements are only as current as the year they were said, so every one of them goes through the
platform-change ledger before a worker uses it — as the first study already found for his 2025 speed advice.

His forecasts are weak. In 2023 his colleague doubted Google would ship AI answers as they stood; early in 2024
he bet on Perspectives and user video over "a ChatGPT type of Google". AI Overviews shipped within months. What
holds across six years are his **procedures**: three pillars, a checklist before every launch, one action at a
time, change nothing on a daily wobble, a tool or a list as the thing that earns links, and press as the top tier.
Those are what the register takes from him; his predictions are not.

The back catalogue also shows that **"Harry Sanders" on YouTube is partly a team**: eleven of these 52 videos are
Lawrence Hitches', several are narrated sketches, one is a client film. The method is StudioHawk's house method,
taught by several voices — which is the model the holding copies with workers instead of staff.
