# Alex Hormozi — offers and business building: for the holding

Companion to `PLAYBOOK.md` (same folder); citations use its short ids. Everything here is a candidate for
the CEO and the board, not a decision. **Prices are candidates in US dollars to be tested; the price list
and the standard terms stop at the CEO** (B84 already says so). Anything about our own speed or cost is an
assumption until measured on the holding's own site — marked ⚠.

## The finding in six lines

1. His whole method prices a service by the value it creates and then spends the margin on removing the
   buyer's risk, delay and effort. A human agency can afford little of that. **We can afford almost all of
   it, because our delivery costs tokens, not payroll.**
2. So our offer is not "a cheaper agency". It is the thing a human agency cannot do: **the work is done
   before the client pays** — the home page built, the audit measured, the ads made — and the guarantee is
   a number the client can read in his own Search Console or ad account.
3. The front door is **repairs** — the small job no agency will show up for — at a fixed price in 24 hours,
   followed by his menu upsell. Volume there gives reviews, cases and cash; that is his roofing play.
4. The money is in **continuity**: the site fee waived or reduced against a 12-month monthly package.
5. By his constraint method our bottleneck is never delivery. It is **proof** today (no client, no case),
   and the day demand arrives it becomes **the CEO's eye on every site job** (B84 decisions 4–5). Both are
   named below with what to do inside his rules.
6. His own verdict on our vehicle is harsh — "an SMB agency" sits in his worst class, labour-heavy
   commodity `[index 01:00]`. AI removes "labour-heavy" and makes "commodity" worse, since every rival gets
   the same models. What stays ours: measured proof, speed, the Media Studio, the data library, and brand.

## 3. His unit-economics rules when delivery costs almost nothing

⚠ "Almost nothing" must be measured per job: tokens against the plan's quota, GPU minutes, paid data
(DataForSEO), and **the CEO's minutes** — his eye is the one cost that does not fall.

| His rule | What changes for us | What it lets us do that a human agency cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Price = 5–10 × delivery cost | The floor falls to near zero; the rule stops saying anything | Price only on value and on what the market's best charge; discount, credit and bonus freely without touching margin |
| Gross margin 80%+ | Ours sits far above that | LTV ≈ gross profit; nearly every dollar above CAC is free to reinvest in acquisition and proof |
| 30-day cash ≥ 2 × (CAC + COGS) | COGS ≈ 0 → **first-month cash ≥ 2 × CAC** | Client-financed growth with no loan and no card — a set-up fee or first month covers it. The interest-free route he reaches through a credit card is ours by construction |
| LTV:CAC 3:1 only if lead generation, sales and delivery are all automated; 6, 9, 12:1 as each turns manual (secondary source) | We can automate all three | We stay healthy at 3:1 where a hand-run agency needs 9–12:1 — **we can pay three to four times its CAC for the same client**: outbid on ads, pay larger referral fees, spend more on the free front end |
| "Work for free for proof" — a cost he pays once per market | Free work costs us little, every time | Proof-first selling as the standard, not the launch exception (idea 1) |
| Strong guarantees must be conditional because refunds burn payroll | A remedy costs tokens | Unconditional and work-free-until guarantees (2.5) |
| Supply constraint: take the expert off the routine, add associates, add a speed fee | Our "expert" is the CEO's approval; the "associates" are workers that scale in minutes | No waiting list for work; the only queue is his eye — which is honest scarcity and a priority product |
| "Do it in half the time, charge twice" for rich buyers | Days, not months ⚠ | Speed as the headline of the flagship line |
| Speed to lead: seconds | An agent answers at once, any hour | His "4–5× sales" claim becomes our default and a product we sell |
| Creative in hundreds | Media Studio | The ads offer (2.4) |
| The cast-aside small job | No minimum job size | The repair line (idea 2) |
| "Nail it before you scale it" | Scaling costs nothing, so the temptation is larger | The discipline matters more: one niche, one city, one offer until it converts |
| Permanent customers: one in ten at an agency | Over-delivering to keep a client costs tokens | A hundred small improvements a month for every client — his "golden BBs" — as the retention plan |
| Volume before judgement | Attempts are cheap | Test with his sample sizes: ten demos tell us nothing; a hundred do |

**What does not change.** CAC (he says it is about the same for every competent seller in a trade); the
need for proof; the buyer's distrust of a new name; negative word of mouth, which travels faster; and
priorities — his AI video's point that more capacity tempts work on low-priority things.

**What gets worse.** If our delivery is cheap, so is every rival's who uses the same models: prices in the
middle will fall. That is the reason for the barbell (idea 12) and for building what a model does not
give a rival — measured cases, the image and film studio, the data library, reviews, a name.

## 4. The constraint method as a standing weekly skill

### 4.1 What the worker does each week

For each revenue line, one chain with counts for the last week and the last four:

`reached → replied → conversation → quote → won → delivered → accepted (his eye) → paid → still paying at 90 days → referred`

Then his questions, in his order — the first that fails is the constraint:

1. **Goal.** Is this line's goal written as current state, desired state, obstacle? No → that is it.
2. **Something to sell.** Is there an offer with a price on a page a stranger can buy from? No → the offer.
   (His media company with 40 million subscribers and nothing to sell.)
3. **Margin.** Does the bundle pass the 30-day test? No → price or money model. Do not push demand.
4. **Supply.** "If customers doubled tomorrow, what breaks?" Days to deliver, the approval queue, GPU,
   model quota. Something breaks → fix that; pause demand work.
5. **Demand.** Nothing breaks → "Why can't we 10× the main input?" The answer names the sub-constraint:
   the list, the creative, the hook, the proof, the reply handling.
6. **Sales.** Close rate above 80% → raise the price. Low with enough volume → education (the video) or
   qualification. Days from request to quote; seconds from enquiry to reply.
7. **Retention.** Share activated in week one; churn; share with a second purchase.
8. **Volume check.** Is the sample large enough to judge? If not, the constraint is the number of
   attempts — say so, with the count needed.
9. **Favourite-subject check.** Where did the most work go in the last four weeks? If that place is not
   the constraint and the money figure did not move, say so in one line.

Then it breaks the constraint into the smallest countable action ("120 demo messages a week at the
approved cap → about N replies → N quotes") and names the leading number to read next week.

### 4.2 The output — one page, same shape every week

- The constraint, one sentence. The evidence, three numbers.
- The one action, as a count per day.
- The leading indicator and its value now.
- What is left burning on purpose this week.
- What stops at the CEO.
- Last week's constraint: did its indicator move? If it did not move in two weeks, the diagnosis is
  re-opened, not the effort doubled.

### 4.3 What it would say today, by his method

- Web Studio and agency seat: step 2. The brief says there is no client yet, and B84 decision 9 holds
  client-finding at "not now"; I did not check whether a priced offer page exists anywhere. On that basis
  the constraint is **proof and an offer**, not capability. The build order is the board's and the CEO's;
  this is named once, and it is the reason ideas 1 and 2 rank first: both can be prepared on the holding's
  own site without a client.
- The day a first campaign runs: step 4 — **his eye on every site job** becomes the throughput limit.
  Inside his rule the remedies are: fixed review slots, review of one direction instead of three for
  demos, the volume line (repairs, Care) kept outside "site jobs" if he rules so, and selling the slot as
  the priority lane.
- The favourite subject of an AI-native holding is building the machine. His rule: "if you're a product
  entrepreneur, product is probably not your problem."

A firm owner somewhere opens a message. It does not ask for a meeting. It shows his own company's new home
page, live, and a one-page measurement of what his present site costs him — and a price. He did not pay
for it and no one sold him. If he wants only the leak fixed, it is fixed by tomorrow for a fixed fee, and
the report that comes with it tells him honestly what he does not need. That honesty is the sales method:
his menu upsell, run by a worker that has no reason to oversell.

If he wants the site, it is live within days; the film and the photographs are ours; the guarantee is
written in things he can read in his own Search Console. He pays in full for a discount, or pays nothing
for the build and joins the monthly package for a year. Every month a live page shows what was done and
what moved. Every month earns him credit, so every quarter there is a reason to talk. When he wants
customers faster, a hundred ads are already made before he decides.

Behind that, the office runs on four of his numbers: first-month cash at least twice the cost of winning
the client; close rate watched so price rises when it passes four in five; the share of clients still
paying at two years, and what those clients look like; and one bottleneck named each week. It takes the
small jobs no agency will touch and the buyers who pay for speed and certainty, and leaves the middle.
It wins a franchisor and builds a hundred sites from one sale. Every delivery leaves a measured before
and after, and ninety days later the numbers are read again in public. After a year the office does not
say it is the best; it shows the count.

What a human agency would need to copy this: free work it cannot afford, guarantees that would sink it,
speed it cannot staff, and creative volume it cannot produce. What we need: the first ten cases, the
prices and terms from the CEO once, and the discipline his whole teaching comes down to — one offer, one
niche, one channel, more of what works.

## 6. Skill candidates

### 1. `weekly-constraint`
- **Owner:** the holding's management worker.
- **Triggers:** every Monday; on request ("what is the bottleneck"); when a line's money figure is flat
  for four weeks.
- **Inputs:** per revenue line — the stage counts of §4.1, CAC, first-30-day cash per client, close rate,
  days-to-quote, seconds-to-reply, days-to-deliver, approval queue length and age, churn, the last four
  weeks' work log, last week's page.
- **Steps:** the nine questions of §4.1 in order; stop at the first failure; decompose to the smallest
  countable action; choose one leading indicator; list what is deliberately left; check last week's
  indicator; apply the two-week re-open rule; run the price-raise check (skill 5) and attach it.
- **Output:** the one page of §4.2, in the CEO's language on his surface, in English in the record.
- **Guards:** one constraint per line, never a list; no judgement on a sample below the stated minimum; a
  dormant line under construction is reported as "not on sale yet", not as a failure.

### 2. `offer-builder`
- **Triggers:** a new product, a new niche, or an offer whose close rate is under target after enough
  quotes.
- **Inputs:** the avatar, the dream outcome in the client's words, competitor prices, our measured
  delivery time and cost, the guarantee ledger, available proof.
- **Steps:** the eight steps of PLAYBOOK §1.6 — problems by the four levers → a solution for each → trim to
  what is high value and cheap for us → price by value and the top of the market → stack with named
  bonuses each answering one objection → guarantees from the ledger only → true scarcity and a true reason
  why → three names by the MAGIC pattern → five wordings of the same deal for testing.
- **Output:** one offer page: name, promise, stack with stated values, price and payment options,
  guarantees, limits, the five wordings; and a boundary check (no untrue claim, no interest, no paid
  review, renewal stated).

### 3. `money-model`
- **Triggers:** after `offer-builder`; before any acquisition spend; when first-30-day cash is under 2 × CAC.
- **Inputs:** the offers on hand, measured CAC and COGS (or the stated assumption), take rates so far.
- **Steps:** choose the attraction offer → the upsell at purchase and at hand-over → the downsell (a
  feature removed, never a bare discount) → continuity and its annual push → compute 30-day cash against
  2 × (CAC + COGS) for each path → fail the bundle or pass it → name the one test to run, at the same
  front price.
- **Output:** the ladder diagram, the arithmetic, the verdict, the test.

### 4. `menu-upsell` (the hand-over conversation)
- **Triggers:** every repair hand-over, every site launch, every quarterly credit talk.
- **Inputs:** the job's measurements, what the client bought, the menu and prices.
- **Steps:** (1) say what he does **not** need, with the measurement that shows it; (2) prescribe the one
  or two things he does need, with what each costs him if left; (3) offer two real ways to proceed; (4)
  ask plainly whether to use the payment method on file.
- **Output:** the script for this client and the record of what was offered and taken.
- **Guard:** step 1 may not be empty; nothing is prescribed without a measurement behind it.

### 5. `price-raise-check`
- **Triggers:** inside the weekly skill; whenever a product passes twenty quotes.
- **Inputs:** quotes, wins, prices per product.
- **Steps:** close rate over the last N; above 80% → propose a 15–20% rise; after a rise compare
  conversion × price before and after; keep while it rises.
- **Output:** a one-line recommendation to the CEO (the price list is his).

### 6. `business-diagnosis` (his five questions, for a client)
- **Triggers:** before quoting ads or the Scale tier to a service firm; as a free front-end for larger
  prospects.
- **Inputs:** the client's answers to the five intake questions and his numbers (ticket, margin, CAC,
  close rate, capacity, booking delay, churn).
- **Steps:** supply or demand ("if customers doubled?") → price (close rate) → cash timing (when is he
  paid) → sales motion (education, speed to lead, quote delay) → continuity (is there one) → three to five
  moves in order, each tied to a number → a date 90 days out.
- **Output:** a one-page plan for the client, and for us the list of which moves we sell.
- **Why it earns:** it sells the site, the video, the answering agent and the monthly package as the
  answer to his own numbers, and it protects our ads guarantee from a broken offer.

### 7. `sales-video-script`
- **Triggers:** a new offer page; a client buying the sales-video product.
- **Inputs:** the offer, three or four proofs by avatar, the real objections from past conversations.
- **Steps:** promise → the pain avoided → proofs → old way / our way → each objection as "people think /
  why that is wrong / what is true / proof" → proofs again → one small action that shows it was watched.
- **Output:** a seven-minute script and shot list for the Media Studio; a second version for the
  after-sale conversation.

### 8. `proof-capture` (a delivery gate)
- **Triggers:** at the start and the close of every job; again at day 90.
- **Steps:** before-measurement stored → after-measurement stored → the client asked for a review with no
  reward → a recorded reaction or case asked for, consent written → the 90-day re-measurement scheduled.
- **Output:** a case record usable as an ad, a page and a line in the next offer. A job does not close
  without it.
