# Alex Hormozi — the sales TEAM system (playbook)

**Source.** One video, read whole: "The Most Valuable Sales Training You'll Ever Watch — $100M Sales Team",
201 min, about 55,000 words — `watch-links/2026-11-23-seo-geo/transcripts/hyt-oVPXgRs0IdI.txt`
(youtube.com/watch?v=oVPXgRs0IdI). Tags like `[T 46:21]` are the minute markers of that file. The transcript is
auto-captions: "ACQ / ECQ / ACU" are all Acquisition.com; "Mozy 20" is "Hormozi 20"; "IF Saron" is "Eye of

**How to read it.** Everything here is what he says unless it is marked **[added]** (my research, with its source)

us. The slice is the sales *team*: recruiting, training, managing, scripts, numbers. The individual closing
method (his CLOSER framework) belongs to `../sales-skill/`.

**His standing.** 15 years in sales, "over 4,000 deals one-on-one", a portfolio "over $250 million per year",
several "$100M per year" sales teams, a phone team that "closed over 1,000 sales in 72 hours" `[T 00:01–01:01]`.
He says the system below "hasn't changed in multiple years" `[T 08:02]`. The video is itself a sales asset: it

## 1. The frame — educate before the conversation (the "VSL sandwich")

**Why.** With the right person present, "all they need is the right information in order to buy". Under-educated
prospects are why sales need several calls. Information put before the talk shortens the cycle and each call and
"can sometimes double or triple the sales efficiency of the team" `[T 18:07]`. The golf picture: with a frame and
a script, the rep only practises "six-inch putts" instead of every shot `[T 16:06]`.

**The video sales letter (VSL), in order** `[T 19:07–24:08]`:

1. **Intro, about 60 seconds:** Promise → Pain → Proof → Plan → Picture (a visual road map).
2. **Core persuasion:** old way vs new way (or them vs us) — the "unique mechanism".
3. **Objections 1…n**, each in four beats: what they think → why it is wrong → what is right → proof.
4. **CTA** that makes the next step feel personal — ask one small fact ("how many square feet is your kitchen?")
   so you know they watched and the next talk feels made for them.
5. **Proof roll:** the rest is customers telling their story. Tell them: "it says 22 minutes; the first seven are
   what I need you to watch, the rest is happy customers." They keep watching.

"Most of this is a proof salad presented in ten different ways" — proof must vary by who the person is, what
they believed, what they did and what came out, so the viewer thinks "that's just like me" `[T 19:07]`. The
floor: "before/after, before/after… obviously compliantly, for 10 minutes, would do more for selling than just
about anything else" `[T 24:08]`.

**Where it goes — friction vs zero-friction** `[T 25:09–28:09]`:

- A **friction step** is before the prospect acts (the opt-in page): keep the video *short* — every added minute
  loses leads.
- A **zero-friction step** is after they acted (after opt-in, after booking, the waiting room, while you fetch
  the quote): make it *as long as they will consume* — "the more they consume before the sale, the less you have
  to say on the sale".
- Each VSL sells only the **next step**: the first sells the booking, the second sells showing up, the third the
  close. The FAQ part answers the questions people have about that next step; proof runs through all.
- On the call, first question: "Did you watch the video?" If not: "Go ahead and watch it — it's 10 minutes, it
  will save us 35 on the call" `[T 26:09]`.
- Home services: never e-mail a quote later. Hand them the tablet with the VSL while you go to the truck, come
  back, close `[T 27:09]`.
- New objections heard by the sales team are cut into the VSL: "a living document" `[T 22:08–23:08]`.
- His scale: about 50 active VSLs, two or three new or updated each week; a path per avatar raises conversion
  `[T 28:09]`.

**Qualification is the second lever.** "If you're closing zero out of 100 people, you're usually talking to the
wrong person." BANT — budget, authority, need, timing — is "the type of friction that gets the right people on
the phone" `[T 27:09]`. His working definitions `[T 169:34]`:

| Letter | Passes when | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | Enough cash flow to invest | Ask whether this is the most important problem to solve; "we do not set people who cannot make the investment" |
| Authority | The person can decide (for him: the sole owner) | All decision makers on the next call; if impossible they watch the VSL and the next call is framed as a "fit call" |
| Need | A strong desired state *in their words* plus a real obstacle | "A desired state with no obstacle, or one they're already solving, is not a strong enough need" |
| Timing | They want it solved as soon as possible | — |

Plus **deal breakers**, asked before the next call is fixed: logistics (for him, travelling in person) or value
(is the gap big enough to justify the price) `[T 170:34]`.

**[test]** Sound and current. The one weak point for a new business: the whole frame runs on proof, and a
business with no clients has none to show. The sound version is to make true proof first (own cases, measured
before/after) — see `FOR-THE-HOLDING.md` §1.3. "Compliantly" is his own word: before/after claims are regulated.

## 2. The script — intro, disco, pitch, close

Four parts; the team's training days follow the same four (§8). "Every closed sale is the same. Every botched
sale is different" `[T 13:05]`. Rules about the script itself:

- **Shorter.** "90% of the time we cut 50 to 75% out of a script and sales go up" `[T 30:09]`. A sale in 20–30
  minutes instead of 45 doubles what each rep can carry.
- **The sneeze trap.** A buyer buys right after something you did, so you learn to do it every time; scripts
  fill with habits that never caused the sale `[T 30:09]`.
- **Not sounding scripted** comes from knowing it cold, plus one technique of active listening: "repeat the last
  thing they said back to them with an upward tone" `[T 13:05]`.
- **The rep is not the expert.** Two frames replace expertise: the *secretary protecting the surgeon* ("I'm just
  here to make sure you're a good fit") and the *mechanic* ("we have to pop the hood before I can tell you what
  we'll do — if someone tells you before looking inside, I'd run") `[T 14:06–15:06]`. His reps are young and
  sell to owners of $1M–$100M businesses on questions alone.
- **Long cycles:** eight times out of ten, enterprise selling is "the same pitch five times in a row" up to the
  decision maker; only truly different buyers need different scripts `[T 15:06]`.

### 2.1 Intro `[T 28:09–31:11]`

The "big four", then the agenda and a first small yes:

> "Hey, this is Alex calling from ACQ on a recorded line. How you doing today? … I'm calling in regards to
> [thing]. … I'm going to ask you a handful of questions to make sure it's a good fit and we'll go from there.
> Sound good?"

Name · company · recorded line · reason for the call. No weather talk. The "sound good? — yes" is the first yes
of the call and surfaces resistance when it is cheapest to handle `[T 187:42]`.

**[added]** He calls the big four "required by law, at least in the US". The honest version: telemarketing rules
require prompt disclosure of who is calling and why; recording disclosure depends on the jurisdiction. For an AI
worker there is a fifth item he does not have — saying it is an AI (§ "What must be added for AI" in
`FOR-THE-HOLDING.md`).

### 2.2 Disco — "the pain is the pitch" `[T 31:11–35:13, 188:43–190:43]`

"If you nail the disco, the pitch and the close are easy." Lack of urgency always traces back here.

1. **Gap:** current state → desired state, in numbers ("200 lb now, 140 wanted").
2. **Pain cycle ("pulling teeth"):** "What's been holding you back?" → "Why?" → then rotate three prompts until
   the answer is specific: **"Tell me more about that." · "Say more about that." · "Can you give me an
   example?"** Two of the three are commands, which keeps the rep in the lead. Then "What else is holding you
   back?"
3. **Recap, label, confirm** — three sentences: "So just to make sure I understand: you [did X], [Y happened],
   and now you're [worried about Z]. So it sounds like the real issue isn't [surface], it's [label]. Does that
   sound right?"

Standards a manager checks on tape: go one or two levels below the first answer ("I want more leads" is the
surface); **attach money, a quantity, a date or a person** to the pain ("I lost 40k in six months and had to
lay off my brother"); the prospect does the talking; do not leave disco until you get "that's exactly my
problem" — "you should know they're going to buy before you ask" `[T 188:43–190:43]`.

### 2.3 Pitch — never more than two minutes `[T 35:13–37:14, 190:43]`

Bridge: "Given everything you just told me, I think [our thing] would really help you. Want me to walk you
through it?" Then the rule of three: **problem → solution (a feature) → benefit**, three times, each closed with
"do you think that would help?". Only what maps to what *they* said; "every extra thing is one more thing to
disagree with". Edify the person who will deliver ("our director of marketing — he's the one who…"), which also
sets delivery up for success. One analogy or story, no jargon, "don't be cute".

**The analogy shelf** `[T 40:18–44:20]` — an analogy "leverages someone's entire history" in one sentence:

| Point to make | Analogy |
|---|---|
| You need all the parts | The car (wheels, engine, fuel) · three legs of a stool |
| One constraint limits everything | Weakest link of a chain · a four-lane road into one lane |
| It takes time | Braces · a tan comes in layers · you can't reverse ten years in a week |
| Best, not cheapest | "Do you want the cheapest surgeon? Tattoo artist? Parachute?" |
| Stop hesitating | "The water doesn't get warmer the longer you wait" |
| Will it work for me | "Gravity doesn't care who's jumping" · the recipe gives the same cake |
| Several routes to the goal | Many ways up the mountain · the Don Shula frame (every play can score, so run them all) |

### 2.4 The ask `[T 37:14–40:18, 193:44–194:45]`

If they know the price range already: "Ready to get started?" If not: **"Okay. It is expensive. It's $8,000.
That work for you?"** — then silence. "It is expensive" makes the poor brace and makes the price sound small to
the rich. "Whoever talks first after the ask gets sold."

Tonality: three constants — loud enough, slow enough (150–170 words a minute), clear enough; two variables —
**pause** to stress what came before, **raise pitch** to ask for a response. Scripts mark three pause lengths;
the long one lasts "until they say something". Both peak at the ask.

**[test]** His figure is "the rep who waits at least eight seconds closes 30% more" `[T 38:15]`, later "23 to
40% more closes" `[T 194:45]`; no source is given, and the two numbers differ. **[added]** The published data I
found is Gong's call analysis: top sellers pause about 2.1 s after price and far longer than average reps after
an objection (gong.io pricing guide and Gong Labs, vendor data). The direction holds; the size is unproven.

## 3. The close — loops (objection handling)

"No is the job." A **loop** is whatever you say when the answer is anything but yes, ending in asking again.
His order `[T 44:20–56:24]`:

**Loop 0 — confirm value, then triage.** Whatever they said:

> "Completely hear you. Do you mind if I ask you a quick question? Do you think [doing this] is something that
> could help you get closer to your goal?"

"Closer", not "hit" — a directional yes is easy. Then "Awesome, then let's do it" — he says this alone closes
"probably 25%". If not: **"What's your main concern? What are you afraid of having happen?"**

**The five universal closes** (work on any objection; taught in this order):

| # | Close | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reason | "The fact that [their reason] is the very reason you should be doing this." |
| 2 | Hypothetical | "If [that one thing] were solved, would you do it?" — isolates the single real variable |
| 3 | Zoom out | "Let's zoom out. You're trying to [goal], we sell [goal]. What part am I missing?" |
| 4 | One to ten | "Where are you, 1–10?" → "What would make it a 10?" → or "Why not a 1?" (they sell you) |
| 5 | Best case / worst case | Best: it changes things. Worst (with a guarantee): you keep what you got and we keep working. Door three, doing nothing, is the only one that guarantees failure |

**AAA between every loop** `[T 53:23–55:24]`: **A**cknowledge ("totally get that") — **A**ssociate what they
said with good buyers ("that's what our best clients ask") — **A**sk the next question. "And", never "but".

**No statements while handling objections.** "They believe none of what you say and everything they say";
"people can't disagree with questions". Never prove them wrong — "you'll be right and not close"; give a "trap
door" for their pride. The **foil**: "Do you mind if I share what I told a client last week who had the same
concern?" lets a hard truth be said about someone else `[T 55:24]`.

**The five specific objections and the argument behind each** `[T 56:24–68:29]`:

| Objection | Core argument | His lines |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Priorities, not time; and the "when-then" fallacy | "I'll go to the hospital once I'm healthy" · learn it while busy so it survives the next busy season · "of all you have going on, what's the least important? would you trade it?" |
| Money | It is a value objection | "If we gave it away free, would you take one? Then it's not *not* about the money" · "if this were a tenth the price, would you believe it works?" · "if all it did was [half the promise], would it be worth it?" · "Can you do it for less?" is a buying question → "I could do it for more" (with a laugh) · "if both were the same price, which would you pick? why? that's why we're not the same price" · good–fast–cheap, pick two |
| Decision maker | Get off the track; it is their objection | "What if they say no?" (one in three then buys) → "What specifically would they object to?" → "Do they know you're unhappy with how it is?" → "Ask for support, not permission." Best cure: have the decision maker on the call from the start |
| Preference / fit | Everything is a trade | "Would you rather keep [the thing you like] or get [the outcome]? You can't have both" |
| Stall | Inaction has a cost | "Did you marry the first person you dated?" (burnt once, don't be burnt twice) · three decisions: like the product, the company, me? · "direction, not perfection" · "time gives you no new information — what information do you lack?" · carry the current path five years forward |

**Where the closes come from** `[T 69:31]`: roughly 30–40% close on the first ask (the script did it), about
40% more with the universal loops ("80% all in"), the last 20% need the specific ones — "diminishing returns",
so a team is taught the five universal first.

**[test]** The structure (ask → listen → isolate → ask again; questions, no statements) is sound and matches
what published call data shows about objections. Four pieces are **not sound as written** and need repair:

- *Reason close on "I can't afford it"* and the *"resourcefulness" close* (find the money: who do you know,
  installments, prepayment) `[T 48:21, 61:26]` push someone who lacks the money into buying — against the
  holding's boundaries (debt, and plain decency). His own BANT rule already says the right thing: do not sell
  to someone who cannot make the investment. Repair: an honest budget "no" ends in a smaller offer or a later
  date.
- *One-to-ten, variant two* — "whatever number they say, tell them they're above most people" `[T 51:22]` — is
  a line said regardless of truth. Not used.
- *Scarcity lines* — "someone's asking for that time", "an opening just came up", "a few spots left"
  `[T 176:36, 179:37, 181:39]` — are honest only when true. Used only from a real calendar.
- *The foil and "our best clients say that"* must refer to a real client and a real conversation.

## 4. After the ask — the two endings

**They did not buy: BAMFAM** `[T 70:31–73:33]`. Keep the last 2–3 minutes to book the next call *on* the call —
"if someone shows to the second call, you're going to close". Ladder when they resist: (1) ignore the first no
and offer two times; (2) the three-point argument — "it's less time if we do it now, neither of us adds a to-do,
and we can always move it"; (3) shrink it to 15 minutes; (4) "when did you plan on letting me know?" — that
moment becomes the meeting. "If you can't close the appointment, you're not going to close the sale."

**They bought: the referral ask** `[T 71:32, 180:38–182:40]` — compliment → intro → give them the words:

> "You're exactly the type of [client] we love working with. Who else do you know who's as [successful] as you
> who would benefit from this? … You against putting us all in a group text? I can send you a message to copy
> and paste."

After a yes: "Anyone else come to mind?" After a no: "How about someone *less* successful than you?" His
numbers: referrals are "20 to 30%" of sales (`[T 71:32]`; "over 30%" at `[T 173:35]`); half come on the closing
call, half from follow-up with buyers there was no time to ask; they "close at way higher rates, have no price
resistance, buy again".

## 5. Recruiting

**Before hiring** `[T 74:33–76:33]`: *never two news* — a new person on a new process tells you nothing; prove
the process yourself first. Hire several at once (training five costs the time of training one; if two of five
succeed the process works, if none does "you suck").

**Who** `[T 76:33–83:36]`:

- **Three Ps + one:** has sold to this **prospect**, in this **process** (volume per day, hot or cold traffic),
  this kind of **product** — two of three, prospect weighted highest; plus at least one year of selling.
- **Traits:** intelligence = rate of learning = how fast feedback is used; work ethic (they are the lead: how
  fast do *they* follow up?); humility.
- **Cost of a hire** = skill gap ÷ (intelligence × your training). With strong training, the fast learner far
  from the standard is cheaper than the experienced slow one — and keeps improving after reaching it.

**Order** `[T 81:35–87:37]`: little lead flow → setters first; plenty of inflow → closers first; spread time
zones before doubling up; a leader first only if you can recognise one. **Farm system:** he now hires only
setters ("SDR") and grows every closer ("BC") inside; result claimed: "we haven't fired a closer in over 12
months", setter churn sits in the first 60 days. **Managers:** do not promote the best closer ("you lose
twice"); pick top-quartile, even-tempered, the one others already bring questions to. Interview: "what numbers
do you track?" then the decisive one — **"how does what you do change those numbers?"** (he wants to hear
modelling, role play, incentives).

**Pay** `[T 87:37–95:39]`:

- "It is cheaper to pay better": his setters earn what closers earn elsewhere; acquisition cost, ramp time and
  churn fall by more than the pay rises; a queue of applicants keeps "pressure from behind".
- **Setters and closers are both paid on closes** (he tried sets, shows, qualified shows): it aligns everyone
  and makes setters qualify properly. Exception: long enterprise cycles → pay on qualified shows.
- **OTE half base, half variable**, attributed to "a mega study by Vista private equity"; the base removes
  "commission breath".
- **Flat amount per sale**, backed out of expected volume: $100k OTE → $50k variable → about $1,000 a week;
  9 calls a day → 6 show → 2 close, 6 days → 12 a week → **$83 a sale**.
- **Accelerators** once the numbers are stable: of 50 sales a month, the first 30 pay $75, the next 10 $95,
  the next 10 $105, then $125, $150 — the average stays near $83 but the end of the month pays double.
- If a percentage cannot be avoided: on **cash collected up front** (or gross margin), never contract value.
- Closers do two hours of outbound daily at **double commission** — no marketing cost on those deals; "a
  closer should never cost you money because self-sets alone should cover them" `[T 144:00–145:01]`.
- Career milestones are tied to **lifetime volume** (100, 300, 500 deals), not time served `[T 199:46]`.

**[test]** The Vista study could not be found; a 50/50 split is a common software-sales norm, nothing more.
Paying on closes and on collected cash is sound incentive design and the part worth keeping.

**The recruiting machine** `[T 95:39–107:43]` ("seven steps"; he names these):

1. **Traffic** (below).
2. **Application page** — a sales letter with its own VSL, not written by HR: vision, who it is for, what we
   offer, what we expect, results, requirements, pay. A **60-second selfie video is required** — "we never
   hired anyone who didn't upload it". (His page: acquisition.com careers, the SDR application.)
3. **Group call** — never one-to-one first interviews. Story: 20 one-to-one calls a week → 4 passed → 1 offer
   had stalled hiring for three quarters; group calls fixed it in six weeks. Expectations are set so hard that
   **one or two people leave the call** — if nobody leaves, the manager was too soft. Q&A with interruptions to
   test speed; yes/no by text afterwards.
4. **Technical (skills) call** — run as the first coaching session: script sent ahead, role play, "we will
   interrupt you 30 or 40 times". Late or unprepared ends the call. Watch how fast feedback is used and whether
   ego flares. Green flag: the candidate closes you ("what would hold you back from offering me this?").
5. **Culture call** — quality control against a manager desperate to fill a seat: the interviewer *unsells*
   until the candidate sells themselves; gives one candid piece of feedback and watches the reaction.
6. **Offer** — with the handbook, pre-work and VSLs; day one shows who did the homework.

**Feeding it** `[T 107:43–113:45]`: the budget is `one-year gross profit per activated rep × share who activate
÷ 3` (example: $250k × 50% ÷ 3 ≈ $40k as the ceiling). Sources in his order: employee referrals with the bonus
paid **at activation (day 90)**, not at hire — the referrer becomes an internal champion (his story: a bonus
raised from $500 to $25,000, the firm from $10M to $400M); content; the customer e-mail list; recruiters on
**contingency only**, several at once, no exclusivity, still pushed through the group call ($5–10k a hire; one
portfolio team went 0 → 50 activated in 90 days: hired 80, kept 50, "$5M a month"); ads and job boards
("trash unless you're good at marketing"); forums; outreach — "you'd be perfect for this role — do you know
anyone who's a clone of you?"

## 6. Training — stranger to working setter in 14 days

**Boot camp, isolated from the team** `[T 114:45–124:50]`. Only four activities: game tape, script
memorisation, role play, handbook (prospect training).

| Day | Content |
|---|---|
| 0 | Team reaches out; weekend one-to-one; homework |
| 1 | Sell the process before teaching it: values, path, ramp, gates |
| 2 | Intro and early friction ("I don't have time") — "confidence is built or lost here" |
| 3 | Discovery — the heaviest day; "calls are won or lost here, not the pitch" |
| 4 | Bridge, pitch, booking (BAMFAM) — easy if days 2–3 went well |
| 5 | Full script under pressure with a prospect who pushes back |
| 6 | First real dials |
| 7 | **Script graduation, pass/fail:** script adherence, tonality, recaps, the top five objections |
| 8 | Start/stop/continue survey (below), then days 8–14: phones, shadowing live calls, game tape |

- **Volume:** the script read aloud **25 times a day** (about two hours); "boredom means you're doing it
  right". The fastest learner did 50 a day, recorded.
- **Prospect before product:** "once you know how to sell a prospect you can sell that prospect anything" — the
  script is identical up to a two-minute pitch. Teach avatars, pains and the **prospect's own words** (gym
  owners say "EFT", not "recurring revenue"): "if you use different language than they do, they will not
  believe you can help them" `[T 115:45–117:45]`.
- **Role play — "the only tool I'd keep"**: frame (what, how, why) → manager **models** → rep **copies** →
  **practise** the one correction until right → **recap**. During boot camp the prospect is an "NPC": scripted
  answers, no curve balls until the line is automatic. **Speed rounds**: each rep takes the next part, no
  replies — "dirty reps". Alternative for a solo learner: the blackout method (black out one more word each
  read).
- **Three one-to-ones a day** with the manager: morning agenda, midday, end-of-day review.
- "There are only two ways to train: modelling and role play. If your sales training is not one of those two
  things, you are wasting your time" `[T 103:42]`.
- **Day-8 survey, by private message** (a group answer anchors everyone): start — what we didn't do and
  should; what could be better; stop — what to delete; continue — what to keep; what there wasn't enough of.
  A point raised by **more than three reps** changes the playbook for the next cohort (and this cohort is told);
  one rep's point is handled one-to-one; praise shows what to protect `[T 122:49–124:50]`.

**Ramp gates — why new reps do not get the leads** `[T 124:50–126:50]`. A keen new setter once burnt 50
connects a day and nobody showed; closers were clogged.

| Gate | Cap | Clears when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 connects (live answers) a day | → 10 true conversations (3 min or more) → 2–3 sets a day, held one week (about 10 sets) |
| 2 | 3 sets a day | Show rate above 60% (target 70–75%) |
| Open | 6+ hours of dial time, full calendar | Fully ramped inside two months: over 12 sets a week, over 70% show, over 3 closes a week |

Every cleared gate is announced to the whole team.

**Lead contact cadence (outbound to opt-ins)** `[T 126:50–127:51]`: they cut 14 dials per lead to **7** — the
last seven produced only 10% of sales, and dropping them doubled team capacity. Pattern over four days:
**3 – 2 – 1 – 1**. A text the moment someone opts in; every dial is a double dial from a local number; day 1
adds one e-mail and one text, day 3 the same. **An e-mail reply is treated as a callback: dial at once.**

**Cutting** `[T 127:51–130:52]`: "you get the team you tolerate". Numbers make it unsurprising: a week behind
during ramp → a two-week coaching plan with targets; a bad culture fit or someone who cannot take feedback →
cut at once; the seat is a "performance seat" with a 60-day window stated on day one, about a month to clear
both gates. Longer plans only for the coachable hard worker. **The most predictive red flag: a new rep who
starts improving the script** — "we've never had anybody do that who succeeds". Feedback must name what can be
seen and heard ("you talk too fast and don't raise your voice on questions"), never "you sound like a dick".

**Career path** `[T 130:52–134:56]`: junior/standard/senior at each role; **many small milestones** beat two big
ones; a promotion needs something "to write home about", not always money. The main perk is **better leads**:
three lead tiers, assigned by the manager and re-dealt monthly by performance — "the best leads go to the best
closers; this maximises the expected value of leads". Each hour a message shows speed-to-lead per tier. The top
of the ladder must be visibly high even if only a few reach it.

**Setter → closer** `[T 134:56–139:58]`: every KPI minimum held **a full quarter** (dial time 6 h a day, sets,
show rate, close rate, **100% admin compliance**, no repeated process violation) makes someone *eligible*, not
promoted — next open seat. First unlock: one self-set a day, closed by themselves; they must do **both jobs at
once**. Then a 7-day closer boot camp inside a 30-day cycle and three closer gates:

| Gate | Leads | Clears when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Own self-sets | 2–3 self-set closes a week, three weeks running |
| 2 | Outbound sets, lowest quality first | Show 60%, offer rate 90%, close rate above 30% → medium → high |
| 3 | Inbound (ads, organic) | Manager's decision by seat availability and rank |

## 7. Managing — the daily and weekly rhythm

**Culture first** `[T 140:58–148:01]`: the most fragile thing in a sales department. No rep is worth it — he
once cut 9 of 14 and sales rose. "If you can't put your finger on it, look at the leader." Written standard: a
**26,000-word handbook** with the path, the daily schedule, every script and the team's stories ("lore":
*force their hand*, *the garbage man* who asked for the worst leads as free practice, *yellows are gold* — the
in-between leads nobody prioritised closed the most volume). When a good rep leaves, the reason is written into
the handbook. "Volume negates luck."

**Leaderboard** — "visible, accurate, real time, and creates real-life outcomes": first a chat bot that posts
each close, then a screen on the wall; a belt for the month's best `[T 147:01–148:01]`.

**Five daily non-negotiables for every rep** `[T 148:01]`: 20 minutes of his material (prospect and product
knowledge) · one role play · one game tape or shadow · a workout · 50 dials (pipeline dials for setters, the
outbound block for closers). Each is posted as a screenshot in the team thread. Dialers are capped at two lines
because his leads are expensive; cheap leads can take more.

**The manager's day** `[T 149:01–156:03]` — every block is revenue-producing:

| Block | What happens |
|---|---|
| 06:00, 30 min | Yesterday's results and today's booked calls per funnel; strip test and fake bookings; **lead-quality check**; stretch targets; update the board; check delivery capacity; confirm reps nurtured today's calls; feedback to marketing |
| 1 h | Onboarding the current cohort (again in the afternoon) |
| Prep | Pull a call for attribution, a customer result, one behaviour to praise |
| **Kick-off** | The daily huddle, at the **slowest calling hour** (below) |
| Office hours | Extra training on request; otherwise systems upkeep |
| The other team's huddle | Setter and closer managers sit in each other's |
| Interviews | Group, technical or culture calls — every day |
| 30 min **pipeline inspection — "looking for dead bodies"** | Open single deals: what happened, last contact, next step; read the actual texts; stages must match the evidence; forgotten leads, missed callbacks, **unworked referrals**, no-shows without follow-up, hand-offs with no owner → assign an owner and a next step |
| **Live listens ("Eye of Sauron")** | Join any live call, speak only to the rep; post a screenshot when someone does it *right* — "catch them doing it right". The secret-shopper effect: one check makes the team behave as if all are checked |
| Hygiene | CRM, spam numbers out of the dial pool, **audit speed-to-lead per tier** |
| Group call review | Team game tape plus role play |
| 2 h office hours | One-to-one tape and role play with under-performers |
| End of day, 30 min | Reps' end-of-day forms (feed a dashboard), admin check (tracking sheets, outcomes, tomorrow's notes, nurture texts), one report to leadership and marketing |

**The kick-off** `[T 156:03–160:04]`. The script decides the week: **Monday intro · Tuesday disco · Wednesday
pitch · Thursday close · Friday hot topic · Saturday pipeline.** "Sales is a perishable skill"; skipping is
allowed only when a closing call ran over. Friday: the team's sticking point of the week or a seasonal issue;
if none, **popcorn role play** (the manager is the prospect and calls on reps at random). Saturday: 5–10
minutes per rep — "what have you got? do this, do this". Every kick-off has the same four parts:

1. **Shout-outs**, under five minutes, naming the *behaviour*: not "Josh had a great day" but "Josh got to
   urgency with a prospect who said he'd handle it himself — he ran the Frank challenge ('can I be frank? this
   sounds like a priorities issue, not timing')". "They can't copy a great day."
2. **A customer result** from someone the team sold — reps "get beat up all day".
3. **Script training** on the day's part.
4. **Role play**: the single biggest issue, modelled, practised, and why it helps the rep's own goal.

**Game tape, six steps** `[T 160:04]`: select calls for the week's focus → state what to listen for and why →
listen → manager models → rep practises → recap.

**The weekly one-to-one** `[T 161:04–164:05, 194:45–196:46]`. The rep fills the template at least 30 minutes
before (not doing so is a culture red flag) and brings one or two calls; the manager reads the data first and
hunts for extra calls the rep did not send ("the skeletons"). Five parts:

1. Personal check-in — handled first if anything is wrong.
2. Last week's focus: the KPI and behaviour committed, done or not, did the number move, why.
3. Data: last week vs this week vs team average, every funnel metric.
4. **This week's one focus: one KPI and the one behaviour that moves it** — "even if ten things are wrong".
5. Action items with dates for rep *and* manager; what "done" looks like at the next meeting.

For closers the manager reviews three calls on a **scorecard that scores each part of the script** and works on
the weakest part ("if this, then that"). A rep on top for three months gets career talk instead. Saturday adds a
15-minute red/yellow/green pipeline review.

**Monthly full-team meeting, 30 minutes** `[T 196:46–198:46]`: agenda → team-to-team kudos → each team's wins
→ top three → belt for number one → what to improve → what is coming. "The belt ceremony and who gets promoted
are the two biggest reinforcing events" — say exactly which behaviours earned them.

## 8. The numbers and what to do when one is off

**Setter targets** `[T 161:04]`: 50 sets a month · 70%+ show rate (60% clears the gate) · 10+ closes a month ·
5 sets per close (good reps 2.5–3) · over 13 deals a month opens the top commission tier, "$10–12k a month".
(The transcript says "about 2½ per week"; with 50 a month and ">12 a week" elsewhere it can only mean per day.)

**Closer capacity** `[T 185:41]`: at most 20 scheduled calls a day, giving 12–15 live. The pay example uses 9
scheduled → 6 show → 2 close.

**The manager's cheat sheet — metric → root cause → fix** `[T 165:05–170:34]`:

| Metric off | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dials made | Too little dial time, poor scheduling, "victory laps" | Fixed dial blocks; "the best time to set an appointment is right after you set one" |
| Connect rate (a live person answers) | Usually the system: spam flags, recycled numbers, slow speed-to-lead, wrong hour, over-worked lists. The rep cause: skipped dials (fired on the spot) | Sprints in the hottest hours; check dialer and lists |
| Connect → conversation (3 min+) | Weak first 30 seconds: flat tone, no clear ask, early objections unhandled | Game-tape only the first 30 seconds of 10–30 calls; role-play the ask ("30 seconds to see if this is even relevant") |
| Conversation → meeting set | Shallow disco; frame (VSL) not consumed | Check BANT on the call; default to same day or next day |
| **Show rate** | Booked too far out; slow first contact; closer slow to greet | **Book within four days at most, same or next day ideally**; a time when they can focus; closer opens the three-way thread within minutes |
| Show → close, right people | One of three: (1) the offer — "fix the offer, not the rep"; (2) not ready — calls run long, many questions → a VSL at every step, a multi-call agenda set up front, specific proof between calls; (3) skill → trace to intro/disco/pitch/close (below) | `[T 186:42]` |

**Closer faults seen on tape** `[T 186:42–193:44]`:

| Part | On the tape | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | Prospect: "so what is this about?" · agenda stated but no "sound good?" · prospect repeats what the setter heard | Own the agenda without apology · get the first yes · read the notes — "a few remembered details buy a lot of influence" |
| Disco | Vague current/desired · accepts "more leads" · rushes to the offer · rep talks most · stays abstract | Numbers and feelings · two levels down · stay until "exactly my problem" · only ask · attach money, date or person |
| Pitch | Over two minutes · features nobody asked for · trails off after the ask | Cut · map only to stated problems · ask and stop |
| Close | Answers "I need to think about it" at face value · stops after one objection · asks twice then quits · argues | Confirm value, then "what are the main variables?" · expect stacked objections · AAA and ask again · "an objection is a buying signal; the uninterested just leave" |

**Two free AI skills he gives away** `[T 170:34–172:35]`: one that **grades a call for BANT** and returns "the
fix for each gap plus a role-play drill", and one that turns **any call transcript into a six-line note** for

## 9. The closer's day — hunt mode and kill mode

"Reps are either hunting or killing. Most orgs only define one" `[T 171:35]`. Kill mode is the call (§2–4). Hunt
mode is everything that puts a prospect on the phone, in four activities:

**1. Call notes** — read and updated the night before and the morning of; setters fill what they can, closers
the rest. Knowing the prospect before dialling lets the rep prepare the objection and the matching proof, and
removes "the number one prospect complaint: the closer made me re-explain everything" `[T 172:35–173:35,
179:37]`.

**2. Work the list and nurture, in priority order** `[T 173:35–180:38]`:

| Priority | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | **Inbound sets** — booked, not yet spoken to | Double-dial at once and **pull the appointment forward**: "Would it be crazy if we did the call right now?" Six gains: 100% show, same-day shows beat later ones, people in the moment close more, a future slot frees up, team momentum, no reminder work |
| 1b | **Outbound sets** — handed over by a setter | Setter posts in a three-way thread ("great talking — excited for you to meet Johnny; here's the material, it'll save time on your call"); **closer replies within minutes** and owns the thread; the setter re-books any no-show |
| 2 | **BAMFAMs** — spoke to a closer, next call booked | "Great talking, we can fix this" + proof matched to *their* objection; one value message three days out; night-before and morning reminders |
| 3 | **Pipeline** — showed and didn't buy, no-showed, stalled; newest to oldest, **60 days back** | The value ladder (below) |
| — | Colour-coded lead status; "no prospect left behind" | |

*No answer after booking — the confirmation ladder:* three texts ("what's going on with [the problem you came
in for]?" · "did you get a chance to look at [the material]?" · "I've got X and Y today if you're free") →
at 12 h without reply: "prepping for our call — could you check my last message?" → 24 h before: "finished
prepping; someone's asking for that time — thumbs-up to hold it" → 12 h before with no confirmation: cancel —
"since I didn't hear back I've cancelled; life gets busy; free tomorrow?" He would rather rebook than carry a
no-show: "a dead hour costs significantly more". Confirmed: night before, check they consumed the material and
send the next piece; morning of, a testimonial from someone with the same problem, **with the relevant part
highlighted** ("minute two is you"); one hour before, "talk soon". A text reading "looking forward to our call
tomorrow" passes phone spam filters because it reads as an appointment.

*No-show:* "a no-show is not a no" — "looks like we got our wires crossed, totally happens — want to grab a new
time?", offering two times, never "when works?". Week one: one call and one text a day; week two lighter; then
the ladder.

*The value ladder for cold pipeline*, one rung at a time, largely automated, always a double dial before a text:
(1) **value** — "you mentioned [problem]; a client just sent me this"; (2) **check-in** — "what questions came
up when you looked at it?"; (3) **bump** — "John?" then "should I assume this isn't a priority right now?";
(4) **re-offer** — "an opening came up this week — want it before I give it away?"

**3. The outbound block, two hours a day** `[T 180:38–183:40]`: first **referrals** (buyers not yet asked, and
anyone who sends a kind message — call them at once): "nothing nets more money per second"; "almost always a
deal a day hiding in your closed list". Then **opt-ins** who never booked, newest first: about two sets an hour
→ four sets → at least one close. Target: one to two extra deals a day per closer from this block.

**4. End-of-day checklist** `[T 183:40–185:41]`: mark every outcome (closed / BAMFAM / pipeline / opt-out) —
the dialer then orders tomorrow's list by itself; update call notes (best done straight after each call);
honour every opt-out; **submit the day's worst call** (five a week feed training); inbox zero on every channel.
