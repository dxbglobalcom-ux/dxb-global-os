# Alex Hormozi's sales-team system — what the holding does with it

Companion to `PLAYBOOK.md` (same folder; § numbers point there, `[T mm:ss]` to the transcript). Lines served:
**B84** DXB Web Studio and its client-finding leg, **B28/B43** the agency seat, **B83** revenue lines, **B64**
the client record, **B32** the approval inbox on his phone.

**The answer in one paragraph.** His system has two halves. One half exists to make *humans* do a boring,
exact thing thousands of times: boot camps, 25 script readings a day, 12-hour days, belts, pay plans, firing.
An AI worker needs none of it — it knows the script on its first conversation, answers in seconds at any hour,
never skips a follow-up and never forgets a note. The other half is the *definition of good and the loop that
keeps it good*: a frame of proof before the talk, a four-part script, five loops, qualification, a cadence for
every state a lead can be in, a scorecard, one focus per week, and a board of funnel numbers with a known fix
for each. That half transfers whole, and it gets stronger with us, because his managers sample calls and ours
can score every one. What does not transfer is the thing his whole frame rests on — **proof** — which the web
office does not have yet, and the things the law and the holding's boundaries add for an AI seller: it
does not cold-call by voice, and it never invents scarcity or a client story.

## 1. The ideas, ranked by value

### 1. Speed-to-lead and the show-rate ladder — the first thing to build, because it is where AI wins outright
- **What.** His largest operational levers are all about time: a text the moment someone opts in, an e-mail
  reply treated as a callback, the appointment pulled forward to "right now", booking inside four days and
  ideally the same day, a confirmation ladder at 12 h / 24 h / 12 h, no-show recovery, a value ladder for
  60 days of cold pipeline (§6, §8, §9). His teams need shifts, hourly speed reports and a manager's audit to
  hold this. A worker that never sleeps holds it by construction.
- **Where.** B84 client-finding leg and B28 — every form, chat, e-mail and reply from a prospect.
- **Form.** A skill plus a state machine on the client record (B64: a prospect is an earlier state of the
  client). States are his own: opt-in → booked → confirmed → showed → BAMFAM → pipeline → closed / opted out.
- **What we build.** `lead-first-touch` and `show-rate-ladder` (§3). "Pull forward" becomes: the prospect who
  just booked is offered the conversation *now*, in chat, by the same worker — there is no calendar to wait
  for. The booking tool is the Cal.diy twin already placed in B84; the conversation inbox is the Chatwoot twin.
- **First test.** On the holding's own site: a contact form and a chat. Measured: seconds to first reply,
  share of bookings that turn into a held conversation, share of no-shows recovered. Target taken from his
  gates: show rate above 70%, first reply under one minute.
- **Earns / improves.** His claim is that same-day conversations show and close at higher rates and that half
  the work of reminders disappears. **[added]** The outside evidence points the same way: the widely cited
  lead-response study (Oldroyd / InsideSales, 2007) reports the odds of qualifying a lead far higher at five
  minutes than at thirty — vendor data, not peer-reviewed, direction only.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing for chat and e-mail on our own site. A sending domain or phone number is an
  identity step.
- **Risk.** Over-contact. His cadence is seven calls in four days; in writing that is spam. Our cadence is
  fewer touches, each carrying something useful (rung one of his value ladder), and one opt-out word stops all.

### 2. One sales conversation, written once: intro → disco → pitch → close, with the five loops
- **What.** A script short enough to be exact (§2–3): agenda and first yes; the gap in numbers; the pain pulled
  to something specific with a number, a date or a person on it; recap–label–confirm; a pitch of three
  problem → solution → benefit pairs mapped only to what they said; the ask; then loops — confirm value, ask
  the main concern, five universal closes, AAA between them, questions only.
- **Where.** The sales seat of the web office and of the agency; the same skeleton for chat, e-mail and voice.
- **Form.** One skill, `sales-conversation`, plus a per-offer script file (the words) and a per-niche prospect
  file (their words). His finding that the script is "identical up to a two-minute pitch" `[T 115:45]` means
  one skeleton serves every offer the holding ever sells; only the pitch block and the prospect file change.
- **What we build.** The skill; a script file per offer; the analogy shelf (§2.3) as a small library the pitch
  picks one from; the loop table (§3) with the repaired versions only.
- **First test.** Before any live prospect: role play. A second model plays his "NPC" prospect, then a
  resisting one, for each of the five specific objections; every transcript is scored by `conversation-score`
  (idea 4). The skill ships when it passes his day-7 graduation: script adherence, recap quality, the top five
  objections handled, no statements during objections.
- **Earns / improves.** A defined standard — "without a definition of good you are asking people to guess". It
  also makes every later change measurable, since versions can be compared on the same board.
- **Needs the CEO.** The offer, its price range and its guarantee, once per offer (he decides what is sold).
- **Risk.** In writing, his loops read as pressure far sooner than on a call. Rule for text channels: at most
  two loops after a no, then BAMFAM; never loop on an honest budget no.

### 3. Make the proof first — the frame cannot run without it
- **What.** His frame is "a proof salad": before/after in ten forms, a VSL at every zero-friction step, proof
  matched to each objection (§1). The web office has no client. This is the real constraint on our first
  sale, and no script fixes it.
- **Where.** B84 (own site as first case), B28, Media Studio for the films.
- **Form.** A proof library and three frame assets.
- **What we build.** (a) **The holding's own site as case one**, measured before and after in the numbers the
  office already plans to keep (speed, Search Console, AI-citation checks). (b) **A measured free teardown as
  the frame:** his mechanic line — "we have to pop the hood before I can tell you what we'll do" — is literally
  available to us, because a prospect's site is public. The prospect receives their own before-state measured
  (speed, crawl, schema, how AI engines describe them) *before* the conversation. That is his VSL's job —
  education before the talk — done with the prospect's own data, which no generic video can match. (c) The
  three frame pieces in his order: a short one before the opt-in, a long one after it, one before the close —
  each selling only the next step, each with the objection block in his four beats. (d) An **objection log**:
  every new objection the sales worker hears is added to the frame within the week — his "living document".
- **First test.** Ten businesses in one niche: half receive the measured teardown before the first
  conversation, half the plain booking. Compare reply rate and conversation-to-proposal rate.
- **Earns / improves.** Shorter conversations and a real answer to "why you?" while the client list is empty.
- **Needs the CEO.** Whether a real person fronts the films (his standing rule: real people); the first three
  to five pilot clients at a reduced price in exchange for measured results and a testimonial is a pricing
  decision, his.
- **Risk.** Inventing proof to fill the gap. Not done: every figure carries its source and date, and a claim
  about a client exists only with that client's record behind it.

### 4. Score every conversation; give the worker one focus a week
- **What.** His management layer is sampling: live listens, game tape, three calls on a scorecard, the day's
  worst call submitted, a weekly one-to-one that picks **one KPI and the one behaviour that moves it** (§7).
  The "Eye of Sauron" makes reps behave as if every call were heard. With us every conversation *is* read.
- **Where.** The quality loop of every selling seat; the same grader later sold as a product (idea 8).
- **Form.** A grader skill, a weekly review job, and a rule about how the script changes.
- **What we build.** `conversation-score` — a rubric built from his own fault tables (§8): per part of the
  script, did the observable behaviour happen. `sales-weekly-focus` — the one-to-one, run on the worker: last
  week's focus and whether the number moved, this week's data against the average, **one** change to the script
  or prospect file, what "done" looks like. His training days become the audit rotation: Monday intros,
  Tuesday discos, Wednesday pitches, Thursday closes, Friday the week's sticking point, Saturday the pipeline.
- **The rule that matters most.** His most predictive red flag is the rep who improves the script on their own
  `[T 130:52]`, and his warning about the "sneeze" `[T 30:09]` is the same point: a sale right after a change
  teaches the wrong lesson. So the selling worker never edits its own script. Changes come only from the weekly
  review, one at a time, and are judged on enough conversations to mean something.
- **First test.** Score the role-play transcripts of idea 2 twice, by two models, and check agreement; then
  plant known faults (no agenda, a statement during an objection, a four-minute pitch) and confirm each is
  caught.
- **Earns / improves.** The work that fills most blocks of his manager's day (§7), done on 100% of
  conversations instead of a sample.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing.
- **Risk.** A grader that rewards form over outcome. Counter: the board (idea 6) holds the outcome numbers, the
  grader only explains them.

### 5. Qualification as a gate, and the six-line note at every hand-off
- **What.** BANT with his definitions, plus deal breakers, before any proposal work (§1); and a short note
  written after every touch so "whoever talks to the prospect next gets the full picture in 30 seconds" (§9).
- **Where.** Between first contact and proposal in B84 and B28; the note lives on the B64 client record.
- **Form.** A gate (`bant-grade`) and a note skill (`call-note`).
- **What we build.** Both skills (§3). The note has double value here: it is also what a *fresh AI worker*
  reads instead of a long transcript — the same reason our own sessions hand over in notes. And where a
  hand-off is needed (sales worker → delivery master → the CEO at the approval gate), the note is the hand-off.
- **First test.** Grade the idea-2 role plays; a prospect scripted without budget or authority must fail the
  gate and be routed to a smaller offer or a later date, not to a close.
- **Earns / improves.** Proposal effort spent only where a sale is possible; no prospect made to repeat
  themselves — his "number one prospect complaint".
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing.
- **Risk.** Over-filtering while we have few leads. He says the same: with low lead flow, drop friction.

### 6. The live sales board: each number carries its cause and its fix
- **What.** His cheat sheet (§8) is a diagnostic tree: a metric, its usual causes in order, the fix for each.
  That is exactly a board that does not only show a number but says what is being done about it.
- **Where.** A surface of the holding for the selling seats (it goes through the surface door when built).
- **Form.** A funnel board fed by the client-record states, plus a `sales-board-diagnose` skill.
- **What we build.** The funnel per channel and per offer: opt-ins → first reply time → conversations (his
  "true conversation" becomes a prospect who answered at least two discovery questions) → qualified → booked →
  showed → proposal → closed → cash collected → referrals asked / given. Beside each: target, last week, and
  when off, the cause the diagnose skill found and the change in progress. His leaderboard has no one to
  motivate here; its real use for us is **script versions and niches competing** on the same numbers. Zero is
  shown as zero.
- **First test.** Feed it from the role-play runs, then from the own-site test of idea 1.
- **Earns / improves.** The CEO sees where the sales machine stands without asking, and sees it move.
- **Needs the CEO.** His eye on the surface.
- **Risk.** Numbers too small to mean anything in the first weeks; the board must show the count behind every
  rate.

### 7. The referral ask, every time, with the words supplied
- **What.** Compliment → ask for a name → give them the message to paste → "anyone else?" (§4). His claim:
  20–30% of sales, half on the closing call and half from following up buyers who were not asked.
- **Where.** At close and at the first delivered result, B84 and B28.
- **Form.** A skill, `referral-ask`, and one state on the client record ("asked / not asked / gave").
- **What we build.** The ask at two moments — on closing, and when the first measured result is delivered
  (stronger for us than his moment, since the client then has proof in hand). The daily sweep: every client
  not yet asked, and anyone who writes something kind, is asked that day.
- **First test.** Every pilot client is asked at the first result; count introductions per client.
- **Earns / improves.** The cheapest clients available, and the only source that needs no proof library.
- **Needs the CEO.** A referral *reward* (cash or a discount) is money out and a term of sale — his decision.
  His own script uses no reward at all, only the compliment.
- **Risk.** None beyond asking at a bad moment; the ask waits for a delivered result or a stated "yes".

### 8. A product: "every sales conversation scored" for businesses that have human sales teams
- **What.** He describes, in full, a service most small sales teams lack: every call graded against the
  script, one focus per rep per week, the pipeline swept for stalled deals, speed-to-lead audited, the day's
  proof for the morning meeting. He gives a BANT grader away as a lead magnet. We can run the whole
  manager's desk as a monthly service.
- **Where.** B83 as a revenue line; the agency seat sells it beside ads (ads bring leads; this stops them
  being wasted — his own argument that wasted leads are the largest cost).
- **Form.** A service with a free entry tool.
- **What we build.** Entry: "send one call recording or transcript, get it scored with the one fix and a
  drill" — free, no gate. Paid: the weekly pack per rep (scorecards, one focus, three calls to review), the
  pipeline sweep list each morning, the funnel board. Built from the skills of ideas 4–6.
- **First test.** Score ten public sales-call recordings and show the output to him; then offer the free
  scoring to five businesses the agency is already approaching.
- **Earns / improves.** A recurring line that uses work we must do anyway. **[added]** The market exists and
  is occupied at the top by call-analysis platforms (Gong, Demodesk, Fireflies' scorecards, GradeMyClose with
  a free tier — seen in search, not tested); the opening is the small business that will not buy and run such
  a platform and wants the result delivered.
- **Needs the CEO.** The price and the terms; call recordings are personal data — the data-handling terms.
- **Risk.** Recording consent differs by country; the client must hold it, and our terms must say so.

### 9. Ramp gates and lead tiers — for a new worker version, not a new person
- **What.** A new rep gets capped, lower-value leads until two gates are cleared; the best leads go to the best
  closers, re-dealt monthly; a setter becomes a closer only after a quarter of sustained numbers (§6).
- **Where.** The release rule for any change to a selling worker.
- **Form.** A gate in the release procedure of the sales skills.
- **What we build.** New script version → role-play graduation → a capped share of the lowest-tier leads
  (his "garbage man": free practice nobody expects to close) → gate on conversation and show rate → full flow.
  Lead tiers by source and score; the proven version holds the top tier.
- **First test.** The second version of the script goes through it.
- **Earns / improves.** His costly lesson — a keen new rep burning 50 leads a day — never happens to us.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing. **Risk.** Too few leads early for tiers to matter; then only the cap applies.

### 10. Prospect files before product files
- **What.** "Spend far more time educating them about the prospect"; use the buyer's own words (§6).
- **Where.** A data library read by the sales skill and by the frame assets.
- **Form.** One file per niche: the three avatars, their pains with numbers, the words they use for them,
  the five objections in their phrasing, the proof that matches each.
- **What we build.** The first file for the first niche the client-finding leg picks; filled from real
  conversations as they happen (the objection log of idea 3 feeds it).
- **First test.** The same role play with and without the niche file; score the recap–label–confirm step.
- **Earns / improves.** The sentence "that's exactly my problem", which he says predicts the sale.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing. **Risk.** None.

### 11. Pay design — for human partners, not for the workers
- **What.** Pay on the close, a flat amount per sale, on cash collected, with the reward released at
  "activation" rather than at sign-up; accelerators at the top (§5).
- **Where.** Only if the holding uses outside people: referral partners, affiliates, a commissioned human
  closer for large deals.
- **Form.** A term sheet, written once.
- **What we build.** Nothing until such a partner exists; then: a flat fee per closed, paid client, released
  after the client's first paid month.
- **Needs the CEO.** Entirely — money out and a contract.

### 12. What he does to earn, that we can run
- **A complete free training that sells its own application.** Ours: the office's whole method published
  openly, with "we apply it to your site" as the offer.
- **"Book a call and watch our team do exactly this."** Ours, and stronger: the prospect's sales conversation
  with the holding *is* the demonstration of the AI sales desk we can install for them (idea 8).
- **A scored lead magnet** (his scaling roadmap: enter details, get your stage and the next step). Ours: the
  measured teardown of idea 3.
- **Free skills as lead magnets.** Ours: the single-call scorer; a BANT grader.

## 2. What must stay human, and what must be added because the seller is an AI

**Stays with the CEO (the approval gate):**
- What is sold, its price range, the guarantee and any discount or exception — once per offer, not per deal.
- Every contract signature, refund, referral reward, partner commission and paid tool.
- Identity steps: sending domains, phone numbers, business profiles, payment accounts.
- The eye on anything a prospect sees before it is first used: the frame films, the teardown page, the first
  wording of the scripts (not each conversation).
- A person, when a buyer asks for one. His own sentence is that founder-led sales convert highest because the
  founder "does all the basics" `[T 10:03]`. For the first clients a short founder word at the close — live or
  recorded once as the before-the-close frame — is the human part worth keeping. Whether he appears is his.

**Does not need a human** (and is where his system spends most of its human effort): first reply, discovery,
qualification, notes, booking, reminders, no-show recovery, pipeline sweeps, the referral ask, scoring,
weekly review, the board.

**Added for an AI seller — not in his system:**
- **The law of each market, researched when the work starts.** In the EU the AI Act's Article 50(1) duty to tell
  people they are interacting with an AI system applies from 2 August 2026 (law-firm summaries: Cooley, Ropes &
  Gray, Reed Smith, August 2026). The CEO's word (2026-11-23): the seller does not announce that it is an AI.
- **No cold voice calls.** His engine is outbound dialling. In the United States a call with an AI-generated
  voice is an "artificial voice" call under the TCPA and needs the called party's prior consent (FCC
  declaratory ruling, February 2024). So voice is used only inbound and for call-backs a prospect asked for;
  outbound is written, and written outreach follows each market's own consent rules.
- **No local-number masking, no invented scarcity, no invented client.** The four repairs listed in
  `PLAYBOOK.md` §3 are part of the skill, not advice.
- **An honest budget no ends the close.** His "resourcefulness" close is not carried over.

## 3. The larger picture — a first-place sales machine with one human

Picture the web office's first month of selling. A business owner in the chosen niche receives one message:
their own site measured — how fast it is, what a crawler sees, how the AI engines describe them today —
with one line of what that costs them. No pitch. That is Hormozi's frame, built from the prospect's own data
instead of a generic film, and it is the one thing a web office run by machines can produce for a thousand
prospects at the cost of one.

When the owner replies, the answer comes within seconds, from a worker of the
studio. It sets the agenda and gets the first yes. It already holds the current state in numbers, so discovery
starts where his reps finish: "this is where you are — where do you want to be, and what has been stopping
you?" It pulls the pain to something with a figure or a date on it, repeats it back in three sentences and
waits for "yes, exactly". The pitch is three pairs and under two minutes of reading. It names the range, says
plainly that it is not cheap, asks, and stops.

If the answer is anything but yes, it confirms the value, asks what the concern is, and uses at most two of the
five loops, in questions. If there is no decision today, the next conversation is booked before this one ends.
A six-line note lands on the client record. If the deal needs a price exception or a contract, one approval
appears on the CEO's phone with the note attached; otherwise he hears of it on the board.

Nothing after that depends on anyone remembering. The booked prospect gets the matching proof the morning of
the talk; the silent one moves down the ladder; the one who went cold three weeks ago gets one useful thing,
then one question, then is left in peace. Every client is asked for an introduction when their first measured
result arrives.

Every conversation is scored the same night against the script. On Monday the intros are audited, on Tuesday
the discoveries, and so on through his week; once a week exactly one thing changes in the script or the niche
file, and the change earns its place on low-value leads before it touches the good ones. The board shows the
funnel per niche and per script version, with the cause and the fix beside any number that is off — so when
the CEO opens it and touches nothing, he sees leads arrive, conversations happen and the numbers move.

And the same machine is a product. The business that just bought a site has a contact form, missed calls and a
sales team of two who follow up when they remember. The holding can run for them what it runs for itself:
instant first reply, the confirmation ladder, the nightly scoring of their calls, the morning list of stalled
deals. Ads bring them leads; this is what stops the leads being wasted — sold by the agency seat, beside the
ads, on a monthly fee.

What Hormozi built with fifty people working twelve hours, six days, is a way to make humans as consistent as
a machine. We start with the consistency. What we have to earn is what he has in abundance and we lack: proof,
an offer worth looping for, and a founder's voice at the moments it counts.

## 4. Skill candidates

Each is written so the skill can be authored from it. "Script file" = the per-offer words; "niche file" = the
per-niche prospect library; "client record" = B64.

### `lead-first-touch`
- **Triggers:** a form, chat message, e-mail or reply from someone not yet in conversation.
- **Inputs:** the message; the source and channel; the client record if one exists; the offer's frame assets.
- **Steps:** (1) create or open the record; (2) reply at once on the same channel — name, studio,
  why it is writing; (3) offer the conversation *now* ("would it suit you to do this right
  here?"); (4) if not now, offer two concrete times inside four days, same or next day first; (5) send the
  after-opt-in frame with one small question whose answer personalises the talk; (6) write the state and the
  first-reply time.
- **Output:** a reply sent, a state (`in conversation` or `booked`), first-reply seconds on the board.

### `sales-conversation`
- **Triggers:** a prospect is in conversation (chat, e-mail thread, or a requested voice call).
- **Inputs:** script file, niche file, the measured current state if a teardown exists, the latest call note.
- **Steps:** *Intro* — identity (five items), agenda, "sound good?"; do not proceed without the yes.
  *Disco* — current state in numbers (state it if measured, ask if not) → desired state → "what has been
  holding you back?" → why → rotate "tell me more / say more / give me an example" until a number, a date or
  a person is attached → "what else?" → recap–label–confirm in three sentences; do not leave until "yes,
  that's it". *Gate* — run `bant-grade`; on a fail, route (smaller offer, later date, decision maker joins),
  never pitch. *Pitch* — bridge question; three problem → solution → benefit pairs mapped to their words, each
  with "would that help?"; one analogy from the shelf; name who delivers; under two minutes. *Ask* — range,
  "it is not cheap", price, "does that work for you?", then nothing more. *Loops* — on anything but yes:
  confirm value → "what is your main concern?" → at most two universal loops, AAA between, questions only;
  repaired lines only; an honest budget no stops the loop. *End* — yes: hand to approval if the gate requires,
  then `referral-ask`; no decision: book the next step before ending; then `call-note`.
- **Output:** an outcome state, a note, a transcript for scoring.

### `bant-grade`
- **Triggers:** end of discovery; any transcript submitted for grading (also the free tool).
- **Inputs:** the transcript; the offer's price range and deal breakers.
- **Steps:** for each of Budget, Authority, Need, Timing — quote the prospect's own words that establish it,
  mark pass / fail / not asked by his definitions (§1 of the playbook); check the offer's deal breakers; for
  each gap give the question that should have been asked and a two-line role-play drill.
- **Output:** four verdicts with quotes, deal-breaker result, the fixes, one routing decision.

### `call-note`
- **Triggers:** after every touch with a prospect or client.
- **Inputs:** the transcript or message; the previous note.
- **Steps:** write six lines — (1) who, role, who else decides; (2) current state, in numbers; (3) desired
  state, in their words; (4) the obstacle and what it costs them — money, date or person; (5) objections
  raised and the proof that fits each; (6) next step, owner and date. Update, never append duplicates. *(The
  six lines are my filling; he names the count, not the lines.)*
- **Output:** the note on the client record; it is the only thing a next worker must read.

### `conversation-score`
- **Triggers:** every finished conversation, nightly; any recording a client submits (idea 8).
- **Inputs:** transcript; script file; the outcome.
- **Steps:** score each part on observable behaviours from his fault tables — *Intro:* identity given, agenda
  stated, first yes obtained, nothing re-asked that the note already held. *Disco:* gap in numbers, two levels
  below the first answer, pain attached to money / date / person, prospect's share of words, recap confirmed.
  *Pitch:* length, three mapped pairs, nothing unasked, one analogy. *Ask:* price stated once, no words after
  the ask. *Loops:* value confirmed first, concern isolated, asked again, no statements, no argument, repaired
  lines only. *End:* next step booked or referral asked, note written. Then name the **single** weakest
  behaviour with the quote, the corrected wording, and a drill.
- **Output:** a scorecard per conversation; the weakest part feeds the weekly review; the day's worst
  conversation is flagged.

### `show-rate-ladder`
- **Triggers:** on a timer for every record in `booked`, `no-show` or `pipeline`.
- **Inputs:** the record's state and timestamps; the note; the proof library.
- **Steps:** *booked, no reply:* one message about their problem and whether they saw the material; at 12 h a
  nudge; 24 h before, ask for a confirmation; unconfirmed 12 h before → release the slot and offer tomorrow.
  *Confirmed:* night before, the next piece of the frame; morning of, one proof item matched to their
  objection with the relevant part pointed out; one hour before, a short line. *No-show:* "we got our wires
  crossed — here are two times", no reproach; one touch a day for a week, lighter the second. *Pipeline, to 60
  days, newest first:* value → check-in → bump ("should I assume this isn't a priority right now?") → re-offer
  only if a real opening exists. Any opt-out stops everything, permanently.
- **Output:** messages sent, states updated, show and recovery rates on the board.

### `pipeline-sweep`
- **Triggers:** daily — his manager's "looking for dead bodies".
- **Inputs:** all open records.
- **Steps:** find records whose state does not match the evidence, with no next step, with a missed reply, a
  referral never contacted, a no-show without follow-up, or a hand-off without an owner; for each, set the
  owner and the next action from the ladder.
- **Output:** a list of recovered deals with the action taken; a count on the board.

### `referral-ask`
- **Triggers:** a close; a delivered first result; any kind message from a client; daily for clients never
  asked.
- **Inputs:** the client record; the result delivered.
- **Steps:** a true, specific compliment → "who else do you know who would benefit?" → offer the ready-made
  introduction text → after a yes, "anyone else come to mind?" → record names; new names enter
  `lead-first-touch` with the introducer named.
- **Output:** introductions on record; "asked" state set.

### `frame-builder`
- **Triggers:** a new offer, a new niche, or the objection log changed.
- **Inputs:** the offer; the niche file; the proof library; the objection log.
- **Steps:** write three pieces — before opt-in (short), after opt-in (long), before close — each: promise,

### `sales-board-diagnose` and `sales-weekly-focus`
- **Triggers:** a funnel number leaves its band; weekly.
- **Inputs:** the funnel per channel, offer and script version; the week's scorecards; the objection log.
- **Steps:** walk his tree in order (first-reply time → reply-to-conversation → conversation-to-qualified →
  booked → showed → closed), system causes before script causes; for the stage that is off, name the cause
  from the cheat sheet. Weekly: last week's one change and whether its number moved; this week's data against
  the average; choose **one** KPI and **one** change; send the change through the ramp gate (idea 9).
- **Output:** the cause and the fix beside the number on the board; a one-change log, week by week.
