# Matt Diamante — what the holding does with it (analyst A)

The reference for every method named here is `PLAYBOOK.md` in this folder (section numbers in
brackets). Where the web office row (B84) already owns a piece, that is said, and only what he adds is
listed. Prices and revenue figures below are his published prices or my arithmetic on them, written
to show the shape of the business — none is a target.

## The answer to the holding's question, first

**The complete operating method of a small SEO agency that wins is short:** money pages first → fix
what blocks the site → turn the owner's own knowledge into one answer a week → link it in → earn a
few real local links a month → keep the Business Profile alive → report monthly against a roadmap the
client signed. He has taught the same six moves for years and sells nothing else.

**What makes his agency win is not the method — every competent agency knows it. It is three things
around it:** (1) a content machine that puts him in front of small-business owners three times a day;
(2) a family of free tools, each of which does one step of the method for the visitor and takes an
email; (3) a price ladder — $10, $97 a month, $3,000 a month — so that nobody who arrives leaves
without a next step they can afford.

**Where AI workers beat him:** his bottleneck is human hours. The client must give 15–45 minutes a
week, his writers produce 4–8 pieces a month, his team builds 2–4 links a month by hand, his tools
answer by email in minutes, in English, for the US and Canada. Workers can interview a client at any
hour in any language, mine the client's own records for the facts nobody else has, run every gap
measurement on the day a prospect appears, and keep a profile, a review flow and a weekly report
running for a hundred clients at once. The method stays; the cadence, the depth and the reach change.

## The ideas, ranked by value

### 1. The interview engine — the owner's knowledge as the raw material of every page

- **What.** Content is no longer written from other pages; an AI interviews the owner (ten questions,
  one at a time, by voice), and the post is written from the answers and from the business's own
  records. His proof case went from $86,000 a year to over $300,000 in six months on less traffic
  [§6]. He has built an internal app for it ("talkseo interview app" appears on his own site).
- **Where.** DXB Web Studio (B84) — the content step of the monthly care package and of every site
  build; the holding's own 25–30 sites first.
- **Form.** A skill of the content worker + a small product surface (a link the client opens on a
  phone and talks to).
- **What we build.** (a) An interviewer that takes one customer question, asks ten follow-ups by voice
  or chat, pushes back on vague answers ("give me a number", "tell me the last job where this
  happened"), in the client's language. (b) A *records miner*: with the client's permission, reads
  job notes, invoices, quotes, support mail and returns the facts only this business has (his
  example: "only 637 of those repairs, about one in 23, ever crossed $3,000"). (c) The writer that turns both into the page, with the
  answer-complete structure [§3.2] and the pre-publish gate (idea 8). The stop-slop pass already
  placed on B84 reads it last.
- **First test.** The holding's own site: five real questions, the CEO or a worker with the holding's
  real records answers for ten minutes each; the five pages are set beside five written without the
  interview; an independent reader marks every sentence that could not appear on any other site.
- **Earns / improves.** It is the one thing that separates a page from the flood, for Google and for
  AI answers alike, and it is what a client cannot get from a chatbot. It also solves the agency's
  old problem — the client never sends the notes — because talking for ten minutes is easy.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing to start. Access to a client's records is the client's consent, per job.
- **Risk.** A model that invents a number where the owner gave none; the gate must reject any figure
  not found in the transcript or the records.

### 2. Free gap tools — build once, use three times

- **What.** His tools are the same few measurements with an email box in front: who links to the rival
  and not to you; what the rival's Business Profile lists and yours does not; which listings disagree
  on name, address, phone; which money pages are missing; which pages fight each other; which
  customer questions exist; is the site ready for AI answers [§8.3]. He says they are made with
  Claude Code.
- **Where.** B84's client-finding leg and B83's customer desk (the "prospect audit" already placed
  there takes a readiness scan as half of itself — these are the other half); the same engines are
  the office's own delivery gates.
- **Form.** A free tool on the holding's site (lead magnet) = a gate a worker runs on a client =
  the evidence page of a proposal. One engine, three uses.
- **What we build, in this order.** (1) **Profile gap** — two Maps links in, every missing service,
  category and attribute out, with a ready 300-character description for each; his is US/Canada
  only and emails the answer; ours works in any country and language and shows the result on the
  page. (2) **Trust gap** — the rival's referring domains minus the client's, each row classified
  (chamber, supplier, sponsor, directory, news, blog) with the action that gets it; data from
  DataForSEO or the free road already written in B84. (3) **Listing checker** — NAP across the
  citation rows of the holding's own library. (4) **Missing money pages** — the site's pages against
  the pattern table [§1.3]. (5) **Question finder** — real questions for a trade in a given language,
  from Suggest, People-also-ask and forums. Later: page conflict, AI-answer test (idea 4).
- **First test.** Profile gap, by hand, on three real businesses of one trade in one city: measure how
  many missing services it finds and whether a business owner shown the list says "I did not know".
- **Earns / improves.** His own figure: "one free plugin can generate 16,000 email addresses." For us
  each result is also a ready first page of a proposal — a prospect is approached with their own
  measured gap instead of a pitch.
- **Needs the CEO.** DataForSEO first load (about $50, already noted on B84) when the trust gap needs
  paid link data; a sending domain for result emails if we gate by email.
- **Risk.** Reading Maps data at volume meets Google's limits; start small and through the data
  provider. A tool must never show a number it did not measure.

### 3. The offer ladder — a paid first step that costs almost nothing

- **What.** $10 live sprint (9,500+ a year, by his page) → $97-a-month membership → $3,000-a-month
  done-for-you with a 12-month commitment, sold by application and a researched roadmap [§8.2]. The
  $10 is not revenue; it is a filter — someone who pays ten dollars and edits their own site for five
  days has shown they are a buyer.
- **Where.** B83 (revenue lines) and B84's client-finding leg.
- **Form.** Three offers.
- **What we build.** (a) **A five-day "get found" sprint run by workers**: the same five days — publish
  one post, audit the money page, fix it, get it found, 30-day plan — but each attendee has a worker
  that reads *their* site and tells them what to do on it, where he can only take questions from a
  thousand people at once. (b) **A monthly "your site, read every week" plan** at the low rung: the
  weekly page-two report (idea 6), the profile and review routine (idea 5), one interview post a
  week, a place to ask. (c) **Done-for-you** on the B84 care package, sold with the roadmap (idea 9).
- **First test.** One sprint for ten invited small businesses, free, to measure completion and what
  they ask; nothing is priced until that is seen.
- **Earns / improves.** On his own published prices: 100 members at the low rung ≈ $9,700 a month; ten
  done-for-you clients ≈ $30,000 a month. His human cost per client is what we remove.
- **Needs the CEO.** The prices, the languages and markets of the first run, a payment account, and
  whether the holding's name or a separate brand carries it.
- **Risk.** A cheap offer attracts people who will never buy more; his answer is the application form
  and the price printed on the page — keep both.

### 4. The AI-recommendation field test — a sales instrument and a monthly proof

- **What.** His November film asks one question of 25 businesses — is Google's AI recommending you,
  and from which source? — and finds 21 of 25 recommended from the Business Profile [§1].
- **Where.** B84 client-finding; the monthly report of every local client.
- **Form.** A skill that produces a one-page report; later a free tool.
- **What we build.** For a trade and a city: run the ten questions a customer would ask, in Google's
  AI answer, ChatGPT and one more, three times on different days (his own rule against one lucky
  read); record who is named, in what order, and which source is cited for each; set the prospect
  beside the three who are named; list the sources that vouch for them and not for the prospect.
- **First test.** One trade in one city where the holding wants its first client; ten businesses; the
  report sent to none until the lead has read it for truth.
- **Earns / improves.** A cold approach that opens with "here is who the machine recommends instead of
  you, and why" is the strongest first message an SEO office can send in 2026. The same run each
  month is the proof of work that rankings alone no longer give.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing for the test. Accounts on the assistants if the free tier limits runs.
- **Risk.** AI answers vary by run, place and person; the report must show the three reads, not one
  verdict, and say so.

### 5. The local presence pack — the Business Profile run as a weekly job

- **What.** The full checklist of §1 and the compliant review flow of §2, done every week for the
  client: services with descriptions, products-as-services with prices, weekly post, monthly photos,
  monthly rival audit, replies to every review, the 2 h / 24 h / 48 h request, listings kept
  identical, the new registrations (Google's message agent, ChatGPT's business hub, Bing, Apple, Yelp).
- **Where.** B84 care package (a local tier); the holding's own companies that have an address.
- **Form.** A productised service + a skill + a gate (the review-request text is checked against
  Google's policy before it is ever sent).
- **What we build.** The profile worker (drafts, the client approves by one tap where Google needs the
  owner); the review-request sender tied to the client's booking or till system; the listing
  kit already written in the library, extended with the local rows.
- **First test.** One real local business — a client or a friend of the holding — for 30 days; before
  and after on profile views, calls, direction requests and the field test of idea 4.
- **Earns / improves.** It is the cheapest thing to deliver and, by his field test, the thing that
  decides local AI recommendations. An easy first product for a first client.
- **Needs the CEO.** Manager access to a client profile is granted by the client. The identity steps
  (Google's agent needs EIN or personal ID; ChatGPT's hub needs government ID) are the business
  owner's own — for the holding's own entities they stop at the CEO.
- **Risk.** Everything in §1.5 marked "do not use" stays out: no keyword names, no staff navigation
  trick, no review prompts on the premises, no hinted wording.

### 6. The weekly page-two worker

- **What.** His most-commented own product (3,288 comments): an agent that reads Search Console each
  week, finds queries at positions 11–20 with many impressions and pages with impressions and no
  clicks, and mails the changes to make [§6].
- **Where.** B84 — every client and every holding site with Search Console; it is the engine of the
  low rung in idea 3.
- **Form.** A scheduled skill; its output is a short list a worker executes and a line in the monthly
  report.
- **What we build.** Search Console API read → the two regex groups (money queries, question queries)
  → for each candidate: the page, the phrase, the exact sentence or section to add, the new title →
  executed where we hold the site, proposed where we do not → re-measured four weeks later so that
  each change has a before and after.
- **First test.** The holding's own property with the longest history; ten changes; four weeks.
- **Earns / improves.** The fastest visible result in SEO, and the one a client understands. It also
  fills the content plan: a question query with impressions and no page is next week's interview.
- **Needs the CEO.** Search Console access to the holding's properties.
- **Risk.** Stuffing the phrase in "a couple more times" (his reel's wording) is the lazy version; the
  worker adds an answer, not a repetition.

### 7. The trust-gap link service — earned links only

- **What.** His link method reduced to what survives his own reversals [§4.3]: the rival's gap, the

- **Where.** B84 decision 15 already owns "measure who links to the rival and not to the client",

- **Form.** Additions to the link step of the care package + a library extension (a `local` tab of

- **First test.** For one client or one holding site: list every supplier, sponsorship and membership
  it already has and count how many link to it today.
- **Earns / improves.** Links that cost a request, not a fee, and that are exactly the sources AI
  answers quote for local trust.
- **Needs the CEO.** Membership and sponsorship fees are money out — the client's for a client, his
  for the holding's own sites.
- **Risk.** None of the three things he used to recommend enters: bought placements, three-way swaps,
  Wikipedia editing.

## The larger picture — a first-place web office built on this

Picture the office on a Monday. Overnight, the **page-two worker** read every client's Search Console
and wrote each a short list: four sentences to add, two titles to change, one question with
impressions and no page. For that question the **interviewer** has already sent the owner a link; he
answers ten questions from his van in eleven minutes, in his own language. The **records miner** adds
the two numbers only his business has. By noon the page exists, has passed the money-page, on-page and
link gates, and is published with three old pages pointing at it. The **profile worker** turns the
same answer into this week's Business Profile post and three more for the month; the **eight-way
cutter** makes the reel, the carousel and the mail. Two hours after each of the day's customers
leaves, the **review sender** asks once, plainly, and twice more at most. On the first of the month
the **field test** asks the assistants who they recommend in that trade and city, three times, and
the report shows the client's name moving up the list — with the source the machine quoted.

Outside, the same engines face the market. A business owner sees a thirty-second film showing a new
Google feature working; the word in the comments brings him a link; he pastes his Maps link and a
rival's and sees, at once, the eleven services the rival lists and he does not, each with a
description ready to paste. That is the free tool. Two days later he has a twelve-month roadmap in
his inbox built from his own site — before anyone has spoken to him. He can take the five-day sprint
for the price of a coffee, the weekly plan for the price of a phone bill, or hand it all over.

Three things make this first place rather than a copy. **Depth:** Matt's client gives fifteen minutes
a week; ours gives the same and also their records, so every page carries a fact no rival has.
**Reach:** his tools and calls are English, North American, at 1 pm Eastern; workers serve any market
in its language at its hour — the holding is global. **Proof:** his report is clicks and a ticked
list; ours adds who the machines recommend, measured three times, and every change with its
before and after. And the office is its own first case: the holding's 25–30 sites run the whole
method first, which is both the test bench and the portfolio.

What the holding does **not** take from him: paid placements presented as earned links, three-way
swaps, keyword-stuffed profile names, review prompts at the till, orchestrated Reddit threads, staff
"navigation" signals, and hooks that are not true. Each has a sound version in PLAYBOOK, and the sound
version is what gets built.

## Skill candidates

Each is written so the skill could be authored from it.

### `owner-interview-post`
- **Triggers** when a content job has a customer question and a reachable owner or expert.
- **Inputs:** the question; the business (trade, city, services, prices); the owner's channel (voice or
  chat) and language; any records the client allowed; the target page type.
- **Steps:** (1) read what already ranks and what AI answers say for the question; list what all of them
  say (the commodity) and what none says. (2) Draft ten questions aimed at the gap: a number, a last
  real case, a mistake, an opinion against the consensus, a local detail, a price with its drivers.
  (3) Ask one at a time; when an answer is vague, ask once more for the figure or the example. (4)
  Pull supporting facts from the records. (5) Write the page: answer in the first sentence; key
  takeaways; H2/H3 only; the owner's words kept where sharp. (6) Mark every figure with its origin
  (transcript line or record); delete any figure with none. (7) Hand to the on-page and link gates.
- **Output:** the page, the transcript, a facts table with origins, the list of sentences no other
  site could carry.

### `gbp-optimise`
- **Triggers** at onboarding of a business with an address or service area, then monthly.
- **Inputs:** profile access (or the public profile for an audit), the site, the top three rivals for
  the main query, the list of services with prices and areas really served.
- **Steps:** run the §1.1 table top to bottom; compare categories, services, attributes, photo count,
  review count and reply rate with the three rivals; write a 300-character description per service
  (service + place + nearby areas + one credibility fact); build products-as-services with prices;
  set service areas inside a 30-minute drive; check the footer NAP against the profile character by
  character and the CID link; list what needs the owner's own hand (verification, the message
  agent, identity).
- **Output:** a filled change sheet (field, now, proposed), the owner's to-do, the monthly rhythm
  (weekly post, 3–5 photos, rival re-audit), and the refusals (name stuffing, fake areas).

### `review-request-flow`
- **Triggers** when a client has customer contacts and wants reviews.
- **Inputs:** the source of completed visits or jobs with contact and time; the review link; language.
- **Steps:** send one plain message two hours after the visit; one reminder at 24 h, one at 48 h; stop.
  Every customer is asked, none filtered. Before first send, check the text against Google's policy:
  no reward, no requested wording, no staff name, no sending while the customer is on the premises.
  Draft a reply to each new review; a negative one names the problem and the fix, never argues.
- **Output:** the three messages, the send log, the reply drafts, a monthly count of asked / reviewed.

### `striking-distance-weekly`
- **Triggers** weekly for every property with Search Console.
- **Inputs:** Search Console data for 28 days and the previous 28; the site's pages.
- **Steps:** filter queries at positions 11–20 by impressions; apply the money regex and the question
  regex; for each, find the ranking page and whether the phrase is in title, H1, first 100 words;
  propose the exact addition (a sentence, a section, a title); list pages with high impressions and
  low clicks with a new title and description; list question queries with no fitting page as
  interview topics; record each change with date and baseline; re-read after four weeks.
- **Output:** at most ten actions, each with page, phrase, change, expected effect; a before/after
  table for last month's actions.

### `trust-gap-links`
- **Triggers** monthly per client, and once at the proposal.
- **Inputs:** the client domain, three rival domains, the client's suppliers, memberships and
  sponsorships, the holding's source library.
- **Steps:** rivals' referring domains minus the client's; classify each (chamber/association,
  supplier, sponsor, directory, news, blog, junk) and drop junk by the test "would a real person
  click this?"; check each existing supplier, membership and sponsorship for a link; search
  `"brand" -site:domain` for unlinked mentions; propose one citable asset from the client's own data;
  write each request as a personal note; stop at every fee or membership and pass it to the client.
- **Output:** the gap table with an action per row, the notes ready to send, the month's result (asked,
  gained), nothing bought, nothing swapped.

### `money-page-audit-fix`
- **Triggers** for every service, location and category page at onboarding, and for any page the
  weekly worker flags.
- **Inputs:** the page, its target query and place, the ranking pages, the owner's price and process.
- **Steps:** check the page against the pattern table (one page per service a customer would call
  for); run the money-page gate; run the situation prompt as a local customer; ask an assistant on the
  page the five customer questions and note what it cannot answer; rewrite the opening as the
  answer; add range, tiers, drivers, caveat; add the unique fact; add local detail and first steps.
- **Output:** the gap list, the rewritten page, the list of pages the site is missing.

### `tech-audit-triage`
- **Triggers** at onboarding and monthly.
- **Inputs:** a crawl (Screaming Frog or the office crawler), Search Console's indexing report.
- **Steps:** sort every finding into serious (not indexable, robots block, no sitemap, no HTTPS,
  orphan, broken link, missing H1), warning (titles, descriptions, multiple H1) and info (speed,
  images, scripts); fix or write the fix in that order; judge "not indexed" pages one by one —
  should it be indexed at all?; test the three key pages on a real phone profile.
- **Output:** three lists with the exact page and fix for each line; what was fixed; what waits on the
  client.

### `ai-recommendation-field-test`
- **Triggers** for a prospect report and monthly for local clients.
- **Inputs:** trade, city, the business, ten customer questions.
- **Steps:** ask each question in Google's AI answer, ChatGPT and one more assistant, three times on
  different days; record businesses named, order, and the source cited for each; tally who is named
  most and from which source type (profile, own site, directory, review site, news, forum); list the
  sources that vouch for the named businesses and not for this one.
- **Output:** one page: the tally, the three reads side by side, the sources to win, and a plain line
  that these answers vary.

### `repurpose-8x`
- **Triggers** when a page or post is published.
- **Inputs:** the page, the brand's channels and voice, the Media Studio for film.
- **Steps:** make the text post, the reel script and film, the carousel, the story, the mail, three
  Business Profile posts spaced over the month; put the target phrase in each caption; where a comment
  keyword is used, tie it to a real free result; every hook is checked for truth before it goes out.
- **Output:** the set, a schedule, and the links to measure each piece.

### `roadmap-12-month`
- **Triggers** on an application from a prospect.
- **Inputs:** the domain, three rivals, any access granted, the trade and area.
- **Steps:** baseline; the weekly worker's first list; the money-page map; the technical triage; the
  trust gap; the field test; twelve months of dated work in his six steps; a monthly tick-list.
- **Output:** a roadmap the client can sign and the office can tick, which promises work and never a
  rank.
