## 0. The whole method on one page

His agency sells one system, and every video is a piece of it (site:/work-with-us, myt-RXxEWOVxmaM,
cap 2026-07-24 "publish consistent, helpful content and earn a handful of quality backlinks every month"):

| Step | What is done | Cadence he states |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Money pages first | Find the searches that bring buyers; one page per service, per location, per product category; fix those pages before anything else | first month |

## 1. Google Business Profile (GBP) — the complete checklist he teaches

"Your Google business profile is step one in the entire funnel" (myt-1dzTGLd5o9s 00:50). His November
2026 field test: of 25 local businesses recommended by Google's AI, 21 were recommended from their
Business Profile, not their website; the winners had every service listed (some with descriptions),
a steady flow of new reviews and an exact primary category (myt-e11i6ut9Xik 02:50; same film as
matt-DfzrUoyxEeH). "Everyone's chasing the latest AI hack while the businesses doing the boring stuff
are the ones that are getting recommended."

### 1.3 On-site local signals

- **Never write "near me" on the site.** Google reads the searcher's location; optimise for the real
  city, neighbourhood and landmark names (myt-IvgpRyK0bl4 00:35; myt-Wi6aA5RN4Is 06:10).
- **Hyper-local landing pages:** one page per service × neighbourhood; H1 with service and place; body
  with local landmarks, a customer story from that area, photos of jobs done there, an embedded map,
  LocalBusiness/Service schema, and reviews that mention the place. "Don't just swap out neighborhood
  names" (myt-IjTaTDpRrME 05:05). He reports +25 % combined impressions against one generic city page
  (myt-HDlmkdOU-68 03:50).
- **Money-page patterns** (site:/money, Rank Week material): service business = `[service] + [city]`,
  one page per service a customer would call for ("A plumber shouldn't lump drain cleaning, water
  heater installation and sump pump repair onto one services page"); premises = `[type] + [city or
  neighbourhood]`, one page per location with address, hours, parking, photos, reviews; products =
  `[product type] + [key attribute]`, and category pages usually earn more than product pages;
  realtors = seller, buyer, neighbourhood and niche pages, winning on local knowledge the portals lack.
- **Situation match (AI Mode, Oct 2026):** recommendations are personalised to the searcher's situation,
  so a service page must speak to it — a flood-damage page that names last week's rainfall in that
  city and the first steps to take. Test prompt: "Read the service page like a customer in [city] who
  needs service because of [specific situation]. Does it speak to my situation, or could it be any
  company's page? List what's missing: local details, first steps, and questions I'd still have."
  (matt-DehlTqYRloY; cap 2026-11-12).
- **Local content:** posts tied to the place ("5 common roofing issues in Hamilton winters")
  (myt-Wi6aA5RN4Is 07:05).

### 1.4 Citations and NAP

## 2. Reviews — the rules after Google's 2026 enforcement

**[his]** (myt-ez6ZCM5kMgY, caps 2026-04-16, 2026-04-27). Not allowed: asking while the customer is in
the shop; a discount or gift for a review; staff contests; asking the customer to name a staff member;
asking friends and family; software that lets only happy customers through ("review gating" — a
business he saw went from hundreds of reviews to about 12 overnight and had not recovered a year
later, myt-GR_A64W-ex4 00:00). He reports real reviews from his own clients never appearing.

**[added, verified today]** Google's Maps user-contributed-content policy
(support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) says merchants must not offer incentives,
"discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews", must not "require
or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises, nor should they request
that specific content be included", and lists as violations asking staff to solicit a number of
reviews or reviews naming a staff member. So his list is accurate, with one nuance: the text forbids
*pressure* on the premises, and his "Chrome knows you are in the parking lot" explanation of how it is
detected is his theory, not Google's statement.

**The compliant review system he gives:**

1. Collect contact details from every customer — without them nothing works, and "you own that list".
2. Send a text or email about **2 hours** after they leave: still satisfied, remembers details, no
   longer on the premises. Wording: "Hey, hope you loved the haircut. Mind taking a minute to leave us
   a quick Google review? Link's below." No hint of content, no reward, no name.
3. If no review: one reminder at **24 hours**, one at **48 hours**, then stop.
4. Ask **every** customer, the unhappy ones included; a mix of four and five stars reads as real.
5. Reply to every review; to a bad one publicly, without defending — name the problem, say what was
   fixed, take it inside as feedback (myt-GR_A64W-ex4 01:45; myt-IjTaTDpRrME 04:45).
6. Invite a photo with the review ("drop a photo of your experience") — it convinces the next
   customer (myt-Wi6aA5RN4Is 04:00; his EXIF reasoning is the weak part, see §1.5).
7. Spread beyond Google: Yelp, Facebook, the industry platform — "AI Overviews are pulling reviews
   from across the web" (cap 2026-07-16); ChatGPT uses Yelp data (cap 2026-07-27).

**[test]** His Reddit tactic — open a thread "[business] reviews from past clients" in a local
subreddit and send it to clients (caps 2026-07-19, 2026-07-25, 2026-09-13) — works for the branded
search result but is self-promotion that most subreddits forbid, and an orchestrated thread is the
"inauthentic mention" Google's own guidance warns against (his cap 2026-08-24 quotes it). Sound
version: never start it; when a real thread about the business exists, answer in it openly as the
business.

## 3. AI search (AEO / GEO) as he teaches it

### 3.1 The model

- **Rank on Google first.** "To rank on ChatGPT and Google AI, you have to first rank on traditional
  Google. You can't skip this step" (myt-Lpp5QdHFk80 01:25). His theory: ChatGPT runs a Google-like
  search and leans on what already ranks (myt-1kN0INvXXJ8 07:05).
- **The four acronyms collapse into one** (myt-_gjfuJY4f8E): SEO = be found; AIO = the machine
  understands you (schema, factual detail, presence on the sources it reads); GEO = it mentions you
  when comparing (comparison and decision pages with real prices: "memberships start at $49 a month
  compared to Peloton's $59 app plus a $2,000 bike"); AEO = your answer is the one lifted (the question
  as heading, the answer in the first sentence).
- **The trust gap** (matt-DgDUJIkhWdn, Nov 2026): AI answers cite their sources exactly as PageRank
  did; the sources come from search results, and those are decided largely by who vouches for you.
  "Google and AI recommend the business other people vouch for." Page one now shows ads, the map
  pack and an AI overview naming three or four businesses — "If you're not one of them, do you even
  exist?" AI repeats what the web says without checking it (his example: ChatGPT repeating a
  competitor's inflated review count).
- **Citations and mentions weigh more than links for AI** (myt-Lpp5QdHFk80 06:25): directories, chamber,
  local news, podcasts, forums; five new places as a first plan; consistency over time.

## 4. Backlinks and trust

### 4.1 What he believes (and what the agency does)

- Most first-page pages have 10–40 backlinks, often fewer; the agency builds **four a month** per
  client (two on the smaller plan) by manual outreach; quality and relevance over count; results in
  two to three months (myt-8YRKTdkTq7o 01:00, 03:05; myt-RXxEWOVxmaM 07:05).
- **Referring domains**, not backlinks, are the measure — he once got 400,000 backlinks from one
  footer link on a client's site, from about 500 domains (myt-RXxEWOVxmaM 07:35). Before choosing a
  keyword, check how many referring domains the top three pages have; 300 against your 5 is not
  winnable by content (myt-IvgpRyK0bl4 08:05).
- **Forget domain authority**: "Moz, Ahrefs and Semrush all give the exact same site different scores…
  Google doesn't use any of those numbers. A link from your local hockey league with a score of 12
  might do more for you than some random site with a score of 60" (matt-DgDUJIkhWdn 09:43).
- The one test of a link: **"Would a real person click on this?"** — related site, looks legitimate,
  active social accounts (09:18).
- Nofollow links still count for brand and traffic; a natural profile has both (myt-8YRKTdkTq7o 03:55).

## 5. Technical SEO — the audit and the order of repair

**Definition:** search engines can find the site, understand it and store it; "content's the engine,
backlinks are the fuel, technical SEO is the piece that connects it" (myt-Groh1TMClbQ 00:05).

**The order (his audit tool's three levels, myt-rF1a808Nahg):**

| Level | Check | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Serious — first** | Google can see the site: `site:domain.com` returns pages; `robots.txt` has no `Disallow: /` (paste it into an assistant and ask what it blocks) | remove the block; set up Search Console as a *domain* property with DNS TXT; submit `/sitemap.xml` |
| | HTTPS | certificate |
| | Orphan pages (no internal link points to them) — "the easy one first" | link from one or two relevant pages |
| | Broken links / 404s | 301 redirect or correct the link |
| | Missing H1 | one H1 stating what the page is about |
| **Warnings — second** | titles too short, too long, missing or duplicated; meta descriptions blank or copied; several H1s | rewrite (paste the page into an assistant for drafts); his extension's rule for the description is 50–160 characters (myt-yg_z6ZyLOvE 05:10); **[added]** a title of roughly 50–60 characters is the common working length |
| **Info — last** | image weight, caching, lazy loading, minified CSS/JS, Core Web Vitals | plugin (he names WP Rocket); on Shopify remove unused apps, fast theme |

Indexing report reading (myt-i41Yg9kptoU 02:55): not every page should be indexed (tags, attachments,
filtered duplicates); "Crawled – currently not indexed" on a service page is the red flag; 404 = deleted
or renamed without a redirect. **The 60-second weekly check:** 15 s Insights page for anything odd;
15 s performance against the previous period; 15 s new indexing errors; 15 s pick **one** query at
positions 11–20 and add it to the list (06:10). Reports are read monthly; daily swings mean nothing.

**Mobile first:** test on a real phone — load time, text readable without zoom, phone number tappable,
form short and autofilled (myt-GR_A64W-ex4 04:35). His example: a contractor's desktop page loaded in 2
seconds and the mobile page in 11.

**Schema as hygiene:** LocalBusiness at minimum (name, services, hours, phone, address, logo), built by
asking an assistant and letting it ask back for the facts it needs (myt-GR_A64W-ex4 05:25).

**Keyword cannibalisation:** two pages competing for one keyword; his free checker
**keywordconflict.com** (caps 2026-07-23, 2026-07-29). Variations of a keyword go as sections into one
page, not as separate posts (myt-IvgpRyK0bl4 05:15).

**[test]** The order is right and is the useful part. The statistics are loosely sourced: "53 % leave
after 3 seconds" is a Google/DoubleClick figure from 2016, not a 2025 PageOptimizer Pro study as he
says; the Deloitte figure he quotes for Shopify (0.1 s faster → +8.4 % conversions, +9.2 % order value,
myt-gniai7LkTwM 02:05) is real (2020 retail study). "Core Web Vitals are baked into the algorithm" is
true but they are a light signal — which is exactly why he puts speed last. In the transcripts
"site semicolon" and "root.txt" are speech errors for `site:` and `robots.txt`.

## 6. On-page rules and internal links

**Keyword placement** — the three places his extension checks first: title tag, meta description, H1;
then the first paragraph, at least one H2, the body a natural number of times, the last paragraph
(myt-Lpp5QdHFk80 04:45; myt-RlWDeKwyBh0 05:25; myt-yg_z6ZyLOvE 02:10). How many times: count how often
the pages ranking for it use it and stay in that band ("if they're using it four or five times, I'll
use it maybe four to six", myt-RlWDeKwyBh0 04:35). Exact match is not required; close variations count
(myt-yg_z6ZyLOvE 06:10). Compare word count and format with the ranking pages; add what they lack — a
table, an infographic (09:30). No emojis in headings, no headings used for styling (myt-LPhfL5hPNfA 11:05).

**Internal links — his "cheat code"** (myt-RXxEWOVxmaM 05:15; myt-gniai7LkTwM 04:10; caps 2026-09-22,
2026-11-04; matt-DeM3SOcxKR6):

1. At every publish: find **three old pages already indexed**, add a link from each to the new page,
   with descriptive anchor text (never "click here"). New posts "start ranking within a week".
2. In the new page: at least three links out to service, product or collection pages.
3. To lift a money page: Google `site:yourdomain.com "keyword"`, open three of the pages that use the
   phrase, link the phrase to the page that should rank for it. Repeat per keyword.
4. Hub: every cluster post links to the pillar and to sibling posts; an internal-link map takes 30
   minutes (myt-Lpp5QdHFk80 05:25).
5. A landing page for ads is the exception: one button, no blog links, may break every SEO rule
   because it is not meant to rank (myt-LPhfL5hPNfA 10:10–14:30).

**Easy wins from Search Console** (myt-i41Yg9kptoU 04:05; matt-Dcd--Ljxd1Q; matt-DVmJspjj7wU):
positions 11–20 with high impressions → add the phrase and a paragraph to the page, rewrite the title;
pages with impressions and few clicks → rewrite title and description. Two query filters (Custom
regex) he hands out:

- money queries: `\b(buy|shop|order|pricing|price|cost|hire|book|schedule|reserve|near me|in stock|available|same day|best|vs|review|reviews|alternative|alternatives|deal|discount)\b`
  — then the Pages tab shows "your money pages" (caps 2026-01-15, 2026-02-11);
- question queries: `^(\S+\s+)*(who|what|when|where|why|how|which|can|do|does|did|is|are|should|will)\b(\s+|\S+){4,}$`
  (cap 2026-09-11).

He automated the page-two hunt as a weekly Claude agent that reads Search Console, sorts by
impressions, and emails "a roadmap to get another 500 clicks per month"; the prompt sits behind
heytony.ca/claude-prompt (matt-Dcd--Ljxd1Q — 3,288 comments).

**Content, the part that changed in 2026** (detail in `../b/`): AI interviews the owner. Prompt 1: "I'm
writing a blog post answering this question: [QUESTION]. Ask me anything you'd need to know the answer
to in order to write a blog post that is truly helpful and unique. It should include my experience,
expertise, case studies, examples, opinions, etc. Keep it limited to 10 questions. Ask me them one at a
time. I'll be using voice mode to answer them." Prompt 2: "Write this as a clean WordPress-ready blog
post using my expertise, experience, opinions, stories, etc. Use H2 and H3 headings only…" (cap
2026-10-03). Then edit, fact-check, add three internal links and real photos: 2 min question + 20 min
interview + 20 min links and images = 45 min a post, 50 posts a year (myt-15CCl7Osv8Y). His proof case
is David, an auto shop: content written entirely this way, drawing also on his customer notes;
marketing-attributed revenue $86,000 in the year to April 2025 and over $300,000 from April to
September 2026, on *less* traffic (matt-DfNYhUqR5bK 06:07). The decisive line of the film: the generic
draft said repairs "could save you thousands"; David's said "only 637 of those repairs, about one in
23, ever crossed $3,000."

## 7. E-commerce, landing pages and the checkout

### 7.1 Shopify / store SEO (myt-gniai7LkTwM; caps 2026-06-16, 2026-07-09)

Descriptive product titles ("men's slim fit blue cotton shirt", not "blue shirt"); unique descriptions,
never the manufacturer's; **collection pages treated as articles** — keyword in title, meta, H1, body,
plus FAQs and buying guidance ("I have seen collection pages single-handedly change a business");
blog twice a week for topical authority (one client: 900+ keywords in the top three after two years,
revenue ×5 — his claim); every post links to products and collections with keyword anchors. Product
pages convert on photos, descriptive names, benefit copy, bullets and real reviews — fix those before
buying traffic.

### 7.2 Seven landing-page rules (myt-rGRcKJOKQOo)

1. The form above the fold, on phone and desktop.
2. Say exactly what they get; headline under seven words; one line naming the format.
3. Mobile: large button, padding round fields; fill the form yourself on a real phone.
4. Embed the form; no pop-up.
5. Ask for less (email, perhaps first name); enable autofill.
6. Social proof directly under the form.
7. **Sell on the thank-you page**: a $17–27 offer tied to the free thing, one-click checkout, a timer
   that really expires. "That's how I recoup my ad spend while I'm building my email list."

Plus, from the live coaching call (myt-LPhfL5hPNfA 09:20–16:20): no stock photo everyone has seen —
the owner's own face; one call to action repeated; a separate thank-you page so a second pixel event
can fire. Measure one change at a time.

**[test]** "I'm not happy with anything less than 50 % conversion" applies to a free opt-in page fed by
his own warm audience, not to a service page; quoted to a client it would mislead. His own homepage
experiment (Oct 2026): everything removed except two buttons, "Do my SEO for me" and "Teach me SEO"
(matt-DeeoGwcR9dY; confirmed on the live site, which adds "Or you're a competitor. Hi.").

### 7.3 Order value at the checkout (myt-Z9eM8A-uOdo)
