## The answer first

253 reels add little *method* to a, b and c — he teaches the same loop every month. What they add is of
three kinds:

1. **Where he is wrong, he is wrong about platform news, and fast.** Of the platform claims in these reels
   checked at the source, four are wrong or overstated (the search box "removed", a Lighthouse audit sold as a
   ranking update, ChatGPT "replacing Google reviews with Yelp", llms.txt taught then dropped), on top of the
   three `c` found. Meanwhile the real 2026 changes — Search Console's generative-AI report, the AI opt-out
   control, GA4's AI Assistant channel, ChatGPT ads, the Yelp licence — are each verifiable in minutes, with
   dates and limits he leaves out. **A worker that keeps a checked ledger of these changes is worth more than
   any single tip.**
2. **The safety steps around the loop**, which he only half says: check the risk before editing a page that
   already ranks; leave a rewritten money page alone until it can be read; check links and traffic before a
   page is deleted; read the page as a customer and against the page that wins.
3. **Small, real research moves** that no study holds: Google's suggestion endpoint with its relevance order,
   an audience map (SparkToro), a rival's e-mail funnel read through a tagged inbox, the longest-running ads in
   Meta's library, a named method as something only one firm can be cited for, and the local community answered
   under the business's own name.

## The ideas, ranked by value

### 1. The platform-change ledger — a checked record every worker reads (NEW; D18–D23, D21, D22)

- **What.** One table, kept by a worker: each change Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Meta or a major tool makes that
  touches search, ads or local presence — with the primary source, the date announced, the date it reached
  everyone, the scope (country, plan, surface), what it does and does not do, and what it changes for our
  workers. Seeded today with the rows verified in PLAYBOOK D18–D23.
- **Where.** The web office (B84) and the ad office (B28); read by skill 25 (`winner-loser`), skill 3, skill 19,
  the carousel line (`c` row 51) and every client explanation.
- **Form.** A data library + a skill (`platform-change-ledger`, row 65) that also serves as `c` idea 9's
  news-truth gate — the gate *writes* the ledger.
- **What we build.** A watcher on the official channels (Google Search Central blog and status, Search
  Console help "What's new", Google Ads and Analytics release notes, OpenAI's newsroom, Bing Webmaster blog,
  Meta's business news); for each item: open the primary page, fill the row, mark **verified / reported only /
  wrong as commonly told**, and link the row from every surface that relies on it.
- **First test.** Take his 20 platform claims of 2026 (D18–D25 here, C1–C5, C18 in `c`) and fill the ledger;
  measure how many rows a worker completes from primary sources without a person, and how many of his claims
  end up "wrong as told".
- **Earns / improves.** Every client report and every post rests on checked facts — the opposite of the
  market's news reels. It also stops our own workers from acting on a rumour (an opt-out flipped, a file
  added because "Google checks it").
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing.
- **Risk.** Staleness: each row carries a re-check date; a row past it is shown as "unchecked".

### 2. The page-change guard — no edit to a page that ranks without a risk read (SHARPER; D4, D5, D45)

- **What.** Before any change to a page that already earns clicks: what it ranks for and earns now, what the
  change touches (title, H1, sections, URL), the clicks at risk, a snapshot, the rollback, and the date it may
  be judged. A rewritten money page is then left alone until that date; feeder posts link to it meanwhile. A
  page is deleted only after its links and traffic are checked, and then redirected to its nearest
  equivalent.
- **Where.** B84 delivery — every edit skill 18, 15 or 16 proposes; and the holding's own sites.
- **Form.** A gate in front of the editing skills (row 62).
- **What we build.** Search Console read for the page (queries, clicks, positions, 16 months); referring
  domains from the link data; the diff of the proposed change; a stored copy of the page; the decision
  (go / smaller change / not now) with the re-read date (four weeks for a line, 8–12 weeks for a rewrite).
- **First test.** The holding's own property: ten proposed edits from skill 18 run through the guard; count
  how many it stops or shrinks, and at the re-read date compare the guarded edits with their baseline.
- **Earns / improves.** Retention. The worst month of an SEO client is the one where a well-meant edit lost the
  page that paid; his $40,000 client (D4) lost traffic for four months after the change and was angry ("they were pissed"). A
  measured, reversible change process is also what we can show a nervous client.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing.
- **Risk.** Paralysis — the guard says "not now" too often. Its stop must cite the clicks at risk; below a set
  threshold it waves the change through.

### 3. The AI line of the monthly report, on first-party data only (SHARPER; D18, D19, D20, D38)

- **What.** For every client, one block in the monthly report from the sources the platforms themselves
  publish: Search Console's generative-AI report (impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode by page, since 18 May
  2026 — a start date from trade press), GA4's AI Assistant channel (labelled a floor; it covers ChatGPT,
  Gemini, Copilot and the like and excludes AI Overviews and AI Mode, which stay in organic search), Bing's AI Performance (grounding queries and cited
  pages), Search Console's social-account properties, the generative-AI control checked "on". Beside it, our
  own sample of buyer questions (skill 3), **labelled as a sample we wrote**, never as "what users asked".
- **Where.** Skill 19 (`monthly-report`), skill 3; B84's care package.
- **Form.** Additions to two skills (REGISTER-CHANGES).
- **What we build.** The connectors (Search Console API for the generative-AI report where exposed, else the
  export; GA4 Data API; Bing API); the block; the one-line honesty note.
- **First test.** The holding's own property and one social account for a month; does the block tell a true
  story a non-expert understands in thirty seconds?
- **Earns / improves.** The only honest way to sell AEO/GEO (the README's reading) — now with Google's own
  numbers. And a point against competitors: their "AI visibility scores" are synthetic prompts (his own jab,
  D18), ours say which part is measured and which is sampled.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing for the holding's own sites; access per client is the client's.
- **Risk.** The report shows impressions only; it is never sold as "AI traffic".

### 4. The customer-eye review of a money page (SHARPER; D36)

- **What.** Four questions as a customer (can I reach them, do I trust them, is it short enough, do I know
  what they sell), the same four on the page that ranks first, the transactional target of every element
  (title, H1, headings, body), and a trust-facts block taken from the client's records (years, guarantee,
  insurance, jobs done, reviews).
- **Where.** Skill 15 (`money-page-audit-fix`); also the prospect brief (skill 5) — "your page against the page
  that wins, through a customer's eyes" is a first message a busy owner reads.
- **Form.** An addition to skill 15 (REGISTER-CHANGES).
- **First test.** Ten money pages of local businesses in one trade: does the read find something the owner
  agrees with, and does every trust fact proposed exist in the client's own material?
- **Earns / improves.** Conversion, which is what the owner pays for; it needs no ranking change to show.
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing for clients' and the holding's own pages; using it as a first message to a
  prospect is client-finding, which waits on his word (B84 decision 9 holds it at "not now").
- **Risk.** A model playing a customer invents feelings; the output is observations only.

### 5. Rival intelligence, read-only and open (NEW; D42, D43, D44, D45)

- **What.** For a client or for the holding: where the buyers spend time (SparkToro or an equivalent panel), what
  the rivals' e-mail sequences send (one tagged inbox per rival, subscribed openly), which of their ads have run
  longest (Meta Ad Library start dates), and what pages they add (a's Watchdog idea).
- **Where.** The ad office (B28) for channel choice and creative briefs; the sales desk (B83) and skill 44
  (`business-diagnosis`); B84's roadmap.
- **Form.** A skill (`rival-intel-watch`, row 64) and a library per client.
- **What we build.** The plus-tagged inbox and its log (day, subject, offer, call to action); the Ad Library
  reader (start date, days running, formats, the hook in words); the audience sheet; a weekly diff.
- **First test.** Three rivals of one of the holding's own brands for four weeks; does the log change a brief?
- **Earns / improves.** Better briefs for the ad office at almost no cost; a credible "here is what your three
  rivals send their customers" page for a prospect.
- **Needs the CEO.** A SparkToro plan if the free tier is gone (money out, small); showing the page to a
  prospect is client-finding and waits on his word (B84 decision 9).
- **Risk.** It stays read-only and truthful: no fake names, no fake purchases, no copying creative (D44).

### 6. The named method — a firm's real method made citable (NEW; D16, D17)

- **What.** When a client really does something distinctive (a diagnostic, a guarantee, a process), it gets a
  name, a page that defines it and shows it done, a short film, and — when it launches or produces a measured
  result — one announcement. Only that firm can then be cited for it (information gain), and the engines learn
  the pairing. The "King of AEO" case shows how strongly this works and why it must be true: the same machinery
  crowned a man on a $6 press release.
- **Where.** B84 (with skills 23 `data-story` and 24 `press-list-and-pitch`); the Media Studio for the film.
- **Form.** An addition to skill 24 with a truth gate (REGISTER-CHANGES).
- **First test.** The holding's own web office: name one method it really runs (for instance its page-change
  guard, idea 2) — page, film, one announcement when the first client result exists; three reads on different
  days of "who does [method]?" in the assistants before and after.
- **Earns / improves.** A reason to be chosen that a rival cannot copy by imitation alone.
- **Needs the CEO.** Paid wire distribution is money out; the name of anything the holding announces is his.
- **Risk.** It turns into a self-awarded title. Refused: titles, "first", "only", "best" not measured; a release
  with no news in it.

### 7. The community answer loop (SHARPER; D31)

- **What.** The business, under its own name, answers questions in the local subreddit, the city's Facebook
  groups and under the most-watched videos of its niche — rules of each group read first, no link unless asked —
  and every question it meets enters the question bank (skill 10).
- **Where.** B84 local care; B28 social; the holding's own brands.
- **Form.** A skill (`community-answer`, row 63).
- **First test.** One holding brand, one city, four weeks: questions found, answers posted, removals or warnings,
  questions turned into pages, enquiries that mention the group.
- **Earns / improves.** Presence where AI answers and neighbours both read, without ad spend.
- **Needs the CEO.** The accounts (an identity step) for the holding's own brands; whether an AI worker may
  write in the business's name, and how it says it is AI where the platform or the holding's rule requires.
- **Risk.** Hidden promotion — refused by design: the name is open, the answer stands without the link.

### 8. Free first-pass keyword research in any language (NEW; D9)

- **Where.** Skill 10 (`question-harvest`); the free tools; prospect briefs in markets the paid tools cover

- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing.
- **Risk.** It is an undocumented endpoint; at volume it goes through the provider. The relevance score is an

## The larger picture — Matt Diamante's whole method, with all four parts read

**His method, in one paragraph.** One loop for one small business: find the question a customer really
asks → let a model interview the owner for twenty minutes → publish one honest answer a week → link it from
three old pages and to the one money page → ask for one link a week from places that already know you →
keep the Business Profile complete and the reviews flowing two hours after each job → read Search Console
for fifteen minutes and fix the page that almost ranks → wait six months. He has taught it unchanged for at
least the ten months on record (and, by his own revisits, for three years). Around the loop sits the business
that sells it: a book and a $10 week to learn it, $97 a month to be held to it, $3,000 a month to have it
done; free tools that each perform one step for an e-mail; three posts a day of platform news and tool demos
to fill the top. **His edge is speed and generosity; his weakness is truth** — the hooks, the news he gets
wrong, the tricks he never withdraws, the reposted case that contradicts his own method.

**The holding's version.** The same loop, because it is right — but run by workers for every client every
week, in every language, with the four things he cannot afford: (1) the owner's twenty minutes made richer by
the client's own records (skills 12–14); (2) every change guarded and measured before and after (idea 2,
proof-capture); (3) every claim about a platform checked at its source before a worker acts on it or a post
says it (idea 1); (4) the client's presence measured where the machines read — profile, reviews, community,
AI reports — with the measured part and the sampled part kept apart (idea 3). Its shop window is his format
with the truth put back: complete walkthroughs on our own pages, a handbook written from measured cases, free
engines that show the result on the page. Where he sells "SEO in an hour a week", the holding sells "your hour
a week, and everything else done and proven".
