# Matt Diamante (HeyTony) — OPEN QUESTIONS, analyst B

## For the CEO to decide

1. **Who is the face and voice of the holding's short videos?** His whole method is a person talking
   ("people don't buy from businesses, they buy from people"). Options: the CEO himself; a named human
   presenter; an openly labelled AI presenter; or faceless formats (screen recording with voice,
   green-screen over a live audit). An unlabelled invented expert is outside the no-deception boundary.
   Recommendation: start faceless with live audits — it needs no identity and the finding carries the
   video — and decide the face after thirty videos of data.
2. **How long do we give the video channel before judging it?** His curve was six months at ~200 views,
   then two videos broke out. A horizon should be set before the first post, or the first flat month will
   end it.
3. **Do we publish our own prices and name competitors** (the Big 5 on the web office's own site)? It is
   the core of Sheridan's method and the fastest trust-builder; it is also a public commitment.
4. **Paid links.** He buys four placements a month (~$225 each, The Hoth) and suggests paying a site to
   switch a link. Recommendation: we do not; links come from listings, partners, original data and the
   answer-engine worklist. If he wants a paid route, placements must carry the sponsored mark.
5. **Money, when reached:** DataForSEO balance for the answer-engine audit (LLM Mentions is billed per
   request and row; two different rates appear on DataForSEO's own pages — check before volume); ManyChat
   above its free tier; Instagram/TikTok accounts (identity steps).

## What could not be established

1. **His templates and prompts themselves.** The 20 ChatGPT prompts, the 43-task local checklist, the
   page-keyword spreadsheet, the backlink-outreach spreadsheet, the audit spreadsheet and the Looker
   template sit behind description links; the transcripts name them but do not contain them. Analyst A
   may have them from his site. The Looker template (credited to Diffuse Digital Marketing) is the one
   worth opening: its exact fields and filters would save a day.
2. **Whether his thresholds carry to our tools.** "KD 0–30" and authority scores are Semrush's scale;
   his own test shows three tools disagreeing by 5× on volume. Needed: one calibration run — 200 keywords
   through DataForSEO difficulty against Semrush's public figures or against what actually ranks for
   young sites — to set our "easy" line. Also to confirm: the exact DataForSEO endpoint names in the
   mapping table (written from knowledge of v3, not from a run), the non-intersecting mode of
   `domain_intersection`, and whether the Backlinks API needs a separate minimum commitment.
3. **Keyword-count and word-count matching as a cause.** He practises it and his clients rank; Google
   says neither is a factor. We cannot tell from his material whether the counting helps or merely
   accompanies the real work (coverage, headings, links). A small controlled test on our own pages
   (coverage-only edits vs coverage plus count-matching) would settle what our skill should do.
4. **His signal order on Instagram** (follows > shares > saves > comments > likes) and targets (50%
   viewed, 10% interactions) are his 2024 reading of one account. Instagram's own statements name watch
   time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Only our own post-mortem table will tell.
5. **The TikTok "link in bio" ban** — one anecdote, cause uncertain by his own account.
6. **How assistants pick local businesses today.** His tests are mid-2025, three queries, one region. The
   2025–2026 studies found cover long-tail informational prompts, not local recommendations. Before the
   answer-engine audit is sold, it needs our own run: ~30 local queries in three cities, app answers
   against API answers, and how stable the cited sources are week to week.
7. **Google Business Profile Q&A and the CID link.** Q&A was being retired in late 2025; the CID-link
   benefit has no published test. Neither should be a deliverable until checked.
8. **Results figures.** "$552,000 of traffic", "$45,000 a month", "200 leads a month", "400% growth",
   "150 keywords at number one" are shown as screenshots in the videos; the transcripts carry only the
   claims. Sheridan's "$100M" is his own stated guess.

## What deserves a deeper study

1. **Sheridan's books, read whole** — *They Ask, You Answer* (the Selling 7, assignment selling, the
   content-manager role, the workshop) and *Endless Customers* (the four pillars, self-service tools, the
   scorecard). The interview gives the stories; the books give the operating manual for the interviewer
   worker and for the sales side (B83). The assignment-selling figure (30 pages read → ~80% close) should
   be read at source before it is repeated.
2. **IMPACT's coaching model** (impactplus.com learning centre): they write nothing and coach an in-house
   content person. That is the nearest published design to "AI workers plus one client expert" and shows
   what the client must be asked for.
3. **His video on writing for answer engines after this set** (analyst A's newer 31) — to see whether his
   llms.txt and "it's all just SEO" position moved once AI Mode reached Canada.
4. **The Ahrefs AI-overlap and llms.txt studies and Google's AI-optimisation guide of 2026-05-15** — read
   at source; they are the evidence base for what the answer-engine audit may and may not promise.
5. **Mosseri's own statements on ranking** (his account, the Creators channel) — to replace second-hand
   summaries in the script and post-mortem skills.
6. **OpenAI's merchant product feed and in-chat checkout** — for e-commerce clients this is the direct
   continuation of the "product discovery" form he found closed; it may be a service line of its own.
7. **ManyChat's current terms and Meta's automation limits** — comment-to-DM volume caps and the 24-hour

8. **Videos he refers to but that are not in this slice:** Step 1 (Search Console setup), "understanding
