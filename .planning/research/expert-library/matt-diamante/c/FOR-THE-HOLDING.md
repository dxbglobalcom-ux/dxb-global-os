# Matt Diamante — what the holding does with his carousels (analyst C)

References in brackets are items of `PLAYBOOK.md` in this folder (C1…C21, §10). Skill numbers are those of
`../../SKILLS.md`. Nothing here is built or tried; every figure of his is his claim; the counts of §10 are
measured from the list.

## The answer first

His 40 strongest carousels teach little that a and b do not already hold. What they add is of three kinds:

1. **The machine now acts for the customer, and the business must be reachable by it.** Google's AI phones
   businesses for prices and availability and e-mails the customer who answered, with what, and who did not
   (C1); ChatGPT and Bing now report when they cite a business (C2, C4); Search Console reads social accounts
   (C5). Each is a new place where a client is either present and measured, or absent.
2. **Several of his half-methods can now be completed at the source** — and three of his posts are
   wrong on the facts (C5 re-dated, C7 broken regex, C18 Discover misread). The holding's edge over him on
   content is not more posts; it is a truth gate he does not have.
3. **The carousel is measurable as a product, and the measurement corrects the library's yardstick.** A
   comment count rises with what is *withheld*; likes follow what is *given* (C20). His single best post
   gave the whole method on the slides and used the word only for a link. The library's reading — the asset
   public on our page, the word at most a second door — is supported; the library's use of comment counts as
   evidence of value is not.

## The ideas, ranked by value

### 1. "Reachable by the machine" — AI-call readiness for local clients (SHARPER, C1)

- **What.** Google's AI calls local businesses for a customer's price and availability, but only when the
  information is not online; the customer gets an e-mail ranking who answered with a price, who answered
  without one, and who "couldn't be reached". The complete method: publish prices, availability and stock
  where the machine reads them (site, Business Profile, Merchant Center); answer every call — with a price;
  never leave the "Google automated calls" setting switched off by accident.
- **Where.** B84 local care package (a line of it); B83 as a service of its own — **an answering worker for
  the client's phone line**, which is the same desk as skill 39 `first-touch`, pointed at voice.
- **Form.** A check (part of skill 8 `gbp-optimise`), a page pattern (price ranges and availability on every
  service page — already in skill 15's "range, tiers, drivers"), and a product (the answering worker).
- **What we build.** (a) The **readiness check**: is every service priced on the site and profile; is stock
  in Merchant Center where the client sells goods; is the automated-calls setting on; does the phone answer
  within N rings in opening hours (one test call a week from our line, recorded with the client's consent).
  (b) The **answering worker**: picks up when staff do not, sounds like a person,
  answers from the client's price sheet and calendar, never invents a price, books or takes a message,
  hands a summary to the owner. (c) A line in the monthly report: calls received, answered, price given.
- **First test.** One US local business of a willing owner (the feature is US-only today): before and after
  the price page is published and the phone is covered, run "Check for me" for its trade and town with "don't
  call" (Google's own option: online search only) and record what the machine finds without phoning; log
  Google's calls as they arrive on the client's own line. Three reads on different days, as the library
  already requires for any AI answer. No request is placed that makes Google phone real businesses for a job
  nobody will buy — that would be deception toward them.
- **Earns / improves.** A reason a local owner understands in one sentence ("Google is phoning you for
  customers; are you picking up?"), and a recurring service that is not SEO and that no SEO agency sells.
- **Needs the CEO.** A telephony provider and number per client (money out); which market first
  (recording and AI-disclosure rules differ by US state and country; researched when the work starts).
- **Risk.** An answering AI that quotes a wrong price commits the client; the worker reads only the
  client's signed price sheet. His "not ranking at the top but recommended first" is one e-mail — the test
  measures it before anyone repeats it.

### 2. The holding's carousel machine — format as a product, judged by what it gives (NEW, C20–C21, §10)

- **What we build.** The text-slide template in code; the screenshot pipeline (a real run of the tool or
  feature, captured and marked); the ledger row per post; the post-mortem with the right measures.
- **First test.** Twenty carousels over four weeks on one account in one language: ten class A (complete,
  no word), ten class B (complete, the word for the page link), same subjects split evenly. Measure saves,
  sends, link clicks to our page, page visits and tool runs — and record comments only to confirm C20 on our
  own account.
- **Earns / improves.** Reach without ad spend and without a face; the posts are by-products of work the
  office does anyway (the engines' findings, the gates' checklists, the platform features it verifies).
- **Needs the CEO.** The account (an identity step); the language and market of the first account.
- **Risk.** His claims. His covers say "destroyed", "killed", "BREAKING" on his own sale; ours are true
  or not posted.

### 3. Brand-DNA preview — the prospect's own brand system and ads, made before he is asked (SHARPER, C16)

- **What.** Pomelli shows the pattern: one URL in → a "Business DNA" (name, offer, tone, colours, type,

- **Where.** B84 and B28 client-finding (skill 38 `first-five-outreach` uses it as its built preview); the

- **Form.** A skill (`brand-dna-preview`), a private page per prospect.
- **What we build.** Site read → DNA sheet (with the source line for every element: "colour #… from the

  look generated) → each image checked by eye before it is shown.
- **First test.** Five businesses of one trade: our preview beside Pomelli's for the same URL, judged blind
  by a reader for "would this owner say: that's us".
- **Earns / improves.** A first message that is the prospect's own brand, finished — the strongest opener
  the library has found, and one Pomelli itself cannot give in most languages (English interface).
- **Needs the CEO.** Nothing to build and test. Sending previews to prospects is client-finding, which B84
  decision 9 holds at "not now" — it starts on his word; publishing one never happens without the business's
  consent.
- **Risk.** Using a business's logo and name in public would be impersonation; the preview is a private page

## The carousel format specification

Written for an AI worker and the Media Studio to follow as is. Measured basis in PLAYBOOK §10.

**Purpose.** One carousel = one thing a business owner can do or check today, complete on the slides. It
exists to be saved and sent, and to bring the reader to the matching public page or free tool on our domain.

**Inputs (one of):** a finding from one of the office's engines (anonymised unless the business agreed);
a platform feature the worker has **opened and run itself**; one gate or checklist of the office; a
measured before-and-after from the proof ledger.

**Slides (4:5 portrait, 1080 × 1350; 5–10 slides; one language per post, written natively):**

| Slide | Job | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Cover | the promise in ≤ 10 words, the object named ("Google now phones plumbers for prices. Here's the e-mail customers get") | true; specific; label from a fixed set — NEW (with the date of the source), HOW-TO, CHECK, MEASURED; never "killed", "destroyed", "illegal", never a news label on our own offer; a real screenshot or our own image, no stock "hacker" |
| 2 | what it is and the source date | one sentence; the primary source named |
| 3 … n−2 | one step or one fact per slide | ≤ 30 words; one phrase in the accent colour; screenshots from our own run with one arrow on the one thing to click; numbered steps |
| middle | an honest re-hook ("what it shows you next") | promises only what the next slides contain |
| n−1 | proof or limit | our measured result, or the primary-source quote; and what it does **not** do (country, plan, beta) |
| n Last | where the full version lives | the page address on our domain, stated on the slide; optionally "or comment WORD and we'll send the link" — the word never unlocks anything the page does not show |

**Caption.** The full steps in text (searchable; indexed in Google through the platform property), the source
link, the keyword in natural words, the disclosure where relevant (AI-made images marked as the platform
requires).

**Measures (per post, at 24 h and 7 days, into the ledger of skill 33):** saves, sends, profile visits, link
clicks, page visits, tool runs, conversations started. Comments are recorded and never used to judge a post
(C20).

**Cadence and reuse.** A winner may be reposted once after three weeks or more with a fresh check of every
line; his reposts reached 19–51 % of the original in four of five pairs (the fifth, with a new cover, 70 % of
the likes and 174 % of the comments) and two of five carried a new error (C21).

**No face needed.** His two strongest posts in both tails (`Da54tyIlfki`, `DTbo3RwDdNY`) and his strongest news
posts used a stock image or a logo on a building, not his face. The carousel line can start before the
CEO's decision on who appears on camera.

**Does the library's reading hold?** Yes, with one refinement. *The asset stands public on our own page; the
word is at most a second door* — supported: the best post of the 40 had the full method on the slides and the
word only for the link, and the posts that withheld the method collected comments, not likes. *Measuring
engines before any content machine* — holds for client-finding, because a post must lead somewhere ours. One
narrow refinement: a carousel whose asset is a **verified walkthrough of a platform feature** (C1, C4, C5)
needs no engine; such posts can run early as a reach test, each pointing to the same walkthrough on our page.
And one correction to the library's evidence: comment counts — including the Harry Sanders measurement the
README cites — show that a word in the comments works as a mechanism, not that the asset behind it was
valued.

## The larger picture — what these 40 add to the reading of him

a found the machine (free tools, a price ladder, three posts a day) and b the loop (easy keywords, interviews,
the cohort report). The carousels show **the surface of that machine and what it is fed with**: a stream of
other companies' features — Google's, OpenAI's, Microsoft's, Canva's — walked through screen by screen, with
his own tools inserted where the walkthrough needs a link. His most-liked work is not his method; it is his
speed at showing a new button. That is exactly the part AI workers do better — they can open the product
the day it ships, in every market and language, and check the claim before posting — and exactly the part
where he is weakest: three of his news and feature posts here carry a factual error (C5, C7, C18), besides
the covers that are untrue by design.

The other thing they show is where the business itself is going. Four of the most recent posts (August to
November 2026) are about machines acting for customers: phoning for prices, answering messages, registering
businesses, reporting citations. A local business is now judged by software that calls, reads and counts.
The holding's web office should therefore sell not only "be found" but **"be answerable"**: every price and
hour published where the machine reads, every call picked up, every AI surface claimed and measured. That
is idea 1 and idea 4 together, and it is the line in this study that is worth money soonest.
