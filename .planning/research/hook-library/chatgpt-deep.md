# Evidence-Only Library of Winning Advertising and Short-Form Openings

## Evidence standard and caveats

I used a deliberately narrow inclusion rule. An item appears below only where I located a published numeric result meeting your criteria: a controlled A/B or split test, a published sales/revenue result tied to the creative, or another documented performance result. I found much stronger evidence for landing-page/homepage/email openings than for TikTok/Reels/Meta-video hooks, because the latter are rarely published with both the account-median denominator and the exact first-frame/copy needed for criterion **(d)**. I therefore did **not** manufacture a 10×-median short-video section from view counts that lacked a denominator.

“**Hook-isolated**” means the headline, image, subject line, badge, CTA, or other opening element was itself the principal manipulated variable. “**Opening bundle**” means several first-screen elements changed together. “**Whole creative/page**” means the ad/page won, but the evidence does **not** establish that its opening caused the lift. A statistically weak but numerically positive controlled result is retained where the source reports the number, but is described as such. “NR” means the source did not report the requested datum. “Opened” means I opened the result page itself; “snippet” means I only obtained the indexed/search-result record. LiftArchive is a secondary database that attributes results to the practitioner that ran/published each test; its current corpus describes itself as containing roughly 1,800 real tests from more than 400 experts/teams. citeturn583289view0turn583289view1

The most important qualification is that **a winning creative is not the same thing as a causally proven winning hook**. For example, DiamondBack's new hero increased conversion while changing both imagery and copy; that is valid evidence for the opening *bundle*, but not for the headline in isolation. By contrast, the dental-plan headline test, the VR-gunstock headline, the “Best Seller” badge, several email subject lines, and the shoe-repair copy test isolate the opening much more cleanly. citeturn104451view0turn799135view1turn799135view5

The literature produces one important methodological lesson: **do not rank hook principles by rhetorical plausibility; rank them by the metric and degree of isolation**. ## Limitations and reliability notes

The library reaches the requested **100 qualifying pieces**, but they are **not 100 equally strong hook proofs**. Roughly the first third contains the cleanest opening-level evidence; a substantial share of later rows are controlled **opening bundles or whole landing-page winners** whose exact first-frame text was not published. I retained them because your rule explicitly asked to distinguish evidence for the creative from causal evidence for the hook, rather than silently treat the two as equivalent.

Several numbers in secondary A/B archives are practitioner-reported rather than independently audited. Where the source disclosed sample size, duration or confidence, I included it; where it did not, I wrote NR. LiftArchive explicitly separates the practitioner’s result from its own commentary, and some indexed experiments have weak confidence intervals or small samples; those are evidence that one treatment numerically beat another, **not proof that the reported lift will replicate**. citeturn583289view0turn799135view1

I also found an important evidence imbalance: public CRO archives make landing-page, ecommerce hero and email-subject tests much easier to audit than Meta/TikTok/YouTube short-video hooks. I did **not** locate enough cases satisfying your very strict criterion (d)—a video at ≥10× the same account’s median **with the denominator documented**—to build a defensible 100-row social-video library. That absence in this report should be read as **“insufficient auditable evidence located,” not “such examples do not exist.”**

The practical implication is that the highest-confidence library should be tagged internally by evidence tier: **Tier A: isolated hook A/B; Tier B: controlled first-screen bundle; Tier C: whole creative/page with published sales/conversion; Tier D: long-run/spend or 10×-median video proof**. For creative ideation, all four can be useful; for claims such as “this hook caused the lift,” only Tier A should be treated as causal.
