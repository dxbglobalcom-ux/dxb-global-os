# THE OPENING CARD — what won, for the writer of openings

*48 rules, 47 drawn from the first 495 rows of `rules.jsonl` and one the library measured itself, without a failure in them (the CEO's order of 2026-11-25). What failed, contradicts or is unproven stands apart, for engineers: `../hook-evidence/rules-for-engineers.md`. Every number was laid against its row by script (327 proof numbers, 0 faults); the lead laid 14 proofs drawn by lot beside their rows: 14 of 14 as printed. The ready openings are examples of the shape, written by us, not proofs.*

**First line of every brief:** write the one win you want. **Seen** = people stop and watch. **Click** = they press, call, answer or leave their name. **Sell** = they buy or give. **Remembered** = they know the name later. Every rule says which win it brought; use it for that win.
**Places decide.** Read "Holds" first. A rule is proven where it says, and is a good first try elsewhere.
**Truth.** Every [bracket] is filled with a true fact: price, count, date, place, name. A saving is measured against a price really asked; a kept place is really kept; advertising is labelled as advertising.
**Known seller** = the reader already trusts the name. **Unknown seller** = the reader meets the name for the first time. Where a row does not tell them apart the line says "any seller".
**Side-by-side test** = chance decides who sees which version. **Lead** = a person who left contact details. **Point** = one person in every hundred. **Odds** = answers against silences; above one means more answers than the letter it is set against.
**Grades.** [A] side-by-side tests of at least two different owners agree. [B] one large test, or a count across many ads, or several tests whose size is not printed. [C] agreeing results of trusted practitioners. [one case] = one test, or one report of one house: told to a client as that one case, never as a rule; tried first, believed after our own test.
**(seller)** = the number comes from someone who sells the space or the tool.

# PART ONE — RULES THAT HOLD IN MOST TRADES

## What to say

### R1. Find the strongest promise first, then put it in the opening of every ad. [B]
- **Holds:** household goods, books, letters and print; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Replies to full-page magazine ads of one client: 502, 511, 550, 666 and 867 with the strongest promise in the headline, against 191 to 263 with it further down. (r-caples-008)
  - Orders per thousand letters: 13.9 against 9.8, the same package with only the headline changed. (r-schwartz-016)
  - Phone calls about a home appliance: two and a half times as many for the comfort the buyer wants ("cool, quiet bedroom—even on hot nights") as for the machine's own feature. (r-caples-032)
  - Replies by mail: the best of eight promises brought almost three times the replies of the eighth. (r-sugarman-052)
- **How:** list eight one-line promises, show them to real buyers, then write many openings round the one they choose.
- **Ready (air-conditioner fitter):** "Sleep in a cool, quiet bedroom, even on the hottest night in [town]."

### R2. Say plainly what people get, in the first line. [A]
- **Holds:** guides, books, letters to businesses, social-media ads; unknown and known seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - E-mail sign-ups from a social-media ad: "Get the Guide" +44.7% against the same ad with a health promise as its headline. (r-abarsiv-001)
  - E-mail sign-ups from another ad of the same kind: "Get this free resource" +45.6% against the same offer under a question. (r-abarsiv-001)
  - Replies to a letter to 5,000 hospitals: 17 percent for the letter that came to the point at once, against 3 percent for the same talking points told later. (r-klasik-010)
  - Copies sold a year by mail: fourteen books went from near zero to 7,000-30,000 under a title that says what is inside, against the same books under their old titles. (r-klasik-001)
- **How:** "[what it does for you] – get [the thing]".
- **Ready (garden shop):** "A lawn that stays green all summer – get the free [town] watering guide."

### R3. Show at once how to answer, and make it the easiest way there is. [B]
- **Holds:** travel, food, schools, guides; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Replies to four travel ads: tripled when the coupon moved to the top, against the same ads with the coupon elsewhere. (r-sugarman-057)
  - Cost of one sample reply: 18 to 22 cents when the coupon was handed in at a local shop, against 70 cents by mail, same ads. (r-hopkins-035)
  - Pupils won by a school: one out of two people who telephoned, against one out of five who wrote. (r-caples-020)
- **How:** phone, button or shop address in the first screen, beside the promise.
- **Ready (driving school):** "Your first lesson this week – call [number] now, we answer until [hour]."

### R4. When something true is new, running low or ending, put it in the first words. [B]
- **Holds:** online shops, social-media ads in the sale season, food on TV, mail-order catalogues; any seller; seen, sell and remembered.
- **Proofs:**
  - Ads that became winners on Facebook and Instagram (winner = money spent on it): 11.37% of openings that bring news (83 winners) and 9.73% of openings with a real last day (4,855 winners). (r-uzman-011) The same count over all ads: about five percent. (r-uzman-010) (seller)
  - Takings per shop visitor: +2.9% with a true stock line and +1.5% with a countdown, against the same page without it; 6,700 experiments. (r-davranis-057)
  - Demand for one catalogue item: up 17% with a sale sign on it, against the same item without a sign, five items on the page. (r-davranis-053)
  - Effect of food TV ads in 512 studies: +17 percent for ads that say "new", against food ads without news. (r-sugarman-059)
- **How:** write the real news, the real stock or the real last day first, one such sign per screen.
- **Ready (bakery):** "New this morning: [bread]. [True count] loaves, then it is gone until [day]."

### R5. At the first meeting offer one thing and ask for one action. [B]
- **Holds:** print ads to strangers, phone orders, official letters; unknown seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Watches sold by a newspaper ad: 3 to 1 for the ad with one model, against the ad with nine. (r-sugarman-019)
  - Response to letters, forms and texts in dozens of public trials: about a 5% increase when the request is made easy, with one action in the first sentence, against the harder text. (r-davranis-008)
  - Extra sales on the phone: over 50 percent of buyers added a second item after a simple first order. (r-sugarman-015)
- **How:** choose the one thing most buyers start with; show the rest after the first order.
- **Ready (car wash):** "One wash, inside and out, [price], while you drink your coffee."

### R6. Put the proof inside the promise, in the same line. [B]
- **Holds:** restaurants, online shops, free samples by mail, business services; unknown seller above all; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - A free aspirin sample: the headline that joins promise and proof won by 71% against the headline with the promise alone. (r-schwartz-009)
  - Sales of dishes marked as most ordered, with the real count: +13-18% against the same dishes without the mark; 13 restaurants; strongest with new guests. (r-davranis-023)
  - Sales of a planner: +26.8% with "172,783+" buyers in the headline, against the page without it. (r-abarsiv-010)
  - Takings per shop visitor: +2.3% with a true line about what others buy, against the same page without it; 6,700 experiments. (r-davranis-057)
- **How:** ask which word in the line proves it – a count, a measured part, a name.
- **Ready (restaurant):** "Our most ordered plate last month: [dish], [true count] times."

### R7. Say the price or the saving as money in the first words of an offer. [B]
- **Holds:** shop e-mails, used cars, offer ads in the sale season; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Takings per thousand e-mails of a shop: $24.99 with the offer first in the subject, against $21.84 (+14.4%). (r-uzman-035)
  - Takings from one e-mail: 170% more for "$50 off" than for "15% off", with equal opens and clicks. (r-abarsiv-002)
  - Customers answering a car dealer: three times as many when the ad names the actual car, year and price, against general praise. (r-caples-009)
  - Ads that became winners on Facebook and Instagram: 10.89% of openings that set a price beside a higher one (71 winners). (r-uzman-011) All ads in the same count: about five percent. (r-uzman-010) (seller)
- **How:** first words of the subject or the shelf card = the sum; films and stories name it later.
- **Ready (phone repair):** "[Price] – new screen for your [model], done in [minutes] minutes."

### R8. Use the true, exact number, written as a digit. [A]
- **Holds:** reminders to customers, official letters, online shops, news headlines; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Missed appointments: 8.2% with the exact cost ("£160 approx"), against 9.9% with the same thought in general words; 9,848 appointments. (r-davranis-018)
  - People who paid after an official letter: +3.4 points with "88% of people agree", against +1.7 points with the same share told as a round fraction. (r-davranis-019)
  - Clicks on news headlines: +3.6% at one newsroom (7,371 tests) and +5.2% at another (22,664 tests) with a digit, against headlines without one. (r-bilimsoz-023)
  - Clicks on a business offer: +104% with "Over 120,000 Doctors", against the earlier version (other things changed too). (r-abarsiv-010)
- **How:** replace the adjective with the one true figure the reader did not know.
- **Ready (dentist reminder):** "Your chair is kept [day], [hour]. An empty chair costs our practice [true sum]."

### R9. Tell the true, harmless flaw yourself, then show the repair. [C]
- **Holds:** goods whose flaw is visible and already answered; known and unknown seller; sell. In each case other things changed with the opening, so the whole ad won.
- **Proofs:**
  - Cars sold in one country: 120,000 (1959) to 430,000 (1967) in a shrinking import market, under ads that named the car's weakness. (r-klasik-014)
  - Sales of the same pizza shops: +14.3% in the first quarter after the film that shows customers' complaints and the new recipe, same shops, on the year. (r-satan-014)
  - Ads that became winners on Facebook and Instagram: 8.74% of openings that confess something (675 winners). (r-platform-002) They drew 1.41 times their share of the money spent. (r-uzman-012) All ads in the same count: about five percent winners. (r-uzman-010) (seller)
  - A thermostat ad opening on its ugly case and silly name ran more than three years, paid by its owner. (r-sugarman-007)
- **How:** take the truest harmless complaint from your reviews as line one; line two is the repair.
- **Ready (shoe repair):** "Our shop is hard to find. Your shoes are ready in [time], so you only look for us once."

## To whom

### R10. Call the people who already want it or already have the trouble. [C]
- **Holds:** devices, bedding, films shown to chosen groups; unknown seller; seen, click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Cost of one inquiry: 34 cents from over a dollar, with a headline that names the group and what it wants. (r-klasik-012)
  - Cost of one customer for sheet locks: cut to a third in under three months with the line about the beds whose sheets slip most, against the earlier ads (a new page ran with it). (r-uzman-041)
  - A film watched by 38% more people with a first frame made for each of four groups, against one frame for all. (r-platform-035)
- **How:** ask which customer has it worst and write the first line to that person only.
- **Ready (mattress cleaner):** "If you wake with a blocked nose in your own bed, read this."

### R11. Name the reader's own place, group or thing in the first words. [A]
- **Holds:** local services, gyms, sample ads, appeals, post; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Gifts on a free online service: a reader 11% more likely to give when the opening line names his country, against the line without it; 10 side-by-side tests. (r-davranis-044)
  - Sample requests from a newspaper ad: +20% with "Car owners!" in front, against the same ad without it. (r-caples-023)
  - Clicks to the sign-up page of a gym chain: +68% with "Find gym" added to the button. (r-abarsiv-016)
  - Replies to questionnaires by post: odds 1.15 for a personal letter against a general one; 75 trials, 98,285 people. (r-davranis-060)
- **How:** town, trade or the thing they own in the first five words; one personal thing.
- **Ready (window cleaner):** "[Street name] neighbours: your windows, inside and out, this [day]."

## The first words

### R12. On a page or in an ad, make the reader's own result the subject of the first line. [B]
- **Holds:** software, appliances, house plans, courses; pages and print; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Sign-ups on a software page: +90% for "Let Your Events Manage Themselves", against a page about the company (other things changed too). (r-abarsiv-011)
  - Ten tested pairs of mail-order ads: in 10 of 10 the winner spoke of the reader's own gain in plain words, by a wide margin. (r-caples-029)
  - Replies for house plans: almost 20% better for the "suit yourself" headline, against a headline on mistakes. (r-sugarman-068)
- **How:** strike the company from line one; start with what the buyer will have.
- **Ready (bookkeeper):** "Close your shop's books in one evening a month."

### R13. In a story or a news line, follow one person who does something. [B]
- **Holds:** headlines over a story – all four proofs come from one American news site; any seller; click.
- **Proofs:**
  - He, she, his or her in the headline: 121 of 365 winners, against 74 of their best rivals; in 71 tests only the winner had it, in 24 only the rival. (r-upw1-002)
  - Of 100 pairs where only one headline says "I", that one wins about 56; with "she" or "he" about 55; 24,333 pairs. (r-bilimsoz-021)
  - A witness speaking for himself ("I", "my"): 57 winners, against 34 best rivals. (r-upw2-001)
  - Chance of being the better headline: +14 points with one woman's own journey, +13 with an unexpected action; 14,128 tests. (r-bilimsoz-024)
- **How:** "a carpenter…", "she…", "I…", then the act, then the one thing that happened.
- **Ready (carpenter):** "A carpenter in [town] took one board out of [customer]'s stair, and she has slept through every night since." (a true story only)

### R14. Say it in the customer's own short, plain words. [A]
- **Holds:** every trade tested – remedies, repair, software, schools; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Purchases: +89.97% with the plain word "supplement" added, against the page without it. Leads: +26% for "Annual Reports", against the trade's word. (r-abarsiv-024)
  - Orders: +20% for "Fix", against the dictionary word for it. (r-caples-023)
  - Replies: "feel rotten" twice as many as the polite phrase. (r-sugarman-067)
  - School letters at fifth-grade level, short and friendly: about 40% more effective at cutting absences, against the official letter; 130,000 students. (r-davranis-027)
- **How:** keep the length; swap each trade word for the one the customer says at home.
- **Ready (plumber):** "Tap dripping all night? We fix it today."

### R15. Let a real, named person say it in his own spoken words. [A]
- **Holds:** appeals, medicines in shops, make-up, small makers; known person or true customer; sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Gifts: 126 for a letter in the person's word-for-word speech, against 96 for the polished version. (r-abarsiv-003)
  - Givers: 0.24% for letters about one named person, against 0.09% for letters with a figure; 184,396 letters. (r-davranis-059)
  - Shop sales, three regions compared: +9.5% with a creator's film, against +8.0% with the brand's own film. (r-platform-031)
  - One ad opening with a real customer: $355,092 in sales and 4,300 purchases of make-up for women over 50. (r-uzman-042)
- **How:** record the owner or a customer; open with a sentence exactly as spoken.
- **Ready (jam maker):** "My name is [name], and I still stir every pot of this jam myself."

### R16. Name the exact trouble the reader already feels, then the way out. [B]
- **Holds:** remedies, home safety, letters to firms, news headlines; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Replies: 3 times as many for "Don't let athlete's foot lay you up", against a mild general line. (r-sugarman-069)
  - Clicks on headlines: +2.3% for each word that names something bad, against the same length without it; 53,699 headlines. (r-bilimsoz-025)
  - Payments by late firms: +15% after a letter with the tip that prevents the mistake they just made, 6 points more than the next best letter. (r-davranis-012)
- **How:** the ache in its own name, then the relief in the same breath.
- **Ready (shoe shop):** "Heels that rub by noon? Try on one pair that does not."

### R17. State it as a fact. [A]
- **Holds:** headlines of news, articles and posts; letters to businesses; any seller; click.
- **Proofs:**
  - Clicks: the statement won over 465 tests on fresh data; a question's odds of the click were 0.8645 of the statement's. (r-bilimsoz-020)
  - Statements ahead in four studies: 22,743 headline tests, 53,030 forum posts, 3,078,791 academic titles and one laboratory test (400 people). (r-bilimsoz-019)
  - Replies to one letter with five openings, 1,000 makers each: 4.3% for asking straight out in the first sentence, against 2.3% for opening with a question. (r-klasik-009)
- **How:** answer the question inside the headline.
- **Ready (accountant):** "[Cost] lowers your tax this year. Here is the form, filled in as an example."

### R18. Give one concrete thing and keep one detail back. [B]
- **Holds:** headlines over a story or a free next step; any seller; click.
- **Proofs:**
  - Clicks: the most complete headlines gained about 10% (1.17 vs 1.06) when one detail was kept back, and the vaguest gained about 5.5% (0.91 vs 0.86) from one concrete detail; 8,977 tests. (r-bilimsoz-018)
  - Clicks on the same sentence: 1.41% with an odd detail (three sticks, a nail and a bucket), against 0.71% without it. (r-upw2-011)
  - Ten very successful mail-order headlines: all ten offered something wanted, four added curiosity on top. (r-caples-004)
  - Wish to read on a free article (one to seven): 4.27 with the sentence left open, against 3.30 closed. (r-bilimsoz-030)
- **How:** could the line describe any product? add a thing. Is everything told? keep one detail. Before a price, close the sentence.
- **Ready (cheese maker):** "Salt, milk, a wooden board – and the one thing my grandmother added on the third day."

## The first picture

### R19. Show the thing doing its job, large, so one glance says what you sell. [B]
- **Holds:** furniture, fashion, drinks, food, household and shop goods; any seller; seen and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - After a tenth of a second 99% name the product from the plain picture, against 27% from a riddle picture; three laboratory tests. (r-bilimvideo-040)
  - The thing living in its room or moment, against the same thing alone on white: IKEA Canada 50% better cost per action (action = the wanted step), Ashley +33% and FULLBEAUTY +45% sales per advertising dollar, Dandy Blend 2.2x orders per visit. (r-platform-030) (seller)
  - Watching time of shop clips: +0.568 seconds with the thing in use, against clips without it; 23,001 clips. (r-bilimvideo-047)
- **How:** hands using the thing the way a buyer would judge it; one subject.
- **Ready (knife sharpener, picture):** hands draw the knife once through a ripe tomato; on it: "Sharpened this morning in [town]."

### R20. Where a person speaks, show that very person's face, close. [A]
- **Holds:** testimonials, personal letters, business video, watching ads; any seller; seen, click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Sign-ups: 6.4% with the photo of the real colleague beside his words, against 2.9% without it; about 1,500 people of one organisation. (r-davranis-001)
  - Gifts: up to 30% more when the writer's face stands beside a personal appeal. (r-davranis-046)
  - People still watching after two seconds: +14% with a person talking to the lens, against the average of 1,678 ads. (r-platform-028) (seller)
- **How:** one true face, the speaker's own, eyes to the reader.
- **Ready (physiotherapist):** [photo of the patient] "I climbed my own stairs again in [week]. – [name], [street]"

### R21. Where you list facts, let the facts or the thing stand alone. [B]
- **Holds:** a service people already use, and catalogues of books; known seller; sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Gifts: 1.6-2.3 times more for the facts alone than for the same facts beside a face. (r-davranis-046)
  - Gifts in four regions: 375 for the facts, against 108 for a personal appeal (Canada); 474 against 145 (Britain). (r-davranis-051)
  - Copies a year: 42,000 under the title of the idea, against 15,000 under the name of its inventor. (r-klasik-003)
- **How:** tool, repair, figures – show the object and the result.
- **Ready (locksmith):** "Three bolts, steel frame, fitted in one visit. [Price]."

### R22. Make the striking moment of the film the product itself. [B]
- **Holds:** cheap goods bought on impulse or for pleasure, in short videos and TV spots; any seller; sell and remembered.
- **Proofs:**
  - Shop sales of 259 products after creators' videos: the one thing in the picture that went with more sales was how far the eye-catching part WAS the product (p<0.001). (r-bilimvideo-001)
  - Wish to buy rose straight with the smiles that came after the brand was seen (0.698, statistically sure); 178 people, 82 TV ads. (r-bilimvideo-012)
  - Memory of the brand: 189 with a well-known creator whose world fits the product, against 100 for an unknown one outside it; 239 ads, worked out by a model. (r-platform-013)
- **How:** cut the product out of the shot; when the shot falls flat, it is right.
- **Ready (popcorn stall, picture):** the lid lifts by itself and the corn climbs over the rim; a hand takes the first piece: "[Stall], [corner], warm now."

### R23. Make it look like the place it stands in: plain, home-made, in the place's own shape. [B]
- **Holds:** Facebook, Instagram, video sites, mail order; unknown seller above all; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Cost of one reply for a mail article: 42 cents with the plain small ad, against 85 cents before; about 250,000 replies a year. (r-hopkins-015)
  - Ads that became winners on Facebook and Instagram: 11.60% of ads that are only text (1,728 winners) and 8.75% of product pictures with words. (r-uzman-013) All ads in the same count: about five percent. (r-uzman-010) (seller)
  - One native upright film added to the usual ads: +13% sales per advertising dollar and +29% buyers per visit, against the usual ads only; 10 tests. (r-platform-024) (seller)
  - A bad-breath test filmed at home sold over 1m units in two years. (r-satan-011)
- **How:** one cut per platform in that platform's own look, plus one ad that is only a sentence.
- **Ready (tailor, text only):** "Trousers too long? Bring them before [hour], wear them tonight."

### R24. Let the words on the picture be few, and let them add what picture and voice leave open. [B]
- **Holds:** Snap (few words), YouTube (picture shows what the title says), TikTok Shop; any seller; seen and click.
- **Proofs:**
  - Views on Snap: +85% for consumer goods with under 5% of the frame in words, among 3,846 ads. (r-platform-038) (seller)
  - Views on YouTube: more where the picture shows what the title says and its words say something else; 10,098 videos, and one test with 996 people for the picture half. (r-bilimvideo-023)
  - A seller with $5M of shop sales writes every film so; the three films taken apart had well over 10 million views each. (r-uzman-033)
- **How:** picture = the thing; written line = why it matters; spoken line = what comes next.
- **Ready (florist, film):** picture: tired roses lift in a vase. Written: "Four more days." Spoken: "I do one thing to the stem." (write the true number of days)

### R48. On your own account, open on something that is already happening. [B]
- **Holds:** short films a maker or a small shop posts on its own account; any seller; seen.
- **Proofs:**
  - Films that went round, each beside an ordinary film of the same account, 341 pairs of July to November 2026, every film labelled blind: an action already under way in the first second in 61 of 100, against 51; a face that talks to the lens in 23 of 100, against 30. (r-ciftler-001)
  - The same films: the thing itself fills the first frame in 46 of 100, against 40; one uncommon thing in 23, against 17. (r-ciftler-002)
  - Alike in both: a question left open (84 and 82 of 100), a number or price (30 and 30), a story begun (31 and 29), pace, loudness, length. (r-ciftler-003)
- **How:** begin the film one second later: the hand already cuts, the pour already runs; the face comes after; change the first picture before you change the words.
- **Ready (baker, film):** the loaf is already being torn open, steam over the hands; on it: "Out at [6:10], gone by [8]."

## Length, pace and after the first line

### R25. Ask a favour or a simple thing in few words. [A]
- **Holds:** e-mails and letters to busy people, survey forms, goods that explain themselves; any seller; click.
- **Proofs:**
  - Answers by e-mail: 4.8% for the short request, against 2.7% for the long one; 7,002 people. (r-davranis-026)
  - Answers to questionnaires by e-mail: odds 3.26 for a brief letter, against a full one. (r-davranis-061)
  - Answers to questionnaires by post: odds 1.58 for a shorter form, against a longer one. (r-davranis-060)
- **How:** first sentence = the ask; the rest fits on a phone screen.
- **Ready (new café to an office manager):** "May I bring your team free coffee on [day]? One word back is enough."

### R26. When the reader must understand a gain, a price or a doubt, tell the whole story under a short first line. [A]
- **Holds:** social-media ads for guides, appeals to people who use the thing often, new or dear goods; known and unknown seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Sign-ups from an ad: +316% with the long text on what it gives, against the short text; 710,592 people. (r-abarsiv-025)
  - Gifts: 699 with all the facts, against 483 with the facts cut and prettier. (r-davranis-050)
  - Cost of a lead for a free book: the medium to long texts brought it cheapest of 7 texts. (r-abarsiv-021)
- **How:** easy first sentence, then every fact a doubtful buyer asks for.
- **Ready (solar fitter):** "What a roof like yours in [town] really brings in a year. Here is every figure, line by line."

### R27. Repeat the promise in the same words at the next step, and pay it off at once. [B]
- **Holds:** service pages (tested in German), video ads, the page after a sign-up; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Button pressed: 58% when it repeats the headline word for word, against 44% for a button with a new argument; 956 visitors of a cleaning service. (r-bilimsoz-010)
  - Wish to buy: 2x when the ad gives more than its hook promised; 150 ads, 7,163 people. (r-platform-029) (seller)
  - Gifts after a sign-up: +75.8% on the page that first confirms what was promised and then asks; 4,612 people. (r-abarsiv-023)
- **How:** write the pay-off first; ad line = page headline = button.
- **Ready (cleaning service):** headline "A clean flat by [day]"; button "Get a clean flat by [day]".

### R28. Keep the picture and the voice changing. [B]
- **Holds:** films that explain a product to people who chose to watch; unknown seller; seen and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Money raised by product films: +12.2% for one step more picture changes per second; 5,297 campaigns, counted as they ran. (r-bilimvideo-032)
  - Price people would pay: $47.98 with five voices carrying the message slowly, against $44.18 with one; side-by-side test. (r-bilimvideo-026)
  - Eyes gather on one spot about 0.66 seconds after a cut: put the thing in the middle there; 30 viewers. (r-bilimvideo-019)
- **How:** new shot before the eye rests; a second voice takes over the same message.
- **Ready (toy maker, film):** owner: "It has one screw." Child: "And I can open it." Three quick shots of hands.

### R29. Put the name on the thing or inside the scene, early and in short flashes. [B]
- **Holds:** YouTube, TikTok, TV; unknown and known seller; remembered.
- **Proofs:**
  - People who remember the brand after skipping: nearly +300% with the logo on the product from frame one, against the film without it, and as many watched; 1,200 people. (r-platform-032)
  - People who recognise the brand after two seconds: 53.1% when it is said and shown, against 7.5% without a sign; 93,000 people. (r-platform-007) (seller)
  - Knowing the brand: +182% with the logo in the scene and +191% with the brand's sound in the first 2 seconds, against ads without them; 847 ads. (r-platform-019) (seller)
  - Fewer people left the ad: 67% with many short flashes of the brand, against 74% with few; 130 people, real ads re-cut. (r-bilimvideo-037)
- **How:** bottle, shirt, van, spoken name; two to four signs.
- **Ready (courier, film):** the van with the name turns into the street; a voice: "[Name] is at your door at [hour]."

### R30. Hand a free thing or a kept place over as already theirs. [B]
- **Holds:** free courses, invitations to people you already helped; known seller; click.
- **Proofs:**
  - Clicks for a free course: +25.7% (1,635 people) and +31.5% (1,235 people) with "Activate", against a general word for joining. (r-abarsiv-008)
  - People who came: 26.8% after a text that says what was already done for them, with a name and good luck, against 10.5% after plain details; 1,224 people. (r-davranis-002)
- **How:** "[Thing] is kept for you" when it really is.
- **Ready (hairdresser):** "[Name], I have kept [hour] on [day] for you. See you – [hairdresser's name]."

## Choosing and keeping

### R31. Write many openings on one body, and choose between two or three that are truly different. [B]
- **Holds:** remedies, mattresses, food, video ads; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Replies: of 36 headlines on one text, one brought 300 percent more than the standing ad and another 200 percent more. (r-caples-034)
  - People stopped by a film: 60.32%, built on the claim that won as a cheap still picture, against 16.86% for the client's own films. (r-uzman-003)
  - Cost of selling one food: 75 percent lower after over fifty plans in five years, against the first plans. (r-hopkins-039)
  - Cost of one customer: 30% lower for the better of two clearly different films, in the tests that had a winner. (r-bilimvideo-046) (seller)
- **How:** one body, many first lines, the same small budget and the same week.
- **Ready (any shop):** write one line from R2, one from R15 and one from R16 and run all three.

### R32. Keep running what won, and freshen the face for people who see you daily. [B]
- **Holds:** courses, building materials, groceries, long campaigns; known seller; seen, click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - One course headline ran seven years on its coupon returns. (r-caples-012)
  - A business ad had top scores on the eleventh run as on the first, and coupons held through the eighth. (r-sugarman-064)
  - Takings for each unit of advertising money: 15.2:1 for a grocer who kept one character for years, against a median of 4.3:1. (r-satan-006)
  - Long campaigns with one character opening every film: 40.9% had very large growth of their share, against 29.7% without one. (r-satan-005)
- **How:** keep the promise or the character; for daily messages change the line in turn.
- **Ready (pizza shop, each week):** the same baker, the same table, a new plate: "[Name] again. Today: [plate]."

# PART TWO — RULES THAT HOLD IN ONE PLACE

### R33. E-mail subject: name the thing exactly and add "for you" or "please". [B]
- **Holds:** e-mail to a list that knows the sender; click.
- **Proofs:**
  - Clicks: +41.9% for "A free copywriting training class for you" and +51.3% for "Download our latest eBook", against vaguer subjects. (r-abarsiv-013)
  - Clicks: +1.0 point with "Please" in the subject of a workplace e-mail, against the same e-mail in command form. (r-davranis-037)
- **How:** subject = the thing + "for you", in a calm voice.
- **Ready (language school):** "A free lesson plan for you: ordering food in [language]"

### R34. Reminder or official letter: send it a second time, days later. [A]
- **Holds:** text reminders and letters from a sender people know; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Of 22 reminder texts to 689,693 people the one sent on two days did best; all 22 together +2.0 points against no text. (r-davranis-014)
  - +0.73 points from sending the letter twice, and +0.89 from telling the new benefit, against the usual letter; 964,870 people. (r-davranis-049)
- **How:** the same plain message again after a few days.
- **Ready (optician):** "Your eye test is still open for you this week: [link]." Again [days] later, same words.

### R35. Post: announce it, write by hand, put a pen and a stamped envelope in. [A]
- **Holds:** paper mail that asks for an answer (tested on questionnaires, not on sales letters); any seller; click.
- **Proofs:**
  - Odds of an answer against the same letter without it: pen 1.44, notice in advance 1.36, hand signature 1.24, stamped return envelope 1.23. (r-davranis-060)
  - Answers: 69% with a handwritten note on the packet, against 34% without a note. (r-davranis-017)
- **How:** the owner signs each letter in ink.
- **Ready (builder, handwritten note):** "[Name], please look at page one. – [builder's name]"

### R36. Landing page: one benefit line, one real customer's words, one picture that stays still. [one case]
- **Holds:** a page with one sign-up button; known seller; click.
- **Proofs:**
  - Sign-ups: the best page had the benefit, one testimonial and no moving pictures; taking the moving pictures off added 3,000 sign-ups; 345,469 visitors. (r-davranis-003)
- **How:** one line, one voice, one button.
- **Ready (swimming teacher):** "Your child swims by [month]." – "[true sentence]", [parent's name]. Button: "Keep a place".

### R37. Thin product page: list what it is, what it does and when you use it. [one case]
- **Holds:** online shops, pages with under fifty words; any seller; sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Sales: +6.5% for products with no or under 50 words of text after the clear list was added, against the same kind of products without it; 4.77 million shoppers in the test. (r-bilimsoz-001)
- **How:** three short lines on every empty page before any clever line.
- **Ready (soap shop):** "Olive-oil soap, [weight]. Cleans without drying. For hands that wash all day."

### R38. Search, shopping ads and catalogues: lead with the words the buyer looks under, written as a normal sentence. [B]
- **Holds:** Google Shopping titles, car-rental search ads, book catalogues; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Clicks on shopping ads: sellers' own titles full of buyers' search words stayed ahead of rewritten ones, whose clicks stood 10.3 % lower; 1.24 million products. (r-bilimsoz-014)
  - Clicks on search ads: 6% to 64% higher in 9 of 12 ad sets with a capital on the first word only, against a capital on each word. (r-abarsiv-022)
  - Copies a year: 16,000 against 2,000 and 21,000 against 7,000, the same books under the heading the buyer looks for. (r-klasik-004)
- **How:** type what the buyer types, then the detail.
- **Ready (car rental):** "Rent a small car in [town], from [price] a day"

### R39. Shop with many kinds: let the visitor choose on the first screen. [one case]
- **Holds:** online shops where the first question is "which one is for me?"; any seller; sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Purchases: +12.18% with a shirt finder on the first screen, against the page without it; 136,000 visitors. (r-chatgpt-001)
  - Orders: +29% with flavour links (139 against 107). Buyers per visit: +12.82% with category tiles. (r-chatgpt-001)
- **How:** first words "Find my …" or "Which … are you?".
- **Ready (tea shop):** "Morning, after dinner or before bed? Find your tea."

### R40. Film meant to be passed on: stir awe, laughter or use, and bring the name at the end. [B]
- **Holds:** brands' YouTube films shared by people, and newspaper articles; shared = people send it on (seen).
- **Proofs:**
  - Shares: films with the name at the end were shared most; those with it at the start stood 29.88% lower and, in a second study, 35.60% lower; 345 and 512 ads. (r-bilimvideo-014)
  - Articles most passed on, for one step more of it: awe +30 %, practical use +30 %, anger +34 %; 6,956 articles; wish to pass on a funny ad 3.97 against 2.92 (one to seven, under 50 people). (r-bilimsoz-047)
  - Shares of a fact-telling film for a NEW product: +27.76% and +28.40% in two studies, against the same kind of film for a known product. (r-bilimvideo-015)
- **How:** useful or astonishing first; name on the last shot; a new product opens on its new fact.
- **Ready (bicycle shop, film):** "The sound your chain makes a week before it breaks." Last shot: the shop's door.

### R41. TikTok ad of a little-known brand: entertain first. TikTok ad of a famous brand: show the name and sell straight. [one case]
- **Holds:** paid TikTok; sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Index of extra buyers (people who saw the ad against people who did not), small brands: 215 for the most entertaining quarter of ads, against 90 for the plainest quarter (2.4x). (r-platform-015) (seller)
  - Same index, small brands: 189 for ads with the warmest feeling, against 107 (+77%). (r-platform-016) (seller)
  - Same index, leading brands: 155 for straight selling, against 44 (3.5x). (r-platform-015) (seller)
  - Same index, leading brands: 103 with the brand clear in two seconds, against 82 (+25%). (r-platform-016) (seller)
- **How:** unknown: a small scene with the product in it; famous: name, product, price.
- **Ready (unknown sauce maker):** the owner tastes it, says nothing, and takes the plate away from the camera.

### R42. Community (Reddit, forums): write the line as a member of the group would. [one case]
- **Holds:** Reddit; any seller; click and remembered.
- **Proofs:**
  - Clicks: +20% with a headline in the group's own voice, against the company's line over the same film. (r-platform-042) (seller)
  - Clicks: +40% and +110% for a second company's lines, against its standard line. (r-platform-042) (seller)
- **How:** read the group for an hour, then write one line and say who you are.
- **Ready (bike mechanic):** "I fix [brand] hubs all week. Here is the one thing I check first."

### R43. LinkedIn: let a named expert speak to the camera. [one case]
- **Holds:** LinkedIn, sales to businesses; engagement = likes, comments and clicks (click).
- **Proofs:**
  - Engagement against the average business film, 13,000+ films: an executive speaking +53%, an expert +40%, face to camera +34%, an office joke +111%. (r-platform-037) (seller)
- **How:** name and trade on screen, one thing he learned at work.
- **Ready (safety adviser):** "I have walked through [true count] warehouses. I look at the door first."

### R44. Live sale: the owner on camera, with things only to be had in the stream. [B]
- **Holds:** live shopping and TV shopping, beauty and household goods; known and followed seller; sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - $1M in one 8-hour live and about $830,000 in 12 hours, owners on camera with products launched on air. (r-uzman-031)
  - 18,000 mops in under half an hour by the inventor, against modest sales by the hosts. (r-satan-002)
- **How:** the maker shows, talks and launches something on air.
- **Ready (candle maker):** "I poured these [count] this morning, they are only here, and I light the first one now."

### R45. Food: show the finished dish close and moving, and give its taste a name. [B]
- **Holds:** food ads and canteens; any seller; seen, remembered and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Next-day memory of food ads: +26 percent with a photograph, against drawings; a recipe more than doubles readers; 512 studies. (r-sugarman-060)
  - Dishes named by their taste were chosen more than dishes with fancy, cheerful or plain-list names; 137,842 decisions at 5 universities. (r-davranis-030)
- **How:** cooking method + place + flavour, over a moving close shot.
- **Ready (grill):** "Slow-smoked [town] pepper chicken, off the fire now."

### R46. Where people come to learn a skill: open with "How to". [B]
- **Holds:** book and course lists, app ads; any seller; click and sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Copies a year: 0 to 30,000 with "How to" in the title, against the same book under its old title. (r-klasik-002)
  - App ads: tutorials +45% installs for the views they had and +17% users who stayed, against testimonials; 1.1M films in the count. (r-platform-040) (seller)
- **How:** "How to [do the thing] with [what you have]".
- **Ready (sewing shop):** "How to shorten your own curtains on one evening"

### R47. Appeal for help: tell the small number of people this one gift reaches. [one case]
- **Holds:** appeals of charities and clubs; known sender; sell.
- **Proofs:**
  - Givers: 2.4% for "Allow 36 More Couples", against 1.2% for a figure in the thousands; 2,109 people. (r-abarsiv-009)
  - Gifts: +64.6% for "just a few gifts short", against the same e-mail without it; 212,249 people (a clock ran beside it). (r-abarsiv-009)
- **How:** one gift, few people, a true count.
- **Ready (school club):** "[Small true number] more children can come to camp this summer."
