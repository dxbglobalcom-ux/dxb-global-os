**3. Ten strongest openings**
1. "Can you spot these 7 common decorating sins?" beat "To every woman who would like a career in Interior Design": +250%, split-run (Ogilvy & Mather).
2. One Swiss Army watch against nine in the same ad: 3 to 1, A/B in the Wall Street Journal (Sugarman).
3. "When Doctors 'Feel Rotten' This Is What They Do": changed to "Don't Feel Up To Par" it pulled half (Schwab).
4. "Don't Let Athlete's Foot 'Lay You Up'": 3 times "Relieve Foot Itch" (Schwab).
5. "This Is Marie Antoinette — Riding to Her Death": a quarter page got 8 times the replies of any double-spread (Schwab).
6. "Burpee's New 1973 Garden Catalog—Free": +112% against a successful $1-offer ad, split-run (Ogilvy & Mather).
7. "Forget it, Heinz," the experts told me: the Mercedes 190D letter of 1965. Ogilvy & Mather report 716 diesels sold in 8 weeks from "an 8-page letter". That this is the same letter is my inference.
8. "A Fluke of Nature… I'm a farmer": two agency ads had not covered the space. His own gave the lowest cost per order of any outside list, ran many years, and he had 80,000 customers.
9. BluBlocker TV, faces reacting and the view never shown: almost 8 million pairs in six years. The print opening "Vision Breakthrough" started the company.
10. KLM: the destination photo pulled twice the coupons of the aircraft photo, split-run. QE2 "Sail to Europe regular fare — Sail home free": half-booked sailings sold out in 8 days.

**4. Surprising or against what is taught**
- **Headline is not always the biggest lever.** In Sugarman's Consumers Hero test the headline moved replies 20%, the guarantee at the very end doubled them, and price hardly mattered. He also says lower prices brought more orders, so the two do not sit well together.
- **The best-read ad can sell nothing.** Rinso's stain ad was the best-read detergent ad in history, and "Sales didn't move" (Roman).
- **Headline length depends on the job.** Ogilvy found 10+ words sold more in a store. Sugarman's winners are 1–3 words with a sub-headline of about 16 words.
- **Ogilvy softened over time.** Long copy went from "invariably" (1963) to "usually, but not always" (1970s direct ad). "Crowd scenes don't pull" stands against KLM's 86 natives, among the best read of the year.
- **"Story appeal" figures disagree.** Hathaway: whole stock sold out in a week (Roman), $2 million to $30 million (CBC), "more than doubled" over 19 years (WWD). Use the first or last.
- **The Rolls-Royce clock headline was not original.** It came from a 20-year-old article, and a 1933 Pierce-Arrow ad had used it. "+50% in 1959" is third-hand, WWD citing a BBC film.
- **Mistake headlines both win and lose.** The decorating quiz won by 250%. "How to avoid these mistakes" lost by almost 20% to "suit yourself" for houses.
- **A business-card-sized point about place.** The coupon moved to the top tripled returns. The back page pulled up to 150% better than inside pages.
- **Naming your own flaw first** carried the Magic Stat ad about three years. Two places in the book differ: "almost" and "more than" three.
