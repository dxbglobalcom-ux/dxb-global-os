**3. Ten strongest**
1. "Build Your Own House in Two Hours": only the headline changed, over 6 times the inquiries; a year's output sold in a month (Starch 1914).
2. "The Deaf Now Hear Whispers": inquiry cost fell to 34 cents, target under a dollar; business quadrupled in a year.
3. "This is Marie Antoinette riding to her death": a quarter page pulled 8 times the coupons of full pages on owning fine books.
4. "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?": near 3 times the response of "How to Speak and Write Masterly English" over ten years; ran 40+ years (second-hand for Schwab).
5. "Einstein's Theory of Relativity Explained" against "An Introduction to Einstein": 42,000 to 15,000 copies.
6. "How to Argue Logically" against "Art of Controversy": from near zero to 30,000 a year.
7. "The Mystery of the Man in the Iron Mask" against "The Mystery of the Iron Mask": 30,000 to 11,000, two words added.
8. "Each cockroach upon your premises is there with your full permission": 17% against 3%, same 5,000 hospitals, same arguments.
9. "The Book of Secrets": same content failed under two titles, then 20 million mailed and over 500,000 sold.
10. WSJ "two young men": in use 28 years (the solid part). The $1–2 billion is Hatch's telephone estimate; the Journal kept no statistics.

**4. Surprises and contradictions**
- **Plain beats clever, counted.** Every before/after title figure Haldeman-Julius prints is a gain for the plainer line; poetic, learned and witty titles are the ones he replaces. A plain "We are manufacturers of… similar to the enclosed sample" brought 12 orders and 10 steady customers; a witty letter to a similar list brought 1.
- **The question opening did not win** a five-way letter test: direct request 4.3%, news 3.3%, question 2.3%, sensation under 2%, sympathy under 2%. N is 1,000 per cell, so 4.3 against 3.3 is within chance (my judgement, no test run).
- **The thing beats the person:** Bernhardt, Einstein and Nietzsche titles rose when the subject led instead of the name.
- **Bad habits beat good ones:** "How to Break Bad Habits" 29,000, "How to Form Good Habits" 20,000.
- **A heading is also a hook:** books moved to another section sold 7,000 → 21,000 and 2,000 → 16,000 (titles changed with some moves).
- **Deadlines** worked while the name was unknown. In 1928 a plain list with no date beat the best dated page, $320 to $265 on the first day.
- **Attention is not value:** the Mask envelope won nearly 10% more donors, but the plain one won about $1 more per gift. The pen doubled response (1.27% against 0.66%, 38,000 each), yet Ken Burnett says those donors drop off faster (opinion).
- **"Amateur" letters won:** an editor (Quest, 6%, 600,000 subscribers) and a priest beat or outlasted professionals. The priest (Covenant House) was later forced out over misconduct.
- **Famous but unproven here:** "Somewhere west of Laramie" had no sales figure, so no row. "Plop plop" rests on a broadcaster's phrase. Hatch contradicts himself on years (Newsweek 15/17, Covenant House 16/27); Haldeman-Julius prints 38,000 and 77,000 for one title. All are named in the rows.
- *Letters That Make Good* calls 7,431 of 45,000 "nearly 12 percent"; it is 16.5%.
