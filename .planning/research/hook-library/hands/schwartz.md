**3. Ten strongest**
1. "Deeply and Irrevocably Personal" (plain envelope): only the envelope changed, response up almost 50% at first, control for years, 200,000–300,000 books sold.
2. WSJ "Two young men" letter: mailed over 25 years, about 55% of mail subscribers, put at $1–2 billion (Hatch's arithmetic on the paper's estimates).
3. Coat-of-arms letter: 7,300,000 cash buyers from 140 million letters.
4. Dollar-bill hospital letter: over 90% response; 175,000 letters returned about $270,000.
5. Same book, third title ("Book of Secrets") after two failures: over 500,000 copies, almost $20 million. The copy changed too.
6. "When Doctors Have Headaches, What Do They Do?" beat "Tension Headache?" by 71%.
7. "If you've got 20 minutes a month, I guarantee…": won its split-run and ran as control for years. The same sentence, headline only changed, then beat another product's control.
8. "How To Collect From Social Security At Any Age": about 35 orders per 1,000 circulation; Parade ad cost $33,214, gross $228,492.
9. Quest magazine editor's own letter: 6% response, over 600,000 subscribers, unbeaten for more than four years.
10. Esquire nine-dot puzzle envelope: beat a control that had stood for years of tests.

Beside these as a rule: Makepeace changed only the headline, and orders went from 9.8 to 13.9 per 1,000, profit from $430 to $800. He does not print the two headlines.

**4. What surprised or contradicts common teaching**
- Schwartz's awareness and sophistication ladders have no test table behind them. They rest on named campaigns. The only tests the book really tells are the TV-manual headline and the Wall Street Journal layout (19 monthly insertions, no drop). His "50%" and "twice the pull" are round statements.
- Opposite openings both won: the bare plain envelope (Halbert, Boardroom) and the loud teaser (65 Hatch lines, Jayme). No mould.
- Personalising fell from about +300% to about +30% as everyone did it (Halbert; Carlton's tests).
- Bencivenga admitted he cannot reliably pick the winner between two strong headlines (second-hand, Bejakovic).
- Halbert's numbers move between letters: 140, 500 and 600 million mailed; $800,000 or over $1,000,000 net on the same ad. The rows carry the figure and the doubt.
- Makepeace calls 9.8 to 13.9 "a 34% lift"; by orders it is about 42%.
- "Control" proves the whole package paid, never the line alone.

**Left out on purpose:** the Tova perfume headline (7,000 people came, but it is sexual innuendo and the story behind the product is invented), Halbert's personal ad and Carlton's Rodale letter (immodest), the reducing-pill headlines and the fake-news blackhead ad (deceiving), the "carbon copy of my earlier letter" trick (false front), AmEx and home-equity teasers (credit), sweepstakes and gambling lines, a "Why do some men…" envelope (meaning unclear).
