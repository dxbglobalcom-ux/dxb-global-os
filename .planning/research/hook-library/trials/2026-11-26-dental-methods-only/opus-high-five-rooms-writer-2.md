**1. The Crown Jewels**

- **Picture (0–3 s):** 0–1 s: a dark frame with one spotlight on a red velvet cushion with gold tassels under a glass dome, staged like the Crown Jewels. On the cushion sits one small, tooth-white dental crown. 1–2 s: a slow push-in until the crown fills a third of the frame, so you can see that it is the colour of a tooth and has no metal. 2–3 s: a gloved hand lifts the dome and takes the crown with tweezers. Dr Wong is soft in the background, in scrubs.
- **On screen:** Crowned in one sitting.
- **Heard:** Two notes of a brass fanfare, cut dead. Then Dr Wong, deadpan: "Broken a tooth, Bracknell? Have a seat."
- **Caption, first line:** Broken tooth? Dr Wong makes your crown here and fits it the same visit: no temporary, no metal, from £750.
- **The seven rooms:**
  - SHOW: the picture shows the real crown alone, presented as a rare object, so nobody needs to explain what is sold.
  - CURIOSITY: the picture leaves a gap, because a tooth is lying where a king's crown belongs.
  - TROUBLE: the voice says "Broken a tooth?" in the first second of speech.
  - GAIN: the screen says "one sitting", and the caption says same visit, no temporary, no metal, £750.
  - TRUST: Dr Wong's own voice, his figure in the frame and his name in the caption.
  - ADDRESS: the voice names "Bracknell", and "Have a seat" speaks straight to him.
  - PLAY: the royal parody, the fanfare cut short, and the double meaning of "sitting" in the screen line and the spoken line.
  - Missing: none. TRUST is the weakest room, because it rests on a voice and a name with no face to camera.
- **Methods used:** m-072, m-076, m-017, m-134, m-080, m-039, m-023, m-061

**2. Two lumps**

- **Picture (0–3 s):** 0–1 s: a white china saucer in bright, plain light with two identical white cubes side by side. Above them is a pair of silver sugar tongs held by a hand in a blue nitrile glove. The glove is the odd detail that says "dentist". 1–2 s: the tongs hover over the left cube, then move to the right one. 2–3 s: they lift the right cube, the ceramic block, which is a little glossier. The sugar cube stays on the saucer. (To check on set: some blocks have a metal holder on one side, which must face away from the lens.)
- **On screen:** One rots teeth. One becomes one.
- **Heard:** The clink of tongs on china. Then Dr Wong, dry: "Broken a tooth, Bracknell? Not that one."
- **Caption, first line:** Broken tooth? Your crown is carved from this block in the practice and fitted the same day, from £750.
- **The seven rooms:**
  - SHOW: the picture shows the real crown block in the gloved hand.
  - CURIOSITY: the picture is a riddle he solves by looking, because it asks which cube is the tooth.
  - TROUBLE: "rots teeth" on screen and "Broken a tooth?" in the voice.
  - GAIN: "becomes one" on screen, and same day and £750 in the caption.
  - TRUST: Dr Wong's voice and the dentist's glove.
  - ADDRESS: the voice says "Bracknell".
  - PLAY: the teatime ritual turned over, plus the twist in the screen line.
  - Missing: none. TRUST is the weakest room here too.
- **Methods used:** m-017, m-054, m-111, m-025, m-134, m-080, m-039

**3. The appointment card**

- **Picture (0–3 s):** 0–1 s: a CrownWood appointment card held right at the lens, sharp, with the practice blurred behind it. It is printed "Your next appointment: ________". 1–2 s: a pen in a gloved hand writes on the line by hand: "None needed. Dr W." 2–3 s: the hand lays the finished crown on the card, just below the writing.
- **On screen:** The card's own handwriting: Next appointment: none needed.
- **Heard:** The scratch of the pen. Then Dr Wong: "Broken tooth? Don't book the second visit."
- **Caption, first line:** Broken tooth in Bracknell? One visit: scanned, made and fitted the same day. No temporary crown, no metal.
- **The seven rooms:**
  - SHOW: the picture shows the finished crown itself, lying on the card.
  - CURIOSITY: the blank line fills with an answer nobody expects from a dentist.
  - TROUBLE: the voice and caption attack the old way, the second visit and the temporary crown, and leave it behind.
  - GAIN: one visit, carried by the card, the voice and the caption.
  - TRUST: a handwritten note signed by one named dentist.
  - ADDRESS: "Your next appointment" on the card, "Bracknell" in the caption, and an order spoken to him.
  - Missing: PLAY. The wit is dry, but it does not land as a joke or a feeling.
- **Methods used:** m-052, m-048, m-069, m-087, m-013, m-080, m-023

**Strongest: 1, the Crown Jewels.** With the sound off, the picture alone carries three rooms in half a second: the product, the open gap and the joke. A rival could not steal it, because the joke is the practice's own name. The words add the trouble, the gain and the address without explaining anything. The risk is that the crown is small on the cushion: if a stranger watching it muted cannot see in one second that it is a tooth, the push-in has to start closer.
