**Hook 1: "The countdown"**
- **Picture (0–3 s):** At 0 s a locked macro shot through the glass of the practice's milling machine. Water sprays over a small tooth-white ceramic block, the burs are already cutting, and half a molar is visible. At 1–2 s the shot holds and a cusp comes out of the spray. At 3 s the machine is still cutting, so the finished crown is not shown yet.
- **On screen:** "Your new tooth: 4:57 left". The clock counts down live and is synced to the real remaining time of that run.
- **Heard:** The machine's own whine and hiss from frame 0. At about 1 s, Dr Wong off camera, calm: "I'm Dr Wong. No paste. No temporary. No metal."
- **Caption, first line:** "Broken a tooth? In Bracknell it's fixed in one visit. From £750."
- **The seven rooms:**
  - SHOW: the picture is the real crown being carved under water (in_use, spectacle).
  - CURIOSITY: the shape is half cut and the clock is running, so the end stays open (unfinished).
  - TROUBLE: the caption opens on "Broken a tooth?", and the spoken "No paste. No temporary." names the old way he dreads (enemy).
  - GAIN: "4:57 left" puts the small cost on screen (fast_easy), and the caption adds one visit and the price (offer).
  - TRUST: the named dentist speaks in his own voice (maker, expert).
  - ADDRESS: "Your new tooth" on screen, and "Bracknell" in the caption (call_out).

**Hook 2: "Bad news"**
- **Picture (0–3 s):** At 0 s Dr Wong in his own surgery holds the crown right at the lens between finger and thumb. It is fresh from the mill and still wet, sharp in the foreground, with his face soft behind it. At 1–2 s he holds still and looks straight into the lens, deadpan. At 2–3 s he turns the crown a quarter so the light shows it is tooth-coloured, not metal.
- **On screen:** "Bad news: no second visit."
- **Heard:** Dr Wong, dry and mock-apologetic: "Apologies. It's already made."
- **Caption, first line:** "Broken a tooth near Bracknell? Crown made and fitted the same visit, from £750."
- **The seven rooms:**
  - SHOW: the real milled crown fills the frame (in_use).
  - CURIOSITY: a small white object at the lens, and "bad news" that holds back the good news for half a beat (odd_detail).
  - TROUBLE: anyone who has had a crown recognises the second visit and the temporary in between, and the caption names the broken tooth (trouble).
  - GAIN: "no second visit" is the benefit itself (plain_gain), and the caption gives the price (offer).
  - TRUST: the dentist himself, in the room where he works, framing his own strength as a confession (maker, honest).
  - ADDRESS: the caption names the place, and the line speaks to "you" (call_out).
  - PLAY: a bad word that turns out to be praise, delivered straight-faced (joke).

**Hook 3: "Binned"**
- **Picture (0–3 s):** At 0 s, in one shot, Dr Wong stands at the surgery bin. One hand holds a dental tray loaded with impression paste over the open bin. The other hand holds the small scanning camera, small and clean. At 0.5–1 s he lets go and the tray drops into the bin. At 1–3 s he stays put and just raises the camera a little toward the lens.
- **On screen:** "Mouthful of paste? Binned."
- **Heard:** The real thud of the tray landing in the bin. Then Dr Wong, flat: "We film the tooth instead."
- **Caption, first line:** "Broken tooth in Bracknell? Scanned, milled and fitted in one visit. From £750."
- **The seven rooms:**
  - SHOW: the old way and the new tool in one frame, and the old one leaves (versus).
  - CURIOSITY: the small camera in his other hand, and "film the tooth", leave how it works unexplained (odd_detail).
  - TROUBLE: anyone who has had an impression remembers that mouthful, and the caption adds the broken tooth (enemy).
  - GAIN: no paste is seen, and the caption gives one visit and the price (plain_gain, offer).
  - TRUST: the dentist himself throws away his own trade's tool (expert).
  - ADDRESS: the caption names Bracknell (call_out).
  - PLAY: a dentist binning his own kit with a straight face, plus the thud (joke).

**Strongest:** Hook 2. It is the only one where the practice's real difference, one visit, sits in the on-screen words, the picture is the finished product, and the joke delivers it. All seven rooms read in half a second with the sound off, and none of them is forced.
