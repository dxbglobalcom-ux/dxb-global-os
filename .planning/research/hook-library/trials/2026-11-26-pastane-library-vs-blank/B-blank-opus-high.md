**1. "Kapı 07:00'de açılıyor"**
- **Hook (0–3 s):** The camera looks from the dark shop window into the lit kitchen at the back. A hand flours a wooden table. On screen: "Kadıköy uyuyor. Biz değil." Sound: one dull thump as the dough hits the table, and nothing else.
- **Scenario (20–30 s):**
  - 3–8 s: Close-ups of hands. Poğaça dough is cut and shaped one by one, and çörek is braided. On screen: "Makine yok. Her sabah, elde."
  - 8–13 s: The oven door opens and steam fills the frame. Gülsüm takes out a tray. Her son carries it out front.
  - 13–18 s: The display fills up: cakes, profiterole, cookies. The light comes on and the clock shows 06:59.
  - 18–22 s: The key turns and the sign flips to "Açık" at 07:00. The first customer comes in and someone breaks a poğaça open. You see the steam.
  - 22–27 s: Gülsüm, to the camera: "1978'den beri böyle. Sabah yapılır, sabah yenir."
  - Ending: "Gülsüm Pastanesi · Kadıköy · 07:00–22:00. Sıcağı kaçırma."
- **Why it works:** It shows what the whole offer rests on, made by hand each morning, as one race against the clock, and ends at the moment the door opens.

**2. "Annem 'olmuş' demeden vitrine giremez"**
- **Hook (0–3 s):** The son holds a fresh tray of profiterole close to the camera, with a slightly nervous smile. On screen: "Annem 'olmuş' demeden bu vitrine giremez." First words, from him: "Bugün geçecek mi bakalım…"
- **Scenario (20–30 s):**
  - 3–9 s: Fast cuts of him making it: filling the choux, pouring the chocolate sauce, sound up.
  - 9–14 s: Gülsüm comes in, wipes her hands on her apron and takes a spoonful without a word. The camera stays on her face.
  - 14–17 s: A pause. On screen: "…"
  - 17–21 s: Gülsüm: "Sos biraz daha koyu olacak." The son sighs and laughs. Jump cut: second try.
  - 21–25 s: She tastes it again, nods and says "Olmuş." The tray goes into the display.
  - 25–29 s: On screen: "1978'den beri her tepsi bu sınavdan geçer." Ending, spoken by the son: "Geçen tepsi vitrinde. Kadıköy'deyiz, gel tat."
- **Why it works:** A real mother-and-son test is a story people watch to the end, it proves the quality without anyone claiming it, and it can become a weekly series ("Bugün geçecek mi?").

**3. "Doğum gününe iki gün mü var?"**
- **Hook (0–3 s):** A calendar or phone screen with a date circled. On screen: "Doğum gününe 2 gün mü var? Tam zamanı." Sound: a single candle being lit.
- **Scenario (20–30 s):**
  - 3–7 s: On screen: "Neden iki gün?" Gülsüm, to the camera: "Çünkü pastanız hazır bekleyen bir pasta değil."
  - 7–15 s: Fast close-ups of the hands at work: the sponge is split, cream is spread, the edges are smoothed with a spatula, and a name is written on top in piping.
  - 15–20 s: Her son carries the finished cake to the counter and puts it in the box.
  - 20–25 s: At the table, candles are lit and blown out. This is a staged scene, not a real customer.
  - Ending (25–30 s): On screen: "Kutlama pastanı 2 gün önceden söyle, elde yapalım. Gülsüm Pastanesi · Kadıköy." Spoken: "Kimin doğum günü? Yaz bize." The ordering channel (phone, DM or in the shop) is not in the facts, so the client fills it in.
- **Why it works:** It turns the two-day ordering rule into proof that the cake is made by hand, and it goes straight for the most valuable sale, cake orders.

**4. Sound first: "Bu ses Kadıköy'de"**
- **Hook (0–3 s):** A black screen with only sound: something crisp cracking. Then light: a poğaça torn in half, steam rising. On screen: "Sesi aç."
- **Scenario (20–30 s):** No music, only natural sound. Each shot about 3 seconds:
  - 3–6 s: A knife goes into a cake.
  - 6–9 s: Chocolate sauce is poured over profiterole.
  - 9–12 s: Çörek is torn.
  - 12–15 s: A cookie breaks in two.
  - 15–18 s: A tray hits the oven rack.
  - 18–22 s: The shop's bustle. A tea glass (an assumption, cut it if they don't serve tea) or a plate is set on a table inside, a few tables full.
  - 22–27 s: Gülsüm, in a low voice: "Hepsi bu sabah, arkadaki mutfakta."
  - Ending: "Gülsüm Pastanesi · Kadıköy · 1978'den beri · 07:00–22:00."
- **Why it works:** On Reels and TikTok, sound is what stops the scroll. It is cheap to shoot, can be filmed again every week, and gives a reason to replay it.

**5. "07:00'den 22:00'ye, 30 saniyede"**
- **Hook (0–3 s):** A big clock or text on screen: "07:00." The sign flips to "Açık" and the first tray goes into the display. On screen: "Bir günümüz, 30 saniyede."
- **Scenario (20–30 s):** Time-lapse, with the hour shown at the corner of each shot:
  - 3–7 s, 07:30: Poğaça for people heading to work.
  - 7–11 s, 10:00: A cake is being decorated in the back.
  - 11–15 s, 13:00: People at the tables.
  - 15–19 s, 16:00: A celebration cake is handed over in its box.
  - 19–23 s, 19:00: Evening customers, profiterole.
  - 23–27 s, 22:00: The display is nearly empty, the lights go off, and Gülsüm and her son wipe down the counter. Gülsüm: "Yarın sabah hepsi yeniden, elde."
  - Ending (27–30 s): "Sabah 7, akşam 10. Kadıköy, Gülsüm Pastanesi. Hangi saatte geliyorsun?"
- **Why it works:** It shows in one film that you can come at any hour and that everything is made fresh every day, and the closing question pulls comments.

**Strongest: number 2.** It is the only one with a real character and tension (will it pass or not). It proves the handmade, family quality on screen, and it can become a series that gets people coming back every week, which brings in customers and cake orders together.

I worked only from the brief, as you asked. One thing the facts don't give: how cake orders are placed (phone, DM or in the shop). So the endings of films 1, 3 and 5 say "yaz bize" or leave a blank, and the client needs to fill that in before shooting.
