## What won in this trade, and why
1. **SOLD** — The same piece standing in a lived-in room beats the piece alone on white. People furnish rooms, not objects: they stop because they see their own corner finished. First second: a kitchen, a child's room, a bed in morning light, the thing already in it; no logo card.
   IKEA Canada: 50% better cost per action (o-platform-036). Ashley Canada, only the room behind the furniture changed: 33% higher return on ad spend, 19% lower cost per purchase (o-platform-014). Slound bedding, creators filming it in their own homes: 2X return (o-platform-062). Cheap and dear alike, known and unknown.
2. **SOLD** — A small household loss everybody had this week, shown falling, bursting or burning. He stops because his own money is in the picture. First second: a hand drops limp celery into a bin straight below the lens, or a pan slips in a cloth mitt, and the voice names the thing at once.
   FoodSaver: under $20 million in 1998 to more than $80 million in 1999 (o-ad2-002). Ove Glove: over 20 million sold, the maker's number (o-satan-098). Fits cheap and middle-priced things whose cure is seen at once.
3. **SOLD** — For a dear thing nobody can try through a screen: say or print the one fact that removes the fear — the price, the delivery, who stands behind it. He stops because the question he would have asked is already answered. First second: that fact alone, large, on or before the product.
   "Free Next Day Mattress Delivery" on the picture: 25% more revenue, 54% higher return on ad spend (o-platform-011). "Now priced under $100", signed by the inventor: over $1 billion, his own figure (o-satan-045).
   **KEPT PAYING**: a man at a dining set in his crowded shop, a red price tag slaps in within the first second: 645 days (o-meta2-007).
4. **SOLD** (known names) — A feeling told in one picture, the goods later. A house everybody knows may begin with a child's face, a falling sleeper or a whisper, because the name ends the riddle. First second: an eye opens, a boy watches a penguin film, or a room where nothing moves and a woman whispers.
   John Lewis penguin: £141m of extra revenue (o-satan-004). IKEA beds, four-year campaign: £755m (o-satan-025). IKEA whispered room: student season 27% up (o-ad3-013). All films of big houses; an unknown shop has no such proof here.
5. One case: **SOLD** — Make him doubt his own thing, then test it before his eyes. "What's a super easy way to know if your mattress is awful?", a fairy-tale girl beside a bed, raw eggs under glass: the best of about 25 spoken openings, over $60m in the first year (o-tec4-031, o-uzman-001).
6. **KEPT PAYING** (no sales figure) — One clear movement that turns the thing into something else. A wall comes down and is a bed; he waits for the movement to end, and then he has understood the product. First second: one part moves, the rest is still, the kind of thing stands in five plain words.
   Wall-bed kit 1303 days (o-meta2-008); 3D sofa that unfolds itself 1693 days (o-meta2-005); an arm thrown at a bare wall and eight frames hang there, 790 days (o-meta3-011); drum grater cut against the old one in the same frame, shown in 35 countries (o-tta3-011).
7. **SEEN** — What is hidden: under the paint, inside the parcel, behind the odd material. He stays for the answer that will surely come. First second: the thing close, one exact question or a name that does not fit what he sees.
   "I found this in the trash. What's under the paint?" 26,000,000 views, 165.8 times the account's usual film (o-to1-025). Pink slab torn, called "iced coffee candle": 28,200,000 (o-to8-008). Old paper parcel opened: 43,600,000 (o-to8-024). Two small makers and one home creator.
8. **SEEN** — Hands at the bench and one thing that fits, sinks or closes. The eye waits for the gap to shut. First second: material fills the frame and already moves; no speech needed, so it travels everywhere.
   Vase pushed into glaze to the rim: 9600000 views, 171.4 times (o-y4-005). Legless slab lowered on a rail: 2,400,000 (o-to2-001).

## What it depends on
- Result seen at once (folds, hides, cleans, hangs): finding 6 for paid, 8 for unpaid. Result felt later (mattress, sheets): findings 3 and 5 — a fact or a test that can be seen.
- Dear and thought over: the room (1), the removed fear (3). Cheap and bought at once: the loss (2), the movement (6).
- Unknown name: 2, 6, 7, 8 were all won by sellers few people knew. Known name: 4, and the unveiling of something really new, as KitchenAid's red curtain (o-to7-020, 4,000,000 views).
- Own hands and workshop: a phone is enough for 7 and 8. Nothing made on site (shop, chain): 1 and 3; the owner himself with a price tag works with one take.
- One-off or made to order: the piece doing what it does (Thibodeau) and the thankful customer (Hutchinson) are the two ways here that are shown to bring orders.
