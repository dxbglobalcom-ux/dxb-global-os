# Travel (hotels, stays, tours, airlines, attractions, luggage) — what won here (25 November 2026)
49 winners of this trade in the library: 16 seen by strangers, unpaid · 12 sold · 11 brought clicks or names · 10 kept paying for (a winner may hold two roles); 23 films on this machine, 16 may be shown to anybody.
## What won in this trade, and why
Roles: SOLD = a published sales result. KEPT PAYING = the owner paid for it for months or years, no sales figure. CLICKS = applied, answered, left a name. SEEN = strangers watched, unpaid.

1. **SOLD** — The cheap or the hard one says so itself, first, in a warm voice. The admission nobody makes is believed, and the price then sounds like good sense.
   First second: a plain voice, no picture needed. "They've got beds; we've got beds", Motel 6: occupancy +6 points in a year and revenue $256 million to $297 million; rooms were improved too (o-ad6-001). "When you're only No. 2, you try harder. Or else.", Avis: from a $3.2 million loss to $1.2 million profit within a year (o-klasik-142).
   Dear and rare, the same way: "It will cost you $10,000 and about 26 days … some discomfort … some danger", a chairman's letter for a flight over both poles: all 50 seats filled (o-ad1-020).
2. **SOLD** — The plain bargain or the exact thing you will do, instead of fine words. It is understood in one second.
   First second: the offer. "Sail to Europe regular fare — Sail home free", the same ship drawn twice, Cunard: both round trips sold out within eight days; the price did much of it (o-sugarman-053). "Enjoy 14 Days of Unlimited Access to both …", Universal Orlando: 26% more conversions, page changed with it (o-abarsiv-146).
   Same shape, **CLICKS**: free London theatre tickets in a letter from the British Tourist Authority, answers as high as 26 percent (o-sugarman-052).
3. **SOLD** — Name the person, or the feeling he goes for, not the product. He feels somebody has watched him.
   First second: his habit or his feeling. "Designing a suitcase for people who like to live out of their luggage", the maker's hand drawing it, Away: 13 weeks of stock in under three weeks; the brand says not from this film alone (o-ad3-020). "Feel pleasure you never knew existed. (For instance, Lenox, Massachusetts.)", Canyon Ranch: 97.4 percent better in inquiries, 50 percent better in sales; its picture was not seen (o-caples-089).
   **KEPT PAYING**: "If you're moving abroad from the US", My Baggage, 604 days (o-meta6-011); "this part, instantly stressful", Send My Bag, 476 days (o-meta6-017).
4. **SOLD**, then **KEPT PAYING** — The one sight people come for, already happening, as a visitor sees it. He is there before he has read a word.
   First second: an animal or the place itself, one take. A farm's "Cow Safari", two cow heads over the rail and a hand feeding bread: from fewer than 70 people a day to as many as 600 (o-sek3-019).
   Paid for long: a guide's flute in a burning orange canyon, Dixie's Lower Antelope Canyon Tours, 496 days (o-meta6-015); an otter asleep in a visitor's arms, Animal EDventure Park, 457 days, first by impressions for "tours" (o-meta6-010); a plain hotel bedroom, then the phone tips over the wall and a second floor lies below, Clementine Hotel, 915 days (o-mb1-004).
   **CLICKS**: a meerkat standing like a sentry, Tswalu, 943 days, visits to its page (o-mb1-005); a photo of the place pulled twice as many coupons as the aircraft, KLM (o-sugarman-036).
5. **CLICKS** — The dream put in the driest form, with a part for the reader. He stops being a reader and becomes a candidate.
   First second: a form nobody expects. "The Best Job in the World", a small job advert for an island caretaker, Tourism Queensland: 34,684 video applications (o-ad4-024). "Be the first person on your block to own a Kangaroo", Qantas: one page, over 7,500 replies (o-ad1-024). Applications and replies, not bookings.
6. **SEEN** — The owner or the staff in the place, serious face, one thing that does not fit. Face, house and words do not belong together.
   First second: a still person before the house, already speaking. Fyfield Manor, 880 years old, the owner reads her daughter's slang: 12.7 million views, 1000 times the usual (o-tec6-012). Hotel Woodstock's couple hurrying through the lobby with the hotel's name behind: 479,600 views, usual 9,441 (o-tec6-013).
   A museum's 1920s grandad stops the thumb with his palm: 570,000 followers in six months, interest in bookings among 18-24-year-olds up 15 per cent (**CLICKS**, o-ad5-034).
7. **SEEN** — A door that is closed to others, or knowledge you are not meant to have. Nobody leaves before it opens.
   First second: the door. An old keeper with a key at a stone door in Egypt: 31.5 million views on an account whose usual film has 825; take the door, not the statue behind it (o-to8-025). "Tour of my house that is a 50 yr old Hotel", the glass door swings at you: 885,200 views (o-to9-006). "Ryanair's hidden gem 29F", a hand showing the free space: 6.8 million (o-tec4-002).
8. **SEEN** — One case: Ryanair lets a customer's joke about it run, then the plane itself answers with the price. 16.9 million views, 153,900 shares (o-to9-003); "what passengers expect for €19.99" 5.2 million (o-tec4-006). It fits a firm whose weakness everybody knows and forgives for the price.

## What it depends on
- A place with one sight of its own (animal, light, height, odd room): finding 4, one take, no speech; a phone is enough (o-mb1-004, o-sek3-019).
- A plain place without a sight: its people, finding 6, or an honest price, finding 1. The owner must enjoy it and do it every week.
- Dear and rare: say price, time and hardship at once, finding 1, to a short list; dear and restful: the feeling, finding 3.
- Services around travel (luggage, moving, booking): the traveller's exact situation, finding 3.
- A big known name: the offer, finding 2, or the dry joke, finding 8; an unknown one is watched for the door or the animal, findings 4 and 7.
- Nothing with bathing, drink or gambling is shown or copied, whatever a resort's own footage holds.
