# Software and apps — what won here (25 November 2026)
41 winners of this trade in the library: 20 brought clicks or names · 11 kept paying for · 7 seen by strangers, unpaid · 3 sold (a winner may hold two roles); 19 films on this machine, 14 may be shown to anybody.
## What won in this trade, and why
1. **CLICKS** (new users) — For a free app of an unknown maker, a real person close to the maker speaks for him, on a phone. One stops for a human story that is whole in one picture, and trying costs nothing. First second: the maker himself, seen in an honest, slightly helpless moment; the plea in one written or spoken line.
   Father with a leaflet nobody takes, "help blow up my dads boating app": over 300,000 downloads in 10 days, first in Navigation in both stores (Argo Navigation; o-ad5-005). Sister in front, shy maker behind: more than 100,000 new users (BiteSight; o-sek3-002). "I made the Locket widget for my girlfriend", one press of the shutter: more than 2 million sign-ups in eleven days (Locket; not for showing; o-ad5-027). The oldest: a founder says his name and shows his screen, 75,000 on the waiting list in 24 hours (Dropbox; o-ad3-002).
2. **CLICKS** (new users) — One kind of person, one chore he has today, the cure on the same desk. He knows himself before any product is named. First second: the chore being done, with a tired hand or face, and one question or short command.
   "Do u hate taking notes during ur online lecture?" over a student who shakes out his hand: 22 such films for $1,820 brought over 150,000 new users, all together (Tactiq; o-sek3-009). "Checking email every 5 minutes? Stop!", the same line left to stand alone: 91% more people finished the setup (AwayFind; o-abarsiv-096).
3. **SOLD** — On the page of a tool people pay for, the buyer's own trouble, or what the great ones do about it, came before any product word. He came with a doubt, and the first line is about his doubt. First second: one sentence that fills the top; in the film, the trouble written large.
   "the big problem that keeps small web businesses, well, small ...": 64% more conversions with the film than without it, about $21,000 more a month (Crazy Egg; o-ad3-001). "When eBay, Disney, and Marriott need SEO help, here's what they do": 52% more sales of the membership, with a whole new long page (Moz; o-abarsiv-087; method m-055).
   One case: when buyers know the usual price, the price is the headline: "PASCAL $49.95" beside a rival's $595, one page brought 1,100 orders in the first month (Borland; o-ad1-013).
4. **CLICKS** — On a sign-up page: the work that disappears, the minutes, the free days, the smaller word. It answers "how much trouble is this for me?". First second: the headline and the button say it in the visitor's words.
   "Let Your Events Manage Themselves": 90% more, with other changes (RegOnline; o-abarsiv-138). "Your Own Extremely Fast WordPress Server, Spun Up in Minutes", words from customer talks: 14.6 percent more (SpinupWP; o-abarsiv-160). "30-day Free Trial on All Accounts": 30% better (Highrise; o-chatgpt-012). "create a page" instead of site: 7.0% against 2.8%, 682 people (CaringBridge; o-abarsiv-046).
   One case: the line under every message its users sent, "Get your free email at Hotmail": 1 million users in six months (o-ad6-014).
5. **CLICKS** (new users) — A happy user tells it unasked, with one tiny exact reason. A friend's news, not a company's. First second: the face close to the lens, already in the middle of it.
   "Guys, I just got my first Be My Eyes call!": more than 100,000 new volunteers in the month after (Be My Eyes; not for showing; o-sek3-001). Reasons counted on the real screen against the giant everybody has: 20,000 new users in six hours, 40,000 by the end of the day (The StoryGraph; not for showing; o-sek3-004).
6. **KEPT PAYING** — Paid films whose owners went on for a year and more show the buyer's own screen or his small shame, then the cure. No sales figure is known. First second: his daily screen, or somebody proudly bad at the thing he fears.
   Endless scroll through the trade's own catalogue: 553 days (Archsynth; o-meta3-001). A singer proud of an awful line, then the producer's face in his hand: 533 days (LyricStudio; o-meta3-005). "in minutes, not hours", played out on the screen in three seconds: 558 days (Resume.co; o-meta3-003). "things just weren't adding up?": 529 days (SocialCatfish; o-meta3-018). An ordinary engineer, an odd word and an exact sum: 610 days (CopyandContent.ai; o-meta3-008). "If you write anything on your computer ...": 564,000,000 views, bought (Grammarly; o-tec4-021).
7. **SEEN** — The screen filmed like a secret or a treasure, never as a plain recording. The words deny or hide what the eye already sees. First second: a glowing laptop in a dark room, or a finger on the lips.
   "it's just an ordinary Novel Writing app..." over a beautiful finished book: 1,400,000 views, 657.9 times the account's median (WriteO; o-to9-012). "Don't tell your boss this Excel trick": 2,700,000 views, 50.7 times (Kevin Stratvert; o-to10-007).
8. **CLICKS** — E-mails of software firms: a gain said exactly beats a question or a wish. First second: the subject line alone.
   "Getting twice as much from your donors": opened by 41.0% against 33.5% (iDonate; o-chatgpt-016). "How to drive opens and clicks with email segmentation": 46.5% against 12% of readers went to the article (Campaigner; o-abarsiv-157).

## What it depends on
- Free app, unknown maker, phone camera: the real person close to him (1) or the happy user (5). The story must be true.
- A tool for one job or one study, seen at once: the chore and the cure in the same room (2), with creators paid per film.
- A dear tool that is thought over: trouble, price or great names on the page (3), the visitor already there; film at the top of that page.
- The last step, the sign-up: the smaller word, the minutes, the free days (4).
- A tool for a trade: its own hated screen (6). A tool with a beautiful screen: film it as an object (7).
