## What won in this trade, and why
1. **SOLD** — The proof or a sample stands in the buyer's own hand before anything is claimed. A buyer for a firm is paid to doubt; a thing he can test ends the doubt. First second: the sheet, the sample, the copy itself. Proven on paper, not in film (m-050).
   A test sheet for his own copier, "IQ TEST ENCLOSED": 138 percent more answers than the old mailing, and more of them bought (Pitney Bowes; o-caples-090). The advert is itself a copy made by the machine: sales double the estimate in two months (Repco Canon; o-sugarman-058). "We are manufacturers of Imported Kraft Stay similar to the enclosed sample": 10 permanent customers from 250 names, $4,135 in six months; the clever letter to a similar list brought one trial order (Manufacturers Paper; o-klasik-098).
2. **SOLD** — Three single cases of dear or dull goods, told by a person instead of a brochure. First second: one calm person, nothing boastful.
   One case: Van Damme, eyes closed, the camera slides back over two reversing trucks: campaign estimated at $170m revenue, sales +24% in the last quarter of 2013 (Volvo Trucks; a newspaper estimate, new trucks came out then too; o-satan-017).
   One case: "I have come from Chicago to consult you about a card": one man sold more in about a week than six salesmen in six weeks, because he asked the baker's advice (Swift and Company; o-hopkins-010).
   One case: a relaxed, humorous user in her own room for a dry company set-up service: 143% higher conversion than the brand film (advertiser not named by Google; o-platform-008).
3. **CLICKS** (answers) — For few and valuable readers, a letter that plainly cost somebody time, about the reader alone. He sees that a person sat down for him. First second: his own name, his own door, a hand's writing.
   An envelope with no address, only a drawn map to his door, to ten heads of post offices: eight of the ten replied (80%) (what3words; o-ad1-041). "Your name has found its way into a little book on my desk": 56 percent replies in two weeks from customers who had gone silent (a hardware seller; o-klasik-115). A "suppose with me" story: 71 percent replies (a glove maker; o-klasik-114). A yellow handwritten note on the envelope: 36.0% against 19.2% (Irish Revenue, to firms; o-davranis-014).
4. **CLICKS** (leads) — One trade's private trouble said aloud, in its own words. The reader knows at once that you know his day. First second: the trade's name and the sore point.
   "... any IT professional who has ever felt like telling an end user ...": 6X more leads than the old letter (a training firm; o-tec5-001). Trouble question, then the cure he had rejected: 11% answered (Envira-Plus filters; o-tec5-007). "Each cockroach upon your premises is there with your full permission": 17 percent returns from 5,000 hospitals (Murray's Roach Doom; o-klasik-113).
   One case in film: the trade's saying "herding cats", played dead serious by cowboys: 10 million visits to the site in the first week, five times the normal (EDS; o-ad6-010).

## What it depends on
- A machine, tool or process whose effect is seen at once: close, one take, phone is enough (7). The audience is the trade first, then everybody.
- A dear thing for few buyers who can be named: the letter that cost time (3) or the proof in the hand (1).
- A service nobody can see: the trade's own sore point (4); in film, the saying taken seriously (film 1) needs a crew and a big budget.
- An adviser or lawyer, urgent need, small budget: one exact question, filmed on the way to work (6).
- Names for later selling: the counted lesson or the plain price button (5). An unknown workshop: the hidden fault (8).
