## What won in this trade, and why
Roles: SOLD = a published sales result. CLICKS = answered, clicked, left a name. SEEN = strangers watched, unpaid. GREW THE ACCOUNT = later films drew twice the views. Most SOLD figures here are the owner's own, told to a newspaper.

1. **SOLD** — The surprising price over the full plate, or the price on a coupon with a last day. It is the whole offer in three words.
   Print, a bicycle in a coupon, "$68.88—Reg. $89.95 … Limit 1 per customer": all sold the first afternoon, over $14,000 from a $480 advert (o-caples-051).
   One case: the size taken at its word, a bucket of iced coffee carried straight at the lens till it fills the picture, "When I say I want a large iced americano": that film doubled, then tripled the shop's sales by the owner's count, 1,200,000 views, 419.9 times the usual (Cowgirl Barista; not for showing; o-sek5-009).
2. **SOLD** — The maker's own hands and one true reason. He is plainly no salesman, and the object is good to look at.
   First second: the maker and the thing in his hands. Sparta Candle Co., an old man with a soap with peach slices made in honour of his wife: 9 million views; in eight months of the series sales tripled (o-ad3-021). Hello Sweets, packing one customer's order without a word: an order every minute, the shop closed to restock (o-uzman-011).
3. **SOLD** — A judge who pays himself tastes it. Praise is not certain, so people wait for the first bite.
   First second: a calm face and bags still closed. Keith Lee at Southern Taste Seafood, "Today I spent $450 on the burger": 900 per cent more sales, the counter is not named (o-ad5-013). At Frankensons Pizzeria: sold out overnight, 2-3 hour waits for days (o-ad6-023). He has millions of followers; that did part of it.
4. **SOLD** — To customers and visitors you already have: an exact fact, a clock or the crowd. Only this shop could know it.
   First second: the line. "Your watch has not been here for cleaning for twenty-three months", a watch repairer's letter: business from over 50 percent of those written to (o-klasik-102). "Order in the next n hours for delivery today", daFlores: 27% more orders (o-abarsiv-166); its thanks to 600,000 followers: 44% more sales (o-abarsiv-167).
   Small paid advert, **CLICKS**: "Quality Shoe Repair 30 years of practice" 69% more clicks, $16.72 spent (o-chatgpt-006).
5. **SEEN** — The work at hand's distance, silent: one thing changes for good and the eye must see it finished.
   First second: white about to touch colour, a lace pulled out, a steel lid. Jim McFarland, a white wool ball into purple dye: 58.4 million (o-to8-009). Wristwatch Revival, golden pins pressed by a finger: 12 million (o-y5-010); a burnt Rolex: 7.5 million (o-y5-011). Denim and lace cleaners' press: 5.6 million, usual 12,600 (o-to8-013).
6. **SEEN**, two of them also **GREW THE ACCOUNT** — A task with a doubt: will it be done, where is the fault? The viewer takes a side.

   A barber, the order in quotation marks over a nearly bald head, "Give Me 10 Years Back": 19.2 million, 67.5 times the usual (o-sek5-010). The same barber, the customer says aloud what he hides, "I've got that bald spot on top": 7.6 million, 26.7 times (o-sek5-011).
   The barber, Grant Branham in a small town in Kentucky, told the radio that with such films he is booked for two months ahead and customers come from ten states; not for one film (o-sek5-010).

## What it depends on
- Cheap and eaten today: the price over the plate, finding 1, or a judge, finding 3. Dear and thought over: hands and choice, finding 7, or the maker, finding 2.
- Result seen at once (repair, cleaning, dye): finding 5. Result unseen or a matter of judgement (driving, flowers, washing), or seen on a person at the end (hair): finding 6.
- Old customers on a list: an exact fact about their own thing, finding 4; it costs a message.
- Unknown names won in findings 1, 2, 5 and 6, nearly all with a phone (o-to8-009).
## What is thin
- Sales figures are mostly the family's own, for the first days or weeks; newspapers printed them as told, and no test stands behind them.
- Grocers, pharmacies, clothes shops and bookshops hardly appear; barbers are one seller, watched, his bookings told by himself; many SOLD rows are small food places.
- Paid local films with a sales figure are missing; the paid ones here only show that the owner kept paying.
