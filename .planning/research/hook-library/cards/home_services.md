## What won in this trade, and why
1. One case: **SOLD** — An unknown worker says, to one group in one town, the true reason why she is the right one for them. The named people stop because they are named; the rest because they want to hear the reason.    Locksmith Girl of NYC: up to 60 jobs a week after this one film, her own count to CNBC; 640,600 views on an account with nine films (o-sek3-011). Phone only, urgent trade.
2. **SOLD** — Give first, then talk: the thing tried in his own house, or a small gift in the hand. He stops because nothing is asked of him. First second: the offer itself in one plain sentence, or a hand holding out samples to choose from.
   "Let us help you for a week without cost or obligation", a motor and a man to show it: about nine in ten trials led to sales (o-hopkins-028). "I was sent here to give you a brush": some 2,000 door sellers worked with it (o-hopkins-029). Old cases, told by the adman; for thought-over buying by an unknown seller.

## What it depends on
- Result seen at once (wash, sand, mow, clear a drain): 6 unpaid, 5 paid; Claeys' way joins them — the free winner becomes the ad.
- Result unseen or feared (locks, foundations, moving, wiring): 1, 3 and 8, Strand's comparison, the fear under the question.
- Urgent need: name the town and say what you are there for (1); one person, one phone.
- Dear and thought over: prices of both choices (5) or a trial at his house (2).
- Unknown name, phone in the hand: 1, 5, 6, 7 and 8 were all won that way. Finding 4 wants a product of one's own and a born seller.
