## What won in this trade, and why
1. **SOLD** — Somebody who is not the seller tries it on camera and could say no. A person stops to see the verdict; the flaw is shown before any praise, so the praise is believed.
   First second: a plain face or the pack held to the lens; a true flaw, or a second person whose face will judge. No studio, no music needed.
   Peter Thomas Roth, a customer of 54 with bags under her eyes: six months' normal sales in under a week, film 44.1m views from an account of about 70 followers (o-ad5-029, not for showing). Peace Out pore strips, a skin adviser watching the trial: nearly 10,000 units and $120,000 the day after (o-ad5-031). Glow Recipe, one wishes and another tests: daily sales up more than 600 per cent (o-ad5-032, not for showing).
   Fits a result seen now and a cheap price; the first customer was unpaid, the later judges had large followings of their own.
2. **SOLD** — A sight that does not belong to beauty: blood-red drops on a cheek, a leather bag on an operating table, a wholly white face. The eye cannot explain it, so it waits for the sense of it.
   First second: the odd thing is whole in the picture and already moving; the product is named only after the eye has asked.
   The Ordinary, red peeling drops: units on its website up 427 per cent, 52,000 in two weeks (o-ad5-030). Vaseline testing the public's own tricks in a joke hospital: whole campaign, e-commerce sales up 43 per cent (o-ad5-033). Skin Body, a face white with product: "hundreds of thousands of dollars" for each film, the seller's own word (o-uzman-014).
3. **SOLD** — An expert who does not say what the trade says. A police artist opens a soap film; a famous make-up woman asks why one would contour a nose at all.
   First second: the expert speaks to one real person's question or names a job nobody expects here.
   Jones Road, the founder answering one comment: revenue nearly 4x, followers 3,000 to 130,000 in a week (o-uzman-009). Dove sketches: $24m incremental sales in under five months, award-entry figure (o-satan-015). Fits considered purchases and a person with real standing.
4. **SOLD** — The maker says it himself, in his own room, and the offer is complete at once. An unknown seller becomes a person before he asks for anything.
   First second: the owner speaks to the lens at his desk or shows the thing working on the face; price or number of pieces stands early.
   Dollar Shave Club, "for a dollar a month": 12,000 orders in 48 hours (o-satan-037; a coarse word later, not for showing). Contour Cube: the first 100 units sold out by morning (o-uzman-012). Hair Club, the president who is also a client: revenue to $10m within three years (o-satan-057).
5. **SOLD** — Name the trouble exactly as the person feels it, without blame. "Let's face it, we're not all born with perfect skin" is kinder than "bad skin".
   First second: the small harm itself is seen (a stocking snags on a heel) or "we" admit it together.
   bareMinerals: net sales +57% a year 2002-2006 (o-satan-048). PedEgg: 45 million sold, the seller's number (o-satan-051). N organic, pollen on the skin: revenue more than 500% up on the year, whole campaign (o-platform-042).
   **CLICKS** — the same in a local paper, "Important news for women with flat or thinning hair": more than 1,200 readers a month cut out the coupon (o-tec5-005).
6. **KEPT PAYING** — The work is already half done in the first second, and the undone half stays beside it. The eye compares by itself; these ads ran long or spent most, no sales figure is published.
   First second: one loaded brush on one bare nail; one eye done, one bare; a doctor with five bottles, his hand going to the one for your complaint.
   DND gel polish: 1,147 days (o-meta1-001). An eyeliner tool: highest spend tier in 8 countries (o-tta1-005, not for showing). Remedy, a dermatologist sorting by complaint: 663 days (o-meta4-002).
7. **SEEN** — The barber's chair: a customer's own wish in quotation marks, an extreme "before", or a price nobody understands; the answer is only at the end. This is how a local chair is watched by millions; bookings are not published.
   First second: an ordinary man in the chair, the wish above him ("Give me 10 years back"), the hair already in the barber's hand; often no word is said.
   Thai Nguyen: 8,400,000 views (o-to6-007) and 10,800,000 views (o-to6-004). Mark Adame, "He paid me $350 for this haircut": 8,000,000 views, 153.6 times his middle film (o-to5-025). Beardbrand, an old man's wild white beard: 2,200,000 views, 209.5 times (o-y2-032).
8. **GREW THE ACCOUNT** — A series shape with a fixed cast: the next films drew at least twice the views. The viewer learns the game in one film and comes back for the next round.
   First second: two pictures one above the other (a famous beard, a mother judging it), or the brand's drawn figure in what looks like a normal post.
   Beardbrand, outsiders judge beards: the next 15 films x11.29 (o-to4-013). Dr. Squatch, the figure falls out of the post: x2.0 (o-to6-011). kush.kuts, black hair turned yellow: x2.49 (o-to6-002, not for showing).

## What it depends on
- Result seen at once (eye patch, polish, strip) → 1 and 6, one take, equal light on both halves, a phone is enough.
- Result later (cream, hair growth) → 3, 4 and 5: a person with standing, the maker, or the trouble named exactly; claims only as far as they are true.
- A barber or salon → 7: the customer's wish, his consent, the result last; for women's hair the same shape with an object or a covered head.
- Soap makers: One case: Royalty Soaps, the batch that turned ugly and was saved: 1,700,000 views, 32.7 times, watched, not sales (o-y1-026).
- A name everybody knows, with a crew → 2 and 3 (Vaseline, Dove); unknown and cheap → 1 and 4. The method behind 7: open with one real customer's sentence (m-022).
