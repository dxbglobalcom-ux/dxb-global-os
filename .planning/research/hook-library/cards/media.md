# News sites, magazines, newsletters, podcasts and channels — what won here (25 November 2026)
228 winners of this trade in the library: 156 brought clicks or names · 28 sold · 24 seen by strangers, unpaid · 21 kept paying for (a winner may hold two roles); 29 films on this machine, 17 may be shown to anybody.
## What won in this trade, and why
1. **SOLD** — To people who already half want the paper, the plain offer won: what you get, and how. They stop because it answers exactly the question in their head; no wit to work out. First second: the words alone, no picture.
   "How to get the Times delivered to your home": best puller of a number of ads tested, has been running for 14 years (The New York Times, book printed long ago; o-caples-077). "Get Free Unlimited Online Access to" (newspaper name): 181% more conversions (o-abarsiv-152). "Save 50% on home delivery": 24% more (o-abarsiv-141). The last two changed more than the line.
2. **SOLD** — To people who do not know the title, a story or one person speaking openly won: the reader wants to know how it ends, or who talks like this to him. First second: a plain letter, the first sentence is the opening, no picture.
   "On a beautiful late spring afternoon, twenty-five years ago, two young men graduated from the same college": mailed for 28 years, 1975 to 2003, about $2 billion by estimate (The Wall Street Journal; o-klasik-084). "I'm Robert Shnayerson, editor of QUEST/77 ... for closet optimists": 6% answered where 2% is typical, over 600,000 subscribers (o-klasik-088). "If the list upon which I found your name is any indication ...": won a five-way copy test, control for nearly 15 years (Newsweek; o-klasik-085).
3. **SOLD** — For busy people and money papers, one line with two numbers that fight: a big heap, a tiny effort. He sees the work he will be spared. First second: the two numbers, large.
   "Read 300 Business Magazines in 30 minutes" helped to sell 60,000 subscriptions at $36 a year (Boardroom Reports; o-ad1-022). "If you've got 20 minutes a month ..." won its split run by a huge margin and stayed for years (Charles Givens; o-schwartz-024). "32 Ways to Save Time & Money": the count itself was tested, unbeaten for 20 years (Good Housekeeping; o-tec5-010).
4. **SOLD** — In a letter or parcel, a small thing the hand must deal with: a coin, a riddle. One cannot throw it away unread. First second: the real thing shows before any word. Proven on paper, not in film (m-050).
   Two real pennies, "send back the other penny as a down payment": 100 million mailed; with his other campaigns circulation went from 4.5 million to over 12 million (Reader's Digest; o-schwartz-040). Nine dots on the envelope, "Can you connect all nine ...?": first time the old letter was beaten in years of testing (Esquire; o-schwartz-035).
5. **CLICKS** — For a news headline: one named person or thing, one odd act, and the end left out; the case, not the rule or the cause. The mind wants the missing half (m-062). First second: ten to fifteen plain words over the same picture.
   "Someone Gave A Guy A Megaphone ...": 3.82% clicked, rival lines over the same picture 0.83% (Upworthy; o-upw1-002). "Meghan and Harry are talking to Oprah. Here's why they shouldn't say too much.": 2.373% against 1.768% for a question with a pun (The Washington Post; o-bilimsoz-004). "Dismissal of Lynn teen's murder conviction upheld", the case instead of the rule: 60% more (Boston Globe; o-abarsiv-148).
6. **CLICKS** — The other headline shapes that won. All but the first two are one house (Upworthy, 2013 to 2015, random tests, same picture and story); read them as one case, tested many times. Figures: clicked, against its rival lines.
   What you believe is wrong: "It's Not Just Wrestling That's Fake. It's the World.": tenfold readers (The New York Times; o-chatgpt-018); "6 Big Things We've Been Led To Believe ...": 2.94% against 1.02% (o-upw1-017); "The Recycling Mistake Millions Of People Make Every Day": 1.89% against 0.66% (o-upw1-027).
   People first, the office later: "Health emergency declared in Boston after hundreds fall ill": 46% more (Boston Globe; o-abarsiv-149).
   An experiment broken off: "A Man Falls Down And Cries For Help, Twice. The Second Time, My Jaw Drops.": 6.0% against 3.4% (o-upw2-072); "2 People Described The Same Person To A Forensic Artist ...": 5.95% against 3.43% (o-upw2-068).
   A true count of the crowd: "... Video That 280,000 People Have Already Watched": 6.47% against 2.9%, the highest share of all (o-upw1-059).
   A tiny cause, a big loss: "One Letter On Her ID Is Making It Impossible": 5.37% against 2.01% (o-upw1-023).
   A small price in time: "This is the most important 20 seconds of ... TED Talk": 2.91% against 1.31% (o-upw2-035); "This Comic Will Get You There In 5 Minutes": 1.06% against 0.41% (o-upw1-060).
   The drawback said first: "I'm Not Going To 'Trick' You Into Watching This. It's Hard To Watch ...": 3.57% against 1.74% (o-upw2-052).
   Then and now: "The Before And After From These Google Images ...": 4.24% against 1.42% (o-upw1-013).
   Calling the reader by what he is or says: "Got Kids In School? You Must Read This.": 1.41% against 0.64% (o-upw2-040); "If Your Default Answer To 'How Are You?' Is 'Busy' ...": 1.43% against 0.68%, 9062 readers (o-upw2-013).
   A parts list that does not add up: "3 Sticks, A Nail, And a Bucket": 1.41% against 0.58%; the same line without the parts drew half (o-upw2-003).
7. **KEPT PAYING** — Serial stories are advertised from the peak of a scene, no lead-in: one face, one impossible sentence, and the viewer must wait for what follows. First second: the face already in the moment, the sentence on the screen.
   "Bring ten Rolls-Royces and park them in front of my house in ten minutes": highest spend tier, 41% still watching at 2 s, shown in 80 countries (DramaBox; o-ttb1-004). "Ah, this is so unfair!": highest spend tier, 39% at 2 s, 82 countries (ReelShort; not for showing; o-tta3-006). In the post, envelope lines were kept for three or more years: "5 Common Etiquette Mistakes even polite people make" (Bottom Line Personal; o-schwartz-047), "Aha! So that's how it's done!" (Artist's Magazine; o-schwartz-066).
8. **SEEN** — Channels: the first frame already holds the thing nobody has seen, and one plain sentence gives it a number or a gap. The eye is paid at once and one question stays open. First second: a big thing that moves, or a door that may stay shut.
   Three pumpkins of three years in resin: 23,200,000 views, 178.1 times the account's median (Evan and Katelyn; o-to8-021). A guarded woman is asked a question: 52,200,000 views, 14.3 times (School of Hard Knocks; o-to10-015). "Ship owning.": 21,000,000, 32.5 times (Noah Kagan; o-y5-025). "10 years of searching ... the correct pencil": 6,000,000, 125.0 times (Van Neistat; o-y6-001). "This company 3D printed an entire neighborhood.": 11,700,000, 294 times (Kallaway; o-tec1-001).

## What its own people say worked
- MrBeast (Jimmy Donaldson), films: pay the cover picture's promise in the first seconds; of 60 million who clicked one film 21 million left in the first minute, which he calls above average (x-tec3-001).
- Paddy Galloway, strategist for channels: say what the sceptic thinks over the silly picture; his film drew 6.3 million views, 13.9 times his median (x-tec3-057). A changed cover with the cursor on the thing: 40 times more views a day for Tim Gabe (x-tec3-072).

## What it depends on
- Readers who know the title: the plain offer (finding 1). Readers who do not: the story or the editor's own voice (finding 2).
- A subscription that saves time or money: the two numbers (3). A general or gift title by post: a thing in the hand (4).
- A free story that lives on clicks: the headline shapes (5 and 6), tested on the same picture; the story must hold what the line leaves out.
- Fiction and serials: the peak of a scene, with a crew (7). Channels and podcasts with a phone: the rare thing or the hard-to-reach person in the first frame (8).
- A small unknown author or host: one true line by somebody close to him (film 1) needs no budget.
## What is thin
- Most of the winners are clicks; almost all of those are one house, Upworthy, ten years old and more, so they prove a shape, not a sale. No newsletter sign-up film and no podcast advert with a sales figure is here.
- The sales figures are mostly letters and print, decades old; many "sold" rows are envelopes kept three years or more, told above as kept paying. Boxcar Willie's and the pennies' sums have more than one cause.
- Film 1 is a book, from the near trade; a news site's own showable film is missing.
