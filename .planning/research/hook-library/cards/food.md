# Food and drink: makers, restaurants, kitchens — what won here (25 November 2026)
135 winners of this trade in the library: 75 sold · 40 seen by strangers, unpaid · 13 kept paying for · 4 brought clicks or names · 3 remembered · 3 made the account grow (a winner may hold two roles); 58 films on this machine, 46 may be shown to anybody.
## What won in this trade, and why
1. **SOLD** — The food itself, very close, being cut, broken, spooned or poured, with few words or none. A person stops because the inside is hidden for a second and the hand is already opening it.
   First second: the whole thing fills the picture and a knife, spoon or bite is already moving; nobody greets, nobody explains; the sound is the real one. Each had one visible surprise: yellow heart, green filling, hot sauce, cream that holds a spoon.
   Kimchi no Ie, cabbage split by hand: sales more than 40-fold in two months, the family's first film (o-ad5-021). FIX chocolate, a bright green inside: single digits to 500 orders a day (o-ad5-018, not for showing). M&S pudding breaking open: +3,000% by its archive (o-satan-076). Straus milk, spoon in thick cream: half-gallon orders doubled (o-ad5-019).
   Same picture as a paid ad, one cookie slowly cracked by two thumbs: its owner kept it running 1,496 days; no sales figure (o-meta1-004, KEPT PAYING).
2. **SOLD** — On a menu, shelf card or label: name the taste and the place it comes from, not the virtue. People choose by the pleasure they expect; "healthy", "light" and "meat-free" name a lack.
   First second: the reader's eye meets the name only ("Herb n' Honey Balsamic Glazed Turnips"); no picture was needed. The cheapest win in this trade: no film, a printed card.
   Five universities, 137,842 diner decisions: +29% chosen against health names, +14% against plain names (o-davranis-061). "Cumberland-Spiced Veggie Sausage & Mash" at Sainsbury's cafes: +76% (o-davranis-056). "Cuban Black Bean Soup" at Panera: +13% (o-davranis-057).
3. **SOLD** — A stranger finds the small maker, and the maker only speaks for himself. Somebody else is on the maker's side before the viewer knows what is sold, so it does not feel like an advertisement.
   First second: the lonely stall, the unposed laugh, the buyer lacing her shoes to go there; the goods or the web address come into the same picture within about four seconds.
   Dawna's Jerky, an empty stall: more than 2,200 orders, production doubled (o-sek3-020). BonBon sweets, a customer's Friday walk: over 1,000 online orders on day one, before about 20 a day (o-sek3-021). Moore Crunch pretzels: three to four months' orders in three days (o-sek3-022). Cocoa Asante, answered by a food critic: sold out in 45 minutes (o-ad5-012).
4. **SOLD** — The seller says the uncomfortable thing himself: the insult, the rule he breaks, that he does not belong here. It is the one sentence an advertiser would hide, so the rest is believed.
   First second: the customer's insult marked on a screen, or a plain man's first sentence ("I'm a farmer"). Best for a product that really was improved or a maker nobody knows.
   Domino's reading out "pizza was cardboard": same-store sales +14.3% in one quarter; the recipe had really changed and an offer ran (o-satan-105). Frank Schultz, grapefruit by post: 80,000 customers by 1980 (o-sugarman-024). Harry and David, "Imagine Harry and Me advertising our PEARS in Fortune!": 87,000 boxes within 4 years (o-ad1-001).
5. **SOLD** — For a name everybody knows: a form the viewer recognises at once, played straight, with one thing wrong in it. He joins the story before he notices the seller; the joke carries the product.
   First second: a rescue van, a cereal factory belt, an old lady in a rough game: ordinary, and one thing cannot be right. These are films with crews and television money behind them.
   Marmite as an animal-rescue programme: value sales +14.1% in eight weeks (o-ad4-001). Shreddies turned 45 degrees and sold as "Diamond": +18% (o-ad4-002). Snickers: global sales +15.9% in one year (o-satan-012). Hellmann's talking cat: +12.7% dollar sales (o-ad4-007). Wendy's "Where's the beef?": revenue +31% (o-satan-059).
6. **SOLD** — Where the person is already choosing: what the others really chose, or one plain price. No wit is needed there; he wants to decide quickly and safely.
   First second: five dish names with "most popular", or the offer whole in one line with its price. It costs a card, a coupon or a still picture.
   A table card with last week's five best sellers: demand for them +13 to 18% (o-davranis-053). "$1 off any large pizza": over 1,200 returns, over $9,600 of sales (o-caples-056). Coop Extra bread, one price on one picture: more than 10% extra bread sales (o-platform-020).
7. **SEEN** — The workshop is the show: a big, strange, shining material and the hand that masters it. Nobody has this at home, so the eye asks what it is; children, a father or a daughter in the picture make people send it on.
   First second: the material already lies there, larger than expected, and a tool or hand touches it; the voice, if any, says a true fact or admits a mistake.
   Logan's Candies: 47,000,000 views, 717.6 times its own middle film (o-y2-020). Sticky, father against daughter: 252.8 times (o-to7-028). Insider in a brie dairy: 351.4 times (o-to8-014). Savsgotcake, a cake green on one half only: 20,200,000 views (o-to8-001). An unknown beekeeper turning a key: 1,900,000 views, 2,159.1 times (o-to7-017).
8. **GREW THE ACCOUNT** — One order too big for the kitchen, or one tin torn open: after it the next films drew at least twice the views. A number is made visible, and one stays to see if it can be done.
   First second: the first cans are being stacked until the woman disappears, or the closed golden crust fills the frame. For small makers who want regular viewers, not one lucky day.
   Marion, 300 canned lattes in two days: the next films x2.64 (o-to7-024). Allison Chen, the biggest cake she ever made: x2.82 (o-to8-006). Pookie Bakery, a spoon into the crust: x2.45 (o-to7-023).

## What it depends on
- Something happens when it is cut, poured or bitten → finding 1, a phone is enough; order page ready the same night. Nothing to see (tins, powders, plain bread) → findings 2, 4 or 6.
- A restaurant or canteen with a menu and no advertising money → 2 and 6 first: they were tested with real eaters and cost a printed card.
- A maker nobody knows, at a stall or by post → 3 and 8; a maker with a window and a big moving stage → 7. Gifts for few rich buyers → the thing itself in the post: 15 letters with boxes of pears brought orders for 489 boxes (o-ad1-002).
- A name everybody knows, with a crew and a budget → 5. The jokes lean on that name; an unknown seller has no such proof here.
- Dear, made-to-order food (cakes, catering) → 7 and 8: the customer's wish and the risky step; watched widely, sales not published.
- The methods behind: the real workroom with the real person (m-025), the taste in the name (m-064), yours beside the famous one with both prices (m-113), one real customer's sentence (m-022).
