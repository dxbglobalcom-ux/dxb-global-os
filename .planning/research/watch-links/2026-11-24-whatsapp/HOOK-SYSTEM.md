## 1. What a hook is, and the first three seconds

A hook is not one sentence. In the first seconds **four channels fire at once**, and each is a hook of its own:

| Channel | What it is | The rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | the first frame and the first motion | "the most compelling visual possible with enough motion to hold their attention … too much motion and you overwhelm them, too little and you bore them" | [LINK dm-024 Kallaway 09:31–11:30] |
| **On-screen text** | 3–6 words, big | read in about one second, before the spoken line is heard; "six words or fewer"; it is the blunter twin of the spoken line, not a copy | [LINK dm-024 10:00] · [RESEARCHED: github.com/Jakeschincariol/instagram-agent-skill `skills/ig-reel/hooks.json` note and rules] |
| **Spoken** | the first sentence | about two seconds; "the video starts at the sentence you would normally reach at second six" | [RESEARCHED: same `hooks.json` rules] |
| **Caption** | the post text | Instagram shows about 125 characters before "… more" | [RESEARCHED: same repo, `/ig-caption` description] |

"None of those hooks should be the same." [LINK cc-008 Jen Gottlieb 00:20–00:25]. "Spoken word + motion + text on
screen … that combo is way more powerful than just the spoken word." [LINK dm-024 11:20]

**Why the first seconds carry the weight — the platforms' own numbers:**
- TikTok: "over 63% of all videos with the highest click-through rate (CTR) highlight their key message or product
  within the first 3 seconds"; vertical 9:16 videos have "an average 25% higher 6-second watch-through rate";
  "33% [of] auction ads with the highest VTR break the 4th wall"; "40% of auction ads with the highest VTR" use text
  overlays; tracks above 120 BPM "often drive higher view-through rate". [RESEARCHED: ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf — undated TikTok PDF, read 2026-11-24]
- Meta (2015 Facebook–Nielsen study): people who saw a video ad for three seconds or less still showed lift — 47% of
  the ad-recall lift arrived in the first three seconds on mobile. Old (2015), measured on Facebook feed video, not Reels.
  [RESEARCHED: techcrunch.com/2016/02/18/facebook-video-metrics; martech.org/even-brief-video-views-drive-brand-lift-facebook-nielsen-study-finds]
- Google/Kantar (April 2021): YouTube ads that follow the ABCDs (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction) are
  credited with "up to 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and 17% lift in long-term brand contribution" —
  Google's own framework validated by a study Google commissioned, 11,000 ads scored by Kantar's AI.
  [RESEARCHED: support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551; kantar.com case study]
- Short-form "speed to value": the viewer's patience is "about four seconds" on short form, "one to two minutes" on
  YouTube long form. [LINK dm-024 13:49–15:02 — Kallaway's experience, not a measurement]
- ⚠ UNVERIFIED: TikTok Marketing Science reportedly finds 50% of an ad's recall impact in the first 2 seconds and
  90% in the first 6 — seen only in search summaries of TikTok's Creative Center; the page was not opened.

## 2. The hook families — one library, every source merged, duplicates folded

Organised by **what the hook is for**. Where two sources teach the same shape, they share one row and both ids are
named. "Trap" is how the shape gets ruined.

### 2.1 The spoken formula that every family can use — Kallaway's three steps [LINK dm-024]

1. **Context lean** — in the first line or two: topic clarity (the viewer self-selects in or out) and a reason to lean
   in: common ground, a benefit or pain, a metaphor, or a fact so interesting it surprises.
2. **Scroll-stop interjection** — one line that stops the lean, built on a contrast word: *but, however, yet,
   although, on the other hand*.
3. **Contrarian snapback** — on topic, in the opposite direction of the lean; "the bigger the shock, the bigger the
   snap".

Worked example (his, 8M views — his figure): "The tech in the Vegas Sphere is insane. Biggest screen ever built,
20 times bigger than an IMAX. **But** get this, the screen is actually the least impressive part. **Because** the most
impressive part is the audio." General template: *"If you want X, don't do Y, do Z"* — and Z must really be worth it,
"or people will just churn and you're a fraud". Five tactics on top: visual hook; lead with benefit or pain
("if you want better sleep, you need to be taking magnesium"); **cult hopping** — wrap the unknown in a known brand,
person or movement; first value within ~4 s; staccato sentences up front, longer later.

### 2.2 The visual first-second hook — one family among others (proof: `.planning/research/hook-library/README.md`) [LINK ha-001] [LINK dm-011]

The reel opens on an event already in motion and **cuts before it resolves**; the brain "needs to see how it ends".
Erika's types, each with her one-line reason (transcripts v01–v12):

| Type | Mechanism (her words) | Ad use |
|---|---|---|
| Anticipation | "Your brain needs to see how it ends, so you physically can't scroll away." | the product is what lands |
| Danger | "One wrong move and it's over, so you can't look away until she's safe." | safety product, insurance-free reassurance (no interest products) |
| Impossible / illusion | "something that shouldn't be possible, so it stops to figure out how" | product capability |
| Fake-out | "She holds it like a baby kitten… and it's an ad" | the reveal is the brand ("ADVERTISE YOUR BUSINESS HERE") |
| Plot twist / backfire | "You expect a goal, but the ball comes flying straight at you" | expectation reversal → product |
| Fail / crash | "showing off with your brand on his back so everyone watches the crash and sees the brand" | brand on the person |
| Trick shot | "She aims straight at the camera and you have to see where it lands" | brand sign at the landing point |
| Stitch | "borrows the attention of something people already love and hands it to your business" | match cut into the product |
| Visual oddity | "so strange your eyes have to stop" | packaging, signage |
| Also named in her captions | near-miss, POV, shock, cliffhanger ("works twice"), contrast, escalation, dramatic irony, cocky, domino, commitment, pattern interrupt ("cuts you off mid-sentence") | — |

Tyler Tometich's **effect hooks** belong here too: fake cliff, jump through the screen, hyper speed, clone, an object
appearing — "a pattern interrupt to stop people mid-scroll"; his own attention claims (+5 to +15 s each) are unmeasured.
[LINK dm-011 00:00–00:24, 04:00]

**How such a clip is written** — the public prompt pattern of her 77 cards [LINK ha-001; `r2-work/fvh/hooks.md`]:
format (vertical 9:16, "looks like real phone footage: slight shake, mild compression, natural colours") · shooting
style · subject · a **second-by-second timeline** (e.g. 0.0–1.2 / 1.2–2.4 / 2.4–4.0, one continuous take) · sound
(crowd murmur, a creak, gasps "cutting off abruptly with the video") · constraints: no slow motion, **the clip ends
before the impact**, no one is hurt, no on-screen text or logos inside the generated clip (text is added in the edit).
Realism vocabulary to add: real camera-and-lens pairs and FACS expression codes [LINK dm-044] (§3.4).

**Three rules the creators do not say** — sourced:
1. **Remake it on our own engine, as she does.** Her clips are themselves AI remakes of real viral videos; inspiration
   and rephrasing are free <!-- CEO-OK: copying-ban-removed-inspiration-and-rephrase-free-2026-11-25 -->. The
   practical reason to remake rather than repost: Instagram stops recommending accounts that
   mainly repost what they did not make or meaningfully transform — Reels since April 2024, extended platform-wide in
   2026 as reported. [RESEARCHED: techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators; petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content]
2. **Bridge within 1–2 seconds.** A hook unrelated to the product buys the wrong attention: "you made a trailer, not
   an ad … eyeballs, not buyers". [HELD: expert-library/mei-man/PLAYBOOK.md §9.1] The bridge line names the viewer's
   problem the moment the visual resolves ("…and that's what a slow website does to your customers").
3. **Judge it by cost per result, not by hook rate alone** (§5).

### 2.3 The stack — four hooks at once, then the loop [LINK cc-008] [LINK cc-015] [HELD: matt-diamante/b PLAYBOOK §10.3]

HOTTI: **H**ook (visual + spoken + on-screen + caption, all different) → **O**pen loop (one sentence that makes them
stay to the end) → **T**rust tag (who you are and why to listen, with a real number) → **T**akeaway (the value) →

## 3. What comes after the hook — so the hook's promise is paid

### 3.1 The short-video skeleton [LINK cc-015, completed]

| Value 2 | 14–20 | the second hit | the step that builds on value 1 |
| Open loop 2 | 20–22 | run it twice | "But the one that changed everything was the third." |
| Value 3 | 22–27 | the payoff the hook promised | the strongest point — the retention prompt of dm-035 (prompt 7) puts the most interesting material late, around 70% |
| CTA | 27–30 | move the warmest viewers | one ask, tied to a concrete free asset or a saved/sent action |

Retention technique for long narration (8-min faceless script): a 15-second hook that raises a question, a small
payoff inside 90 seconds, a fresh open loop about every 90 seconds, every loop closed by the end, the most
interesting material near 70%, the ending lands on the hook's answer. [LINK dm-035, prompt 7, read verbatim]

## 5. Testing and measuring — what judges a hook, and how many views it takes

### 5.1 What each platform reports for the first seconds

| Platform | The hook's own number | Definition and source |
|---|---|---|
| Meta ads (FB/IG) | **hook rate** = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions; **hold** = ThruPlays ÷ impressions (or 15-s plays ÷ 3-s plays) | [HELD: mei-man §9.1 — she never defined hold; the trade's two forms]; ThruPlay = 15 s or completion, with a 6-second option reported in 2026 ⚠ (ppc.land, not Meta's page) |
| TikTok ads | 2-second and 6-second view rates, VTR, CTR | TikTok's own PDF speaks of "6-second watch-through rate" and VTR [RESEARCHED PDF above] |
| YouTube Shorts | **Viewed vs. swiped away** — "the percentage of times viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away" | [RESEARCHED: support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281]; YouTube publishes no benchmark; third-party "75–80%" rules of thumb are not used here |
| YouTube long form | CTR of the thumbnail, first-30-second retention; the native A/B test of up to three titles/thumbnails **picks the winner by watch time, not CTR**, and is not available for Shorts | [RESEARCHED: support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400] |
| Instagram (organic) | watch time, likes per reach, **sends per reach** (sends weigh more for non-followers, likes for followers) — Mosseri, early 2025, as reported | [RESEARCHED: socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034 — secondary] |
| Instagram (testing) | **Trial reels** go to non-followers first; the creator chooses whether followers see it | [RESEARCHED: about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first, Dec 2024] |
| X | video views; X advises movement or a person in the first seconds, the brand within the first three seconds, about 15 seconds | ⚠ UNVERIFIED at source — business.x.com refused us (HTTP 403); seen in search summaries only |
| Web hero | opt-in rate of qualified leads; scroll depth; session recordings (Clarity free) | [LINK dm-021], [LINK dm-034 "watch sessions… 9/10"] |

**The rule over all of them:** a hook metric diagnoses; it does not decide. "Some of the best-performing ads had
worse metrics than the losers … you can build an elite hook-and-hold-rate machine that prints zero money."
[HELD: mei-man §9.1, 2026] Dara Denney's report prompt says the same in one line: a high-hook/low-ROAS creative "is
buying attention, not customers"; a low-hook/high-ROAS one "has upside in its first 3 seconds". [LINK dm-022]

### 5.2 Decision rules

- **Ads:** judge on cost per result in the client's own account; hook rate is read beside it, never alone. A
  creative under the significance spend (Dara's $500 in 7 days, "adjust proportionally") is "directional only — never
  called a winner or loser"; variants are judged as one concept (blended). [LINK dm-022] Meta's learning phase and
  significant-edit rules govern any change [HELD: register #71 `ad-change-guard`].
- **Ad platform tests:** TikTok recommends split tests of at least 7 days and a budget giving at least 80%
  "estimated testing power"; the two groups must differ clearly. [RESEARCHED: ads.tiktok.com/help/article/split-test-best-practices]
  Meta's own A/B guidance is reported at 7–30 days ⚠ (third-party pages; Meta's page not reached).
- **Organic:** B28's loop already rules: judged after 1,000 views and 48 hours; most clicks per 1,000 views wins by
  20% or more, otherwise the opening's retention decides; the weighted score (7-day view velocity 30% · retention 30% ·
  shares + saves 20% · conversion 20%) compared within one platform and one length band. [board B28, 2026-11-07 and
  2026-11-18 legs] Parker's prompt adds: one test a day, one variable, no verdict under 24 hours. [LINK cc-013]
- **Pre-publish scores are a lint, not a judge.** The open pack's `hookscore.py` (length, specificity, stakes,
  front-loading, address; dealbreakers: "stop scrolling", greetings, "in this video", hashtags or emoji in the hook)
  states its own test: it separated real hooks from deliberately bad ones (AUC 0.83) but a creator's hits from the
  same creator's misses barely at all (AUC 0.56) — the author's test on 74 hooks. [RESEARCHED: repo `hookscore.py`
  header] The TRIBE v2 and Virality-Predictor-style scores on B28 are trials against known results, not gates.

### 5.3 How many views a comparison needs — computed, not guessed

Two-proportion test, 95% confidence, 80% power, per variant (standard formula; computed 2026-11-24) [INFERENCE — arithmetic]:

| Comparing | Needs per variant |
|---|---|
| hook rate 25% vs 30% | ~1,250 impressions |
| hook rate 25% vs 27.5% | ~4,900 impressions |
| viewed-vs-swiped 60% vs 66% | ~1,000 views |
| viewed-vs-swiped 60% vs 65% | ~1,500 views |
| CTR 1.0% vs 1.5% | ~7,750 impressions |
| CTR 1.0% vs 1.2% | ~42,700 impressions |
| purchase rate per click 2% vs 3% | ~3,800 clicks |
| email open 20% vs 25% | ~1,100 sends |
| DM/email reply 3% vs 4.5% | ~2,500 sends |

What this means: B28's "judge after 1,000 views" can see a difference of about **six percentage points** in a
viewed-vs-swiped or hook rate near 30–60% — not less. Smaller differences need more views or are read as "too few to
say" (the register's `ad-winner-read` already carries an exact Poisson interval and "too few to say" while intervals
overlap [HELD: SKILLS.md #30]). Conversion differences need thousands of clicks: on a small budget a hook is chosen
by the attention metric, and the buying metric confirms it later.
