## 1. Ad creatives built to sell — the hook factory as a service

**One client's story** [INFERENCE — illustration]. A family producer of cold-pressed olive oil sells online in Germany and
the Gulf. Its ads list benefits — "pure, healthy, natural" — the 1/10 ad of Chase Chappell's scale [LINK dm-003]. The
holding's ad office takes the brief, mines the buyers' own words from reviews into a quote bank [HELD: register #27],
writes a belief brief [#28], and on Wednesday runs a hook session: fifty hooks, forty remade from proven shapes, ten new
[HELD: Hormozi]. The studio makes the four launch ads [LINK dm-014] — an explainer ("Tried supermarket 'extra virgin'
that tastes of nothing? …"), a before/after of the same salad, a real seasonal offer, and an us-vs-them table with true
data — each opening on a first-second visual hook made on our own engine with the brand at the payoff [LINK ha-001].
On Monday the account's own numbers pick the winner; ten one-variable variants of it go out the next week.

## 2. The creator army — a new service for e-commerce brands (no row owns it)

**One client's story** [INFERENCE — illustration on Chase Chappell's method, LINK dm-001, dm-002]. A small brand of
natural toothpaste wants to grow on TikTok without paying one influencer $10,000 and hoping. The holding's social-media
seat finds creators already posting in oral care and wellness, sends invitations at scale, qualifies them before
(audience match, engagement, content quality, niche fit) and after (sales, CTR, ROAS, GMV), and enrols the best in the
brand's TikTok Shop affiliate program: free product, a commission (10–15% on Chase's screen), a one-page creator brief
in the Bloom shape — customer, problem, mechanism, benefits, objections, hooks, proof ("Not a script. This is your
guide") — and a weekly call showing which videos are winning. TikTok Shop becomes the test bench: which hooks, angles,
objections and offers convert. The winners' usage rights are bought (a contract), the creators whitelisted, and the
winning videos run as Meta ads; the website catches the high-intent buyers, Google the brand searches, Amazon the
overflow. Bloom's scale, from Chase's screen: 71.6k affiliates, 195.6k shoppable videos, $31.5m TikTok Shop GMV (an
analytics panel he does not name — unverified).

## 3. The ad-account agent — run every week by an agent, launched only on approval

**One client's story** [LINK dm-038 — the creator's own account; INFERENCE for the client]. A furniture shop with two
Meta campaigns spends about $200 a month and does not know whether it earns. The holding's paid-media seat connects the
shop's ad account to an agent through **Meta's official Ads MCP server** (`https://mcp.facebook.com/ads`: measured
2026-11-24 — it answers HTTP 401 with an OAuth challenge, scopes including `ads_read`, `ads_management`,
`business_management`, `pages_manage_ads`; Meta's docs list seven tool groups — reporting, ad creation and management,
catalog, signals, help, A/B tests and lift studies, activity logs [RESEARCHED:
developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/ads-ai-connectors/ads-mcp-server/ads-mcp-server-overview]).
Before it says anything, the agent asks what İsa Nurdoğdu's skill asks: what the shop sells, the price, the profit
margin per sale ("without this no ROAS can be read"), how many leads become customers, the monthly budget. Then it
reads: "2 campaigns, 30 days, $194 spent, 109 appointments — but an appointment is not a sale" (his account's figures).
Each Monday it writes Dara Denney's report: where the money went, the top creatives at meaningful spend, hook rate
against ROAS, spend drifting into under-performers [LINK dm-022]. It drafts the next campaign **paused**; the client
approves; only then does it go live. Google Ads, which has no such connector, is driven through the browser, which works
only while the browser is open (his own words) [LINK dm-038].

## 4. Repairing the page the ads land on

**One client's story** [INFERENCE on LINK dm-005, dm-026, dm-009]. A dog-food brand's ads work, but most clicks leave
the product page. The web office reads the leak in order [HELD: mei-man funnel-leak read — "one audit sells both
offices"] and finds what Chase Chappell showed on Smooche: no price anchor, no guarantee near the button, reviews on a
separate page, no answer to "is it safe for my dog?". It rebuilds the page as a "decision engine" — every block answers
one question (what is the problem, is this normal, does it work, is it safe, is it for me) [LINK dm-026] — puts the
reviews next to the add-to-cart button and the shipping/returns line above the fold (Jessie Hackett's A-tier fixes,
her opinion [LINK dm-033]), and adds a three-step "dog health check" quiz that recommends a product and saves the
plan to an email (Maev's pattern [LINK dm-009]). Clarity session recordings show where people still drop
[LINK dm-034]. The landing pages for lead offers follow Hormozi's ten rules [HELD; LINK dm-021].

## 5. The business-level report — CAC, MER, LTV

**One client's story** [LINK dm-007, dm-008]. A breakfast-cereal brand cuts its Meta budget because Meta shows the lowest
ROAS (1x on Chase's illustrative table) — and total sales fall, because Meta was creating the demand that Google and
Amazon captured. The holding's report would have read the whole business instead: **blended CAC** = total marketing
spend ÷ total new customers; **new-customer CAC** = paid spend ÷ new customers from paid; **MER** = total revenue ÷
total marketing spend; **contribution margin** = revenue − variable costs; **incremental revenue** = revenue with
marketing − revenue without; and **LTV** against CAC ($60 CAC → $240 LTV is "a very different business" from the same
$60 with no repeat purchase). Adi Elena's ratio [LINK dm-010]: 1:1 loses money; her "3:1 breaking even at best" is
stricter than the common 3:1 "healthy" rule because she counts costs CAC leaves out; her LTV is revenue-based — the
holding's report uses gross-margin LTV [INFERENCE: standard practice].

|---|---|
| **Gain** | the Monday report is worth paying for when it answers "what happens to the whole business when I put another dollar into marketing?"; the same formulas run the holding's own revenue engine (realised net, G5) |
