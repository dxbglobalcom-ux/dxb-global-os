## 5. The build project

| # | Project | What is built | Owning spec (adaptation — no new spec) | Closes when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P07-1** | **The CEO's fifteen minutes** (CAP-07-D) | Write down what the CEO's day with the OS *is*: what he sees on waking, what he is asked to decide, what he never has to touch. Then make the surfaces obey it. This is not documentation for its own sake — the CEO's own complaint is that he cannot tell what the system wants from him. | `CEO_COMMAND_CENTER_SPEC` | A written daily rhythm exists, and the morning surface matches it on a real day. |
| **P07-2** | **The brief ends in an action, not a report** (CAP-07-C) | The morning briefing finishes with the one or two things awaiting his word — the approvals queue, the decision that is blocking work — so that reading it *is* running the company. | `CEO_COMMAND_CENTER_SPEC` + U37 adaptation | The briefing's last block is a real pending decision with a one-tap path to acting on it. |
| **P07-3** | **Four levers** (CAP-07-E) | One control surface holding the small number of settings that actually reconfigure the holding — which model tier, which budget, which departments are awake, which objective is live — instead of four separate pages. | `CEO_COMMAND_CENTER_SPEC` | Changing one lever visibly changes company behaviour, and the change is in `audit_log` with the CEO as actor. |

**Not bought, and not needed.** The $97 kit is a starter repository, prompt libraries and a
playbook for six agents. We have 199 written personas, a governed spec corpus and a live
database. Buying it would be paying for a smaller copy of what already exists here.

## 6. Verdict

**`daha iyisi` on substance — `geride` on one thing we keep rediscovering: the CEO's day is
undefined.**

- **We are far past their product.** Six agents against 199; a diagram of authority against an
  enforced approvals gate; a prompt library against a persona corpus.
- **But they answer a question we have not.** *"What does running this thing look like on a
  Tuesday morning?"* They answer in one line — fifteen minutes, four levers, one brief that
  ends in review-approve-send. DXB has no such answer, and that is precisely the hole the CEO
  keeps falling into: a company that does not tell him what it wants from him feels like a
  company that is not working.
- **Worth telling him plainly:** somebody is selling, for $97 rising to $497, a smaller version
  of what he already owns. The gap is not capability. It is that theirs is *legible* on day one
  and ours is not yet.
