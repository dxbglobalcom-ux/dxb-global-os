## 3. Capabilities — the mechanism 35 adds

**The runtime is three named parts, and the film points at each one.** A **conductor** — Claude Code
run headless so his subscription is used instead of the API, carrying `claude-fable-5` — takes what
the owner pastes in and delegates. **paperclip** (our source 08) is the app the delegation travels
through: it holds the tasks, the roster, the dashboard and the audit trail, and it is reachable at
`larps-mac-mini.tail090dce.ts.net:3100`. **Hermes** is the action layer between intent and execution,
running `glm-5.2`, carrying **9 tools and 78 skills in 35 categories**, with a gateway that reports
`Running`, a session count, and its own `Restart Gateway` / `Update Hermes` buttons. Above all three
sits **G-Brain**, *"markdown + pgvector knowledge store"*, which every agent reads so, in his words,
*"none of the agents hallucinate."*

**The model is a property of the department, printed where the department lives.** Marketing runs
`claude-opus-4-6`, Finances `claude-fable-5`, Communications `claude-haiku-4-6`, TECH `gpt-5.4` on
Codex — and TECH is the one showing `ERROR`. The roster screen puts model, last activity and state on
one line for all ten. **Six model tiers, two vendors, one page, no configuration file.**

**The brain is walkable, and it is the same picture at every depth.** One input takes everything —
`text · voice · drag or upload`. Two views, `Radial` and `Neural`. From the whole company you step
into a department and get the same grammar four layers deep: **tool → worker → work item →
department**, with the dotted edges of the work converging on the department node. Human names sit in
the worker layer beside the agents (`Mia Torres` in Communications, `Dana Whitfield` in Finances), so
the map is of the COMPANY and not of the software.

**Every capability is a card with a rung on a ladder.** `FULLY AUTONOMOUS` with a toggle, the rule it
enforces, the sub-skills it breaks into, what it builds on, **what it replaces in human terms**, and
the ladder from `HUMAN-LED` through `HUMAN-ASSISTED`. Autonomy is presented as a position that moves,
not a switch that is on.

**And the machine argues its own case in the owner's units.** Three asks ordered *"by payoff",* each
with the number it moves — API keys `11 → 20 of 22`, brew installs `2/9 → ~5/9` — the two loose ends
he has not ruled on, and the sentence that decides whether a system can be left alone: *"I haven't
touched your machine without asking."* It reports `972/973 pass`, that **nothing is committed**, that
**none of it is live yet**, and that **neither service survives a reboot**. It closes by asking what
he wants to pick up.

### Aliveness — how 35 shows this system is built to live

**1. What runs on its own clock, and by what machinery.** A **daily persisted issue monitor** with its
next wake printed on the board — *"next 2026-08-06 ~20:48 UTC"* — created by the conductor itself
after it diagnosed a recovery loop. A **watchdog review** (`BEN-26`) that closed clean without being
asked. The **CRON** rail inside the Hermes console, and a `CRON JOBS 0` tile that says plainly how
many are set. **Environment leases** taken and released around agent runs. The whole thing on a Mac
mini behind Tailscale, so *"I can go anywhere in the world and it's always gonna be running."* And
against all of it, the machine's own admission that **neither service survives a reboot yet** —
LaunchAgents with auto-login off. The clock runs; the clock is not yet armed.

**2. What makes the surface breathe, and the screen's own movement, MEASURED.** The film is handheld,
so the screen was measured against a static control region using the queue's own instrument
(`scripts/rival-intel/motion.py 35 --screen 100,300,1000,1300 --control 100,60,900,240`). Of 71
consecutive frame-pairs the camera held still on only **5** — `t013, t028, t038, t056, t069`. On those
five pairs: **screen 22.645 against a control of 1.970 — a ratio of 11.5×**, with a per-pair range of
**7.365 … 40.085**. **The surface never rests while the camera does.** The sample is small and is
declared: five pairs, not seventy-one.

The timed figures the screen prints about itself are firmer than any pixel measurement, and they are
all elapsed-work counters rather than decoration: **`Working for 9 seconds · called 2 tools`**,
`startup step: workspace.resolve (0ms)`, **`worked for 2 minutes`**, **`Worked for 12s`**,
**`Baked for 10m 19s`**, `Compacting conversation… (31s)`, a connector probe timeout set to **2.5 s**
so *"a slow vendor degrades to an honest error instead of hanging the whole page"*, and a Hermes
status line carrying **`4s`** as its live latency. **Time on this surface always means work, never
animation.**

**3. How it answers the human.** Three doors, all of them a sentence rather than a form: the conductor
box on the hierarchy screen (`Chat with Conductor — reaches …`), the CEO line on the runtime page
(`Message the CEO — it can delegate … pull real data`), and G-Brain's single input that takes text,
voice, a drag or an upload. Hermes adds a fourth from the terminal side, with `voice off` shown as a
state that can be turned on. **A latency figure for the answer is not published in this film —
UNREADABLE from the source**; what is published is the elapsed time of the WORK, which is the number
this operator chose to show.

**4. What DXB takes.** In §5, and only what 30 and 33 did not already hand over.
