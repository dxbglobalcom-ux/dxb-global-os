## 1. The one paragraph

Taken together, the rivals show a living AI-run company to be five things at once. **A loop that closes without the
owner inside it:** one sentence goes in, owned work comes out, and the machine reports back first — what it did,
what it repaired, what it refused to do without him, and the one decision it needs (01, 17, 18, 20, 23, 35).
**A gate that is an object:** the act that runs is the text he signed, once (08 R; 10, 18 V). **A company written
down:** every procedure a card that says what it does, what human act it replaces and how much of it the machine may
do alone (30, 33, 35). **A surface that is attached to the machine, not opened to ask it:** the working part lit and
named, the resting part at rest, every link flowing while it is up, the screen moving to what is being said, and the
machine saying when it last knew (10, 12, 14, 22, 24, 29, 32, 33). **And a memory the workers share** (11, 29, 34,
35). What DXB takes is less than that list, because most of it is already his: the loop, the gate, the desk, the
single engine and the money records are in PLAN; the studios, the revenue lines, the voice and the customer desk are
on the board, in rows that already carry what the rivals show of them (section 2, "SAME"). What the rivals add is
narrow and exact: **nine things the chassis must carry that PLAN does not say** (section 3) — above all the contract for installing
an application, a true signal of life for every part at rest, and the surface following the talk — and **a list of
upgrades to rows that already exist** (section 2, "BETTER IN THE RIVAL"). Nothing in the queue asks for a second
engine, a second store or a second gate.

## 3. What this asks of the foundation

PLAN §9 risk 3 says the design is read against this synthesis and what changes comes back to him. This is that list.
Each item: the change · the section it touches · the reason · what happens without it. None of it is designed here.

**F0 — The contract for installing an application is stated once.** *Touches:* §2 (a new short part, or the head of
§2 D); today its pieces lie in §2 A, B, C, D, G, H, I, J, §3 and §4 and in three specs and one row file outside the plan. *Reason:* his
picture of 2026-11-22 rests on it, and every rival that grows does it by a registered card, not by engine work — the
nine-field procedure card and the company preset (30 T, V), the skill row (14 V), versioned and gated skills (08 R),
pipeline manifests with stage gates (OpenMontage, (R)), replaceable tasks (34 (R)). *Without it:* the Web Studio and the
advertising job types are each installed by their own adaptation of the orchestration spec and their own changes to
the foundation's files — a patch per application. The gaps are named in section 4.

**F1 — A liveness row for every resident part and every link.** *Touches:* §2 E (its third bullet), §2 F.
*The change:* PLAN keeps the last look, the next wake and what each seat is doing as rows — for the engine and the
seats only. The same row is needed for the outbox executor, the voice line, the model door (the gateway answers; the
subscription window is known or unknown), each lane's readiness (the card), and each channel, connector and tool
server — written by code on the part's own beat or by a clock routine's probe, never by a model. With it, one
decision: the engine's beat at rest is the one-minute sweep, so a stopped engine can show no sooner than a missed
sweep; a shorter beat is one row update, no model. *Reason:* his premise — alive at rest, every beat riding the system's
real liveness and stopping with it <!-- CEO-OK: alive-at-rest-like-a-body-2026-10-19 --> — and the rivals:
`HEARTBEAT` on every card (22 V), `0/21 systems live` (30, 33 V) and `16/22 systems live` (35 (V)), the tool shelf
with a state per account (32 (V)), a probe with a 2.5 s timeout so a slow vendor shows as an honest error (35 (V)), three polling speeds that
are themselves a status (34 (R)). *Without it:* at rest only the engine and the seats have a true signal; Hamza, the
flows between the parts and the tool shelf either stand still or move on nothing.

**F2 — Which writes tell a surface to look.** *Touches:* §2 E (first bullet), verdict row 6. *The change:* PLAN's
event is written at a state change. Say whether a tool call's start and end, a desk entry, a new artifact version, a
new attempt and an outbox row's move also notify. *Reason:* the rivals' beats between hand-overs — the step named
while it runs (23 V), `called 2 tools` (35 (V)), the named feed (24 V), the activity panel fed by the tools' own
instrumentation with no reporting step (OpenMontage, (R)). *Without it:* a part that works for twenty minutes shows one
beat when it is claimed and one when it ends.

**F3 — A conversation turn carries where he stands and where the answer lives; Hamza can put a surface in front of
him.** *Touches:* §2 D (the conversation store, named in one line and not drawn), §2 C (an inward "show this" act at
the tool door), §2 I (the page he is on is per-turn material and belongs last in the assembler's order). The store
also has to say whom he is talking with and about which job (A26: "connect me to this job's master"). *Reason:*
01, 09, 12, 21, 24, 32, 33 — the view lands on what the sentence names within half a second (12 V, T), in 2.2 s and
before the voice (32 (V)), and a docked panel prints what the assistant sees (33 T, V); the fourth clause of the first
law; C26. *Without it:* that clause and C26 arrive later as a change to the conversation store and to the assembler's
order — the seams PLAN orders for the cache.

**F4 — Time is something a job or a part can wait on.** *Touches:* §2 D (jobs: "what it waits on"; parts), §2 F
(the sweep wakes it; the clock stays for system routines). *Reason:* every revenue system of 28 is a time relative to
one record ((T)); a lead that fades at 21 days and is archived at 90 (33 V); publishing on its own clock (17 V, C); a
next wake printed on the board (35 (V)); `once` tasks (16 R). B83's customer desk is made of such waits. *Without it:*
every reminder, follow-up, scheduled publication and retry-later becomes a clock routine — which §2 F reserves for the

## 4. Installing an application

**The question.** On this foundation, what does adding a job type or a business line consist of — and does PLAN
state that in one place, completely enough that a web-studio or an advertising job type is added without touching the
foundation's tables?

**What PLAN states, and where.**

- §2 B, the closest thing to the contract: "the web studio, the media studio and the advertising agency are cupboards
  of staff (departments) and job types on the menu"; their clients and money belong to the company that sells, "a
  company of its own the day he founds one, by one row"; a place where a company sells is a `channels` row; watching
  and running one is a line whose runs are jobs.
- §2 A: the job-type menu, the approval rules, the routing rules, settings and budgets are his policy — seeded once,
  then the database is the truth, changed through his doors. Event types, the handler registry, tool tags and role
  ceilings are vocabularies the code owns: the file is the truth.
- §2 D: `job_types`, `job_patterns` (versioned), `lines` (the line card: standing owner, schedule or event, budget,
  target, autonomy step; versioned, undoable); a job keeps the pattern and book versions it started on; a part names
  its lane and its seat role.
- §2 C: what a seat may do is its role's ceiling and its tools; §3 item 5: a seat receives the tools of its own role
  only; §4 item 3: a seat's tools are part of what it is given.
- §2 G: routing rules in the database. §2 H: the rule table classifies every outward request in the database; one
  handler registry, and the executor refuses to start when its handlers and the registry differ.
- §2 I, §3, §4: the assembler loads the short part of the job type's book; the book is B64's.
- §6 stage 0: one scan of the board's open rows — "does this row need something from the foundation that this plan
  does not carry?" — is the way an application's needs come back into the plan.

**The answer.** It is stated in pieces, not in one place, and not completely. Rows alone carry the plain case: a
department, its agents, a menu entry, a pattern, tool grants, approval and routing rules, a line card, a budget.
Past the plain case an application reaches into what the foundation's code owns, and PLAN does not say how.

**What is missing — named, not designed.**

1. **No single list of what a job type registers.** Half of it is outside PLAN: the menu card's "when chosen" text
   and examples (A26), the book's folder (B64's note of 2026-11-08), the service in the price policy that maps a
   signed deal to its type (REVENUE_ENGINE §7sexies 6), the "service type" his eye is lifted by (APPROVAL_ENGINE A9).
2. **`job_types` has a name and no stated content.** Which master owns the type's jobs; its starting seats (B84
   decision 6: three core seats and called hands); the checks its jobs must pass (B84's release gates and sample
   gate, B43's QC list) — the law of §2 D refuses a close "while a mandatory part or check is open", and nothing
   says where a type declares which are mandatory; and its rung: a line carries an autonomy step, a job type carries
   none, though B15's note of 2026-11-08 lifts his eye per job type.
3. **Where a book is registered and versioned.** A job carries a book version; §2 names no table and no path rule
   for books.
4. **Seat roles and lanes are code-owned or unstated.** Role ceilings are a file the code owns (§2 A). Whether a job
   type may bring a seat role — a creative director, a shot router — without a change to that file is not said.
   A part's lane decides its capacity (§2 F: seats, the studio's hands, machine work); whether a new kind of machine
   work is a new lane, and where its capacity is read, is not said.
5. **An application's outward acts.** Publishing a site, delivering a film, posting to a channel, sending a WhatsApp:
   each is a handler inside the executor and a line in a code-owned registry. How an application adds one is not
   said; by §2 A it is a change to the foundation's files.
6. **An application's own records.** §11 leaves the studios' records to B43 and B84. PLAN gives the rule every such
   row must obey (the job's company on it, by composite key) and not the convention: its own tables in the same
   chain, its own door functions, its own service login or the engine's. The foundation's tables are not touched;
   its chain is — and that is unsaid.
7. **Events.** The catalogue is closed and code-owned. Whether an application's moments — a shot rendered, a page
   deployed — are new types or ride the part's transitions is not said.
8. **A part worked under another type's book.** The Web Studio's site needs film from the Media Studio (B84's leg
   and B43's leg of 2026-11-18: one call form, priced on the same card). A26 makes it a part of the site master's
   plan; nothing says that part runs with the media type's book, checks and price card.
9. **The order half.** His description of 2026-11-22 runs from order to delivery. Offers, deals, the price policy
   and contracts are "inventory first" in verdict row 28d and have no target in §2; stage 1's story starts at a
   mail and skips the deal. An application that is sold has no stated place for its service and its price.
10. **The surface.** How an application's area on his screen reads its rows — directly, or through the read contracts
    verdict row 27 leaves to an inventory — is not said.
11. **Retiring or upgrading an application.** A running job keeps the versions it started on; switching a job type
    off, or removing it, has no stated rule.

**So, to his question:** a web-studio or an advertising job type can be added without changing the foundation's
*tables* in the plain case. It cannot be added today without changing the foundation's *files* — at the least the
handler registry, probably the role ceilings, the event catalogue and a lane — and without the adaptation of
AGENT_ORCHESTRATION A26–A30 that B84's own plan already schedules for itself. That is the gap F0 names.
