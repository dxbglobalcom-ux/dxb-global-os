## 3. Capabilities — the mechanism this source describes

Seven named systems, ranked, with a stated reason for each. What follows is the reason he gives,
then what a search of the primary sources says about it — because the CEO's order for this source

### D tier — "the quickest way to lose trust"

**AI content `T` (00:05.96–00:16.36).** Ranked lowest, and his rule is a boundary rather than a
dismissal: *"You might want to automate the research and the analytics, but don't automate your
authenticity."*

**What the outside evidence says, measured this session `R`:** his direction is carried by 2026
survey data. Consumer trust in AI-powered search fell from **82 % to 54 % in twelve months**; the
share of consumers saying heavy AI use would *decrease* trust in a brand **doubled from 20 % to
40 %** over the same year; **39 %** say they would trust a brand less for using AI-generated
content; **83 %** report they can spot an AI video and **36 %** say it lowers brand trust; and only
**20 %** of organisations always disclose AI use against **84 %** of consumers who want it labelled.

**Chatbot `T` (00:16.36–00:25.08).** *"It's like a checkbox. You gotta have it, but is it really
gonna multiply your revenue? No way."* — a support cost, not a revenue multiplier.
`U` **This one was NOT settled by research in this pass.** What would settle it: a controlled
revenue-per-visitor comparison with and without a chat widget, which no source in this pass
provided. It is recorded as his position, not as a verified finding.

### A tier — money that was already earned once

**Reactivation `T` (00:25.08–00:30.56).** *"You're already paid for them once and AI will just make
them pay you again."*
**Outside evidence `R`:** win-back is repeatedly reported at **20–40 %** conversion against **5–20 %**
for a cold prospect; reactivating a lapsed customer is put at **5–7× cheaper** than acquiring a new
one; a typical direct-to-consumer base carries **30–50 %** of its customers in a lapsed state at any
moment. These are vendor and practitioner benchmarks rather than one controlled study, and are
labelled as such.

**No-show win-back `T` (00:30.56–00:41.88).** *"You can't be forgetting to rebook the calls. AI will
chase those leads down for you and get them on the calendar. Unsexy, but it prints money."*
**Outside evidence `R`:** the average no-show rate across service industries is reported at **≈ 23 %**
(healthcare ≈ 27 %, dental without reminders ≈ 30 %); a Cochrane systematic review is cited for
automated reminders reducing no-shows **by up to 50 %**, with SMS alone at ≈ 38 %. This is the item
with the strongest independent literature behind it, and he calls it the least glamorous.

### S tier — the two that multiply

**Upsell `T` (00:41.88–00:52.12).** *"You have all this data about your customer, so using AI to tell
you what they're gonna buy next is a solid way to make money."*
**Outside evidence `R`:** the figure this rests on is Paul Farris's *Marketing Metrics* — **the
probability of selling to an existing customer is 60–70 %, to a new prospect 5–20 %.** It is a
textbook rule of thumb, quoted everywhere and not a published dataset; recorded here with that
limit stated.

**Speed-to-lead `T` (00:52.12–00:62.36), and this is the claim the whole reel turns on.** His
sentence: *"Answering your lead in 60 seconds is a surefire way to make 21 times more money than
everybody that doesn't."*

**The primary source was found and read in full this session `R`** — the 2007 *Lead Response
Management* study, Dr James B. Oldroyd of MIT's Sloan School with InsideSales.com, three years of
data across six companies, **15,000+ leads and 100,000+ call attempts**. Its finding, quoted
verbatim from the study's own page 3:

> *"4‐ The odds of contacting a lead if called in 5 minutes versus 30 minutes drop 100 times. The
> odds of qualifying a lead if called in 5 minutes versus 30 minutes drop 21 times."*

**So the study's own sentence differs from the reel's in two places, and both matter to anyone
building on it:** the window is **5 minutes**, not 60 seconds; and the 21× is the **odds of
qualifying a lead**, not money earned. The **100×** figure — the odds of making contact at all — is
the larger of the two and is the one the reel leaves out. **The direction he is selling is carried
by the strongest dataset on this whole queue; the two numbers in his sentence belong to a different
sentence in it.** For a build, the study's own threshold is the one to engineer against, and the
study adds a second usable finding the reel never mentions: **the odds of contacting fall more than
10× within the first hour**, so the curve is steep from the first minute rather than at a cliff.

### What must NOT be copied, and why

**The 60-second number, quoted as his.** If this holding sells speed-to-lead, it sells the figure the
study publishes with the study named. Selling a service on a number that is not in the source it
comes from is how a consultancy loses a client on the first audit — and it collides with this

**The ranking as a plan.** He is ranking systems for a small operator with customers already in
hand. This holding has none yet by the CEO's own decision, so his A and S tiers are **things to
sell**, not things to install internally — and reading them the other way would build a
reactivation engine for zero customers.
