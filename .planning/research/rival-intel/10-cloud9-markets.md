### 2.2 The three mechanics that make it feel alive

1. **The speaker is lit.** Only the node currently talking is bright; the rest are dim. There is
   never any doubt about who is speaking.
2. **The speaker is named in a chip** under the centre — `You`, `+ATLAS`, `+ORACLE`,
   `+SENTINEL` — so the state is legible even in a still frame.
3. **The words appear on the HUD**, not in a side panel. The screen carries the conversation
   instead of hosting it next to something else.

officer and the book of bad calls. We do not take the desk.** If the CEO ever wants a market
capability, that is his ruling to make separately, with the boundary question answered first.

## 6. Verdict

**`daha iyisi` — we have ten times the company and none of the theatre, and the theatre is the
part the CEO is missing.**

- **They have nine agents and show all nine.** We have **199** and show none. Their whole
  advantage is one screen.
- **Their governance is a line of dialogue; ours is enforced.** "Sentinel signs off, I execute"
  is a nice sentence. Our outbox refuses to send without an allowlisted approval. The right move
  is to make our real gate **visible and voiced** the way theirs is — the CEO should *hear*
  that the risk officer said no, because in our system it actually did.
- **Their best line is already our law.** "Probabilities, darling. Never promises." is RULE
  #0-A. We enforce it; they perform it. Nothing stops us doing both.
- **The one thing to take without changing anything else:** the constellation. It costs no new
  data — `agent_runs` and `tasks` already carry everything it needs — and it converts the CEO's
  "the panels are dead" into "I can see my company working" in a single surface.
