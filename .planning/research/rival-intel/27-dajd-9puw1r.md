## 3. Capabilities — the mechanism this source describes

Four parts, in his order, and the fourth is the one he calls decisive.

**1 — The qualifier `T` (00:04.44–00:13.92).** As the following grows, the agent **scores each new
person against a written ideal client profile**, then **starts the qualifying conversation itself**
in the messaging channel, and carries it to one of two ends: **a call booked, or a purchase**. Three
things are stacked in that one sentence — a stored definition of who is worth talking to, a score
per person against it, and an agent with a hand in the conversation channel.

**2 — The content worker `T` (00:13.92–00:20.24).** It **studies what the buyers are actually
saying** and **builds the content that pulls in more people like the best existing clients**. The
input is the customer's own language; the output is marketing. It is a loop from the mouth of the
buyer back to the top of the funnel, and its raw material is a conversation that already happened.

**3 — The preparer `T` (00:20.24–00:38.60).** Before **every** call, a brief covering **what the
client is working on, what their status is, and what their goals are** — and the same worker
**runs the reminders and the scheduling**. He states the reason as a business rule rather than a
convenience: capacity and monthly revenue are *"directly related to all the prep work that goes
in."*

**4 — The glue `T` (00:38.60–00:48.32).** *"The glue that makes all these agents actually work is a
shared knowledge base… everything you can record, everything that's stuck in your head, needs to be
put in an actual file."* One store, written as files, shared by all three workers. He does not
present it as a fourth agent — he presents it as the condition under which the other three function
