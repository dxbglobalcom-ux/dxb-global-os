# Conflicts

## C1 — Speed/cost multiplier: 193x/444x (vendor) vs 4–7x/30–96x (six independent re-tests)
- **Claim A**: JEV is 193x faster and 444–445x cheaper than the alternative. **Source**: TypeSafe launch materials.
- **Claim B**: Real-world multiples are 4–7x faster and 30–96x cheaper. **Sources**: Islam (`benchmarks/2026-09-22-islam-jev-multiplier-reality-check.md`), LiteLLM/Khalil (`benchmarks/2026-09-20-litellm-khalil-jev-vs-haiku.md`, 5.43x/96%), Near Here/Reed, r6i/Reghenzi (7x, self-flagged as generous), Taparelli.
- **Possible explanation**: TypeSafe's own number compares JEV against MULTI-STEP FRONTIER-MODEL AGENTIC WORKFLOWS (many model calls, tool loops, judge passes). The independent re-tests mostly compare against SINGLE-CALL mid-tier models. A multiplier is "a property of a comparison, not of a model" (Islam's own words) — both numbers can be honestly measured under their own stated conditions.
- **Unresolved?** No — this is EXPLAINED, not contradictory, once the comparison baseline is made explicit. Opus should treat "193x/444x" as the CEILING under a specific favorable comparison, and "4–7x/30–96x" as the EXPECTED range for a single-call replacement scenario, which is the shape most DXB candidate uses would take.

## C2 — Calibration quality: well-calibrated (Lindfors) vs poorly-calibrated (scienthoon, AnthusAI, colinmcnamara)
- **Claim A**: JEV's confidence is meaningfully calibrated — "when Jev said about 0.8, the reference agreed at least 80 percent of the time." **Source**: Lindfors, policy-document stance/argument classification task (`benchmarks/2026-09-18-lindfors-jev-vs-deepseek-policy-docs.md`).
- **Claim B**: JEV's confidence is poorly calibrated, task- and primitive-type-dependent, with `Score` reaching 74% mean confidence at only 44.7% actual accuracy. **Sources**: scienthoon (OOD ticket routing), AnthusAI (sentiment classification), colinmcnamara (mixed task set) — all in `negative-evidence/02_calibration-problems.md`.
- **Possible explanation**: Different task types and different question PRIMITIVES (Choice vs Score vs Noul) show different calibration quality — this is not a simple "good" or "bad" verdict on JEV globally. TypeSafe's own official jaggedness doc (`official/docs/jev-1.13-model-jaggedness.md`) does NOT specifically warn about calibration variance by primitive type, which is itself a gap between vendor documentation and independent findings.
- **Unresolved?** YES, genuinely unresolved as a general property. What IS resolved: calibration cannot be assumed and must be measured per task, per primitive type, before any unattended threshold-based use. No DXB-shaped task has been independently calibration-tested.

## C3 — Novelty/priority dispute: "genuinely new architecture" vs "prior art exists"
- **Claim A** (implicit in TypeSafe's positioning and most press coverage): JEV/System One models are a new architectural category. TypeSafe's own homepage hedges this to "the first PUBLIC system-one model" (noted by Adam Gardner as likely deliberate, lawyer-reviewed phrasing) — not "the first system-one model," full stop.
- **Claim B**: HN user `niutech` claims a project "now called Laya" predates JEV by about a year with a similar architecture; HN user `nandakishor_ml` claims to have published a similar architecture on arXiv roughly a year earlier (associated with PyPI package `hallunox`).
- **Claim B, expanded with named source and direct quotes** (via `experts/videos/00_VIDEO_INDEX.md` #7, Adam Gardner's video, which relays Nandakishor Mukkunnoth's own LinkedIn/dev.to posts verbatim): Mukkunnoth states he built a non-autoregressive structured-decision architecture starting March 2025 — "months of hard work, sweat, and sleepless nights" — published a paper, released model weights on HuggingFace, published an open dataset, built a PyPI package, and posted the approach on Reddit. He published a second paper in September 2025 describing "the exact framework for schema-based decisions guided by reinforcement learning," and states the guiding mechanism was always reinforcement learning, not just an embedding model or autoregressive LLM. His own words on JEV's September 2026 launch: TypeSafe "proposed the exact same non-auto-regressive decision concept as if it was a brand new scientific breakthrough... without technical papers, without open weights, and with zero open training datasets." His response: open-sourced his own system as **Laya**, stating he "fixed every architectural limitation of the old approach" and built "a completely open, horizontal, system-one decision model, RL agent."
- **Counter to Claim B**: HN user `verdverm` disputes this, arguing the cited prior paper describes an embeddings+RAG+orchestrator system, not the same typed-primitive architecture, and questions its academic rigor.
- **Additional independent data point** (via `negative-evidence/07_fleet-counter-evidence.md`, item 6): on large label sets (77 options, Banking77-scale), Laya's accuracy reportedly collapses (38%, ECE 0.51) while JEV holds up; on small label sets (2-4 options), the two are comparable and Laya is faster (7.8x) with tighter calibration after temperature scaling. This is evidence about RELATIVE PERFORMANCE, not about who invented what first — it does not resolve the priority dispute, but it does mean "Laya is a viable open alternative" is itself task-shape-dependent, not a blanket truth.
- **Unresolved?** YES — this is a genuine, unadjudicated priority dispute, now with a named, quotable, directly-accountable claimant (Mukkunnoth) rather than an anonymous HN handle. Still not independently investigated at the primary-source level (the disputed papers and the `hallunox` package were not read directly in this research pass). Flagged for Opus or future research, not resolved here.

## C4 — "Zero hallucinations" — schema-validity guarantee vs semantic correctness
- **Claim A**: JEV has "zero hallucinations." **Source**: TypeSafe marketing framing.
- **Claim B**: This only means the OUTPUT always conforms to the requested schema/type — it does not mean the CONTENT is correct. JEV can and does produce confidently wrong typed answers (76% tool-hallucination rate in `baibizhe/jev-decision-benchmarks`'s no-tool-needed test; the backnotprop poker trap-hand failure; TypeSafe's own CEO publicly admitting "it's also possible to be confidently wrong").
- **Unresolved?** No — this is a terminology/scope clarification, not a factual contradiction. Both claims are technically true under their own definition of "hallucination." Opus should treat "zero hallucinations" as meaning "zero schema-malformed output," full stop, never as "zero wrong answers."

## C5 — Uptime/reliability: self-reported high uptime (99.84–99.99%) vs zero independent monitoring
- **Claim A**: status.typesafe.ai reports 99.839% API uptime, 99.988% console uptime.
- **Claim B**: No independent third-party uptime monitor's data was found corroborating or contradicting this figure — it is entirely self-reported.
- **Unresolved?** Partially — the status page format (Better Stack-backed) is a standard, harder-to-quietly-fabricate pattern, and it DID record a real outage in launch week (2026-09-20/21) rather than showing a suspiciously perfect record, which is a mild point in favor of its honesty. But no independent cross-check exists. Treat the specific percentage as UNVERIFIED beyond the vendor's own (plausible, self-disclosing-of-flaws) status page.

## C6 — "TypeSafe skipped published evals" vs "a published evaluation exists, checked against reference models across four workflows"
- **Claim A**: backnotprop's poker write-up states plainly: "TypeSafe skipped published evals in this release." **Source**: `benchmarks/2026-09-17-backnotprop-jev-poker.md`.
- **Claim B**: An r/aigossips comment (call_me_ninza, score 13) states a "published evaluation checks agreement with reference models across four workflows" — i.e. a published eval DOES exist. **Source**: `community/discussions/reddit-hn-findings.md` #3.
- **Possible explanation**: These may describe two different things — backnotprop may mean no eval specific to *this release* (jev-1.13) was published, while the Reddit comment may refer to the general model-card-level four-workflow eval (`official/evals.md`, the same four named workflows: security_incidents, agent_trace_observability, invoice_processing, customer_service) published at initial launch and not re-run per point release. Not confirmed — both readings are plausible from the source text alone.
- **Unresolved?** YES. Neither source was re-contacted or re-read for clarification (out of scope for corpus-building). Opus should treat "does TypeSafe publish evals" as **release-version-dependent and unresolved**, not as a settled yes/no — check `official/evals.md`'s own stated scope (which workflows, which model version) directly before relying on either claim.
